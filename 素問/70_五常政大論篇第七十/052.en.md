# 素問 五常政大論篇第七十 §52 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Thus the governing qi has what it restrains, and when the year is established it has what it generates. Earth's qi restrains what it overcomes; heaven's qi restrains what overcomes it. Heaven restrains color; earth restrains form. The decline and abundance of the five kinds each follow what suits their qi. So there is conception without growth and incomplete treatment; this is the norm of qi, the so-called inner rooting. Those rooted outside are also five. Hence the distinctions of generation and transformation are the five qi, five flavors, five colors, five kinds and five suitabilities."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Earth's qi restrains what it overcomes; heaven's qi restrains what overcomes it. Heaven restrains color; earth restrains form"] Heaven's qi restrains according to what does not overcome oneself, meaning it restrains color; earth's qi restrains according to what oneself overcomes, meaning it restrains form. Hence "heaven restrains color; earth restrains form". Thus between heaven and earth the generation and transformation of the five kinds mutually overcome, mutually transform, mutually generate and mutually restrain.

[On "The decline and abundance of the five kinds each follow what suits their qi"] When suited, they thrive and multiply.

[On "So there is conception without growth and incomplete treatment; this is the norm of qi"] Living things between heaven and earth are of these five kinds: furred, feathered, naked, scaled and shelled. Hence it is said: "Of the three hundred and sixty furred creatures the *lin* is chief; of the three hundred and sixty feathered creatures the phoenix is chief; of the three hundred and sixty naked creatures man is chief; of the three hundred and sixty scaled creatures the dragon is chief; of the three hundred and sixty shelled creatures the tortoise is chief." All things with form that crawl, fly or run, that breathe or breathe in the womb, great and small, high and low, green, yellow, red, white and black, whose bodies are covered with fur, feathers, scales or shells, are generally called creatures (虫); those without any of these four are naked creatures. All these five have live birth, egg birth, birth from moisture, and birth by transformation. Because the person asked, the five kinds are mentioned.

[On "the so-called inner rooting"] Where the root of the living qi issues from within the body, it is inner rooting. For things not of these five kinds, the rooting of living qi depends wholly on external things for its establishment; take them away and the living qi is cut off.

[On "Those rooted outside are also five"] This means the kinds of the five flavors and five colors. The forms of wood, fire, earth, metal and water can generate and transform only by borrowing the 色蔵 of external things; once the external things are gone, the living qi departs and is cut off; hence they are all rooted outside.
Song editors: On examination, the two characters 色蔵 in the note should be 已成 ("already formed").

[On "Hence the distinctions of generation and transformation are the five qi, five flavors, five colors, five kinds and five suitabilities"] These twenty-five are all present in both inner and outer rooting. The five qi are rank, scorched, fragrant, fishy and putrid; the five flavors sour, bitter, acrid, salty and sweet; the five colors green, yellow, red, white and black. The five kinds are of two sorts: one is furred, feathered, naked, scaled and shelled; the other is dry, damp, liquid, hard and soft. Such things have mutual suitabilities among the myriad things.

## Notes

The principle by which the qi of heaven and earth restrain the five kinds, and the distinction between inner rooting (spirit mechanism) and outer rooting (qi-established). Qibo's speech ends here.
- 已 in 制已勝 and 制勝已 in the main text is read as 己 "oneself".
