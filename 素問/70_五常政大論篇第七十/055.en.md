# 素問 五常政大論篇第七十 §55 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Emperor said: "When qi begins, there is generation and transformation; when qi disperses, there is form; when qi spreads, there is flourishing; when qi ends, the image changes. What they bring about is one. Yet in what the five flavors depend on, generation and transformation are thin or thick, ripening is less or more, and beginnings and ends differ. Why?"

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When qi begins ... What they bring about is one"] 始 means first stirring; 散 means flowing and dispersing into things; 布 means spreading the transformation into formed shapes; 終 means reaching the limit in the function of gathering and storing. So first it moves and generates and transforms, flows and disperses to have form, spreads and transforms to form, and at the end all phenomena change. Verified by fact, things with form between heaven and earth are soft and weak at birth and hard and stiff at death; all such are the forms and substances changing at the times of life and death. This is called the end of qi.
Song editors: The *Tianyuan ji dalun* says "the birth of things is called transformation; the extreme of things is called change", and the *Liuwei zhi dalun* says "the birth of things follows transformation; the extreme of things comes from change; the pressing together of change and transformation is the source of success and failure".

## Notes

A question on why the generation and transformation of the five flavors differ despite the single working of qi.
