# 素問 五常政大論篇第七十 §39 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "When lesser yang controls heaven, fire qi presides below and lung qi follows above; white [things] rise [in price] and metal is put to use; plants suffer calamity; fire appears burning; hides and metal are also used up; great summer heat proceeds; there are cough, sneezing, nasal congestion and nosebleed, blocked nose, sores and ulcers, cold and heat, and puffy swelling. Wind moves on the earth, dust and sand fly up; there are heart pain, pain in the stomach duct, reversal and obstruction of the diaphragm. What it governs is violence and speed.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When lesser yang controls heaven ... and puffy swelling"] The signs of *yin* and *shen* years. 臨 means presiding over what is below; 従 means serving what is above; 起 means prices rising in the market; 用 means applying punishment. 臨, 従, 起 and 用 are alike throughout. 革 means hides, and also means change. 金 means utensils. 耗 means being spent. Fire qi burns and scorches, so "sores arise"; 瘡 means sores on the body and 瘍 sores on the head. Cold and heat means first chills and then fever, that is, malaria. The lungs are harmed by heat, and water comes to their rescue; water guards within the lungs, so there is puffy swelling, which means swelling that does not rebound when pressed. These are produced by heaven's qi.
Song editors: On examination, the note says "hence sores arise; 瘡 is sores on the body, 瘍 sores on the head", but the classic has only 曰瘍. We suspect a 瘡 has dropped out of the classic. Another edition writes 口 for 曰.

[On "Wind moves on the earth ... What it governs is violence and speed"] *Jueyin* is in the spring, so wind moves on the earth. Where wind runs rampant and prevails, these illnesses arise. The transformations of lesser yang and *jueyin* are swift, so the qi of illness arises quickly; hence "what it governs is violence and speed". They arise because the qi is not in accord.
Song editors: On examination, when *jueyin* or lesser yang is in the spring, it says "what it governs is violence and speed" or "its outbreak is as swift as a trigger", so it does not say "in severe cases such-and-such illness".

## Notes

The weather and illnesses from heaven's qi and the earth qi in the spring (*jueyin*) in a year when lesser yang controls heaven. Qibo's speech continues to unit 044.
- 曰瘍 is obscure. Wang Bing explains it as 瘡瘍; the Song editors suspect a missing 瘡, and another edition reads 口瘍. The translation follows Wang Bing with "sores and ulcers".
