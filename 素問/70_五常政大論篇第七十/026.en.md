# 素問 五常政大論篇第七十 §26 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "It is due to the qi of yin and yang, the principle of high and low, and the difference of greater and lesser. The southeast is yang; the essence of yang descends below; hence it is hot on the right and warm on the left. The northwest is yin; the essence of yin is offered upward; hence it is cold on the left and cool on the right. Thus the land has high and low, and the qi has warmth and coolness: where it is high the qi is cold, where it is low the qi is hot. So those who go to cold, cool places get distension, and those who go to warm, hot places get sores. Purging cures the distension; sweating cures the sores. This is the norm of the opening and closing of the interstices and the difference of greater and lesser."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "It is due to the qi of yin and yang, the principle of high and low, and the difference of greater and lesser"] High and low refer to the terrain; greater and lesser refer to the difference in abundance and decline of the qi of yin and yang. Now in the terrain of the central plain, the northwest is high and the southeast low; the west is cool, the north cold, the east warm and the south hot; the transformation of qi is likewise.

[On "The southeast is yang ... hot on the right and warm on the left"] The essence of yang descends, so the land is known to be low by its warmth. Yang qi arises in the east and is abundant in the south; hence the east is warm and the south hot; the amount of qi is clear.

[On "The northwest is yin ... cold on the left and cool on the right"] The essence of yin is offered upward, so the land is known to be high by its cold. Yin qi arises in the west and is abundant in the north; hence the west is cool and the north cold. The ruler speaks facing Xun (southeast), and the minister answers facing Qian (northwest).
Song editors: On examination, the account of heaven and earth being insufficient and of yin and yang is also given in the *Yinyang qi*[*ying*]*xiang dalun*.

[On "Thus the land has high and low ... where it is low the qi is hot"] Song editors: The *Liuyuan zhengji dalun* says "on the highest land winter qi is always present; on the lowest land spring qi is always present".

[On "So those who go to cold, cool places get distension ... the difference of greater and lesser"] Northwest and southeast speak of the large scale. Verifying by climate, in the inhabited terrain of the central plain, wherever one lives high it is cold, and wherever one lives low it is hot. Observing it, high mountains have much snow and level river plains much rain; high mountains are mostly cold and level plains mostly hot; thus the cold and heat of high and low can be verified. In the land of China, the major divisions of high and low are threefold both east–west and north–south. North–south: first, from Han, Shu and Jiangnan to the sea; second, from north of the Han River to Pingyao county; third, from the northern mountains north of Pingyao to the northern sea at the frontier. So the southern division is very hot, the middle division half cold and half hot, and the northern division very cold. Beyond the southern and northern divisions, cold and heat are even more extreme. In the very hot division the cold is slight; in the very cold division the heat is slight. But if one climbs to the top of a very high mountain, the south face and north face differ widely in cold and heat, and their flourishing and withering differ twofold. East–west there are also three divisions of high and low: first, from Qianyuan county west to Shazhou; second, from Kaifeng county west to Qianyuan county; third, from Kaifeng county east to the sea. So the eastern division is very warm, the middle division half warm and half cool, and the western division very cool. In the very warm division the cold is two-fifths; in the very cool division the heat is two-fifths. Beyond the warm and cool divisions, warmth and coolness are even more extreme, changing into great warmth and great cold. Roughly, this is the general outline. In the land divided into nine, cold is extreme in the northeast and heat in the southwest. Within the land divided into nine there are also differences of high and low: high places are damp and low places dry; this is a small difference within one region. Speaking on the large scale, high and low are one. Why? The terrain of the central plain is high in the west and north and low in the east and south; now the hundred rivers all converge eastward to the sea; so the high and low of southeast and northwest can be known. First, because the terrain is high or low, cold and heat differ; second, because the qi of yin and yang are less or more, differences of warmth and coolness appear. Now verifying by climate: spring qi moves west, autumn qi moves east, winter qi moves south, summer qi moves north. Checking the middle division, from Kaifeng to Qianyuan the climate exactly matches the calendar. Checking eastward, from Kaifeng to the sea, for every hundred *li* autumn qi arrives a day later and spring qi issues a day earlier. Checking westward, from Qianyuan county west to the stony desert of the frontier, in places facing south and northwest or southeast, for every forty *li* spring qi issues a day later and autumn qi arrives a day earlier; in places facing north and northeast or southwest, for every fifteen *li* spring qi issues a day later and autumn qi arrives a day earlier. Checking southward, where rivers face north and northeast or southwest, for every five hundred *li* [Song editors: another edition writes "fifteen *li*".] yang qi moves a day later and yin qi a day earlier; on rivers facing south and southeast or northwest, for every fifteen *li* hot qi arrives a day earlier and cold qi a day later; on broad level land, for every fifty *li* yang qi issues a day earlier and cold qi arrives a day later. Checking northward, where rivers face south and southeast or northwest, for every twenty-five *li* yang qi moves a day later and yin qi a day earlier; on rivers facing north and northeast or southwest, for every fifteen *li* cold qi arrives a day earlier and hot qi a day later; on broad level land, for every twenty *li* 然 [yang] qi moves a day later and cold qi arrives a day earlier. This is the general rate. Moreover, on high and steep places winter qi is always present; on level and low places summer qi is always present; this can be known by watching snow fall and grass grow. The land also has bow-shaped rivers, snaking rivers and crescent-shaped rivers; the terrain differs, and generation and killing, flourishing and withering differ by heaven though the land is the same. In such places, those facing Li, Bing, Xun, Yi and Zhen have spring qi arrive early and autumn qi late, the difference being fifteen days; those facing Ding, Kun, Geng, Dui, Xin, Qian, Kan and Gen have autumn qi arrive early and spring qi late, the difference again being twenty days. This is the so-called land girdled by mountains. By carefully observing which way it faces, the climate can be known. In cold, cool lands, the interstices open little and close much; closing much, yang qi does not disperse; hence on going to cold, cool places the abdomen is sure to be distended. In damp, hot lands, the interstices open much and close little; opening much, yang disperses; hence on going to warm, hot places the skin is sure to have sores. Purging leaves no surplus of central qi, so the distension stops; sweating lets yang qi leak outward, so the sores heal.

## Notes

Cold and heat differ by the height of the terrain and the amount of yin and yang qi, and illnesses and treatments differ accordingly. Wang Bing divides China into three zones both east–west and north–south and discusses in detail the differences of climate by terrain and aspect.
- 陰陽気象大論 in the Song editors' note is presumably an error for 陰陽応象大論.
- Where the Song editors' note is inserted in Wang Bing's note (after 毎五百里), Wang Bing's note is read as continuing afterward. 然 in 然気行晩一日 is translated as an error for 陽 by analogy with the surrounding examples, but this has not been checked.
- The place names in Wang Bing's note (Han, Shu, Pingyao, Qianyuan, Shazhou, Kaifeng, etc.) are translated as written.
