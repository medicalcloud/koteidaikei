# 素問 五常政大論篇第七十 §58 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Cold, heat, dryness and damp differ in their transformations. So when lesser yang is in the spring, cold poisons do not arise; its flavor acrid [transforms little]; what it treats is bitter and sour; its grains are green and red. When *yangming* is in the spring, damp poisons do not arise; its flavor sour [transforms little]; its qi is damp; what it treats is acrid, bitter and sweet; its grains are red and white. When greater yang is in the spring, hot poisons do not arise; its flavor bitter [transforms little]; what it treats is bland and salty; its grains are yellow and black millet. When *jueyin* is in the spring, cool poisons do not arise; its flavor sweet [transforms little]; what it treats is sour and bitter; its grains are green and red; its qi is unified and its flavors proper. When lesser yin is in the spring, cold poisons do not arise; its flavor acrid [transforms little]; what it treats is acrid, bitter and sweet; its grains are white and red. When greater yin is in the spring, dry poisons do not arise; its flavor salty [transforms little]; its qi is hot; what it treats is sweet and salty; its grains are yellow and black millet. When transformation is mild, saltiness holds; when qi is unified, acridity transforms and they govern together.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Cold, heat, dryness and damp differ in their transformations"] Citing that the four qi cold, heat, dryness and damp differ, it can be known that warmth and coolness also transform differently.

[On "So when lesser yang is in the spring ... its grains are green and red"] The qi transformation of 己 [*si*] and *hai* years. Great poisons are all produced by the abundant and violent qi at the tips of the five phases. Now fire is within the earth and its qi is truly hot; things with cold poison differ in qi from the earth, and their life and death are not the same; hence few arise. Fire restrains metal qi, so things of acrid flavor do not transform. The qi of lesser yang serves *jueyin* above; hence that year transforms bitter and sour. Of the six qi governing the years, only this year is harmonious throughout, wood and fire supporting each other; hence there is no intervening qi. Bitter and red are transformed by earth's qi; sour and green are generated by heaven's qi. All the other generations and transformations have overcoming between above and below; hence they all have intervening qi.

[On "When *yangming* is in the spring, damp poisons do not arise; its flavor sour; its qi is damp"] Song editors: On examination, among the springs, only in years when *yangming* or greater yin is in the spring does it say "its qi is damp" or "its qi is hot", presumably because dampness and dryness do not yet show the qi of cold or warmth, so "its qi" is stated again.

[On "what it treats is acrid, bitter and sweet; its grains are red and white"] The qi transformation of *zi* and *wu* years. Dryness is within the earth, its qi cool and clear; hence medicines with damp-warm poison seldom generate and transform. Metal and wood restrain each other, so things of sour flavor transform little. The qi of *yangming* serves lesser yin above; hence that year transforms acrid and bitter. Acrid and white are earth's qi, bitter and red heaven's qi, sweet the intervening qi; sweet is also treated, to intervene in the overcoming of metal and fire.

[On "When greater yang is in the spring ... yellow and black millet"] The qi transformation of *chou* and *wei* years. Cold is within the earth and [differs] from heat in the transformation of flavors; hence that year things with hot poison do not arise. 木 [wood?] overcomes fire, so the flavor should be bitter [not transforming]. The qi of greater yang serves greater yin above; hence that year transforms bland and salty. The earth qi of 大 [greater] yin rises to heaven, its qi far and high, so the transformation of sweetness is thin and becomes blandness. Bland flavor also belongs to sweet, of the sweet kind. Bland and yellow are heaven's transformation; salty and black millet are earth's transformation. 黅 means yellow.
Song editors: On examination, 味故当苦 in the note should be 故味苦者不化 ("hence things of bitter flavor do not transform"); it is a copying error.

[On "When *jueyin* is in the spring ... its grains are green and red"] The qi transformation of *yin* and *shen* years. Warmth is within the earth and differs in nature from clarity; hence that year things with cool poison do not arise. Wood overcomes earth, so things of sweet flavor transform little. The qi of *jueyin* unites with lesser yang above, and the uniting qi has no discord; hence what it treats and transforms is sour and bitter. Sour and green are earth's transformation; bitter and red are heaven's transformation. There is no overcoming of qi, so it does not intervene with the transformation of sweetness.

[On "its qi is unified and its flavors proper"] In years when *jueyin* or lesser yang is in the spring, the transformation of qi is unified and its flavors pure and proper. But in the other years there is overcoming qi between above and below, so all have intervening qi and intervening flavors.

[On "When lesser yin is in the spring ... its grains are white and red"] The qi transformation of *mao* and *you* years. Heat is within the earth and transforms differently from cold; hence that year the cold poison of medicines is very slight. Fire qi melts metal, so things of acrid flavor transform little. So lesser yin and *yangming* govern heaven and generate in earth; hence what they treat is bitter and acrid. Bitter and red are nurtured by earth's qi, acrid and white generated by heaven's qi, and sweet is the intervening qi, to intervene and stop the conquest.

[On "When greater yin is in the spring ... yellow and black millet"] The qi transformation of *chen* and *xu* years. There is damp within the earth, unlike dryness; hence things with dry poison do not generate and transform. Earth is restrained by wood [?], so things of salty flavor transform little. The qi of greater yin receives greater yang above; hence that year transforms sweet and salty. Sweet and yellow are earth's transformation; salty and black millet heaven's transformation. Cold and damp do not greatly conflict, so the intervening qi is the same and the qi that is hot responds to it.

[On "When transformation is mild, saltiness holds; when qi is unified, acridity transforms and they govern together"] 淳 means mild. "Transformation is mild" refers to years when lesser yang is in the spring: fire comes to reside in water yet can generate and nurture; this is the saltiness of water holding itself without contending with fire in transformation. "Qi is unified" refers to the qi of years when *jueyin* is in the spring: wood resides in water and again transforms downward, and metal is not harmed; hence acridity again generates and transforms and flourishes together with saltiness. Only in these two years do the qi above and below have no threat of conquest; hence acridity can flourish together with saltiness and generate and transform. In all other years there are changes of overcoming between above and below, so in between, sweet flavor transforms as well to ease their restraint. The other three flavors, bitter, salty and sour, differ in their generation and transformation. Hence among medicines between heaven and earth, acrid and sweet ones are many.

## Notes

For each of the six qi in the spring, the poisons that do not arise, the flavor that transforms little, the flavors used in treatment, and the grains. Qibo's speech continues to unit 060.
- "Its flavor X" for each spring is read, following Wang Bing's note, as that flavor transforming little.
- 其其気熱 has a doubled 其.
- 己亥歳 in Wang Bing's note is read as 巳亥歳. 木勝火 and 土制於木 do not fit the context and may be errors, but this has not been checked.
