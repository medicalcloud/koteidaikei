# 素問 繆刺論篇第六十三 §13 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a pathogen lodges in the foot *yangqiao* vessel, it causes eye pain beginning from the inner canthus. Needle about half a *cun* below the outer ankle, two punctures each, needling the right for the left and the left for the right; it ends in about the time it takes to walk ten *li*.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When a pathogen lodges in the foot *yangqiao* vessel, it causes eye pain beginning from the inner canthus"] Its vessel starts at the foot, ascends to the head and connects to the inner canthus; hence when it is ill, the eye pain begins from the inner canthus. How is this shown? The *Eighty-one Difficulties* says: "The *yangqiao* vessel starts in the heel, follows the outer ankle upward and enters Fengchi." The *Zhenjing* says: "The *yinqiao* vessel enters the cheekbone, connects to the inner canthus, and joins the greater yang and *yangqiao* to ascend." Following this, [the *yangqiao*] reaches the inner canthus.

[On "Needle about half a *cun* below the outer ankle, two punctures each"] This means the Shenmai point, where the *yangqiao* arises, in the depression below the outer ankle, the size of a fingernail; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain six breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: On examination, the note to the 血脈痛 [presumably *Ci yaotong*] says "five *fen* below the outer ankle".

## Notes

Crosswise needling of the foot *yangqiao* vessel.
- The Song editors' 血脈痛注 gives the same content as the Song editors' note in the *Tiaojing lun* (刺腰痛注云在踝下五分), so it is presumably an error for 刺腰痛.
