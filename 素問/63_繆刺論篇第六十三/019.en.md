# 素問 繆刺論篇第六十三 §19 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a pathogen lodges in the network vessel of the foot lesser yin, it causes throat pain with inability to take in food, groundless proneness to anger, and qi rushing up above the *ben*. Needle the vessel in the center of the sole, three punctures each, six needlings in all, and it ends at once. Needle the right for the left and the left for the right. For swelling in the throat with inability to take in saliva, and at times inability to spit, needle in front of the Ran bone to let blood, and it ends at once. Needle the right for the left and the left for the right.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "it causes throat pain with inability to take in food ... qi rushing up above the *ben*"] The branch of its channel emerges from the lungs, networks the heart and pours into the chest. Also its main channel goes up from the kidneys through the liver and diaphragm, enters the lungs, follows the throat and flanks the root of the tongue. Hence the illness causes dryness and pain of the throat, inability to take in food, groundless proneness to anger, and qi rushing up above the *ben*. 賁 means the qi rushing (奔).
Song editors: On examination, Wang's note taking 賁上 as qi rushing is wrong. The *Nanjing* calls the stomach's [upper opening] the *ben* gate, and Yang Xuancao says "*ben* is the diaphragm". So it is qi rushing up above the diaphragm. Since the classic already says "qi rushes up", why should 賁 be further explained as "rushing up"?

[On "Needle the vessel in the center of the sole, three punctures each, six needlings in all, and it ends at once"] This means the 勇 [Yong]quan point, the well point of the lesser yin, in the depression of the sole, in the hollow formed when the foot is bent and the toes curled; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "needle in front of the Ran bone to let blood ... the left for the right"] This too is the foot lesser yin network vessel. Since its network vessel runs with the great channel [along] the throat, it is needled. These twenty-nine characters were originally misplaced before "when a pathogen lodges in the network vessels of the hand and foot lesser yin, greater yin and foot *yangming*"; they are now moved here.
Song editors: On examination, Wang's note "its network vessel runs with the great channel along the throat" confuses [channel and network vessel]. According to the *Jiayi jing*, the foot lesser yin network vessel goes up with the channel to the pericardium, and the lesser yin channel follows the throat. Wang's note interchanges channel and network vessel; the *Jiayi jing* should be taken as correct.

## Notes

Crosswise needling of the foot lesser yin network vessel. The latter half is text that Wang Bing moved as misplaced.
- The "twenty-nine characters" of Wang Bing are presumably from 嗌中腫 to the final 右刺左, but in the base text they are twenty-eight characters. Two 殆知閣 texts have the character 繆 (see the variants); with it there are twenty-nine.
- 勇泉 in Wang Bing's note is an error for 涌泉.
