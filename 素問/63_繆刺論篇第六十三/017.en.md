# 素問 繆刺論篇第六十三 §17 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a pathogen lodges in the collateral of the foot *Yangming*, it causes nasal congestion and nosebleed, and cold in the upper teeth. Needle above the nail of the toe next to the middle toe where it meets the flesh, one puncture each. Needle the right for the left and the left for the right.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When a pathogen lodges in the meridian of the foot *Yangming* ... cold in the upper teeth"] Its vessel starts at the nose, crosses at the bridge, descends along the outside of the nose, enters the upper teeth, comes back out flanking the mouth, encircles the lips, crosses below at Chengjiang, withdraws along the lower edge behind the chin, emerges at Daying, follows Jiache and goes up in front of the ear. Hence the illness causes nasal congestion, nosebleed and cold in the upper teeth. Also its vessels cross left and right on the face. Hence a meridian illness is cited to clarify the class of askew places. Hence the text below says:
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition and the *Jiayi jing* write "collateral of the *Yangming*" for "meridian of the *Yangming*".

[On "Needle above the nail of the toe next to the middle toe ... the left for the right"] 中 should be 大; this too is a copying error between 中 and 大. According to the *Lingshu jing* and the *Kongxue tujing*, there is no point above the nail of the toe next to the middle toe. It should say needling above the nail of the toe next to the big toe, which is the Lidui point, the well point of the *Yangming*; there should not be the further two characters 次指. Lidui: one may needle one body-*fen* and retain one breath; for moxibustion, one cone.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* says "needle above the nail of the middle toe", without 次指. It presumably takes the toe next to the big toe as the middle toe, in the same sense as Wang's note. "Above the nail of the middle toe of the foot *Yangming*" in the text below also means this point. Lidui is at the tip of the toe next to the big toe, a leek-leaf's breadth from the corner of the nail.

## Notes

Crosswise needling of the foot *Yangming* collateral.
- Wang Bing's closing 故下文云 presumably points to some following text, but which is unclear.
- Wang Bing both changes 中 to 大 and calls 次指 superfluous; the two explanations do not fit well together. The translation stays close to the characters.
- The base's 足陽明之経 is emended to 足陽明之絡. The Song editors say Quan Yuanqi's edition and the *Jiayi jing* read 陽明之絡, and the *Taisu*, the *Leijing* and Wikisource (the plain text) also have 絡. This chapter (on crosswise needling) deals with pathogens lodging in the collaterals, so this reading is followed. Wang Bing explains it with 経 ("citing a disease of the meridian to clarify crosswise treatment"), and Zhang Zhicong's *Jizhu* also reads 経.
