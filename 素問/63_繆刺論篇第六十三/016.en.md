# 素問 繆刺論篇第六十三 §16 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In all cases of impediment that comes and goes with no fixed place, needle the painful places in the divisions of the flesh, using the waxing and waning of the moon for the number. In using the needle, follow the waxing and waning of qi for the number of punctures. If the needling exceeds the number of days, qi is lost; if it falls short of the number of days, the qi is not drained. Needle the right for the left and the left for the right. When the illness ends, stop; if it does not end, needle again by the method. As the moon grows, one puncture on the first day, two on the second, gradually more; fifteen on the fifteenth, fourteen on the sixteenth, gradually fewer.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Needle the right for the left ... needle again by the method"] It explains why the number is set by the waxing and waning of the moon: to follow the waxing and waning of qi.

[On "fifteen on the fifteenth, fourteen on the sixteenth, gradually fewer"] Needling thus, there is neither excess nor shortfall in number.

## Notes

Crosswise needling for wandering impediment, and why the number of punctures follows the moon.
