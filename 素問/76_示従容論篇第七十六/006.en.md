# 素問 示従容論篇第七十六 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

I asked you about the obscure, and you answer by citing the upper and lower chapters. Why?

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "I asked you about the obscure, and you answer by citing the upper and lower chapters. Why"] The obscure is what cannot be seen, namely form-qi and the nutritive and defensive. In the *Bazheng shenming lun*, Qibo answers the Yellow Emperor: "Observing the obscure means that form-qi and the nutritive and defensive do not take shape outside and only the practitioner knows them. He combines and compares the cold and warmth of the sun, the depletion and fullness of the moon, and the floating and sinking of the qi of the four seasons, and regulates them. The practitioner always sees them first, though they do not take shape outside; hence 'observing the obscure'." Hence the Emperor says, "I asked you about the obscure." But liver, kidney and spleen depletion are the subject of the upper and lower chapters; hence the Emperor says, "You answer by citing the upper and lower chapters. Why?"

## Notes

The Emperor reproaches him for answering a question about the invisible with the book's upper and lower chapters.
