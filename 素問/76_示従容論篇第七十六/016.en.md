# 素問 示従容論篇第七十六 §16 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Limp limbs mean the spleen's essence is not moving; panting and coughing mean water qi has combined with *yangming*; bloody discharge means the pulse is urgent and the blood has nowhere to go. To take it as lung injury comes from error and wildness. Not drawing on comparison of categories shows unclear knowledge.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Limp limbs mean the spleen's essence is not moving"] Earth governs the four limbs, so the limbs are limp; the spleen's essence does not transform, causing this.

[On "panting and coughing mean water qi has combined with *yangming*"] Kidney qi counterflows into the stomach, so water qi combines with *yangming*.

[On "bloody discharge means the pulse is urgent and the blood has nowhere to go"] 泄 means leaking out. The pulse qi is rapid and urgent, blood overflows within and does not enter the channels, hence bloody discharge. Because the pulse rushes and the blood overflows, "the blood has nowhere to go".

[On "To take it as lung injury comes from error and wildness. Not drawing on comparison of categories shows unclear knowledge"] It means that with unclear understanding, unable to compare categories, calling it lung injury is merely erroneous, wild talk.

## Notes

The Emperor explains each symptom in Leigong's case and says that calling it lung illness is mistaken.
