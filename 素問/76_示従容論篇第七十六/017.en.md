# 素問 示従容論篇第七十六 §17 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In lung injury, spleen qi does not hold, stomach qi is not clear, channel qi does not serve, the true viscus breaks down, the channels are cut off at the side, the five viscera leak, and if there is no nosebleed there is vomiting [of blood]. These two do not resemble each other.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "the five viscera leak, and if there is no nosebleed there is vomiting. These two do not resemble each other"] When lung qi is injured, the spleen comes to its rescue from outside; hence "spleen qi does not hold". When the lung viscus is damaged, qi does not move; when it does not move the stomach is full; hence "stomach qi is not clear". The lungs govern the movement of the nutritive and defensive, yin and yang; so when the lungs are injured, the channels cannot be moved and used by them. The true viscus is the lung viscus. If the lung viscus is damaged and broken, its membranes burst, the channels cut off at the side and not flowing, and the qi of the five viscera overflow upward and leak, then if there is no nosebleed there is vomiting of blood. Why? The lungs govern the nose, the stomach corresponds to the mouth; mouth and nose are the gates of qi. Now the lung viscus is already damaged and stomach qi is not clear; if [the blood] does not rise as nosebleed, it flows down into the stomach; hence if it does not come out as nosebleed it comes out as vomiting. But lung injury and spleen injury, nosebleed and bloody discharge, differ in how the tip appears and in where the root returns; hence these two do not resemble each other.

## Notes

The Emperor lists the symptoms of lung injury and says they do not resemble Leigong's case (a spleen illness).
