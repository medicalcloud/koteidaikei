# 素問 示従容論篇第七十六 §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Emperor said, "That is what is meant by unhurried [comparison]. For the old, seek it in the bowels; for the young, seek it in the channels; for those in their prime, seek it in the viscera. Now what you say is all mistaken. The eight winds have pent up and festered, the five viscera are consumed, and transmitted pathogens are received in turn. Floating and string-like means insufficiency of the kidneys; sunken and stony means kidney qi is lodged within; timidity and shortage of qi mean the waterways do not move and form and qi are wasting away; coughing with vexation and distress is counterflow of kidney qi. In one person's qi, the illness is in one viscus; to say the three viscera are all involved is not according to the rules."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "That is what is meant by unhurried [comparison]"] It means comparing categories.

[On "For the old, seek it in the bowels; for the young, seek it in the channels; for those in their prime, seek it in the viscera"] The old overindulge in flavors; the young toil in labor; those in their prime are excessive in the bedchamber. Excess in the bedchamber depletes and harms the essential qi; toil in labor lets wind pathogens strike the channels; indulgence in appetite harms the bowels. Hence what is sought differs.

[On "Floating and string-like means insufficiency of the kidneys"] A floating pulse is depletion; string-like is liver qi. Because kidney qi is insufficient, the pulse is floating and string-like.

[On "sunken and stony means kidney qi is lodged within"] Stony means hard. Lodged means kidney qi presses inward, lodges and does not move.

[On "timidity and shortage of qi mean the waterways do not move and form and qi are wasting away"] Kidney qi is insufficient, so the waterways do not move; the lung viscus is struck, so form and qi disperse and are used up.

[On "coughing with vexation and distress is counterflow of kidney qi"] Kidney qi lodges within and rises to return to its mother.

[On "to say the three viscera are all involved is not according to the rules"] The classic is not so.

## Notes

The Emperor answers that this is illness of the kidneys alone.
