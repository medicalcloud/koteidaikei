# 素問 示従容論篇第七十六 §7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the spleen is depleted and floating, it resembles the lungs; when the kidney [pulse] is small and floating, it resembles the spleen; when the liver [pulse] is tense, sunken and scattered, it resembles the kidneys. These are what often confuse practitioners; but by unhurried [comparison] one grasps them.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "These are what often confuse practitioners; but by unhurried [comparison] one grasps them"] When the spleen is depleted and the pulse floating, the sign resembles the lungs; when the kidney [pulse] is small and floating, the upper sign resembles the spleen; when the liver [pulse] is tense, sunken and scattered, the sign resembles the kidneys. Why so? Because the three viscera are close to one another, their pulse images are mixed and similar. Hence practitioners are confused by them and make errors in treatment. Even so, by calmly and slowly comparing and categorizing them one can grasp the forms and signs of the three viscera. How to take them? Floating and moderate is the spleen; floating and short is the lungs; small, floating and slippery is the heart; tense, tight and scattered is the liver; beating, sunken and slippery is the kidneys. Without comparing categories, doubt and confusion grow ever worse.

## Notes

The Emperor says the pulses of the three viscera resemble one another and are confusing.
