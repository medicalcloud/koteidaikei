# 素問 刺志論篇第五十三 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Qi depleted with fever is called contrary. Much grain taken in but little qi is called contrary. No grain taken in but much qi is called contrary. An abundant pulse with little blood is called contrary. A scant pulse with much blood is called contrary.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Qi depleted with fever is called contrary"] Depleted qi means insufficient yang qi; with insufficient yang qi the body should be cold. When the body is instead hot, the pulse qi should be abundant; if the pulse is not abundant but the body is hot, the signs do not match; hence it is called contrary.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* says, "Qi abundant with the body cold, qi depleted with the body hot: this is called contrary." These four characters [気盛身寒] should be added.

[On "Much grain taken in but little qi is called contrary"] What the stomach sends out is grain qi, which spreads through the channel vessels; when grain enters the stomach, the pathways of the vessels disperse it. Now much grain enters but there is little qi, meaning the stomach qi does not disperse; hence contrary.

[On "No grain taken in but much qi is called contrary"] The stomach qi disperses outward and the lung combines with it.

[On "An abundant pulse with little blood ... A scant pulse with much blood is called contrary"] The channel vessels move qi and the network vessels receive blood; the channel qi enters the network vessels and the network vessels receive the channel qi. When the signs do not correspond, all go against the norm.

## Notes

Concrete examples of "contrary". Qibo's speech continues to unit 006.
- The Song editors hold that, following the *Jiayi jing*, the four characters 気盛身寒 "qi abundant with the body cold" should be added at the beginning (corresponding to 気盛身寒 in the next unit).
