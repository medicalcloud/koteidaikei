# 素問 刺志論篇第五十三 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "When the qi is replete the form is replete, and when the qi is depleted the form is depleted; this is the norm; the contrary is illness. When grain is abundant the qi is abundant, and when grain is deficient the qi is deficient; this is the norm; the contrary is illness. When the pulse is replete the blood is replete, and when the pulse is depleted the blood is depleted; this is the norm; the contrary is illness."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the qi is replete ... the contrary is illness"] The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* says, "Form returns to qi"; thus their depletion and repletion are the same. "Contrary" means they do not correspond and the normal, balanced signs are lost. When form and qi are contrary, illness arises. Qi means the qi of the pulse; form means the body.

[On "When grain is abundant the qi is abundant ... the contrary is illness"] The *Lingshu jing* says, "The way of the nutritive qi takes the intake of grain as its substance; when grain enters the stomach, its qi is transmitted to the lung, and the refined and concentrated part rises through the channel tunnels." Thus the depletion and repletion of grain and qi, when observed, must be the same; when the signs do not correspond, it is illness.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 宝 for 実.

[On "When the pulse is replete the blood is replete ... the contrary is illness"] The vessels are the house of the blood; therefore their depletion and repletion are the same; when contrary and not corresponding, it is illness.

## Notes

Normally depletion and repletion correspond between qi and form, grain and qi, pulse and blood; when they do not, it is illness.
