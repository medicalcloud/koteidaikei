# 素問 刺志論篇第五十三 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qi abundant with the body cold is contracted from cold damage; qi depleted with the body hot is contracted from summer-heat damage. Much grain taken in but little qi is contracted from loss of blood, with damp residing below. Little grain taken in but much qi means evil is in the stomach and the lung. A small pulse with much blood means retained fluid with heat within. A large pulse with little blood means there is wind qi in the vessels and water and broth do not enter. This is what is meant.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Qi abundant with the body cold ... from summer-heat damage"] 傷 means being exposed to. Cold harms the form; therefore the qi is abundant and the body cold. Heat harms the qi; therefore the qi is depleted and the body hot.

[On "Much grain taken in but little qi ... damp residing below"] Loss of blood depletes the blood; when the blood is depleted, the qi is abundant and stagnates within, transforming into fluids that flow into the lower burner; hence "damp residing below".

[On "Little grain taken in but much qi ... the stomach and the lung"] The stomach qi is insufficient and the lung qi flows down into the stomach; therefore evil is in the stomach. When lung qi enters the stomach, the lung qi does not guard itself; when it does not guard itself, evil qi follows; hence "evil is in the stomach and the lung".

[On "A small pulse with much blood means retained fluid with heat within"] 飲 means retained fluid. When fluid is retained in the spleen and stomach, spleen qi overflows; when spleen qi overflows, heat in the centre breaks out.

[On "A large pulse with little blood ... This is what is meant"] When wind qi is abundantly full, water and broth do not enter the vessels.
