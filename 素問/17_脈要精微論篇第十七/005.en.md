# 素問 脈要精微論篇第十七 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The five zang are the guardians of the interior. When the interior is abundant, the zang full, the qi overbearing and harmed by fear, the voice sounds as if speaking from inside a room; this is dampness of the central qi. When speech is faint and one speaks again only after a whole day, the qi has been seized. When one does not keep clothing and bedding in order and speaks good and evil without regard to close or distant, the spirit illumination is disordered. When the granary does not hold, the gate is not secured. When the springs of water do not stop, the bladder does not store. Those who keep their guard live; those who lose it die.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The five zang are the guardians of the interior"] Within the body, they are where the five spirits keep guard in peace. This is observing the five zang clearly.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* and the *Taisu* write 府 "storehouse" for 守.

[On "When the interior is abundant ... dampness of the central qi"] "Interior" means the abdomen; "abundant" means qi abundant; "zang" means the lung; "qi overbearing" means overbearing in breathing, so that the breath changes. When the qi in the abdomen is abundant, the lung is full, the qi overbearing and the breathing changed, one is readily harmed by fear, and the voice does not carry, as if in a room — all this is because there is damp qi in the abdomen.

[On "When speech is faint ... the qi has been seized"] If the voice is faint and fine and the sound breaks off without continuing, it is because the qi has been severely seized.

[On "When the granary does not hold, the gate is not secured"] The granary means the spleen and stomach; the gate means the *po* gate. The *Linglan midian lun* (chapter 8) says, "The spleen and stomach are the office of the granary," and the *Wuzang bielun* (chapter 11) says, "The *po* gate also serves the five zang, so that water and grain cannot be stored for long." The *po* gate is the anus. 要 means restraining.

[On "When the springs of water do not stop, the bladder does not store"] The springs of water mean the flow from the front yin.

[On "Those who keep their guard live; those who lose it die"] In all these cases — the granary not holding, the qi overbearing and harmed by fear, clothing not kept in order, the springs of water not stopping — if the spirit qi dwells and guards, one lives; if it loses its guard, one dies. How does one know the spirit qi is not guarding? Not keeping clothing in order and speaking good and evil without regard to close or distant are signs of disorder; when the disorder is severe, it does not guard the zang.

## Notes

The five zang are the guardians of the interior; from the voice, speech, behaviour, stools and urine one judges whether the guard is kept or lost, and so decides life or death.
- "Guardians of the interior": according to the Song editors, the *Jiayi jing* and the *Taisu* read "storehouses of the interior".
- Daizhige (the plain text) writes 盛 for 勝 in 気勝.
