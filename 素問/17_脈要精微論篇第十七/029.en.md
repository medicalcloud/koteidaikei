# 素問 脈要精微論篇第十七 §29 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Therefore one struck by malign wind receives it in the yang qi. When the pulses are all sunken, fine and rapid, it is reversal of the lesser yin; sunken, fine, rapid and scattered, it is cold and heat; floating and scattered, it is dizziness and falling. All pulses that are floating but not agitated are in the yang and mean heat; those that are agitated are in the hand. All pulses that are fine and sunken are in the yin and mean bone pain; those that are still are in the foot. A pulse that beats rapidly and pauses once indicates illness in a yang vessel, with diarrhoea and passing of pus and blood.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Therefore one struck by malign wind receives it in the yang qi"] Because the upper part is depleted, the yang qi receives it.

[On "When the pulses are all sunken, fine and rapid, it is reversal of the lesser yin"] When the pulse at the *chi* position is sunken, fine and rapid, it is counterflow of the lesser yin qi of the kidney. Why? A rapid pulse should not appear at the *chi*; since it is rapid, it is called reversal. "All sunken, fine and rapid" refers to the left and right *chi*.

[On "sunken, fine, rapid and scattered, it is cold and heat"] Yang invades yin and the yin qi is insufficient; therefore there is cold and heat. The *Zhengli lun* says: "Rapid is yang."

[On "floating and scattered, it is dizziness and falling"] A floating pulse is depletion; scattered is insufficiency. The qi is depleted and the blood insufficient; therefore the head spins and one falls.

[On "All pulses that are floating but not agitated ... those that are agitated are in the hand"] This states the general rule. If merely floating and not agitated, the illness is in the yang vessels of the foot; if agitated, it is in the yang vessels of the hand; hence "those that are agitated are in the hand". Yang is the qi of fire; therefore there is heat.

[On "All pulses that are fine and sunken ... those that are still are in the foot"] If fine, sunken and agitated, the illness arises in the yin vessels of the hand; if still, in the yin vessels of the foot; hence "those that are still are in the foot". Yin governs the bones; therefore the bones ache.

[On "A pulse that beats rapidly and pauses once ... passing of pus and blood"] 代 means stopping. Beating rapidly and stopping once means the yang qi produces illness; hence "illness in a yang vessel". The reason is that in diarrhoea and with pus and blood the pulse is like this.

## Notes

From whether the pulse is sunken, fine, rapid, scattered, floating, agitated, still or intermittent, one judges the yin or yang of the illness, whether it is in the channels of the hand or foot, and the illness itself.
- The passage from the *Zhengli lun* cited by Wang Bing has not been checked in other sources.
- Daizhige (the plain text) writes 朐 for 眴.
