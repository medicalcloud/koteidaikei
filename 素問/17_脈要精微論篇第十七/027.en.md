# 素問 脈要精微論篇第十七 §27 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

On both sides within the *chi* are the lower ribs; outside the *chi* one observes the kidney, and inside the *chi* the abdomen. Attached above [the *chi*]: on the left, outside one observes the liver and inside the diaphragm; on the right, outside the stomach and inside the spleen. Above the attached part: on the right, outside the lung and inside the chest; on the left, outside the heart and inside the *danzhong*. The front observes the front and the back observes the back. The uppermost limit concerns matters in the chest and throat; the lowermost limit concerns matters in the lower abdomen, lower back, thighs, knees, shins and feet.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "On both sides within the *chi* are the lower ribs"] "Within the *chi*" means inside Chize; "both sides" means the outer sides of the *chi* respectively. The lower ribs are near the kidney, which the *chi* governs; therefore on both sides within the *chi* are the lower ribs.

[On "outside the *chi* one observes the kidney, and inside the *chi* the abdomen"] "Outside the *chi*" means the outer side of the *chi*; "inside the *chi*" means its inner side. Next to the outer *chi*, on both sides below, is the region of the lower ribs; above the lower ribs is the region of the kidney; inside the lower ribs is the region of the abdomen.

[On "Attached above: on the left, outside one observes the liver and inside the diaphragm"] The liver governs the *ben*; *ben* is the diaphragm.

[On "on the right, outside the stomach and inside the spleen"] The spleen lies in the centre, so it is observed inside; the stomach is a market, so it is observed outside.

[On "Above the attached part: on the right, outside the lung and inside the chest"] The lobes of the lung hang outward, so it is observed outside; the chest governs the windpipe, so it is observed inside.

[On "on the left, outside the heart and inside the *danzhong*"] The heart governs the centre of the diaphragm. The *danzhong* is the sea of qi; it is the throat.
Song editors: Mr Wang's taking the *danzhong* as the throat is probably an error.

[On "The front observes the front and the back observes the back"] The upper front means the left *cunkou*; the lower front means the breast in front of the chest and the sea of qi. The upper back means the right *cunkou*; the lower back means the back behind the chest and the windpipe.

[On "The uppermost limit ... matters in the lower abdomen, lower back, thighs, knees, shins and feet"] The uppermost limit reaches the thenar border. The lowermost limit means going to the end of where the *chi* pulse beats. The womb in the lower abdomen and the sea of qi are at the bladder. The movement and stillness of the qi in the lower back, thighs, knees, shins and feet are all observed by distinguishing their nearness and distance and the names of the places where they connect, so as to know whether they are good or bad.

## Notes

The positions of the wrist pulse (*chi*, the part attached above, and the part above that) are divided into left and right, outside and inside, each assigned to observe particular organs and body regions. This is regarded as a source of the later assignment of organs to the three positions (*cun*, *guan*, *chi*) of the radial pulse.
- The Song editors suspect that Wang Bing's identification of the *danzhong* with the throat is an error.
- The passage is read in various ways; the translation follows the characters with reference to Wang Bing.
