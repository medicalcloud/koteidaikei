# 素問 脈要精微論篇第十七 §12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

If the heart pulse beats hard and long, the illness should be a curled tongue with inability to speak; if it is soft and scattered, it should disperse in its circuit and end of itself. If the lung pulse beats hard and long, the illness should be spitting blood; if soft and scattered, the illness should be from washed sweat, which to this day has not been released again. If the liver pulse beats hard and long and the colour is not green, the illness should be from a fall or blow, with blood under the flanks making the person pant with counterflow; if soft and scattered with a lustrous colour, the illness should be overflow rheum — overflow rheum is when one is thirsty, drinks a great deal suddenly, and [the water] easily enters outside the skin, flesh, intestines and stomach. If the stomach pulse beats hard and long and the colour is red, the illness should be as if the thigh were broken; if soft and scattered, the illness should be food impediment. If the spleen pulse beats hard and long and the colour is yellow, the illness should be shortness of qi; if soft and scattered with a lustreless colour, the illness should be swelling of the shins like water. If the kidney pulse beats hard and long and the colour is yellow and red, the illness should be as if the lower back were broken; if soft and scattered, the illness should be scant blood, which to this day has not recovered."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "If the heart pulse beats hard and long ... inability to speak"] 搏 means striking against the hand. When any pulse beats hard and long, it is always that the heart has been overworked and the pulse qi of the zang is extremely depleted. The heart's hand lesser yin vessel rises from the heart connector alongside the throat; therefore the tongue curls and shortens and one cannot speak.

[On "if it is soft and scattered, it should disperse in its circuit and end of itself"] When any pulse is soft and scattered, the qi is replete and the blood depleted. 消 means dispersing; 環 means circling. It says that the channel qi, like a ring going round, disperses of itself when its fire becomes dominant.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 渇 "thirst" for 環.

[On "If the lung pulse beats hard and long, the illness should be spitting blood"] When the lung is extremely depleted the network vessels counterflow; when they counterflow, blood leaks; therefore it is spat out.

[On "if soft and scattered ... has not been released again"] When sweat is leaking from the sweat pores and the fluids are rushing out, if one washes with cold water, the skin closes and the sweat is stored. Because the sweat was stored by washing, it says "washed sweat, which to this day has not been released again". 灌 means washing; it is often done in great heat.
Song editors: In the text below each organ is given a colour, but the heart and lung are not; we suspect a lacuna.

[On "If the liver pulse beats hard and long and the colour is not green ... pant with counterflow"] When a pulse shows the qi of its own channel but the colour does not correspond, the illness does not arise from within but is an external illness that has come to prevail. The pulse of the liver is straight and long; hence "if the colour is not green, the illness should be from a fall or blow". The liver governs both flanks; hence "blood under the flanks". The liver's *jueyin* vessel spreads over the flanks and ribs and follows behind the throat; its branch separates from the liver, pierces the diaphragm and pours upward into the lung. Now, with blood under the flanks, the blood and qi fumigate the lung above; therefore the person pants with counterflow.

[On "if soft and scattered with a lustrous colour ... outside the skin, flesh, intestines and stomach"] A floating, lustrous facial colour means dampness within. When the blood is depleted and there is dampness within, fluids are not dispersed; hence "the illness should be overflow rheum". Because the water fills and overflows, it seeps and overflows, easily entering outside the skin, flesh, intestines and stomach.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 溢 "overflow" for 易 "easily".

[On "If the stomach pulse beats hard and long and the colour is red, the illness should be as if the thigh were broken"] The stomach is depleted and the colour red because fire qi governs it; the heart resembles fire, hence red. The stomach's *yangming* vessel descends from Qichong down the thigh to Futu; therefore when ill, the thigh feels broken.

[On "if soft and scattered, the illness should be food impediment"] 痺 means pain. A branch of the stomach's *yangming* vessel descends from in front of Daying to Renying, follows the throat, enters the supraclavicular fossa, descends through the diaphragm, belongs to the stomach and connects with the spleen; therefore on eating there is pain and oppression, and the qi does not disperse.
Song editors: Taking 痺 as pain does not yet make sense.

[On "If the spleen pulse beats hard and long and the colour is yellow, the illness should be shortness of qi"] When the spleen is depleted, the lung has nothing to nourish it; the lung governs qi; therefore there is shortness of qi.

[On "if soft and scattered with a lustreless colour ... like water"] A floating, lustrous colour and qi are a sign of water; since the colour is not lustrous, it says "like water". The spleen's greater yin vessel rises from the front edge of the inner ankle inside the calf, follows behind the shin bone, crosses and emerges in front of the *jueyin*, rises along the front inner edge of the knee and thigh and enters the abdomen; therefore the illness is swelling of the shins.

[On "If the kidney pulse beats hard and long and the colour is yellow and red ... as if the lower back were broken"] A yellow and red colour means the heart and spleen invade the kidney; the kidney receives a guest yang; therefore the lower back feels broken. The lower back is the storehouse of the kidney; therefore the illness arises within it.

[On "if soft and scattered ... has not recovered"] The kidney governs water and generates the fluids; now the kidney qi does not transform; therefore the illness is scant blood, which to this day has not recovered.

## Notes

For the heart, lung, liver, stomach, spleen and kidney, the illnesses indicated when the pulse "beats hard and long" or is "soft and scattered" are described together with the colour. Qibo's speech, begun in unit 009, ends here.
- The Song editors suspect a lacuna, since no colour is given for the heart and lung.
- "Food impediment": Wang Bing takes 痺 as pain, which the Song editors find unconvincing.
- Daizhige writes 軟 for 耎.
