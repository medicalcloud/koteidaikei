# 素問 脈要精微論篇第十七 §20 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "When wind becomes established, there is cold and heat; when *dan* (damp heat) becomes established, there is central wasting; when reversal becomes established, there is illness of the vertex; long-standing wind becomes undigested diarrhoea; when vessel wind becomes established, there is pestilential wind. The changes of illness cannot be counted."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When wind becomes established, there is cold and heat"] The *Shengqi tongtian lun* (chapter 3) says, "When due to dew and wind, cold and heat arise"; therefore when wind becomes established there is cold and heat.

[On "when *dan* becomes established, there is central wasting"] 癉 means damp heat. Heat accumulates within; therefore it changes into central wasting. The sign of central wasting is eating much and becoming thin.
Song editors: Wang Bing's note takes "eating much and becoming thin" as central wasting, but in this classic eating much with frequent urination is central wasting, while eating much and becoming thin is the sign of *shiyi*. It should say "eating much with frequent urination".

[On "when reversal becomes established, there is illness of the vertex"] Reversal means counterflow of qi. When counterflowing qi rises without stopping, it changes into illness of the vertex above.

[On "long-standing wind becomes undigested diarrhoea"] When long-standing wind does not change but stays in the stomach, food is not transformed and there is diarrhoea. Because the liver qi joins within and encroaches on the stomach, this illness occurs. The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* (chapter 5) says, "The qi of wind communicates with the liver"; therefore it corresponds within to the liver.

[On "when vessel wind becomes established, there is pestilential wind"] The *Fenglun* of the classic says, "When wind and cold lodge in the vessels and do not leave, it is called pestilential wind," and also, "In pestilential wind, the nutritive qi is hot and clings, its qi is not clear, so that the bridge of the nose is ruined, the colour spoiled, and the skin ulcerates." This is leprosy. All of these arise from vessel wind becoming established, binding and changing.

[On "The changes of illness cannot be counted"] Song editors: This answers the Emperor's earlier question, "How does one know how the illness changes?"

## Notes

Examples of established illnesses changing: wind into cold and heat, damp heat into central wasting, reversal into illness of the vertex, long-standing wind into undigested diarrhoea, vessel wind into pestilential wind (leprosy).
- "Central wasting": Wang Bing describes it as eating much and becoming thin; the Song editors say it should be eating much with frequent urination.
- 癩 in Wang Bing's note was supplied where the transcription gave only a component description (see the 【補】 note).
- The Song editors point out that this answers "How does one know how the illness changes?" in unit 008.
