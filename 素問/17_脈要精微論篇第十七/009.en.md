# 素問 脈要精微論篇第十七 §9 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Let me speak of how [the pulse] turns with heaven in its great cycle. Beyond the myriad things and within the six directions, heaven and earth change and yin and yang respond. The warmth of spring becomes the heat of summer; the resentment of autumn becomes the anger of winter. With the movements of the four changes the pulse rises and falls: spring corresponds to the compass, summer to the square, autumn to the beam, and winter to the weight. Therefore for forty-five days after the winter solstice the yang qi rises slightly and the yin qi descends slightly; for forty-five days after the summer solstice the yin qi rises slightly and the yang qi descends slightly. Yin and yang have their times and set their terms with the pulse; when the terms are missed, one knows how the pulse separates; separation has its time, and so one knows the time of death. The subtle lies in the pulse and must be examined. Examining it has an order: it begins with yin and yang. The beginning has a rule: it arises from the five phases. Arising has its measure: the four seasons are what is fitting. Do not err in supplementing and draining; be one with heaven and earth. Grasping the reality of the One, one knows death and life.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Qibo said"] Song editors: This answer does not correspond well to the question. On the movement of the pulse with the four seasons, where the illness is and how it changes, the text answers fairly well, but on whether the illness is within or without, the later text does not correspond at all.

[On "Let me speak of how [the pulse] turns with heaven in its great cycle"] He points to the visible turning of yin and yang to clarify the invisible yin and yang.

[On "Beyond the myriad things ... the pulse rises and falls"] The six directions are the four quarters, above and below. "The warmth of spring becomes the heat of summer" means yang arising and reaching abundance; "the resentment of autumn becomes the anger of winter" means yin going from small to strong. 忿 is written 急 in one version, meaning the qi of autumn is strong and urgent.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's annotated edition writes 緩 for 緩 [as in the base text].

[On "spring corresponds to the compass"] The spring pulse is soft, weak, light, empty and slippery, like the image of a compass, round inside and out; therefore spring corresponds to the compass.

[On "summer to the square"] The summer pulse is surging and large, also slippery and rapid, like the image of a square that can make things level; therefore summer corresponds to the square.

[On "autumn to the beam"] The autumn pulse is floating and hair-like, light, rough and scattered, like the beam of a steelyard, which must be level high and low; therefore autumn corresponds to the beam.

[On "winter to the weight"] The winter pulse is like stone, sunken and slippery, like the weight of a steelyard, hanging below far from the beam; therefore winter corresponds to the weight. Saying autumn corresponds to the beam and winter to the weight expresses that the pulse lies high or low in different places. This follows the qi of yin and yang, hence these four different correspondences.

[On "Yin and yang have their times ... one knows the time of death"] Examining the rules of the rising and descending of yin and yang, one knows the images of the successive shifts of the channels; examining the failures in the successive shifts of the seasons, one knows the times of separation and union of qi and blood. If the times of separation and union do not err, one knows when a person will die.

[On "The subtle lies in the pulse ... it begins with yin and yang"] Inferring the subtle function of the rising and descending of yin and yang, all of it lies in the signs of the channel qi; therefore it must be examined, and so yin and yang are taken first as the order of examination.

[On "The beginning has a rule ... the four seasons are what is fitting"] How does one know at the outset that there is examination and correspondence of the channels? It is by taking the decline and dominance of the five phases as the standard. In seeking the forms of excess and insufficiency, what corresponds to the four seasons is taken as what suits the vital qi.
Song editors: The *Taisu* writes 数 "number" for 宜.

[On "Do not err in supplementing and draining; be one with heaven and earth"] Drain what is in surplus and supplement what is insufficient; this corresponds to the constant way of heaven and earth. The way of heaven and earth reduces surplus and supplements insufficiency; this is following the way of heaven and earth. What is fitting in draining and supplementing the practitioner must examine closely; governing the qi is likewise.

[On "Grasping the reality of the One, one knows death and life"] If one understands the way of heaven and earth, supplements and drains without error, and has grasped the reality of the One, one can know the criteria of life and death.

## Notes

The pulse turns with heaven and earth; the seasonal pulses are likened to the compass, square, beam and weight (round and soft in spring, surging in summer, floating in autumn, sunken in winter). When the times of yin and yang and the pulse fail to match, the time of death can be known; in examining the pulse one takes yin and yang, the five phases and the four seasons as standards and does not err in supplementing and draining. Qibo's speech continues to unit 012.
- The Song editors point out that this answer does not correspond well to the five questions of the previous unit.
- "Spring corresponds to the compass, summer to the square, autumn to the beam, winter to the weight" is a well-known image of the seasonal pulses.
- The Song editors' note "Quan Yuanqi's annotated edition writes 緩 for 緩" is as in the base text (the two characters are identical, probably through some error).
