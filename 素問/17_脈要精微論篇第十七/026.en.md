# 素問 脈要精微論篇第十七 §26 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "How thorough your question is! If on examination the pulse is small but the colour is not lost, it is a new illness. If the pulse is not lost but the colour is lost, it is a chronic illness. If both pulse and the five colours are lost, it is a chronic illness. If neither pulse nor the five colours are lost, it is a new illness. If the liver and kidney pulses arrive together and the colour is green and red, the illness should be from an injury, without visible blood; if blood is already visible, it is as if from dampness or being struck by water.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "If on examination the pulse is small but the colour is not lost, it is a new illness"] The qi is weak but the spirit is still strong.

[On "If the pulse is not lost but the colour is lost, it is a chronic illness"] The spirit holds out but the evil oppresses its qi.

[On "If both pulse and the five colours are lost, it is a chronic illness"] Spirit and qi have both declined.

[On "If neither pulse nor the five colours are lost, it is a new illness"] Spirit and qi are both strong.

[On "If the liver and kidney pulses arrive together ... being struck by water"] The colour of the liver is green and that of the heart red. When a red colour appears the pulse should be surging; when the kidney pulse appears the colour should be black. Now the kidney pulse comes but the colour of the heart appears instead; therefore it should be due to injury, with blood not visible. If blood is already visible, it means damp qi and water are in the abdomen. Why? Because the inner and outer signs of the pulse and colour of the heart and kidney do not correspond.

## Notes

Whether the pulse and complexion are "lost" tells whether the illness is new or chronic. If the liver and kidney pulses arrive together with a green-red colour, it is from injury and blood should not be visible. Qibo's speech continues to unit 030.
