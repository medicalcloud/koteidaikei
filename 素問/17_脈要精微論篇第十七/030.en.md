# 素問 脈要精微論篇第十七 §30 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

For all faulty pulses, feel them: if rough, the yang qi is in surplus; if slippery, the yin qi is in surplus. With surplus yang qi, the body is hot without sweating; with surplus yin qi, there is much sweating and the body is cold; with surplus of both yin and yang, there is no sweating and cold. If, when pushed outward, it stays inside and does not come out, there is an accumulation in the heart and abdomen. If, when pushed inward, it stays outside and does not come in, the body has heat. If, when pushed upward, it stays up and does not come down, the lower back and feet are cold. If, when pushed downward, it stays down and does not come up, the head and nape ache. If, when pressed to the bone, the pulse qi is scant, the lower back and spine ache and the body has impediment."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "if rough, the yang qi is in surplus; if slippery, the yin qi is in surplus"] When yang is in surplus the blood is scant, so the pulse is rough; when yin is in surplus the qi is plentiful, so the pulse is slippery.
Song editors: "The qi is plentiful" is probably an error; it should be "the blood is plentiful".

[On "With surplus yang qi ... much sweating and the body is cold"] Little blood and much qi can thus be known.

[On "with surplus of both yin and yang, there is no sweating and cold"] With surplus yang there is no sweating; with surplus yin the body is cold; if both yin and yang are in surplus, there should be no sweating and cold.

[On "If, when pushed outward ... an accumulation in the heart and abdomen"] When the pulse lies against the tendons of the forearm and cannot be clearly taken, one pushes the tendon aside to make the pulse move outward; if it stays inside and does not come out, there is an accumulation in the heart and abdomen.

[On "If, when pushed inward ... the body has heat"] When the pulse is far from the tendons of the forearm, one pushes it closer; if it stays far and does not come close, the yang qi is in surplus; therefore the body has heat.

[On "If, when pushed upward ... the lower back and feet are cold"] Pushing the tendon and pressing, searching upward, if the pulse surges up abundantly, the yang qi is in surplus; therefore the lower back and feet are cold.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 下而不上 for 上而不下.

[On "If, when pushed downward ... the head and nape ache"] Pushing the tendon and pressing, searching downward, if the pulse sinks and pulls down, the yang qi is in surplus; therefore the head and nape ache.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 上而不下 for 下而不上.

[On "If, when pressed to the bone ... the body has impediment"] Because the yin qi is excessive.

## Notes

Roughness or slipperiness of the pulse shows surplus of yang or yin; how the pulse behaves when pushed inward, outward, upward or downward indicates accumulation in the heart and abdomen, bodily heat, cold lower back and feet, or pain in the head and nape. This ends the chapter, and Qibo's speech begun in unit 026.
- According to the Song editors, the *Jiayi jing* interchanges 上而不下 and 下而不上.
