# 素問 脈要精微論篇第十七 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The pulse is the storehouse of blood. If long, the qi is ordered; if short, the qi is ill; if rapid, there is vexation of the heart; if large, the illness is advancing; if abundant above, the qi is high; if abundant below, the qi is distended; if intermittent, the qi is declining; if fine, the qi is scant; if rough, there is heart pain. If it comes turbid and leathery like a gushing spring, the illness is advancing and the complexion is worn; if it is faint and departs like a snapped string, death.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The pulse is the storehouse of blood"] 府 means to gather. It says that the amount of blood is all gathered and shown in the channels. Hence the *Cizhi lun* (chapter 53) says: "If the pulse is replete, the blood is replete; if the pulse is depleted, the blood is depleted. This is the norm; the opposite is illness." This is why.

[On "If long, the qi is ordered ... if large, the illness is advancing"] A long pulse means the qi is harmonious, hence ordered; a short one means insufficiency, hence illness; rapid and urgent means heat, hence vexation of the heart; large means evil is abundant, hence the illness advances. A long pulse goes and comes long; a short pulse goes and comes short; a rapid pulse goes and comes urgently and fast; a large pulse goes and comes full and large.

[On "if abundant above, the qi is high"] Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 鬲 "diaphragm" for 高.

[On "if fine, the qi is scant"] Song editors: The *Taisu* writes 滑 "slippery" for 細.

[On "if intermittent, the qi is declining; if fine, the qi is scant; if rough, there is heart pain"] "Above" means the *cunkou*; "below" means the *chi* position; "abundant" means full. An intermittent pulse beats and stops midway and cannot resume of itself. A fine pulse beats like a fine weed. A rough pulse at times does not come and go freely but is halting and rough.

[On "If it comes turbid and leathery ... death"] 渾渾 means the pulse qi is turbid and disordered. "Coming leathery" means the pulse comes wiry and large, replete and long. "Like a gushing spring" means the pulse gushes out without returning. 綿綿 means faint, as if present but hardly responding to the hand. "Like a snapped string" means the pulse suddenly breaks off like a string snapping. If the signs of illness advance daily, the complexion is worn and bad, and there is such a pulse, all will surely die.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* and the *Maijing* read: 渾渾革革至如涌泉，病進而色弊弊，綽綽其去如弦絶者死.

## Notes

The pulse is the storehouse of blood; long, short, rapid, large, abundant above or below, intermittent, fine and rough pulses and their illnesses are described, ending with the forms of a fatal pulse.
- Wang Bing takes "above" as the *cunkou* and "below" as the *chi* position.
- According to the Song editors, Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 鬲 for 高, the *Taisu* 滑 for 細, and the *Jiayi jing* and *Maijing* word the last phrase differently.
- Wikisource (the plain text) writes 僅僅 for 綿綿.
