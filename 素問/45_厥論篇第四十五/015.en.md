# 素問 厥論篇第四十五 §15 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Greater yin reversal: the shins are tense and cramped, and heart pain draws to the abdomen; treat what governs the illness. Lesser yin reversal: deficiency with fullness, vomiting and change, and clear diarrhea below; treat what governs the illness. *Jueyin* reversal: cramps with lumbar pain, deficiency with fullness, blockage of the front [urination], and delirium; treat what governs the illness. When all three yin counterflow, neither front nor back passes, the hands and feet become cold, and one dies in three days.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Greater yin reversal ... treat what governs the illness"] The foot greater yin vessel arises at the tip of the big toe, follows the inner side of the toe, rises along the anterior edge of the inner ankle, up the inside of the calf, behind the shin bone, up the anterior inner edge of the knee and thigh, and enters the abdomen; a branch goes again from the stomach up through the diaphragm and pours into the heart. Therefore the shins are tense and cramped and heart pain draws to the abdomen. The greater yin vessel runs on left and right; one should observe where it is at fault and take it there; hence "treat what governs the illness".
Song editors: On examination, from "Greater yin reversal" to the end of the chapter is in juan 9 in Quan Yuanqi's edition; Mr Wang moved it here.

[On "Lesser yin reversal ... treat what governs the illness"] Its vessel rises from the kidney through the liver and diaphragm, enters the lung and follows the throat; hence this.

[On "*Jueyin* reversal ... delirium"] Song editors: Quan Yuanqi says, "Delirium is talking to oneself because the qi is deficient."

[On "treat what governs the illness"] Its vessel follows the inner thigh into the pubic hair, encircles the genitals, and also rises along the back of the throat to connect with the root of the tongue; hence this.
Song editors: In the *Jiayi jing* the *Jueyin* meridian does not connect with the root of the tongue. Mr Wang, in his notes to the *Cire pian*, *Ci yaotong pian* and here, says it connects with the root of the tongue, while in his notes to the *Feng lun* and *Bi lun* he does not. Wang's notes are inconsistent; the *Jiayi jing* should be taken as correct.

[On "When all three yin counterflow ... one dies in three days"] The three yin are cut off; therefore one dies in three days.

## Notes

The symptoms and prognosis of reversal of the three foot yin.
