# 素問 厥論篇第四十五 §16 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Greater yang reversal counterflow: rigid falling, vomiting of blood and frequent nosebleed; treat what governs the illness. Lesser yang reversal counterflow: the joints are not free — that is, one cannot walk with the lumbus or turn the head with the nape; if intestinal abscess breaks out it cannot be treated, and those who are frightened die. *Yangming* reversal counterflow: panting and cough, fever, frequent fright, nosebleed and vomiting of blood. Hand greater yin reversal counterflow: depletion with fullness and cough, frequent vomiting of froth; treat what governs the illness. Hand heart-master and lesser yin reversal counterflow: heart pain drawing to the throat and fever; one dies and it cannot be treated. Hand greater yang reversal counterflow: deafness and tearing, inability to turn the head, inability to bend the lumbus forward or back; treat what governs the illness. Hand *yangming* and lesser yang reversal counterflow: throat impediment, swollen throat and convulsions break out; treat what governs the illness."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Greater yang reversal counterflow ... treat what governs the illness"] Its vessel arises at the inner canthus and also follows the spine and connects with the brain; hence this.

[On "Lesser yang reversal counterflow ... turn the head with the nape"] Its vessel runs down the neck, winds around the pubic hairline and enters transversely into the hip socket; hence this.

[On "if intestinal abscess breaks out it cannot be treated, and those who are frightened die"] The foot lesser yang vessel passes through the diaphragm, connects with the liver, belongs to the gallbladder, follows the inside of the flanks and emerges at Qijie. When intestinal abscess breaks out, the channel qi is cut off; therefore it cannot be treated, and those who are frightened die.

[On "*Yangming* reversal counterflow ... vomiting of blood"] Its vessel follows the throat, enters the supraclavicular fossa, descends through the diaphragm, belongs to the stomach and connects with the spleen; hence this.

[On "Hand greater yin reversal counterflow ... treat what governs the illness"] The hand greater yin vessel arises in the middle burner, descends to connect with the large intestine, returns along the mouth of the stomach, rises through the diaphragm and belongs to the lung; hence this.

[On "Hand heart-master and lesser yin reversal counterflow ... cannot be treated"] The hand heart-master vessel arises in the chest and emerges to belong to the pericardium; a branch of the hand lesser yin vessel rises from the heart connector alongside the throat; hence this.

[On "Hand greater yang reversal counterflow ... treat what governs the illness"] A branch of the hand greater yang vessel goes from the supraclavicular fossa along the neck up the cheek to the outer canthus and turns back into the ear; another branch goes from the cheek up the cheekbone, reaches the nose and the inner canthus. Therefore there are deafness, tearing and inability to turn the head. Inability to bend the lumbus forward or back does not correspond to the vessel; it is feared to be a displaced passage of the ancient text.

[On "Hand *yangming* and lesser yang reversal counterflow ... treat what governs the illness"] A branch of the hand *yangming* vessel rises from the supraclavicular fossa up the neck; a branch of the hand lesser yang vessel rises from the *danzhong*, emerges at the supraclavicular fossa and rises to the nape; hence this.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 痙 for 痓.
