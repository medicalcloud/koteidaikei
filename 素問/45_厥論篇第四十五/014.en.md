# 素問 厥論篇第四十五 §14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Reversal of the greater yang: the head is swollen and heavy, the feet cannot walk, and dizziness and falling break out. Reversal of the *yangming*: epilepsy with the urge to run and shout, abdominal fullness preventing lying down, a red and hot face, seeing and saying wild things. Reversal of the lesser yang: sudden deafness, swollen and hot cheeks, pain in the flanks, and the shins cannot be moved. Reversal of the greater yin: abdominal fullness and distension, impeded stool, no desire to eat, vomiting on eating, and inability to lie down. Reversal of the lesser yin: dry mouth, red urine, abdominal fullness and heart pain. Reversal of the *jueyin*: swelling and pain of the lower abdomen, abdominal distension, impeded urination, a liking for lying with the knees bent, retraction and swelling of the genitals, and heat inside the shins. When abundant, drain; when depleted, supplement; when neither abundant nor depleted, take it by the channel.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Reversal of the greater yang ... dizziness and falling break out"] The greater yang (巨陽) is the 太陽. The foot greater yang vessel arises at the inner canthus, rises over the forehead and crosses at the vertex; a branch goes from the vertex to the upper corner of the ear; the straight course enters from the vertex to connect with the brain, comes out again, branches down the nape, follows the inside of the shoulder blade, runs alongside the spine to the lumbus, enters along the spinal muscles, connects with the kidney and belongs to the bladder; a branch descends from the lumbus through the buttocks into the popliteal fossa; another branch separates left and right from inside the shoulder blade, descends through the paraspinal muscles, passes the hip joint, follows the posterior outer edge of the thigh down to join in the popliteal fossa, then descends through the calf, emerges behind the outer ankle, follows Jinggu and reaches the outer side of the tip of the little toe. When this reverses, these signs appear outwardly. 腫 is sometimes written 踵, which is wrong.

[On "Reversal of the *yangming* ... seeing and saying wild things"] The foot *yangming* vessel arises at the nose, crosses at the bridge, descends along the outside of the nose, enters the upper teeth, comes out alongside the mouth and around the lips, crosses below at Chengjiang, turns back along the lower edge behind the chin, emerges at Daying, follows Jiache, rises in front of the ear, passes Kezhuren and follows the hairline to the forehead; a branch descends from in front of Daying to Renying, follows the throat, enters the supraclavicular fossa, descends through the diaphragm, belongs to the stomach and connects with the spleen; the straight course descends from the supraclavicular fossa along the inner edge of the breast, down alongside the navel into Qijie; a branch arises at the lower mouth of the stomach, runs inside the abdomen down to join at Qijie, then descends to the thigh, reaches Futu, enters the kneecap, descends along the outer edge of the shin to the instep and enters the inner side of the middle toe; another branch separates three *cun* below the knee and enters the outer side of the middle toe; another branch enters between the big toes on the instep and emerges at its tip. Hence such reversal. 癲 is written 巓 in one version, which is wrong.

[On "Reversal of the lesser yang ... the shins cannot be moved"] The foot lesser yang vessel arises at the outer canthus, rises to the corner of the head, descends behind the ear, follows the neck in front of the hand lesser yang, reaches the top of the shoulder, crosses behind the hand lesser yang and enters the supraclavicular fossa; a branch enters the ear from behind it, emerges in front of the ear and reaches behind the outer canthus; another branch goes from the outer canthus down to Daying, joins the hand lesser yang below the cheekbone, passes Jiache, descends the neck to join at the supraclavicular fossa, descends into the chest, passes through the diaphragm, connects with the liver and belongs to the gallbladder, follows the inside of the flanks, emerges at Qijie, winds around the pubic hairline and enters transversely into the hip socket; the straight course descends from the supraclavicular fossa to the armpit, follows the chest past the lower ribs, descends to join in the hip socket, then descends along the outer thigh, emerges at the outer edge of the knee, descends in front of the outer auxiliary bone, goes straight down to the end of Juegu, emerges in front of the outer ankle, follows the instep and emerges at the tip of the fourth toe. Hence such reversal.

[On "Reversal of the greater yin ... inability to lie down"] The foot greater yin vessel arises at the tip of the big toe, rises along the anterior inner edge of the knee and thigh, enters the abdomen, belongs to the spleen and connects with the stomach, rises through the diaphragm alongside the throat, joins the root of the tongue and spreads below the tongue; a branch goes again from the stomach up through the diaphragm and pours into the heart. Hence such reversal.

[On "Reversal of the lesser yin ... heart pain"] The foot lesser yin vessel rises along the posterior inner thigh, passes through the spine, belongs to the kidney and connects with the bladder; its straight course rises from the kidney through the liver and diaphragm, enters the lung, follows the throat and runs alongside the root of the tongue; a branch comes out from the lung, connects with the heart and pours into the chest. Hence such reversal.

[On "Reversal of the *jueyin* ... heat inside the shins"] The foot *jueyin* vessel, one *cun* from the inner ankle, crosses behind the greater yin eight *cun* above the ankle, rises along the inner edge of the popliteal fossa, follows the inner thigh into the pubic hair, encircles the genitals, reaches the lower abdomen, runs alongside the stomach, belongs to the liver, connects with the gallbladder and rises through the diaphragm. Hence such reversal. One version writes "heat outside the shins" for "heat inside the shins", a copying error of 内 and 外 in running script.

[On "When abundant, drain ... take it by the channel"] "Neither abundant nor depleted" means the evil qi is not yet abundant and the true qi not yet depleted. In such a case, take it by the channel method of the points, with the appropriate number of breaths of retention.

## Notes

The symptoms of reversal of each of the six foot channels and the principle of supplementing and draining. Wang Bing's notes describe the course of each channel in detail. Qibo's speech continues to unit 016.
- 賢 in Wang Bing's 属賢 is presumably an error for 腎 "kidney". 脊 in the same note was supplied where the transcription gave only a component description (see the 【補】 note).
