# 素問 標本病伝論篇第六十五 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The way of yin and yang, contrary and compliant, branch and root, is small yet great: by speaking of one thing one knows the harms of the hundred illnesses. It is little yet much, shallow yet broad: one can speak of one thing and know a hundred. Through the shallow one knows the deep; examining the near one knows the far. Branch and root are easy to speak of, yet none reach [them in practice].

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The way of yin and yang ... one knows the harms of the hundred illnesses"] It is the utmost of clarity. It means that when the method of distinguishing yin and yang and knowing contrary and compliant is clear, the subtle is plainly seen. Looking at what it raises, it is small; seeking what it benefits, it is great. Being thus clear, "by speaking of one thing one knows the harms of the hundred illnesses".

[On "It is little yet much, shallow yet broad: one can speak of one thing and know a hundred"] Why can one speak of little and penetrate much, raise the shallow and gauge the great? Because the method is clear. So if not the way of the sages, who could reach this? So those who learn it too can speak of one thing and know the hundred illnesses. 博 means great.

[On "Through the shallow one knows the deep ... yet none reach [them in practice]"] Although the matter is extremely deep and profound and people do not take it as close at hand, it is roughly penetrated through the shallow and near. And although the way of branch and root is easy to speak of, the understanding of ordinary people cannot reach it.

## Notes

The way of branch and root is simple yet far-reaching.
