# 素問 咳論篇第三十八 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "In lung cough, one coughs with panting and noisy breathing; when severe, one spits blood. In heart cough, coughing brings heart pain, the throat feels obstructed as if something were stuck, and when severe the throat swells and is blocked. In liver cough, coughing brings pain below both flanks; when severe one cannot turn, and turning brings fullness below both sides. In spleen cough, coughing brings pain below the right flank, a dull ache drawing to the shoulder and back; when severe one cannot move, and moving makes the cough violent. In kidney cough, coughing makes the lumbus and back pull on each other and ache; when severe one coughs up drool."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In lung cough ... one spits blood"] The lung stores qi and corresponds to breathing; therefore coughing brings panting and sound in the throat. When severe, the network vessels of the lung counterflow; therefore one spits blood.

[On "In heart cough ... the throat swells and is blocked"] The hand heart-master vessel arises in the chest and emerges to belong to the pericardium. The lesser yin vessel arises in the heart and emerges to belong to the heart connector; a branch rises from the heart connector alongside the throat. Hence such illness.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 喝喝 for 介介如梗状. Moreover, the lesser yin vessel rises alongside the gullet; it is not said to run alongside the larynx.

[On "In liver cough ... turning brings fullness below both sides"] The foot *jueyin* vessel rises through the diaphragm, spreads over the flanks and follows the back of the throat; hence this. 胠 also means flank.

[On "In spleen cough ... moving makes the cough violent"] The foot greater yin vessel rises through the diaphragm alongside the throat; a branch goes again from the stomach up through the diaphragm. Hence such illness. Spleen qi connects with the lung; therefore the pain draws to the shoulder and back. Spleen qi governs the right; therefore below the right side there is a deep, dull ache.

[On "In kidney cough ... one coughs up drool"] The foot lesser yin vessel rises along the posterior edge of the inner thigh, passes through the spine, belongs to the kidney and connects with the bladder; its straight course rises from the kidney through the liver and diaphragm, enters the lung, follows the throat and runs alongside the root of the tongue. Also, the bladder vessel branches down from inside the shoulder blade, runs alongside the spine to the lumbus, enters along the spinal muscles and connects with the kidney. Hence such illness.

## Notes

The symptoms of the cough of each of the five zang, explained by the course of the channels.
