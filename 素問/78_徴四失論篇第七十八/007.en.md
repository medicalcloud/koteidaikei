# 素問 徴四失論篇第七十八 §7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Not adapting to the dwellings of the poor and rich, noble and humble, the thinness or thickness of their seats, the cold or warmth of their bodies, not adapting to what food suits them, not distinguishing people's courage and timidity, and not knowing how to compare categories: this is enough to confuse oneself but not to enlighten oneself. This is the third failure in treatment.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "this is enough to confuse oneself but not to enlighten oneself. This is the third failure in treatment"] The poor and humble toil; the rich and noble are at ease. At ease, they cannot be harmed by pathogens but are easily harmed by toil; toiling, they are easily harmed by pathogens. As for toil, the rich are at half of the noble; as for pathogens, the poor are at half of the humble. The general rule is thus, though in families with hereditary salaries it may differ. The courageous are hard to affect, the timid easily harmed; the two differ presumably because their spirit qi is strong or weak. Observing the meaning of poverty, humility, wealth and nobility, the principles of the thickness of seats, warmth of bodies and suitability of food can be known. Without knowing how to compare categories, use must go astray and 哀 [weaken], serving only to confuse one's mind; how could one 妄 [hope] for clear understanding? Hence the third failure.

## Notes

The third of the four failures: not adapting to the patient's circumstances, constitution and temperament, and not knowing how to compare categories.
- In the latter part of Wang Bing's note, 哀 in 用必乖哀 is read as 衰 and 妄 in 豈通明之可妄乎 as 望, but these have not been verified.
