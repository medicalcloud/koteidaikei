# 素問 徴四失論篇第七十八 §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Sitting and grasping the inch opening, the diagnosis does not hit the five pulses or [find] where the hundred illnesses arise. At first one resents oneself, [then] lays the blame on one's teacher. Hence treatment cannot follow principle, the art is cast away in the marketplace, and when reckless treatment sometimes cures, the foolish mind is self-satisfied.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "At first one resents oneself, [then] lays the blame on one's teacher"] Unable to study the art of the Way deeply, one causes errors in diagnosis, and then voices resentment and slander upward, laying blame on one's teacher; such a thing has never been [right].

[On "the art is cast away in the marketplace, and when reckless treatment sometimes cures, the foolish mind is self-satisfied"] Unable to cultivate the utmost principle, one peddles one's art in the market and shops, and people do not trust it; being empty and erroneous, it is called "casting the art away in the marketplace". But a fool who thinks a hundred times gets it right once; what credit is that?
Song editors: The Quan Yuanqi edition has 巧 for 自; the *Taisu* has 自功.

## Notes

The Emperor describes the folly of blaming one's teacher for missed diagnoses and gloating over chance cures.
