# 素問 徴四失論篇第七十八 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Not completing what one receives from one's teacher, recklessly making up miscellaneous arts, taking erroneous words for the Way, changing names to claim credit oneself, recklessly using stone needles, and later bringing blame on oneself: this is the second failure in treatment.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "recklessly making up miscellaneous arts, taking erroneous words for the Way, changing names to claim credit oneself"] Song editors: The *Taisu* has 巧 for 功.

[On "recklessly using stone needles, and later bringing blame on oneself: this is the second failure in treatment"] Not finishing the teacher's art, doing only what is reckless, altering the ancient and changing the constant, claiming credit and following 已 [oneself]: is it not fitting that blame falls on oneself? Hence the second failure. Laozi says, "Bringing no calamity on oneself is called following the constant"; this is presumably aimed at recklessness.

## Notes

The second of the four failures: recklessly inventing arts without completing one's teacher's instruction.
