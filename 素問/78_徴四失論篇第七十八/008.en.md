# 素問 徴四失論篇第七十八 §8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In diagnosing illness, not asking how it began — worry and hardship, irregular eating and drinking, excess in daily habits, or injury from poison — not first discussing these but abruptly grasping the inch opening: what illness could one hit upon? Making up names with reckless words, one is exposed even by crude [doctors]. This is the fourth failure in treatment.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Making up names with reckless words, one is exposed even by crude [doctors]. This is the fourth failure in treatment"] 憂 means worry and fear; 患 means hardship and trouble. Irregular eating and drinking means overeating. Excess in daily habits means wearing oneself out. Injury from poison means illnesses that cannot be treated by sticking to the rules of the viscera and bowels overriding one another. Abruptly grasping the inch opening means not first checking whether the inch-opening pulse is balanced or not. Even a skilled practitioner equipped with the four arts still has doubts; so if the diagnosis cannot hit the form and name of the illness and the words cannot fit the classic, and one makes things up recklessly, even crude doctors can expose the errors; how much more would those of deep understanding see it and call it wrong? Hence the fourth failure.

## Notes

The fourth of the four failures: naming the illness from the pulse alone without asking about its beginning and the patient's life.
