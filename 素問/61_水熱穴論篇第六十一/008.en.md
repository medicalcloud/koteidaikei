# 素問 水熱穴論篇第六十一 §8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The fifty-seven kidney points are where accumulated yin gathers and where water goes out and comes in. The five rows of five above the sacrum are the kidney points. Therefore in water illness, when there is puffy swelling and a large abdomen below, and panting and wheezing with inability to lie down above, root and branch are both ill. Thus the lungs produce panting and wheezing, the kidneys produce water swelling, and the lungs produce counterflow so that one cannot lie down. They are divided yet transport to each other, and both receive [the illness], because they are where water qi lodges. The two rows of five on each side above Futu are the thoroughfare of the kidneys, where the three yin cross and bind in the legs. The one row of six on each side above the ankles is the downward course of the kidney vessel, called Taichong. All fifty-seven points are the yin network vessels of the depots, where water lodges."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The five rows of five above the sacrum are the kidney points"] The points on the back are in five rows in all. The middle one is where the qi of the *du* vessel issues; the next four rows on both sides are all foot greater yang vessel qi.

[On "Therefore in water illness, when there is puffy swelling and a large abdomen below, and panting and wheezing above"] When water goes down and resides in the kidneys, there is puffy swelling from the abdomen to the feet; when it goes up into the lungs, there is panting, hurried breathing and loud wheezing.

[On "with inability to lie down above, root and branch are both ill"] Root and branch: the lungs are the branch and the kidneys the root. In such cases lungs and kidneys are both ill from water.

[On "Thus the lungs produce panting and wheezing ... cannot lie down"] The lungs produce panting, wheezing and counterflow of qi with inability to lie down because they govern breathing. The kidneys produce water swelling because they govern water.

[On "They are divided yet transport to each other ... where water qi lodges"] Naming them by their separate locations, the qi transports and responds between them; taking as the basis that both receive the pathogenic qi, all are where water lodges.

[On "The two rows of five on each side above Futu are the thoroughfare of the kidneys"] 街 means road. The proper abdominal points are in five rows in all. Those flanking the navel on both sides are where the qi of the kidney depot's foot lesser yin vessel and the *chong* vessel issues; the next on both sides are where the vessel qi of the stomach bowel's foot *yangming* issues. These four rows of points are above Futu.

[On "The one row of six on each side above the ankles ... called Taichong"] The kidney vessel runs down together with the *chong* vessel, follows the foot, and joins to become abundant and great; hence it is called Taichong ("great thoroughfare").

[On "All fifty-seven points are the yin network vessels of the depots, where water lodges"] The fifty-seven of the classic are as follows. Of the five rows of five above the sacrum, those in the central row of the spine where the qi of the *du* vessel issues are Jizhong, Xuanshu, Mingmen, Yaoshu and Changqiang. Next, those flanking the *du* vessel on both sides where the foot greater yang vessel qi issues are Dachangshu, Xiaochangshu, Pangguangshu, Zhonglüneishu and Baihuanshu. Next again, those further out on both sides where the foot greater yang vessel qi issues are Weicang, Huangmen, Zhishi, Baohuang and Zhibian. Of the two rows of five on each side above Futu, the proper abdominal points flanking the central *ren* vessel on both sides, at the meeting of the *chong* vessel and foot lesser yin, are Zhongzhu, Siman, Qixue, Dahe and Henggu. Next, those flanking the *chong* vessel and foot lesser yin on both sides, where the foot *yangming* vessel qi issues, are Wailing, Daju, Shuidao, Guilai and Qijie. As for the one row of six on each side above the ankles: above the inner ankle the foot lesser yin and *yinqiao* vessels run together up along the calf. The foot lesser yin vessel has three points, Taichong, Fuliu and Yingu; the *yinqiao* vessel has three points, Zhaohai, Jiaoxin and Zhubin. Since the *yinqiao* is a branch of the foot lesser yin vessel, it can be treated as governed together with it. Counting these together, one point is still lacking.
Jizhong is in the space below the eleventh vertebra; take it with the patient bending forward; one may needle five body-*fen*; moxibustion is prohibited; 今 [it would make] the person hunchbacked. Xuanshu is in the space below the thirteenth vertebra; take it lying prone; one may needle three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones. Mingmen is in the space below the fourteenth vertebra; take it lying prone; one may needle five body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones. Yaoshu is in the space below the twenty-first vertebra; one may needle two body-*fen* [Song editors: the *Jiayi jing* and the notes to the *Miuci lun* and the *Rexue* all say "needle two *cun*", while the notes to the *Cire*, the *Qifu* and this note write "two *fen*"; the two *fen* reading should be followed.] and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Changqiang is at the end of the coccyx, a separate network vessel of the *du* vessel where the lesser yin binds; one may needle two body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. These five points are all where the qi of the *du* vessel issues. [Song editors: On examination, Wang says one point is lacking; according to the note to the *Qifu lun*, there is the Yangguan point below the twelfth vertebra. If Yangguan is counted, none is lacking.]
Next, flanking the *du* vessel on both sides. Dachangshu is below the sixteenth vertebra, flanking the *du* vessel on both sides, one and a half body-*cun* from the *du* vessel on each side; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain six breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Xiaochangshu is on both sides below the eighteenth vertebra; spacing, needling, moxibustion, *fen* and cones are as for Dachangshu. Pangguangshu is on both sides below the nineteenth vertebra; spacing, needling, moxibustion, *fen* and cones are as for Dachangshu. Zhonglüneishu is on both sides below the twentieth vertebra; spacing, needling, moxibustion, *fen* and cones are as for Dachangshu; it is on the raised flesh of the 𦛗肺 flanking the spine; retain ten breaths. Baihuanshu is on both sides below the twenty-first vertebra, spaced as Dachangshu; take it lying prone; one may needle five body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones. [Song editors: the *Jiayi jing* says "one may needle eight *fen*; no moxibustion".] These five points are all where the foot greater yang vessel qi issues; these are the so-called kidney points.
Next again, the outer pair of rows. Weicang is on both sides below the twelfth vertebra, three body-*cun* apart on each side; one may needle five body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones. Huangmen is on both sides below the thirteenth vertebra; spacing, needling, moxibustion, *fen* and cones are as for Weicang. Zhishi is on both sides below the fourteenth vertebra; spacing, needling, moxibustion, *fen* and cones are as for Weicang; take it sitting upright. Baohuang is on both sides below the nineteenth vertebra; spacing, needling, moxibustion, *fen* and cones are as for Weicang; take it lying prone. Zhibian is on both sides below the twenty-first vertebra; spacing, needling, moxibustion, *fen* and cones are as for Weicang; take it lying prone. These five points are all where the foot greater yang vessel qi issues.
Next, the two rows above Futu. Zhongzhu is five body-*fen* below the navel, five body-*fen* from the *ren* vessel on each side. [Song editors: the *Jiayi jing* agrees. The note to the *Qifu* says "one *cun* square flanking the central row"; the wording differs but the sense is the same.] Siman is one body-*cun* below Zhongzhu, Qixue one body-*cun* below Siman, Dahe one body-*cun* below Qixue, and Henggu one body-*cun* below Dahe, each one body-*cun* apart horizontally. All are meetings of the *chong* vessel and foot lesser yin; one may needle one body-*cun*; for moxibustion, five cones.
Next, the outer points on both sides. Wailing is one body-*cun* below the navel [Song editors: the note to the *Qifu lun* says "Wailing is one *cun* below Tianshu", exactly the same as this.], one and a half body-*cun* from the *chong* vessel on each side. Daju is one body-*cun* below Wailing, Shuidao three body-*cun* below Daju, Guilai three body-*cun* below Shuidao, and Qijie below Guilai [Song editors: the notes to the *Qifu*, *Cire* and *Rexue* say "on the abdomen below the navel, at both ends of the pubic bone, one *cun* above the groin"; the note to the *Cijin* says "below the abdomen flanking the navel, four *cun* apart, one *cun* above the 鼠僕, where the pulse is felt under the hand"; the note to the *Gukong* says "on both sides of the hairline above the groin". As the notes disagree, we record them all here.], one body-*cun* above the groin, each two body-*cun* apart horizontally. These five points are all where the foot *yangming* vessel qi issues. Shuidao: one may needle two and a half body-*cun*; for moxibustion, five cones. Qijie: one may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. The remaining three points: one may needle eight body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones each. These are the so-called thoroughfare of the kidneys.
The one row of six on each side above the ankles. Taizhong is in the thoroughfare behind the inner ankle [Song editors: the *Jiayi jing* says "in the thoroughfare (衝) behind the heel"; the notes to the *Cinüe* and *Ci yaotong* write "at the pulsing vessel in the thoroughfare (街) behind the heel". This note's "behind the inner ankle" is wrong.], where the foot lesser yin network vessel branches off to the greater yang; one may needle two body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Fuliu is in the depression two body-*cun* above the inner ankle, where the foot lesser yin vessel travels; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, five cones. Zhaohai is below the inner ankle; one may needle four body-*fen* and retain six breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Jiaoxin is two body-*cun* above the inner ankle, between sinew and bone in front of the lesser yin and behind the greater yin, the cleft point of the *yinqiao*; one may needle four body-*fen* and retain five breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Zhubin is above the inner ankle in the division of the calf, the cleft point of the *yinwei*; one may needle three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones. Yingu is below the knee, behind the inner auxiliary bone, below the large sinew and above the small sinew; pressing it, it responds under the hand; it is found with the knee bent; it is where the foot lesser yin vessel enters; one may needle four body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones. This is the so-called "downward course of the kidney channel, called Taichong".

## Notes

The arrangement of the fifty-seven water points (kidney points), and water illness as illness of both root and branch, lungs and kidneys. Wang Bing lists all fifty-seven points.
- Wang Bing gives the three foot lesser yin points above the ankle as "Taichong, Fuliu, Yingu", then lists "Taizhong" in the detailed list. The relation between the main text's Taichong (the name of the row above the ankle) and the point names Taizhong/Taichong is translated as Wang Bing has it.
- 今 in Wang Bing's 今人僂 is presumably an error for 令 ("it would make the person hunchbacked"), and 鼠僕 in the Song editors' quotation for 鼠鼷. 𦛗肺 in 侠脊𦛗肺起肉 is obscure and may contain an error; not checked.
- Which way of counting leaves Wang Bing's "one point still lacking" has not been checked. The Song editors say adding Yangguan to the *du* vessel row on the back makes up the number.
- The Song editors' location of Yangguan (below the twelfth vertebra) does not match Wang Bing's note in the *Gukong lun* (below the sixteenth vertebra).
