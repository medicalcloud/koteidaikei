# 素問 水熱穴論篇第六十一 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "The kidneys are the utmost yin; the utmost yin is what fills with water. The lungs are the greater yin. The lesser yin is the winter vessel. Therefore its root is in the kidneys and its branch in the lungs, and both accumulate water."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The kidneys are the utmost yin ... both accumulate water"] Yin means cold. The winter months are utterly cold, and kidney qi corresponds to them; hence "the kidneys are the utmost yin". Water flourishes in winter; hence "the utmost yin is what fills with water". The kidney lesser yin vessel goes up from the kidneys through the liver and diaphragm and enters the lungs; hence "its root is in the kidneys and its branch in the lungs". When kidney qi counterflows upward, water qi lodges in the lungs; hence "both accumulate water".

## Notes

The kidneys and lungs as the root and branch of water.
