# 素問 水熱穴論篇第六十一 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The kidneys are the gate of the stomach. When the gate does not work freely, water gathers and follows its kind. It overflows above and below into the skin, causing puffy swelling. Puffy swelling is illness produced by gathered water."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The kidneys are the gate of the stomach ... follows its kind"] A gate controls going out and coming in. The kidneys govern the lower burner, have the bladder as their bowel, govern its separation and flow, and are the gate-orifice of the two yin [orifices]. So when kidney qi transforms, the two yin are open; when the two yin are shut, the stomach becomes full and stuffed. Hence "the kidneys are the 開 [gate] of the stomach". When the 𨳩 [gate] is closed, water accumulates; when water accumulates, qi stops; when qi stops, water is produced; when water is produced, qi overflows. Qi and water are of the same kind; hence "when the gate is closed and does not work freely, water gathers and follows its kind". The *Lingshu jing* says "when the lower burner overflows, it becomes water"; this is what is meant.

[On "It overflows above and below into the skin ... illness produced by gathered water"] Above means the lungs; below means the kidneys. When lungs and kidneys both overflow, water gathers in the abdomen and produces illness.

## Notes

The kidneys as the gate of the stomach; when the gate fails, water gathers and causes puffy swelling.
- 開 in Wang Bing's 胃之開也 and 𨳩 in 𨳩閉 presumably stand for 関 in the main text, by error or as variant forms. Wang Bing's note has 関閉不利, and the main text in 殆知閣 (重广补注黄帝内经素问) also reads 関閉 (see the variants).
