# 素問 水熱穴論篇第六十一 §18 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The five rows of five on top of the head serve to dispel the counterflow of heat of the various yang. Dazhu, Yingshu, Quepen and Beishu, these eight, serve to drain heat in the chest. Qijie, Sanli, and the upper and lower Juxu, these eight, serve to drain heat in the stomach. Yunmen, Yugu, Weizhong and Suikong, these eight, serve to drain heat in the four limbs. The five beside the *shu* points of the five depots, these ten, serve to drain heat in the five depots. All these fifty-nine points are where heat is governed."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The five rows of five on top of the head serve to dispel the counterflow of heat of the various yang"] Of the five rows on the head, the central row is Shangxing, Xinhui, Qianding, Baihui and Houding. The next on both sides are Wuchu, Chengguang, Tongtian, Luoque and Yuzhen. The next again on both sides are Linqi, Muchuang, Zhengying, Chengling and Naokong. Shangxing is on top of the skull, directly above the center of the nose, in the depression one body-*cun* inside the hairline, which holds a bean; one may needle three body-*fen*. Xinhui is in the depression one body-*cun* behind Shangxing; one may needle four body-*fen*. Qianding is in the depression between the bones one body-*cun* five *fen* behind Xinhui; needle as for Xinhui. Baihui is one body-*cun* five *fen* behind Qianding, at the center of the crown in the hair whorl, in a depression that admits a finger, the crossing of the *du* vessel and foot greater yang vessel; needle as for Shangxing. Houding is two body-*cun* five *fen* behind Baihui, above the occipital bone; needle as for Xinhui. These five are all where the qi of the *du* vessel issues. 上骨 [presumably Shangxing]: retain six breaths. For moxibustion, five cones each. The next points on both sides: Wuchu is one body-*cun* five *fen* on each side of Shangxing; Chengguang one body-*cun* behind Wuchu; Tongtian one body-*cun* five *fen* behind Chengguang; Luoque one body-*cun* five *fen* behind Tongtian; Yuzhen seven body-*fen* behind Luoque. These five are all where the foot greater yang vessel qi issues; one may needle three body-*fen*; retain seven breaths at Wuchu and Tongtian, five at Luoque, and three at 玊 [玉] zhen; for moxibustion, three cones. [Song editors: in the *Jiayi jing* Chengguang receives no moxibustion, and Yuzhen is needled two *fen*.] Further, needle the points on both sides: Linqi is directly above the eye on the head, five body-*fen* inside the hairline, the meeting of the three vessels foot greater yang, lesser yang and *yangwei*. Muchuang and Zhengying are successively one body-*cun* apart; Chengling and Naokong successively one body-*cun* five *fen* apart. These five are all meetings of the two vessels foot lesser yang and *yangwei*. Naokong alone may be needled four body-*fen*; the rest may all be needled three body-*fen*. At Linqi retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, five cones.

[On "Dazhu, Yingshu, Quepen and Beishu, these eight, serve to drain heat in the chest"] Dazhu is in the depressions below the first vertebra of the nape, one and a half body-*cun* to each side, the meeting of the qi of three vessels: the separate network vessel of the *du* vessel and the hand and foot greater yang; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, five cones. [Song editors: the *Jiayi jing* and the note to the *Qixue* write "seven cones"; the notes to the *Cinüe* and *Cire* write "five cones".] Yingshu is the point in the chest; its proper name is Zhongfu, on both sides of the central line of the chest, six body-*cun* apart, one *cun* below Yunmen, in the depression between the ribs three ribs above the nipple where the pulse is felt under the hand; take it with the patient facing up; it is the meeting of the hand and foot greater yin vessels; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain five breaths; for moxibustion, five cones. Quepen is in the depression of the transverse bone above the shoulder, where the hand *yangming* vessel qi issues; one may needle two body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Beishu is the Fengmen Refu point, one body-*cun* three *fen* on each side below the second vertebra, the meeting of the *du* vessel and foot greater yang; one may needle five body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, five cones. Although the present *Zhonggao kongxue tujing* does not name it, since it is called Fengmen Refu ("wind gate, heat residence"), it is the back point for treating heat.
Song editors: In his note to the *Cire lun* Wang says "where Beishu is, is unclear"; here he names it Fengmen Refu; in his note to the *Qixue lun* he takes Dazhu as Beishu. That the three places disagree is presumably because he too was in doubt.

[On "Qijie, Sanli, and the upper and lower Juxu, these eight, serve to drain heat in the stomach"] Qijie is on the abdomen below the navel, at both ends of the pubic bone, one body-*cun* above the groin, where the pulse is felt under the hand, where the foot *yangming* vessel qi issues; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. [Song editors: the notes on Qijie disagree; they are given in full in the earlier note on the water points.] Sanli is three body-*cun* below the knee, in the division between the two muscles at the outer edge of the shin, where the foot *yangming* vessel enters; one may needle one body-*cun* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Upper Juxu is the uniting point of the foot *yangming* with the large intestine, three body-*cun* below Sanli, where the foot *yangming* vessel qi issues; one may needle eight body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones. Lower Juxu is the uniting point of the foot *yangming* with the small intestine, three body-*cun* below the upper one, where the foot *yangming* vessel qi issues; one may needle three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "Yunmen, Yugu, Weizhong and Suikong, these eight, serve to drain heat in the four limbs"] Yunmen is below the great bone, on both sides of the central line of the chest, six body-*cun* apart, where the pulse is felt under the hand, where the foot greater yin vessel qi issues. [Song editors: the *Jiayi jing* agrees; the note to the *Qixue* writes "hand greater yin", and the note to the *Cire* also writes "hand greater yin".] Take it with the arm raised; one may needle seven body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones. Checking the present *Zhonggao kongxue tujing*, there is no Yugu point, but there 冇 [is] the Jianyu point, between the two bones at the tip of the shoulder, the meeting of the hand *yangming* and the *qiao* vessel; one may needle six body-*fen* and retain six breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Weizhong is in the bend behind the knee, at the pulsing vessel in the crease at the center of the popliteal fossa, where the foot greater yang vessel enters; one may needle five body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. The present *Zhonggao kongxue tujing* says: "The Yaoshu point, one 各 [name] Suikong, is in the middle of the spine below the twenty-first vertebra; it governs absence of sweating and cold, numb feet; it is where the qi of the *du* vessel issues; one may needle two body-*cun* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones."
Song editors: On examination, "needle two *cun*" for Yaoshu should be "two *fen*", as given in full in the earlier note on the water points.

[On "The five beside the *shu* points of the five depots, these ten, serve to drain heat in the five depots"] The five beside the *shu* points are Pohu, Shentang, Hunmen, Yishe and Zhishi, flanking the spine on both sides, each three body-*cun* apart, all where the foot greater yang vessel qi issues. Pohu is on both sides below the third vertebra; take it sitting upright; one may needle five body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones. Shentang is on both sides below the fifth vertebra; one may needle three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones. Hunmen is on both sides below the ninth vertebra; take it sitting upright; one may needle five body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones. Yishe is on both sides below the eleventh vertebra; take it sitting upright; one may needle five body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones. Zhishi is on both sides below the fourteenth vertebra; take it sitting upright; one may needle five body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones.

## Notes

The locations and functions of the fifty-nine points for heat illness. Wang Bing gives the location and needling and moxibustion for every point.
- 上骨 in Wang Bing's 上骨留六呼 is presumably an error for 上星 from the context, but this has not been checked. 玊枕 stands for 玉枕, 冇 in 冇肩髃穴 for 有, and 各 in 一各髄空 for 名.
- In the note on the head, 又刺両傍 after the Song editors' 按甲乙経承光不灸玉枕刺入二分 is read as the continuation of Wang Bing's note (perhaps meaning 又次両傍), but where the Song editors' note ends is unclear.
- 熱之左右 is obscure; it is translated as "where heat is governed".
