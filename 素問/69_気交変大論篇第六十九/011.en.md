# 素問 気交変大論篇第六十九 §11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the year's metal is excessive, dry qi prevails and liver wood receives the pathogen. The people suffer from pain below both flanks and in the lower abdomen, red and painful eyes, sores at the canthi, and inability to hear. When austere killing is extreme, the body is heavy, with vexation and oppression, chest pain drawing to the back, and fullness and pain of both flanks drawing to the lower abdomen; above, it corresponds to the Great White Star. In severe cases there are panting, coughing and counterflow qi, pain of the shoulders and back, and illness of the sacrum, inner thighs, knees, hips, calves, shins and feet; above, it corresponds to the Sparkling Deluder. The gathering qi is harsh and the generating qi sinks; plants contract, and dry and fall while still green. The illness on the contrary is sudden pain, inability to turn over because of the flanks, severe coughing counterflow with bleeding. Those whose Taichong [pulse] is cut off die and cannot be treated; above, it corresponds to the Great White Star.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the year's metal is excessive, dry qi prevails and liver wood receives the pathogen"] It is so when metal is violent.

[On "The people suffer from pain below both flanks ... inability to hear"] Both flanks means below the breasts and below the flanks. The lower abdomen means both sides below the navel, inside the hip bones. Red eyes means the whites turning red. Pain means a seeping pain. The canthi are the roots of the eyelashes at the four edges.

[On "When austere killing is extreme ... corresponds to the Great White Star"] Metal qi is already excessive and austere killing extreme; wood qi is inwardly afraid, and illness arises in response. Metal is abundant and corresponds in heaven; when the Great White Star is bright and large and presides over the associated lodges, they 心 [surely] suffer disaster.
Song editors: The *Zangqi fashi lun* says "when the liver is ill, there is pain below both flanks drawing to the lower abdomen; when the liver is depleted, the eyes are dim and see nothing and the ears hear nothing"; and the *Yuji zhenzang lun* says "when the liver pulse is deficient, it causes chest pain drawing to the back, and below, fullness of both flanks".

[On "In severe cases there are panting ... corresponds to the Sparkling Deluder"] Fire qi retaliates against it, and it falls ill itself. The heavenly sign is in the Sparkling Deluder; if it retrogrades to guard the associated lodges, there is cause for worry.
Song editors: The *Zangqi fashi lun* says "when the lungs are ill, there are panting, coughing, counterflow qi, pain of the shoulders and back, sweating, and pain of the sacrum, inner thighs, knees, hips, calves, shins and feet".

[On "The illness on the contrary is sudden pain, inability to turn over because of the flanks"] Song editors: On examination, it says "on the contrary sudden pain" without saying where. The *Zhizhen yao dalun* says "sudden pain of the heart and flanks, inability to turn over"; so this is sudden pain of the heart and flanks.

[On "severe coughing counterflow with bleeding ... corresponds to the Great White Star"] All *geng* years. Metal qi is harsh and 瘧 [cruel], wood qi is punished, and fire has not yet come to retaliate; then it is so. 斂 means branches and leaves already grown contract and cling to the body. Taichong is the liver pulse; metal prevails and wood is cut off, hence death. At this time the Great White Star responds; if it retrogrades to guard the associated stars, the illnesses are all dangerous.
Song editors: In the *gengzi*, *gengwu*, *gengyin* and *gengshen* years lesser yin or lesser yang appears above as controller of heaven; this is called heaven punishing the movement. The transformation of metal is halved; so it should be abundant but cannot be; it is neither excessive nor deficient.

## Notes

The weather, illnesses and astral correspondences of a year of excessive metal.
- 心 in Wang Bing's 心受災害 is translated as an error for 必 "surely", but this has not been checked. 瘧 in 峻瘧 is read as 虐 "cruel".
