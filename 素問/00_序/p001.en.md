# 素問 序 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Your servants have heard that not forgetting danger in times of safety and not forgetting ruin in times of survival was the first task of the sages of old, and that seeking out the people's ailments and pitying their hidden suffering is the deep benevolence of the ruler above. In ancient times, when the Yellow Emperor ruled, he governed the world with what was left over from ordering his own person; seated in the Bright Hall, he surveyed the eight extremities and established the five constants. He considered that human life carries yin on its back and embraces yang, eats flavors and is clothed in colors; outside, cold and heat shake it; inside, joy and anger assail it in turn; early death and pestilence occur in every age of the state. Wishing to gather the five blessings of the time and bestow them on his people, he with Qibo explored the order of heaven above and the principles of earth below, drew on things afar and on the body near at hand, questioned and challenged each other, and handed down rules to bless ten thousand 出 [generations]. Then Leigong and others received the teaching and transmitted it, and the *Neijing* was made. Every dynasty treasured it, and it was never lost. When the Zhou arose, He of Qin set out the theory of the six qi, clearly recorded in the *Zuo* history. Later Yueren obtained one or two parts of it and expanded them in the *Nanjing*. In the Western Han, Canggong transmitted its old learning; in the Eastern Han, [Zhang] Zhongjing compiled its surviving discussions; in the Jin, Huangfu Mi 刺 [arranged] it into the *Jiayi*; and in the Sui, Yang Shangshan compiled it into the *Taisu*. At that time there was one Quan Yuanqi, who first wrote explanations of it, lacking the seventh fascicle. In the Baoying era of the Tang, the Grand Servant Wang Bing loved it deeply, obtained the fascicles kept by his late master, and greatly rearranged and annotated it; it is still the legacy of the Three Sovereigns, brilliant and worth seeing. Alas, the Tang ordinances classed it under medicine and assigned it to the craftsmen, and the scholar-officials seldom spoke of it. Far removed from the sages, its art grew obscure, so that text and notes were confused and meaning and principle mixed up. People did not know at all that it is the legacy of the Three Mounds, the high achievement of emperors and kings, the work of sages and worthies; that Yao of Tang's granting of the four seasons, Shun of Yu's regulating of the seven governors, the divine Yu's ordering of the six stores to raise the imperial achievement, King Wen's deriving of the six children to set out the qi of the trigrams, Yi Yin's blending of the five flavors to reach his lord, and Jizi's setting out of the five phases to aid the world all come to one. How could the most refined and subtle way have been transmitted to the lowest and shallowest of people? That it has not been abandoned and lost is already fortunate. Recently, in the Jiayou era, Renzong, mindful that the legacy of the sage ancestors was about to fall to the ground, issued an edict to those versed in this learning to correct it. Your servants, filling a vacancy, took charge of the collation, and after a full year's reflection searched within and outside [the court], gathered many editions, gradually traced their meaning and corrected their errors, getting three or four out of ten, unable to complete the rest. Considering this insufficient to answer the enlightened edict and meet the sage intent, we further drew on the ancient medical classics extant in the bibliographies of the Han and Tang histories, obtaining several dozen authors, and arranged and examined them. Threading through and interweaving, broadly synthesizing, sometimes correcting the root to trace the branches, sometimes going upstream to search out the source, we settled what could be known and ordered it by the old table of contents; we corrected some six thousand miswritten characters and added over two thousand annotations on meaning; every word kept or removed has its basis in research, and erroneous texts and doubtful meanings are now made clear. Using it to govern the body, one can dispel trouble before it shows; applying it to government, one can extend life without limit. Reverently we consider that the Emperor holds the fortune of the Great Unity and possesses boundless blessings, carrying on the will of his forebears to bring it to completion and reviving subtle learning to correct it forever; then harmonious qi can be summoned, calamities will not arise, and the people of the whole age, being molded, will together ascend to the realm of long life.

## Notes

The preface of the Song editors (Gao Baoheng, Lin Yi and others) when they presented the *Chongguang buzhu Huangdi neijing suwen* to the throne. It recounts the origin and transmission of the *Neijing* and the course of the collation. There are no notes.
- 天 in 天昏札瘥 is read as 夭 ("early death") and 出 in 福万出 as 世 ("generations"), following the readings of other sources shown in the collation notes. 刺 in 晋皇甫謐刺而為甲乙 is the same in the Daizhige texts; it is read in the sense of 次 ("arrange"), but this has not been verified.
