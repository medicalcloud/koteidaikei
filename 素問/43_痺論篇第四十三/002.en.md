# 素問 痺論篇第四十三 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "Wind, cold and damp, the three qi, arrive mixed and combine to form impediment. Where wind qi prevails it is moving impediment; where cold qi prevails it is painful impediment; where damp qi prevails it is fixed impediment."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Wind, cold and damp ... combine to form impediment"] Though they combine to form impediment, their onsets differ.

[On "Where wind qi prevails it is moving impediment ... fixed impediment"] With wind, yang receives it; therefore the impediment moves. With cold, yin receives it; therefore the impediment is painful. With damp, the skin, flesh, sinews and vessels receive it; therefore the impediment is fixed and does not leave. Thus impediment arises from wind, cold and damp.

## Notes

Impediment arises from the combination of wind, cold and damp, and is divided into moving, painful and fixed impediment according to which qi prevails. A frequently quoted passage.
