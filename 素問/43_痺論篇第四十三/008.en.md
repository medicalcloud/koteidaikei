# 素問 痺論篇第四十三 §8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

As for the yin qi, when calm the spirit is stored; when agitated it wastes away. When eating and drinking are doubled, the intestines and stomach are harmed. Excessive qi with panting means impediment gathers in the lung; excessive qi with worry and pensiveness means impediment gathers in the heart; excessive qi with incontinence of urine means impediment gathers in the kidney; excessive qi with exhaustion means impediment gathers in the liver; excessive qi with wasting of the flesh means impediment gathers in the spleen. If the impediments do not end, they go further inward. Those in whom wind qi prevails recover easily."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "As for the yin qi, when calm the spirit is stored; when agitated it wastes away"] Yin means the five spirit-storing zang. Spirit storage and wasting away are mentioned because when a person is calm and not involved with evil qi, the spirit qi is peaceful and stored within; when a person is agitated and exposed to evil qi, the spirit is harmed and scatters, and the zang have nothing to guard; hence "wastes away". This refers to the five zang receiving evil and forming impediment.

[On "When eating and drinking are doubled, the intestines and stomach are harmed"] The zang are harmed by agitation, the fu are damaged by eating and drinking; in all cases, overuse beyond one's nature means receiving evil. This refers to the six fu receiving evil and forming impediment.

[On "Excessive qi with panting ... impediment gathers in the spleen"] Excessive qi means qi moving recklessly; it enters according to what each zang governs and forms impediment.
Song editors: On examination, from "When impediment lodges in the five zang" above to here was in the *Yinyang bielun* in Quan Yuanqi's edition; Mr Wang moved it here.

[On "If the impediments do not end, they go further inward"] If they do not leave from the outside, they go ever deeper into the body.

## Notes

The conditions under which the zang and fu receive impediment (agitation, excessive eating and drinking), and the signs of zang impediment due to excessive qi. Qibo's speech, begun in unit 006, ends here.
