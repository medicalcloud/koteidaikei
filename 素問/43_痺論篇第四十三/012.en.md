# 素問 痺論篇第四十三 §12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Here too eating, drinking and dwelling are the root of the illness. The six fu also each have their transport points. When wind, cold and damp strike their transport points and eating and drinking respond to them, they enter along the transport points and each lodges in its fu."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Here too eating, drinking and dwelling are the root of the illness"] Although the four quarters differ in the warmth or coolness and height of the land, and in the hardness or softness of things, food and dwelling do not differ; but when activity exceeds one's portion, the six fu are harmed. The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* says, "When the cold and heat of water and grain affect one, they harm the six fu."
Song editors: The *Shanghan lun* says, "The hardness and softness of things, and food and dwelling, also differ."

[On "The six fu also each have their transport points ... each lodges in its fu"] The transport points of the six fu are also the back transport points. The gallbladder transport point is beside the tenth vertebra, the stomach's beside the twelfth, the triple burner's beside the thirteenth, the large intestine's beside the sixteenth, the small intestine's beside the eighteenth, and the bladder's beside the nineteenth. Take them according to the length of the body. Each is one and a half body-*cun* from the spine, and all are where the vessel qi of the foot greater yang issues.
Song editors: On examination, the transport points of the six fu are all on both sides below the respective vertebra; this note's "beside the vertebra" is an abbreviation.

## Notes

Impediment of the six fu arises when irregular eating, drinking and dwelling are met by wind, cold and damp entering through the back transport points.
- The *Shanghan lun* passage quoted by the Song editors has not been checked.
