# 素問 痺論篇第四十三 §16 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The nutritive is the essential qi of water and grain. It harmonizes the five zang and is sprinkled through the six fu, and then it can enter the vessels; therefore it follows the vessels up and down, passes through the five zang and connects with the six fu. The defensive is the fierce qi of water and grain. Its qi is swift and slippery and cannot enter the vessels; therefore it follows within the skin and between the divisions of the flesh, fumigates the membranes and disperses in the chest and abdomen. Going against its qi brings illness; following its qi brings recovery. They do not combine with wind, cold and damp; therefore they do not form impediment."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The nutritive is the essential qi of water and grain ... then it can enter the vessels"] The *Zhengli lun* says, "When grain enters the stomach, the pathways of the vessels move; when water enters the channels, blood is formed." The *Lingshu jing* also says, "The way of the nutritive qi takes the intake of grain as its substance."
Song editors: Another edition writes 宝 "treasure" for 実. When grain enters the stomach, its qi is transmitted to the lung, and the refined and concentrated part rises through the channel tunnels; thus the essential qi of water and grain combines with the nutritive qi and circulates, entering the vessels.

[On "therefore it follows the vessels up and down ... connects with the six fu"] The nutritive travels within the vessels; therefore there is nowhere it does not reach.

[On "The defensive is the fierce qi of water and grain ... cannot enter the vessels"] Fierce qi means floating, abundant qi. Because it is floating and abundant, it is swift and slippery and cannot enter the vessels.

[On "therefore it follows within the skin ... disperses in the chest and abdomen"] "Within the skin and between the divisions of the flesh" means outside the vessels. 肓膜 means the membranes of the diaphragm between the five zang. Because it is floating and abundant, it can spread through the empty spaces in the chest and abdomen and fumigate the membranes, letting the qi flow freely.

## Notes

The nature and circulation of the nutritive and defensive qi; neither combines with wind, cold and damp, so neither forms impediment. A frequently quoted explanation of the nutritive and defensive.
- 滑 in Wang Bing's note was supplied where the transcription gave only a component description (see the 【補】 note).
- The *Zhengli lun* passage quoted by Wang Bing has not been checked.
