# 素問 痺論篇第四十三 §7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When impediment lodges in the five zang: lung impediment brings vexation and fullness, panting and vomiting. Heart impediment: the vessels do not flow; with vexation the area below the heart drums [with distension], the qi suddenly rises with panting, the throat is dry with frequent belching, and when reversal qi rises there is fear. Liver impediment: one is startled when lying down at night, drinks much and urinates often, and above there is drawing as if pregnant. Kidney impediment: frequent distension; the buttocks substitute for the heels and the spine for the head. Spleen impediment: the four limbs are languid, cough breaks out with vomiting of fluid, and above there is great blockage. Intestinal impediment: one drinks often but cannot pass it out; the qi within pants and contends, and at times diarrhoea with undigested food breaks out. Uterine impediment: pressing the lower abdomen and bladder causes inner pain as if hot water were poured in; urination is rough; and above there is clear nasal mucus.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When impediment lodges in the five zang: lung impediment ... vomiting"] The qi of this zang corresponds to the breath, and its vessel returns along the mouth of the stomach; therefore it causes vexation and fullness, panting and vomiting.

[On "Heart impediment ... there is fear"] The heart corresponds to the vessels; when it receives evil, the vessels do not flow freely. The evil qi disturbs within; therefore there is vexation. The vessel of the hand heart master, the pericardium, arises in the chest, emerges to belong to the pericardium and descends through the diaphragm. The hand lesser yin heart vessel arises in the heart, emerges to belong to the heart connector, descends through the diaphragm and connects with the small intestine; a branch rises from the heart connector alongside the throat; the straight course goes from the heart connector back up to the lung. Therefore with vexation the area below the heart drums with fullness, the qi suddenly rises with panting, and the throat is dry. The heart governs belching; because of the drumming fullness below, one belches to let out the qi. If counterflowing qi rises to encroach on the heart, there is fear, because the spirit is frightened and overpowered.

[On "Liver impediment ... as if pregnant"] The liver governs fright and the qi correspond; therefore one is startled when lying down at night. The liver vessel follows the inner thigh, enters the pubic hair, encircles the genitals, reaches the lower abdomen, runs alongside the stomach, belongs to the liver and connects with the gallbladder, rises through the diaphragm, spreads over the flanks, follows the back of the throat and rises into the nasopharynx; therefore one drinks much water, urinates often, and above it pulls the lower abdomen as if pregnant.

[On "Kidney impediment ... the spine for the head"] The kidney is the gate of the stomach; when the gate is not free, the stomach qi does not turn; therefore there is frequent distension. "The buttocks substitute for the heels" means the feet are contracted; "the spine substitutes for the head" means the body is curled and bent. 踵 means the heel. The kidney vessel arises below the little toe, runs obliquely to the sole, emerges below the Rangu bone, follows behind the inner ankle, branches into the heel, rises inside the calf, emerges at the inner edge of the knee crease, rises along the posterior inner thigh, passes through the spine, belongs to the kidney and connects with the bladder; its straight course rises from the kidney through the liver and diaphragm into the lung. The qi is insufficient and receives evil; therefore one cannot stretch out.
Song editors: On examination, 然骨 is written 然谷 in one version.

[On "Spleen impediment ... great blockage"] Earth dominates in the four seasons and outwardly governs the four limbs; therefore the limbs are languid. Also, its vessel arises at the foot and follows the calf and shin up to the knee and thigh. The spleen vessel enters the abdomen, belongs to the kidney, connects with the stomach, rises through the diaphragm and runs alongside the throat; therefore cough breaks out with vomiting of fluid. Spleen qi nourishes the lung, and the stomach connects with the throat; therefore above there is great blockage.

[On "Intestinal impediment ... diarrhoea with undigested food breaks out"] The large intestine vessel enters the supraclavicular fossa, connects with the lung, descends through the diaphragm and belongs to the large intestine. The small intestine vessel also enters the supraclavicular fossa, connects with the heart, follows the throat, descends through the diaphragm, reaches the stomach and belongs to the small intestine. Now when there is evil in the small intestine, the vessel does not descend through the diaphragm; when it does not, the intestines do not move and transform, and the stomach qi accumulates heat; therefore one drinks much water but it cannot pass out below. The yang qi and evil qi in the intestines and stomach rush, pant and contend, and at times obtain passage; because the intestinal qi does not transform, when it occasionally passes there is diarrhoea with undigested food.

[On "Uterine impediment ... clear nasal mucus"] The bladder is the fu of fluids, and the uterus resides within it. The lower abdomen lies within Guanyuan and stores the uterine organ within. The bladder vessel arises at the inner canthus, rises over the forehead, crosses at the vertex, enters to connect with the brain, comes out again, branches down the nape, follows the inside of the shoulder blade, runs alongside the spine to the lumbus, enters along the spinal muscles, connects with the kidney and belongs to the bladder; a branch descends from the lumbus through the buttocks into the popliteal fossa. Now when the uterus receives wind, cold and damp, the bladder greater yang vessel cannot flow down to the feet; therefore pressing the lower abdomen and bladder causes inner pain as if hot water were poured in, and urination is rough. Since urination is rough and the greater yang vessel cannot descend, it rises and scorches the brain, becoming clear mucus that comes out of the nostrils. 沃 means pour.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 両髀 "both thighs" for 内痛 "inner pain".

## Notes

The symptoms of impediment of the five zang, the intestines and the uterus.
- 腎 in Wang Bing's 脾脈入腹属腎絡胃 is presumably an error for 脾 from the context, but the translation keeps the characters as they are.
- 扺 and 㢘 in Wang Bing's notes are variants of 抵 and 廉.
