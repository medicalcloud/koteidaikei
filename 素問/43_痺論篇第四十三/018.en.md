# 素問 痺論篇第四十三 §18 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Pain means there is much cold qi; because there is cold, there is pain. Those without pain but with numbness have long-standing illness that has entered deeply; the circulation of the nutritive and defensive is rough, and the channels and network vessels are at times empty; therefore there is no flow, and the skin is not nourished; therefore there is numbness. Those who are cold have little yang qi and much yin qi, which adds to the illness; therefore they are cold. Those who are hot have much yang qi and little yin qi; the qi of the illness prevails, and the yang meets the yin; therefore there is hot impediment. Those with much sweating and dampness have encountered damp severely; yang qi is scant and yin qi abundant, and the two qi affect each other; therefore sweat comes out and they are damp."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Pain means there is much cold qi; because there is cold, there is pain"] Wind, cold and damp lodge between the divisions of the flesh and press to form froth; with cold it gathers; when it gathers, it pushes the divisions of the flesh apart; when the flesh splits, there is pain; therefore with cold there is pain.

[On "Those without pain but with numbness ... therefore there is no flow"] Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 不痛 "no pain" for 不通 "no flow". On examination, in this item of the *Jiayi jing*, "no pain" and "numbness" are two things, and saying "no pain" later repeats it to clarify the absence of pain.

[On "and the skin is not nourished; therefore there is numbness"] 不仁 means the skin is insensitive and does not know presence or absence.

[On "Those who are cold ... therefore they are cold"] The illness originally arises from wind, cold and damp; therefore the yin qi adds to it.

[On "Those who are hot ... therefore there is hot impediment"] 遭 means meet. It says that when it meets the yin qi, the yin qi does not prevail; therefore there is heat.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 乗 "ride on" for 遭.

[On "Those with much sweating and dampness ... they are damp"] When the interior and exterior correspond, they affect each other.

## Notes

The reasons for pain, numbness, cold, heat and dampness in impediment. No reason for dryness is given.
