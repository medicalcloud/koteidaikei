# 素問 陰陽類論篇第七十九 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the first month of spring had just arrived, the Yellow Emperor sat at leisure, surveyed the eight extremities, set right the qi of the eight winds, and asked Leigong, "Among the categories of yin and yang, the way of the channels, and what the five centers govern, which viscus is most noble?"

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Among the categories of yin and yang ... which viscus is most noble"] The arrival of the first month of spring means the day of the Beginning of Spring. 燕 means at ease. Surveying the eight extremities means looking at the colors at the far edges of the eight directions. Setting right the eight winds means watching the winds arriving from the eight directions that pay court to the Supreme One. The five centers are the five viscera.
Song editors: On examination, the eight winds paying court to the Supreme One is detailed in the *Tianyuan yuce*. Also Yang Shangshan says: "Heaven is yang, earth yin, humans harmony. If yin lacks its yang, decline and killing do not cease; if yang lacks its yin, growth does not stop. Unceasing growth harms yin, and when yin is harmed, yin disasters arise; unceasing decline harms yang, and when yang is harmed, yang calamities arise. Hence a sage must stand between heaven and earth, harmonize the qi of yin and yang, and make the myriad things live. The way of harmonizing qi means first cultivating oneself to make virtue; then the qi of yin and yang are harmonized; then the winds of the eight periods are regulated; then the depletion winds of the eight [directions] cease; then pestilence does not arise and auspicious omens all gather. This too happens without one knowing why. Hence the Yellow Emperor asked about the nobility of the body's channels, relying on them to regulate and nurture, cultivating virtue in himself so as to set right the qi of the eight winds."
