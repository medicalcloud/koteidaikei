# 素問 陰陽類論篇第七十九 §8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The three yin are what the six channels are governed by. When they meet at greater yin, hidden and drumming, not floating, they rise and empty the will-heart. When the two yin arrive at the lungs, their qi returns to the bladder and outwardly connects with the spleen and stomach. When the one yin arrives alone, the channel is cut off and the qi floats, not drumming, hook-like and slippery. These six pulses are now yin, now yang, interlinked and combined, intertwining and connecting the five viscera, matching yin and yang. What arrives first is host; what arrives later is guest."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The three yin are what the six channels are governed by"] The three yin are greater yin. Why do all pulses arrive at hand greater yin? Because it is the governor of the six channels. The six channels are the channels of the three yin and three yang. Why do they arrive at hand greater yin? Because the lungs receive the court of the qi of the hundred vessels, which all meet at the qi opening. Hence the text below says [what follows].

[On "When they meet at greater yin"] This precisely clarifies the meaning of the lungs receiving the court of the hundred vessels. The *Jingmai bie lun* says, "The lungs receive the court of the hundred vessels."

[On "hidden and drumming, not floating, they rise and empty the will-heart"] A pulse hidden and drumming that does not rise and float means insufficiency of heart qi; hence it pulls upward on the heart and causes illness. The will-heart means the small heart. The *Ci jin lun* says, "Beside the seventh vertebra, within, is the small heart"; this is what it means.
Song editors: Yang Shangshan says, "The lung pulse is normally floating and rough. Now a hidden, drumming pulse appearing is the kidney pulse. The foot lesser yin vessel threads the spine, belongs to the kidneys, rises into the lungs, and emerges from the lungs to network the heart. Lung qi descends into the kidneys, and the will rises into the heart-spirit." Wang's reading of the will-heart as the small heart does not make sense.

[On "When the two yin arrive at the lungs, their qi returns to the bladder and outwardly connects with the spleen and stomach"] The two yin are the vessel of foot lesser yin, the kidneys. The divergent course of the lesser yin vessel enters the heel, rises to the posterior inner thigh, threads the spine, belongs to the kidneys and networks the bladder; its straight course rises from the kidneys through the liver and diaphragm into the lungs. Hence it rises to the lungs, its qi returns to the bladder, and outwardly connects with the spleen and stomach.

[On "When the one yin arrives alone, the channel is cut off and the qi floats, not drumming, hook-like and slippery"] If the one yin arrives alone and the lung channel qi is cut off within, the qi floats and does not drum against the hand; if the channel is not cut off within, it is hook-like and slippery.
Song editors: Yang Shangshan says, "The one yin is *jueyin*."

[On "These six pulses are now yin, now yang ... matching yin and yang"] Sometimes a yang pulse appears in yin, sometimes a yin pulse in yang; hence "now yin, now yang". It is so because the qi meet and interlink; one should examine and compare categories to know yin and yang.

[On "What arrives first is host; what arrives later is guest"] When the pulse qi now shows yang in yin and now yin in yang, how are they distinguished? Take what arrives first as host and what arrives later as guest. Arriving means arriving at the inch opening.

## Notes

The Emperor describes the three, two and one yin pulses and how to distinguish the six pulses when yin and yang are intermixed. His speech ends here.
