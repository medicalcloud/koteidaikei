# 素問 陰陽類論篇第七十九 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Emperor said, "The three yang are the warp, the two yang the cords, the one yang the roving divisions; by this the end and beginning of the five viscera are known. The three yang are the exterior, the two yin the interior; the one yin reaches extinction and makes the new moon and the dark of the moon; then all are combined to set right its principle."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The three yang are the warp, the two yang the cords, the one yang the roving divisions"] 経 means warp and weft, which accomplishes tasks; 維 means maintaining, which ties the heavenly true; 游 means roving; 部 means the divisions of the body. Hence what governs qi accomplishes tasks, what transforms grain ties the heavenly true, and what governs color spreads the essence and roves through the divisions.
Song editors: Yang Shangshan says: "The three yang are the foot greater yang vessel: from the inner canthus it rises over the head, divides into four paths and descends the nape; with its proper and divergent vessels, six paths go up and down the back, forming the warp of the body. The two yang are the foot *yangming* vessel: rising from the nose and descending the throat, it divides into four paths; with its proper and divergent vessels, six paths go up and down the abdomen, binding the body. The one yang is the foot lesser yang vessel: starting at the outer canthus, it networks the head, divides into four paths and descends to the supraclavicular fossa; with its proper and divergent vessels, six paths go up and down, managing the hundred joints and circulating qi through the three regions; hence 'roving divisions'."

[On "by this the end and beginning of the five viscera are known"] Observing the meanings of warp, cords and roving divisions, one may be said to know the end and beginning of the five viscera.

[On "The three yang are the exterior, the two yin the interior"] The three yang are greater yang, the two yin lesser yin. Lesser yin and greater yang are exterior and interior to each other; hence "the three yang are the exterior, the two yin the interior".

[On "the one yin reaches extinction and makes the new moon and the dark of the moon; then all are combined to set right its principle"] The one yin is *jueyin*; 厥 means exhaustion. The *Lingshu jing* says, "*Hai* is *jueyin* of the left foot, *xu* *jueyin* of the right foot; both yin are exhausted, hence *jueyin*." Exhaustion of yin is the dark of the moon; birth of yin is the new moon. *Jueyin* takes exhaustion of yin as its meaning. Taking the flourishing of its qi as the sign fits the new moon; speaking of the exhaustion of its qi is the dark of the moon. Seeing its new moon and again meeting its dark, it is said "the one yin reaches extinction and makes the new moon and the dark of the moon". Taking that wholly exhausted yin as the sign and combining it with this burgeoning wood, it corresponds exactly to the principle of the five phases and cycles without fail; hence "then all are combined to set right its principle".
Song editors: The note says "exhaustion of yin is the dark of the moon; birth of yin is the new moon"; perhaps it should be "birth of yang is the new moon".

## Notes

The Emperor explains the functions of the three, two and one yang and the two and one yin.
