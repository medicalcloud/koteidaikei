# 素問 陰陽類論篇第七十九 §13 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the two yin and two yang [combine], the illness is in the lungs; the lesser yin pulse is sunken; it overcomes the lungs and harms the spleen, and outwardly harms the four limbs.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "the lesser yin pulse is sunken; it overcomes the lungs and harms the spleen, and outwardly harms the four limbs"] The two yin are the vessel of hand lesser yin, the heart; the two yang are also the stomach vessel. Heart and stomach are ill together, the pathogen combines above and below, so within it harms the spleen and outside it overcomes the lungs. This is because the stomach is the bowel of the spleen, and heart fire overcomes metal. The spleen governs the four limbs, so when the spleen is harmed, the four limbs are harmed outwardly. The lesser yin pulse is the Shenmen pulse, five tenths of an inch behind the palm on the little-finger side.
Song editors: On examination, these two yang are hand *yangming*, the large intestine, the bowel of the lungs; lesser yin [heart] fire overcomes the bowel of metal, hence "the illness is in the lungs". Wang's taking the two yang as the stomach does not make much sense; still less [the passage about] seeing the stomach making the kidneys ill [meaning unclear]; this is the heart making the lungs ill. Also the Quan Yuanqi edition, the *Jiayi jing*, the *Taisu* and others all have "two yin and one yang".

## Notes

Illness of the two yin and two yang.
- The phrase 況又以見胃病腎之説 in the Song editors' note is hard to construe.
