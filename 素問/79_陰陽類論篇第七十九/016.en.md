# 素問 陰陽類論篇第七十九 §16 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the one yin and one yang [pulses] are intermittent and cut off, yin qi has reached the heart; [illness] is above and below without constancy, intake and output are not perceived, the throat is dry, and the illness is in the earth, the spleen.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the one yin and one yang are intermittent and cut off ... the illness is in the earth, the spleen"] The one yin is the *jueyin* vessel and the one yang the lesser yang vessel, both wood qi. Intermittent and cut off means moving and stopping midway; because it is so, there is illness. Wood qi generates fire, so illness arises and yin qi reaches the heart. The qi of the liver and gallbladder reaches the head above and the lower back and feet below, and governs the abdomen and rib-sides in the middle; hence illness breaks out without a constant place above or below. If one takes things in without knowing their taste, discharges from the orifices without knowing the measure, and the throat is dry, it is because behind the throat belongs to the gullet, which is the envoy of the gallbladder; hence when ill the throat is dry. Though the illness is in the spleen-earth, it is presumably the doing of the liver and gallbladder.

## Notes

Illness of the one yin and one yang.
