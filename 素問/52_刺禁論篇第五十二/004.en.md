# 素問 刺禁論篇第五十二 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

If needling the instep strikes the great vessel and bleeding does not stop, one dies. If needling the face strikes the flowing vessel, unfortunately there is blindness. If needling the head strikes Naohu and enters the brain, one dies at once. If needling under the tongue strikes the vessel too much and bleeding does not stop, there is loss of voice. If needling under the foot strikes the vessel of the spreading network and no blood comes out, there is swelling. If needling the cleft centre strikes the great vessel, one falls and loses colour. If needling Qijie strikes the vessel and no blood comes out, there is swelling like a crouching mouse [in the groin]. If needling between the vertebrae strikes the marrow, one becomes hunchbacked. If needling above the breast strikes the breast, there is swelling with eroding roots. If needling the supraclavicular fossa sinks in, the qi leaks, causing panting, coughing and counterflow. If needling the fish belly of the hand sinks in, there is swelling.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "If needling the instep strikes the great vessel ... one dies"] 跗 is the instep. The great vessel that pulsates without stopping is the great channel of the stomach. The stomach is the sea of water and grain; if bleeding does not stop, the stomach qi is about to collapse, the sea dries up and the qi perishes; therefore one dies.

[On "If needling the face strikes the flowing vessel, unfortunately there is blindness"] The flowing vessel in the face is the meeting of the hand greater yang and the *ren* vessel. The hand greater yang vessel runs obliquely from the cheekbone to the inner canthus, and the *ren* vessel rises from both sides of the nose to below the pupils; therefore needling the flowing vessel in the face may unfortunately cause blindness.

[On "If needling the head strikes Naohu and enters the brain, one dies at once"] Naohu is the name of a point, above the occipital bone, communicating with the brain. The brain is the sea of marrow, where the true qi gathers; if the needle enters the brain, the true qi leaks; therefore one dies at once.

[On "If needling under the tongue ... loss of voice"] The vessel under the tongue is the spleen vessel; the spleen vessel runs alongside the throat, joins the root of the tongue and spreads under the tongue. If bleeding does not stop, the spleen qi cannot nourish the tongue; therefore one loses the voice and cannot speak.

[On "If needling under the foot strikes the vessel of the spreading network ... swelling"] The spreading network means the network vessels spreading in the hollow under the foot in front of the inner ankle, exactly in the region of the Rangu point. The vessel struck through the network is the thoroughfare vessel, which runs down with the lesser yin channel behind the inner ankle into the sole. If needling it lets no blood out, the qi of the kidney vessel and the thoroughfare vessel together return into Rangu; therefore there is swelling.

[On "If needling the cleft centre strikes the great vessel, one falls and loses colour"] Examining what the cleft centre governs in this classic, it is exactly the same as the Weizhong point in the *Zhonggao liuzhu jing*. "Cleft centre" is named after the channel point and "Weizhong" after the location, just as *cunkou*, *maikou* and *qikou* are the same place. The great vessel at the cleft centre is the foot greater yang channel. The foot greater yang vessel arises at the inner canthus and joins the hand greater yang; the hand greater yang vessel runs obliquely from the inner canthus to connect with the cheekbone; the foot greater yang vessel rises to the head, descends the nape and follows down to the foot. Therefore needling it beyond the limit makes one fall and the face lose colour.

[On "If needling Qijie strikes the vessel ... swelling like a crouching mouse"] Within Qijie are the vessels of the gallbladder and stomach. The gallbladder vessel follows the inside of the flanks and emerges at Qijie; the stomach vessel runs alongside the navel into Qijie; a branch arises at the lower mouth of the stomach and follows the inside of the abdomen to join at Qijie. If needling it lets no blood out, the qi of the blood vessels gathers within; therefore it knots inside into a swelling shaped like a crouching mouse. Qijie is below the abdomen, four *cun* apart on both sides of the navel, one *cun* above the groin, where the pulse is felt.
Song editors: Another edition writes 鼷 for 僕. The note to the *Qifu lun* says, "Qijie is at both ends of the pubic bone below the navel, one *cun* above the groin."

[On "If needling between the vertebrae strikes the marrow, one becomes hunchbacked"] 傴 means hunchbacked, the body curled. "Between the vertebrae" means between the joints of the spine. Striking the marrow lets the essential qi of the bones leak; therefore one becomes hunchbacked.

[On "If needling above the breast strikes the breast ... eroding roots"] Above and below the breast is the foot *yangming* vessel. Within the breast milk seeps, and the qi and blood of the chest all converge outward there. If the needle strikes the breast, the qi converges further; therefore there is a great swelling with a root of pus within, eroding the flesh and skin, turning into pus and not healing for a long time.

[On "If needling the supraclavicular fossa sinks in ... counterflow"] Of the five zang, the lung is the canopy and the supraclavicular fossa its pathway. The lung stores qi and governs breathing, and in terms of qi produces coughing. If needling the supraclavicular fossa sinks in, the lung qi leaks out; therefore one pants, coughs and counterflows.

[On "If needling the fish belly of the hand sinks in, there is swelling"] Inside the fish belly of the hand is where the lung vessel flows; therefore if needling it sinks in, there is swelling.
Song editors: According to the *Jiayi jing*, 流 in "where the lung vessel flows" should be 留 "lingers".

## Notes

Places where wrong needling has grave consequences (instep, face, Naohu, under the tongue, under the foot, cleft centre, Qijie, between the vertebrae, breast, supraclavicular fossa, fish belly of the hand).
- 鼠僕 is often understood as the same as 鼠鼷 "groin" (another edition cited by the Song editors writes 鼷); Wang Bing also explains it as a swelling like a crouching mouse.
