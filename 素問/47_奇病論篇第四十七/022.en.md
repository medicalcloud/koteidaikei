# 素問 奇病論篇第四十七 §22 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Emperor said: "There are those with urinary retention who pass urine dozens of times a day — this is insufficiency; whose bodies are hot as charcoal, whose necks and breasts seem blocked, whose Renying pulse is agitated and abundant, with panting and counterflowing qi — this is surplus; and whose greater yin pulse is faint and fine like a hair — this is insufficiency. Where is the illness, and what is it called?"

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "whose bodies are hot as charcoal ... this is surplus"] This is yang qi greatly abundant outside and yin qi insufficient; hence surplus.
Song editors: On examination, these fifteen characters were formerly written as main text. The *Jiayi jing* and *Taisu* both lack them. On further examination, they are Quan Yuanqi's note, mistakenly written here by a later hand; they are now written as a note.

[On "and whose greater yin pulse is faint ... what is it called?"] 癃 means inability to urinate; 溲 means urination. "Necks and breasts seem blocked" means the neck and chest seem to resist each other and do not correspond. "Renying agitated and abundant" means the pulse on both sides of the laryngeal prominence beats full, tense and rapid, unusually agitated and fast; it is the stomach pulse. "The greater yin pulse faint and fine like a hair" refers to the place where the pulse beats on the high bone one body-*cun* behind the thumb; this is the lung pulse, precisely where the vessel qi of the hand greater yin flows, by which the five zang can be observed.

## Notes

Huangdi asks about an illness in which signs of insufficiency and surplus appear together.
- According to the Song editors, the first note (fifteen characters) is Quan Yuanqi's note that had entered the main text; in the base material it appears as Wang Bing's note. The translation keeps it as it is.
