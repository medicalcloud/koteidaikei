# 素問 奇病論篇第四十七 §25 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The five surpluses are surpluses of the qi of five illnesses; the two insufficiencies are also insufficiencies of the qi of illness. Now outside there are five surpluses and inside two insufficiencies; this body is neither exterior nor interior, and death is clearly certain."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The five surpluses ... death is clearly certain"] The five external surpluses are: first, the body hot as charcoal; second, the neck and skin [breast] blocked; third, Renying agitated and abundant; fourth, panting; fifth, counterflowing breath. The two internal insufficiencies are: first, urinary retention with dozens of urinations a day; second, the greater yin pulse faint and fine like a hair. In such a case, if one says the illness is in the exterior, there are two insufficiencies inside; if one says it is in the interior, there are five surpluses outside. Since neither exterior nor interior can be relied on, supplementing and draining can hardly be applied; hence "this body is neither exterior nor interior, and death is clearly certain".

## Notes

Explains the five surpluses and two insufficiencies, a fatal pattern that can be assigned to neither exterior nor interior.
- 膚 in Wang Bing's 頸膚 is presumably an error for 膺 "breast" in the text.
