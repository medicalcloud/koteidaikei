# 素問 奇病論篇第四十七 §23 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The illness is in the greater yin, its abundance in the stomach, and partly in the lung. The illness is called reversal; one dies and it cannot be treated. This is what is called having five surpluses and two insufficiencies."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The illness is in the greater yin ... it cannot be treated"] In illness with urinary retention and frequent urination, body hot as charcoal, neck and breast blocked, and counterflowing breath, the hand greater yin pulse should be surging, large and rapid; now that it is instead faint and fine like a hair, the illness and the pulse contradict each other. What causes this? The lung qi counterflows and encroaches on the stomach, rising to produce this and making Renying agitated and abundant; hence "the illness is in the greater yin, its abundance in the stomach". Because of panting and counterflowing qi, it says "partly in the lung". Because the illness arises from counterflow of qi and the signs do not correspond, it is called reversal; one dies and it cannot be treated.

## Notes

The illness is called reversal and is incurable.
