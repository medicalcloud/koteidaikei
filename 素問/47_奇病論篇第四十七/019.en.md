# 素問 奇病論篇第四十七 §19 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "This is an overflow of the five qi, called spleen heat. The five flavours enter the mouth and are stored in the stomach; the spleen moves their essential qi for it; the fluids are in the spleen; therefore the mouth tastes sweet. This arises from rich, fine food. Such a person surely eats sweet and fine food often and is fat. Fat makes one hot within; sweetness makes one full in the centre; therefore the qi overflows upward and turns into wasting-thirst. Treat it with orchid to remove the stale qi."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "This is an overflow of the five qi, called spleen heat"] 癉 means heat. When the spleen is hot, the other four zang receive it alike; therefore the five qi overflow upward. Because it arises from spleen heat, it is called spleen heat.

[On "The five flavours enter the mouth ... the mouth tastes sweet"] Spleen heat seeps within, the fluids are in the spleen, and the surplus essential qi from the grain transformed in the stomach overflows with it; the mouth communicates with the spleen qi; therefore the mouth is sweet. "The fluids are in the spleen" refers to dampness of the spleen.

[On "This arises from rich, fine food"] Song editors: The *Taisu* writes 致 for 発.

[On "Such a person surely eats sweet and fine food often ... wasting-thirst"] Eating fat makes the interstices dense, so the yang qi cannot leak out; therefore fat makes one hot within. Sweetness is mild and slow in nature and opposes dispersal; therefore sweetness makes one full in the centre. When the inside is hot, the yang qi blazes upward; when it blazes upward, one wants to drink and the throat is dry. When the centre is full, stale qi is in surplus; when in surplus, spleen qi overflows upward; hence "the qi overflows upward and turns into wasting-thirst". The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* says, "Acrid and sweet disperse and are yang," and the *Lingshu jing* says, "Eating much sweet makes one oppressed." It arises from fullness in the centre.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 消癉 for 消渇.

[On "Treat it with orchid to remove the stale qi"] Orchid means the orchid herb (*lancao*). Shennong says, "The orchid herb is acrid, hot and neutral; it frees the waterways and wards off inauspicious things and phlegm in the chest." 除 means remove; 陳 means old. It says orchid removes the long-standing, untransformed qi of sweet and fat because acridity disperses. The *Zangqi fashi lun* says, "Acridity disperses."
Song editors: According to the *Bencao*, orchid is neutral; it is not said to be hot.

## Notes

Spleen heat, in which a sweet taste in the mouth from eating rich, sweet food turns into wasting-thirst, and its treatment with the orchid herb. Frequently quoted as an account of the origin of wasting-thirst.
- The passage Wang Bing quotes from "Shennong" (presumably the *Shennong bencao jing*) has not been checked.
