# 素問 平人気象論篇第十八 §18 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

People take water and grain as their root; therefore if people are cut off from water and grain they die, and if the pulse has no stomach qi they also die. What is called having no stomach qi is obtaining only the true-zang pulse without the stomach qi. What is called the pulse not obtaining the stomach qi is the liver not wiry and the kidney not stony.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "What is called the pulse not obtaining the stomach qi is the liver not wiry and the kidney not stony"] "Not wiry" and "not stony" both mean not slightly resembling [them].

## Notes

People take water and grain as their root; without stomach qi in the pulse they die. Having no stomach qi means only the true-zang pulse appears — the liver pulse does not slightly resemble wiry, the kidney pulse does not slightly resemble stony.
- Wang Bing takes "the liver not wiry" as not being in the state of slightly resembling wiry (with stomach qi); compare "slightly wiry" in unit 005.
