# 素問 平人気象論篇第十八 §12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

If the pulse follows yin and yang, the illness ends easily; if the pulse goes against yin and yang, the illness ends with difficulty. If the pulse is in accord with the four seasons, it is said there is nothing else wrong; if the pulse goes against the four seasons and does not skip a zang, it is said to end with difficulty.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "If the pulse follows yin and yang ... ends with difficulty"] When the pulse and the illness correspond, it is called following; when they are opposed, it is called going against.

[On "If the pulse is in accord with the four seasons ... it is said to end with difficulty"] Getting an autumn pulse in spring, a winter pulse in summer, a summer pulse in autumn, or a pulse of the seasonal ends in winter is called going against the four seasons; the qi do not correspond; therefore it ends with difficulty.

## Notes

If the pulse follows yin and yang and the four seasons, illness is easily cured; if it goes against them, it is hard to cure.
- "Does not skip a zang" (不閒蔵): Wang Bing does not comment. One reading takes it as transmission directly to the next zang in the controlling cycle; the translation follows the characters.
