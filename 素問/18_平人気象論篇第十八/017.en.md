# 素問 平人気象論篇第十八 §17 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Pulses may go against or follow the four seasons. When there is not yet any form of the zang, [yet] in spring and summer the pulse is thin and in autumn and winter it is floating and large, this is termed going against the four seasons. Wind-heat with a quiet pulse; diarrhea and loss of blood with an excess pulse; illness in the center with a deficient pulse; illness outside with a hesitant, hard pulse — all are hard to treat, and this is termed contrary to the four seasons.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When there is not yet any form of the zang, in spring and summer the pulse is thin"] Song editors: The *Yuji zhenzang lun* (chapter 19) writes "deep and hesitant" for "thin".

[On "and in autumn and winter it is floating and large, this is termed going against the four seasons"] "Thin in spring and summer" means deep and fine. Floating and large in autumn and winter does not correspond to the season. As a general rule, in spring and summer it should be floating and large but is instead deep and fine; in autumn and winter it should be deep and fine but is instead floating and large; hence it does not correspond to the season.

[On "Wind-heat with a quiet pulse"] Song editors: The *Yuji zhenzang lun* writes 病 "illness" for 風 "wind".

[On "diarrhea and loss of blood with an excess pulse"] Song editors: The *Yuji zhenzang lun* reads "diarrhea with a large pulse, loss of blood with an excess pulse".

[On "illness in the center with a deficient pulse"] Song editors: The *Yuji zhenzang lun* reads "the pulse excess and hard, illness outside".

[On "illness outside with a hesitant, hard pulse"] Song editors: The *Yuji zhenzang lun* reads "the pulse not excess and hard".

[On "all are hard to treat"] With wind-heat the pulse should be agitated but is instead quiet; with diarrhea and loss of blood it should be deficient but is instead excess; with pathogenic qi within it should be in excess but is instead deficient; with illness qi outside it should be deficient and slippery but is instead hard and rough. Therefore all are hard to treat.

[On "this is termed contrary to the four seasons"] All are contrary to the qi of the four seasons and so are like this.
Song editors: The six characters 命曰反四時也 are probably an ancient displaced slip and should be removed. The fifty-three characters from "When there is not yet any form of the zang, in spring and summer" to here repeat the text of the *Yuji zhenzang lun* below.

## Notes

Pulses that go against the four seasons (deep and thready in spring-summer, floating and large in autumn-winter) and pulses that contradict the illness (quiet in wind-heat, excess in diarrhea and blood loss, etc.) are hard to treat.
- The Song editors list in detail how this passage overlaps with and differs from the *Yuji zhenzang lun* (chapter 19), and say the six characters "this is termed contrary to the four seasons" should be removed as a displaced slip.
- The punctuation follows Wang Bing; following the *Yuji zhenzang lun* text would divide the phrases differently.
