# 素問 平人気象論篇第十八 §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

To know excess and insufficiency at the *cunkou*: if the *cunkou* pulse strikes the hand short, it is headache; if long, pain in the feet and shins; if it strikes the hand hurried and upward, pain in the shoulders and back. If the *cunkou* pulse is sunken and hard, the illness is in the centre; if floating and abundant, the illness is outside. If the *cunkou* pulse is sunken and weak, it is cold and heat with conglomerations and pain in the lower abdomen. If sunken and crosswise, there is an accumulation below the flanks and a crosswise accumulation in the abdomen with pain. If sunken and panting, it is cold and heat.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "if the *cunkou* pulse strikes the hand short, it is headache; if long, pain in the feet and shins"] Short means the yang qi does not reach; therefore the head is ill. Long means the yin qi is excessive; therefore the feet are ill.

[On "if it strikes the hand hurried and upward, pain in the shoulders and back"] Yang is abundant above; therefore the shoulders and back ache.

[On "If the *cunkou* pulse is sunken and hard ... the illness is outside"] Sunken and hard are yin, so the illness is within; floating and abundant are yang, so the illness is outside.

[On "If the *cunkou* pulse is sunken and weak, it is cold and heat with conglomerations and pain in the lower abdomen"] Sunken is cold and weak is heat; hence cold and heat. Also, sunken means abundant yin and weak means surplus yang; when surplus and abundance press against each other, there should properly be cold and heat, not conglomerations and pain in the lower abdomen. It is probably an ancient displaced slip.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* lacks these fifteen characters; moreover the text below already has "if the *cunkou* pulse is sunken and panting, it is cold and heat" and "if the pulse is tense, it is conglomerations and pain in the lower abdomen". This text is an interpolation and should be removed.

[On "If sunken and crosswise ... with pain"] This too is yin qi binding within.

[On "If sunken and panting, it is cold and heat"] Panting is yang drawing in; sunken is yin contending; contending and drawing in press against each other; therefore there is cold and heat.

## Notes

The location and nature of illness are judged from whether the *cunkou* pulse is short or long, hurried, sunken or floating, hard or weak, crosswise or panting.
- "If the *cunkou* pulse is sunken and weak, it is cold and heat with conglomerations ...": Wang Bing considers it a displaced slip; the Song editors call it an interpolation absent from the *Jiayi jing* that should be removed.
- Daizhige (the plain text) writes 肩脊 for 肩背 and 沈而渋 for 沈而喘.
