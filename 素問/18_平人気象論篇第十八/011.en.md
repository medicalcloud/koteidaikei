# 素問 平人気象論篇第十八 §11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

If the pulse is abundant, slippery and hard, the illness is outside; if small, replete and hard, the illness is inside. If the pulse is small, weak and rough, it is called a chronic illness; if slippery, floating and fast, it is called a new illness. If the pulse is tense, it is conglomerations and pain in the lower abdomen. If the pulse is slippery, it is wind; if rough, impediment; if slack and slippery, heat in the centre; if abundant and tight, distension.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "If the pulse is abundant, slippery and hard ... the illness is inside"] Abundant and slippery are yang; small and replete are yin. In yin illness the illness is inside; in yang illness it is outside.

[On "If the pulse is small, weak and rough, it is called a chronic illness"] Small means depleted qi; rough means no blood; blood and qi are depleted and weak; hence a long-standing illness.

[On "if slippery, floating and fast, it is called a new illness"] Slippery and floating mean sufficient yang; a fast pulse means whole qi; with sufficient yang and whole qi, it is a new and shallow illness.

[On "If the pulse is tense, it is conglomerations and pain in the lower abdomen"] This corrects the earlier pulse of conglomerations and lower-abdominal pain: sunken and weak is not necessarily conglomeration; only sunken and tense corresponds to that diagnosis.

[On "If the pulse is slippery, it is wind; if rough, impediment"] Slippery is yang; when yang receives illness, it is wind. Rough is yin; when yin receives illness, it is impediment.

[On "if slack and slippery, heat in the centre; if abundant and tight, distension"] 緩 means a slack, loose form, not a slow beat. Yang is abundant in the centre, so the pulse is slippery and slack; cold qi blocks and fills, so the pulse is abundant and tight. "Abundant and tight" means abundant and full.

## Notes

Whether the illness is inside or outside, chronic or new, and what it is, are judged from the pulse being abundant, slippery, hard, small, replete, weak, rough, floating, fast, tense, slack or tight.
- Wang Bing takes "if the pulse is tense, it is conglomerations ..." as correcting the "sunken and weak ... conglomerations" of the previous unit.
- Wang Bing takes 緩 not as a slow pulse but as a slack form.
- Daizhige (the plain text) writes 堅 for 緊.
