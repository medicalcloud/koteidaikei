# 素問 平人気象論篇第十八 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a person breathes out once and the pulse beats once, and breathes in once and the pulse beats once, it is called shortness of qi. When a person breathes out once and the pulse beats three times, breathes in once and it beats three times and is agitated, and the *chi* [skin] is hot, it is called warm illness; if the *chi* is not hot and the pulse slippery, it is called wind illness; if the pulse is rough, it is called impediment. When a person breathes out once and the pulse beats four times or more, it is called death; when the pulse is cut off and does not come, it is called death; when it is now sparse and now rapid, it is called death.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When a person breathes out once and the pulse beats once ... shortness of qi"] Each breath out and in with one beat of the pulse is the standard sign of half that of a normal person. Counting 270 settled breaths, the qi travels about eight *zhang* one *chi*; in 13,500 settled breaths it travels 405 *zhang* in all. The principle of shortness of qi can be known from this.

[On "When a person breathes out once and the pulse beats three times ... if the pulse is rough, it is called impediment"] Each breath out and in with three beats exceeds that of a normal person by half. Counting 270 breaths, the qi travels about twenty-four *zhang* three *chi*; the signs of illness appear from this. The *chi* is the position of the yin portion and the *cun* that of the yang portion. When both yin and yang are hot, it is warm illness; when yang alone is agitated and abundant, wind has struck the yang. The *Maiyao jingwei lun* (chapter 17) says: "One struck by malign wind receives it in the yang qi." Slippery means yang is abundant, so the illness is wind; rough means there is no blood, so there is numbness. 躁 means agitation.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* lacks the phrase "if the pulse is rough, it is called impediment"; it also recurs in the text below.

[On "When a person breathes out once and the pulse beats four times or more ... now rapid, it is called death"] Each breath out and in with four beats is the standard sign of more than double that of a normal person. Counting 270 breaths, the qi travels about thirty-two *zhang* four *chi* — and still more with more beats. The *Maifa* says: "With four beats the essence is lost; with five, death." Four beats or more is close to five; therefore death. When the pulse is cut off and does not come, the qi of heavenly truth is already gone; when it is now rapid and now sparse, the essence of stomach and grain is likewise [spent]; therefore all are signs of death. ## Notes

Diagnosis by the number of pulse beats per breath: too few is shortness of qi; too many and agitated indicates warm illness, wind or impediment; still more means death, as does a pulse that is cut off or erratic.
- Wang Bing calculates for each rate the distance travelled by the qi in a day.
- 斯 in Wang Bing's note was lost in the transcription and supplied from the Daozang edition (see the 【補】 note).
- According to the Song editors, the *Jiayi jing* lacks "if the pulse is rough, it is called impediment".
