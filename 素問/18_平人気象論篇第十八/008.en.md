# 素問 平人気象論篇第十八 §8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In autumn, with stomach and slightly hair-like, it is called normal; more hair-like and less stomach, it is called lung illness; only hair-like without stomach, it is called death; hair-like with wiry, it is called spring illness; markedly wiry, it is called present illness. The true qi of the zang is high in the lung, by which it moves the nutritive and defensive qi and yin and yang.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "more hair-like and less stomach ... only hair-like without stomach, it is called death"] It means floating like a thing, like wind blowing hair.

[On "hair-like with wiry, it is called spring illness"] Wiry is the spring pulse, the qi of wood. By the order of encroachment, wiry should be hooked; because metal qi presses on the liver, the wiry pulse comes and appears; therefore it is not hooked but wiry.

[On "markedly wiry, it is called present illness"] When wood qi comes in counterflow and encroaches on metal, the illness is present.

[On "The true qi of the zang is high in the lung ..."] The lung is in the upper burner; therefore the true qi of the zang is high. The *Lingshu jing* says: "The way of the nutritive qi takes the taking in of grain as its treasure. When grain enters the stomach, the qi is passed to the lung, flows over within and disperses without; the pure essence moves in the channel passages." Because it is spread from the lung, it says "by which it moves the nutritive and defensive qi and yin and yang".
Song editors: In another edition 実 is written 宝.

## Notes

The autumn pulse: with stomach qi and slightly hair-like is normal; if wiry appears, illness comes in spring.
- Wang Bing explains why the controlling pulse expected in autumn would be hooked (fire), yet here it is wiry.
- 実 in the *Lingshu jing* passage cited by Wang Bing is written 宝 in another edition (Song editors); the translation follows 宝.
