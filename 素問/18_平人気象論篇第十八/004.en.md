# 素問 平人気象論篇第十八 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The constant qi of a normal person is received from the stomach; the stomach is the constant qi of a normal person. When a person has no stomach qi, it is called adverse, and the adverse die.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The constant qi of a normal person ... the constant qi of a normal person"] The constant, balanced qi is brought by the sea of the stomach. The *Lingshu jing* says, "The stomach is the sea of water and grain," and the *Zhengli lun* says, "When grain enters the stomach, the paths of the vessels then move."

[On "When a person has no stomach qi ... the adverse die"] "Adverse" means contrary to the signs of a normal person.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* says: "People constantly receive qi from the stomach; the pulse takes the stomach qi as its root; without stomach qi it is called adverse, and the adverse die."

## Notes

The constant qi of a normal pulse comes from the stomach; without stomach qi one dies. The following units discuss the seasonal pulses with stomach qi as the standard.
