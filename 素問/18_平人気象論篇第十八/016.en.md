# 素問 平人気象論篇第十八 §16 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the hand lesser yin pulse of a woman beats strongly, she is pregnant.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "the hand lesser yin of a woman"] Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition writes "foot lesser yin".

[On the whole sentence] The hand lesser yin pulse is the one that beats against the hand in the hollow behind the palm on the little-finger side. The *Lingshu jing* says [in answer to the question], "The lesser yin has no transport point; does the heart not fall ill?" — Qibo said: "Its outer channel falls ill but the zang does not; therefore one takes only its channel at the tip of the sharp bone behind the palm." This is what is meant. "Beat" means a moving pulse; a moving pulse is as large as a bean and throbs vigorously. The *Zhengli lun* says: "When yin and yang of the pulse press on each other, it is called moving." Also, the *Jingmai bielun* says: "When yin presses and yang is distinct, it is called having a child."
Song editors: This text is not in the *Jingmai bielun*.

## Notes

Diagnosis of pregnancy: when a woman's hand lesser yin pulse beats strongly, she is pregnant.
- According to the Song editors, Quan Yuanqi's edition reads "foot lesser yin".
- Wang Bing cites the *Jingmai bielun*, but the Song editors point out that the passage is not there (it is in fact in the *Yinyang bielun*, chapter 7: "When the yin beats and is distinct from the yang, it is called having a child").
