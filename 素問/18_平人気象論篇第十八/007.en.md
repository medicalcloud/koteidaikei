# 素問 平人気象論篇第十八 §7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In long summer, with stomach and slightly soft and weak, it is called normal; more weak and less stomach, it is called spleen illness; only intermittent without stomach, it is called death; soft and weak with stony, it is called winter illness; markedly weak, it is called present illness. The true qi of the zang moistens the spleen; the spleen stores the qi of the flesh.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "more weak and less stomach ... only intermittent without stomach, it is called death"] It means beating and stopping midway, unable to resume of itself.

[On "soft and weak with stony, it is called winter illness"] Stony is the winter pulse, the qi of water. By the order of controlling, stony should be wiry; because earth is cut off in long summer, it says stony.

[On "markedly weak, it is called present illness"] Marked weakness means the earth qi is insufficient; therefore the illness is present.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 石 "stony" for 弱.

[On "The true qi of the zang moistens the spleen ..."] Because it contains and stores water and grain, the true qi of the zang moistens.

## Notes

The long-summer pulse: with stomach qi and slightly soft and weak is normal; intermittent without stomach qi is death.
- Wang Bing explains that by the controlling cycle the pulse expected to appear in long summer would be wiry (wood), and that it says stony here because earth is cut off in long summer.
- According to the Song editors, the *Jiayi jing* writes 石甚 for 弱甚.
- Daizhige (the plain text) has almost the same text repeated at the end of unit 005 (see the textual variants).
