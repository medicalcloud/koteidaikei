# 素問 平人気象論篇第十八 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In summer, with stomach and slightly hooked, it is called normal; more hooked and less stomach, it is called heart illness; only hooked without stomach, it is called death; with stomach and stony, it is called winter illness; markedly stony, it is called present illness. The true qi of the zang communicates with the heart; the heart stores the qi of the blood vessels.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "more hooked and less stomach ... only hooked without stomach, it is called death"] It means bent in front and halting behind, like handling a belt hook.

[On "with stomach and stony, it is called winter illness"] Stony is the winter pulse, the qi of water.

[On "markedly stony, it is called present illness"] Fire is invaded by water; therefore the illness is present.

[On "The true qi of the zang communicates with the heart ..."] It resembles the blazing abundance of yang qi. The *Zangqi fashi lun* says, "The heart desires softening; quickly eat salty to soften it" — this takes following its qi.

## Notes

The summer pulse: with stomach qi and slightly hooked is normal; if the winter (stony) pulse appears, illness comes in winter.
- Daizhige (the plain text) writes 旡 for 无.
