# 素問 平人気象論篇第十八 §14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When [the true-zang pulse of] the liver appears, death on *geng* and *xin* days; of the heart, on *ren* and *gui*; of the spleen, on *jia* and *yi*; of the lung, on *bing* and *ding*; of the kidney, on *wu* and *ji*. This is called: when the true zang appears, all die.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "of the liver, death on *geng* and *xin* days"] *Geng* and *xin* are metal, which cuts down liver-wood.

[On "of the heart, on *ren* and *gui*"] *Ren* and *gui* are water, which extinguishes heart-fire.

[On "of the spleen, on *jia* and *yi*"] *Jia* and *yi* are wood, which controls spleen-earth.

[On "of the lung, on *bing* and *ding*"] *Bing* and *ding* are fire, which melts lung-metal.

[On "of the kidney, on *wu* and *ji*"] *Wu* and *ji* are earth, which punishes kidney-water.

[On "This is called: when the true zang appears, all die"] This too clarifies, in connection with the *Sanbu jiuhou lun* (chapter 20), that when the true-zang pulse appears one dies on being overcome. The same holds when the *chi* is coarse and the zang appears.

## Notes

When the true-zang pulse of an organ appears, death comes on the days (heavenly stems) of the phase that controls it.
- The base text writes 戊巳 for 戊己 (as does Wang Bing's note); it is translated as 己.
