# 素問 平人気象論篇第十八 §19 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the greater yang pulse arrives, it is surging, large and long. When the lesser yang pulse arrives, it is now rapid, now sparse, now short, now long. When the *yangming* pulse arrives, it is floating, large and short.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the greater yang pulse arrives, it is surging, large and long"] Because the qi is abundant it can be so.
Song editors: Bian Que's *Yinyang maifa* says: "The greater yang pulse is surging, large and long; it comes floating above the sinews and moves nine *fen*; it dominates on the *jiazi* of the third and fourth months." Lü Guang says: "The greater yang dominates in the fifth and sixth months; its qi is greatly abundant; therefore its pulse is surging, large and long."

[On "When the lesser yang pulse arrives, it is now rapid, now sparse, now short, now long"] Because its qi is partly extended and partly not yet extended.
Song editors: Bian Que's *Yinyang fa* says: "The lesser yang pulse is now small, now large, now long, now short; it moves six *fen*; it dominates at midnight on the *jiazi* of the eleventh month and on the *jiazi* of the first and second months." Lü Guang says: "The lesser yang dominates in the first and second months; its qi is still faint; therefore its pulse comes advancing and retreating without regularity."

[On "When the *yangming* pulse arrives, it is floating, large and short"] Because the qi of grain is full and abundant.
Song editors: The pulses of the three yin are missing; the ancient text is probably defective. The *Nanjing* says: "The greater yin arrives tight, large and long; the lesser yin arrives tight, fine and faint; the *jueyin* arrives sunken, short and thick." Lü Guang says: "The *yangming* dominates in the third and fourth months; its qi begins to sprout and is not yet abundant; therefore its pulse comes floating, large and short." Bian Que's *Yinyang maifa* says: "The lesser yin pulse is tight and fine, moving six *fen*; it dominates at midday on the *jiazi* of the fifth month and in the seventh and eighth months. The greater yin pulse is tight, fine and long, riding above the sinews and moving nine *fen*; it dominates on the *jiazi* of the ninth and tenth months. The *jueyin* pulse is sunken, short and tight, moving three *fen*; it dominates on the *jiazi* of the eleventh and twelfth months."

## Notes

The forms of the pulses of the three yang (greater yang, lesser yang, *yangming*).
- The Song editors note that the three yin pulses are missing, probably a lacuna, and cite at length the *Nanjing*, Bian Que's *Yinyang maifa* and Lü Guang.
