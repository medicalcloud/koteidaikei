# 素問 平人気象論篇第十八 §22 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a normal liver pulse comes, it is soft, weak and swaying, like raising the tip of a long pole; this is called a normal liver. In spring the stomach qi is the root. When a sick liver pulse comes, it is full, replete and slippery, like stroking a long pole; this is called liver illness. When a dying liver pulse comes, it is tense and increasingly strong, like a newly strung bowstring; this is called liver death.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When a normal liver pulse comes ... this is called a normal liver"] "Like the tip of a pole" means long and soft.

[On "In spring the stomach qi is the root"] Only when the pulse has stomach qi is it long and soft like the tip of a pole.

[On "When a sick liver pulse comes ... liver illness"] It is long but not soft; therefore like stroking a pole.

[On "When a dying liver pulse comes ... liver death"] 勁 means extremely strong and tense.

## Notes

The normal, sick and dying forms of the liver pulse.
- Daizhige writes 軟 for 耎.
