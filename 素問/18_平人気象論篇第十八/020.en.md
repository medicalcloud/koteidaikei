# 素問 平人気象論篇第十八 §20 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a normal heart pulse comes, it is string upon string like a necklace of pearls, like stroking jade; this is called a normal heart. In summer the stomach qi is the root. When a sick heart pulse comes, it pants and pants in succession, with a slight bend in the middle; this is called heart illness. When a dying heart pulse comes, it is bent in front and halting behind, like handling a belt hook; this is called heart death.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When a normal heart pulse comes ... this is called a normal heart"] It means the pulse is full and abundant, slightly like pearls striking the hand. *Langgan* is a kind of pearl.

[On "In summer the stomach qi is the root"] When the pulse has stomach qi, it is string upon string, slightly like a string of pearls.

[On "When a sick heart pulse comes ... heart illness"] "Bend" means bending as it strikes the hand.
Song editors: Yueren says "pecking in succession with a slight bend in the middle is called kidney illness", which differs from the *Suwen*.

[On "When a dying heart pulse comes ... heart death"] 居 means motionless; 操 means to hold; 鉤 means the hook of a leather belt.

## Notes

The normal, sick and dying forms of the heart pulse described in images. Units 020 to 024 treat the lung, liver, spleen and kidney in the same way.
- The Song editors point out that the *Nanjing* (Yueren) gives a different account.
- Daizhige (the plain text) writes 復 for 夏.
