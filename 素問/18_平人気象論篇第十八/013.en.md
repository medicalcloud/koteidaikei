# 素問 平人気象論篇第十八 §13 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Many blue vessels on the forearm mean loss of blood. A slack *chi* [skin] with a rough pulse is called languor. Lying quietly with an abundant pulse is called loss of blood. A rough *chi* with a slippery pulse is called profuse sweating. A cold *chi* with a fine pulse is called diarrhoea. A coarse pulse and *chi* that are always hot are called heat in the centre.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Many blue vessels on the forearm mean loss of blood"] When the blood is scant and the vessels empty, guest cold enters, and the cold congeals the blood; therefore the vessels are blue.

[On "A slack *chi* with a rough pulse is called languor"] The *chi* is the yin region, governed by the abdomen and kidney. Slack means heat in the centre; rough means no blood. Hot and without blood, there is languor that cannot be named: cold yet not cold, hot yet not hot, weak yet not weak, strong yet not strong — a weariness that cannot be named is called languor. The *Maiyao jingwei lun* (chapter 17) says, "Outside the *chi* one observes the kidney, and inside the *chi* the abdomen"; this is the sense in which the abdomen and kidney govern the *chi*.

[On "Lying quietly with an abundant pulse is called loss of blood"] Lying long harms the qi; when the qi is harmed, the pulse should respond faintly. Now the pulse is abundant and not faint because the blood has gone and the qi has nothing to govern. "Abundant" means rapid, tense, large and drumming.

[On "A rough *chi* with a slippery pulse is called profuse sweating"] It means the skin at the *chi* is rough and the *chi* pulse slippery. Rough skin means the nutritive blood has dried within; a slippery pulse means the yang qi is in surplus within. When the blood is dried yet the yang qi is still in surplus and there is much sweating, the pulse is like this.

[On "A cold *chi* with a fine pulse is called diarrhoea"] The *chi* governs the lower burner, and its diagnosis corresponds to the intestines and abdomen; therefore cold skin and a fine pulse indicate diarrhoea. The *Maifa* says, "When yin is faint, there is purging," meaning the qi of the *chi* is depleted and scant.

[On "A coarse pulse and *chi* that are always hot are called heat in the centre"] It means the centre of the lower burner.

## Notes

From the blue veins of the forearm and the state of the *chi* skin and pulse one judges loss of blood, languor, profuse sweating, diarrhoea and heat in the centre.
- "Languor" (解㑊): Wang Bing describes it as an indescribable weariness, neither cold nor hot, neither weak nor strong.
- "*Chi*" sometimes refers to the skin of the forearm and sometimes to the *chi* pulse; the translation distinguishes them following Wang Bing.
