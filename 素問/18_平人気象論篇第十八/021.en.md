# 素問 平人気象論篇第十八 §21 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a normal lung pulse comes, it is light and gentle, like falling elm pods; this is called a normal lung. In autumn the stomach qi is the root. When a sick lung pulse comes, it neither rises nor falls, like stroking a chicken's feathers; this is called lung illness. When a dying lung pulse comes, it is like something floating, like wind blowing hair; this is called lung death.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When a normal lung pulse comes ... this is called a normal lung"] It is floating, thin and empty.
Song editors: Yueren says, "Light and gentle like stroking elm leaves is called the normal pulse of spring; abundant like a carriage canopy, growing larger on pressure, is called the normal pulse of autumn," which differs from the *Suwen*. Zhang Zhongjing says, "An autumn pulse abundant like a carriage canopy is called yang binding; a spring pulse light like elm pods blown by the wind is called rapid." We fear Yueren's account is mistaken.

[On "In autumn the stomach qi is the root"] When the pulse has stomach qi, it slightly resembles the lightness and emptiness of elm pods.

[On "When a sick lung pulse comes ... lung illness"] It means hard in the centre and empty on both sides.

[On "When a dying lung pulse comes ... lung death"] Flickering like something floating, scattering like wind blowing hair.
Song editors: Yueren says: "When pressed it disappears, like wind blowing hair; this is called death."

## Notes

The normal, sick and dying forms of the lung pulse.
- The Song editors note that the *Nanjing* (Yueren) applies the elm image to the normal pulse of spring and cite Zhang Zhongjing.
