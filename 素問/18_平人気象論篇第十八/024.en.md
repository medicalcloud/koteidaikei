# 素問 平人気象論篇第十八 §24 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a normal kidney pulse comes, it pants and pants string upon string like a hook, and is hard when pressed; this is called a normal kidney. In winter the stomach qi is the root. When a sick kidney pulse comes, it is like pulling a creeper, growing harder when pressed; this is called kidney illness. When a dying kidney pulse comes, it starts like a rope being snatched, snapping like flicking a stone; this is called kidney death."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When a normal kidney pulse comes ... a normal kidney"] It means like the heart pulse, hooked, and slightly hard when pressed.
Song editors: Yueren says: "When it comes large above and pointed below, moist and slippery like a sparrow pecking, it is called normal." Lü Guang says: "Large above is the foot greater yang; pointed below is the foot lesser yin. Yin and yang are in their places and the stomach qi is strong; hence it is called normal. 'A sparrow pecking' means large at the base and pointed at the tip."

[On "In winter the stomach qi is the root"] With little stomach qi it is hard even without pressing.

[On "When a sick kidney pulse comes ... kidney illness"] Its form like pulling a creeper makes clear that it is hard even without pressing and harder still when pressed.

[On "When a dying kidney pulse comes ... kidney death"] "Starts like a rope being snatched" is like a snake darting; "snapping like flicking a stone" means hurried and hard.

## Notes

The normal, sick and dying forms of the kidney pulse; this ends the chapter, and Qibo's speech begun in unit 002.
- The Song editors cite the *Nanjing* (Yueren) and Lü Guang.
