# 素問 平人気象論篇第十八 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "When a person breathes out once, the pulse beats twice; when he breathes in once, the pulse also beats twice. In one complete breath the pulse beats five times, the remainder being made up by a deep sigh. This is called a normal person. A normal person is one who is not ill. One always uses one who is not ill to gauge the sick person. The physician is not ill; therefore, for the sake of the sick person, the rule is to gauge [the pulse] by evening one's own breathing.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When a person breathes out once ... A normal person is one who is not ill"] One circuit of the channels through the body is in all sixteen *zhang* two *chi* long. In breathing out and in, the pulse beats twice each, and in settling the breath it beats once more, making five beats. Counting 270 settled breaths, the qi can complete a circuit; to complete the fifty circuits takes 13,500 settled breaths, in which the qi travels 810 *zhang* in all. In this way it corresponds to the constant measure of heaven; the pulse qi is neither insufficient nor excessive, and the qi is balanced and regulated. Hence it is called a normal person.

## Notes

In a normal person the pulse beats twice on breathing out, twice on breathing in, five times per complete breath; the physician counts the patient's pulse using his own even breathing as the standard. Qibo's speech continues to the end of the chapter (unit 024).
- Wang Bing calculates, from the total length of the channels (16 *zhang* 2 *chi*) and the daily number of breaths (13,500), that the qi circulates fifty times through the body.
