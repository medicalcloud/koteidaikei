# 素問 霊蘭秘典論篇第八 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "How thorough your question is! Let me answer it in order. The heart is the office of the sovereign; spirit illumination comes from it. The lung is the office of the chancellor; regulation comes from it. The liver is the office of the general; planning comes from it. The gallbladder is the office of the upright judge; decision comes from it. The *danzhong* is the office of the envoy; joy and pleasure come from it. The spleen and stomach are the office of the granary; the five flavours come from them. The large intestine is the office of conveyance; transformation comes from it. The small intestine is the office of receiving; the transformation of things comes from it. The kidney is the office of forceful action; skill and ingenuity come from it. The triple burner is the office of the sluices; the waterways come from it. The bladder is the office of the regional capital; fluids are stored in it, and when the qi transforms they can come out. All these twelve offices must not lose one another. Therefore if the sovereign is enlightened, those below are at peace; nourishing life thereby, one lives long and is not endangered to the end of one's life; governing the realm thereby, it greatly prospers. If the sovereign is not enlightened, the twelve offices are endangered, the paths of the envoys are closed and blocked and do not communicate, and the body is greatly harmed; nourishing life thereby, there is calamity; for one who governs the realm thereby, the ancestral temple is in great danger. Beware, beware! The utmost Way lies in the subtle; its transformations are endless. Who knows its source? How essential! Those who seek it are diligent, yet who knows its key? Before what is profound, who can be called good? The numbers of the indistinct arise from hair-breadths; the numbers of hair-breadths arise from measures of length and volume; multiply them by thousands and tens of thousands and they can grow ever greater; extend and enlarge them, and the form is then regulated."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The heart is the office of the sovereign; spirit illumination comes from it"] It is entrusted with governing things; therefore it is the office of the sovereign. Clear and still, it houses the numinous; therefore it is said that spirit illumination comes from it.

[On "The lung is the office of the chancellor; regulation comes from it"] Its position is high but it is not the ruler; therefore its office is the chancellor. It governs the circulation of the nutritive and defensive qi; therefore regulation proceeds from it.

[On "The liver is the office of the general; planning comes from it"] It is brave and decisive; therefore it is called the general. It acts secretly before things have sprouted; therefore planning comes from it.

[On "The gallbladder is the office of the upright judge; decision comes from it"] It is firm, upright and resolute; therefore its office is the upright judge. Straight and without doubt; therefore decision comes from it.

[On "The *danzhong* is the office of the envoy; joy and pleasure come from it"] The *danzhong* is in the chest between the two breasts and is the sea of qi. The heart-master, as ruler, spreads and proclaims instructions; the *danzhong* governs qi and spreads yin and yang by qi. When the qi is harmonious and the will at ease, joy and pleasure arise from it. Because it distributes yin and yang, its office is the envoy.

[On "The spleen and stomach are the office of the granary; the five flavours come from them"] They contain the five grains; this is the office of the granary. They nourish all around; therefore it says the five flavours come from them.

[On "The large intestine is the office of conveyance; transformation comes from it"] "Conveyance" means the path conveying what is unclean; "transformation" means changing the form of things. Therefore it says it is the office of conveyance and transformation comes from it.

[On "The small intestine is the office of receiving; the transformation of things comes from it"] It receives from the stomach and governs receiving the dregs; having received them, it transforms them again and conveys them into the large intestine. Therefore it says it is the office of receiving and the transformation of things comes from it.

[On "The kidney is the office of forceful action; skill and ingenuity come from it"] It is strong in action; therefore it is called forceful action. It creates and shapes forms; therefore it is called skill and ingenuity. In women it corresponds to skill and ingenuity; in men it is properly called forceful action.

[On "The triple burner is the office of the sluices; the waterways come from it"] It guides yin and yang and opens what is closed and blocked; therefore its office governs the sluices and the waterways come from it.

[On "The bladder is the office of the regional capital ... when the qi transforms they can come out"] Its position is that of the solitary fu; therefore it is called the capital office. It lies below and is empty within; therefore it stores fluids. If it obtains the bestowing transformation of the qi of the sea of qi, urine flows out; if the qi of the sea of qi does not reach it, it is closed and does not pass. Therefore it says "when the qi transforms they can come out". The *Lingshu jing* says, "The kidney connects above with the lung, so it leads two zang; the bladder is the solitary fu" — this is what is meant.

[On "All these twelve offices must not lose one another"] If they do, calamity comes; therefore they must not lose one another.
Song editors: These are in fact eleven offices, because the two organs spleen and stomach share one office.

[On "Therefore if the sovereign is enlightened ... governing the realm thereby, it greatly prospers"] "Sovereign" means the ruler, the office of the heart. When the ruler is wise and enlightened, punishment and reward are consistent; when they are consistent, the officials uphold the law; when the officials uphold the law, the people are not wrongly or excessively punished. Therefore when the sovereign is enlightened, the realm is at peace. When the heart is enlightened within, it weighs good and evil; weighing good and evil, it discerns safety and danger; discerning safety and danger, the body is not cut short or harmed by straying from the Way. Therefore, nourishing life thereby, one lives long and to the end of life does not come to danger. Applied to nourishing life, one is not endangered to the end of life; applied to the ruler, the realm gains peace, and as master of the realm, the fortune of the state prospers.

[On "If the sovereign is not enlightened ... Beware, beware!"] "The paths of the envoys" means the paths by which the spirit qi is sent and employed. When the heart is not enlightened, evil and correct are the same; when they are the same, loss and gain are not distinguished; when loss and gain are not distinguished, movement brings misfortune and the body falls into emaciation. Therefore the body is greatly harmed and nourishing life thereby brings calamity. When the ruler is not enlightened, he relies on those around him; relying on those around him, power is wielded recklessly; when power is wielded recklessly, the officials cannot uphold the law; when they cannot, the people lose their place and all suffer injustice. Moreover, the people are the root of the state; when the root is firm, the state is at peace. If the state does not gain peace, what will the country have? How can the standing of the ancestral temple not come to danger? Therefore "beware, beware" speaks of deep caution.

[On "The utmost Way lies in the subtle; its transformations are endless. Who knows its source?"] 孰 means "who". This speaks of the function of the utmost Way: made small, it is subtle and fine and enters everywhere; made great, it is vast and distant and its transformations are endless. But who knows and discerns its deep source?

[On "How essential! Those who seek it are diligent"] Song editors: The *Taisu* reads 肖者濯濯.

[On "yet who knows its key? Before what is profound, who can be called good?"] 窘 means essential; 瞿瞿 means diligent. The key of the human body is the Way. But those who seek in the principles of things by waxing and waning, sameness and difference, wishing thereby to know the source of transformation, even if they diligently seek clear understanding, who can know its essential subtlety? Before they can know it, it turns deep and distant, profound and mysterious, and again one does not know who is good at knowing its essential subtlety. What is mysterious and deep certainly cannot be obtained by seeking through principle; taking it near at hand in the body, the twelve offices can roughly be explored and made into the way of governing the body. 閔閔 means deep and distant; 良 means good.
Song editors: These four phrases overlap with the text of the *Qijiaobian dalun* (chapter 69), where 消 is written 肖.

[On "The numbers of the indistinct arise from hair-breadths"] "Indistinct" means seeming to exist and seeming not to exist; 忽 is also a number. Seeming not to exist and seeming to exist, the numbers of hair-breadths arise within it. The *Laozi* says, "Indistinct and vague, within it there is something" — this is what is meant. The *Suanshu* says: "What seems to exist and seems not to exist is a *hu*."

[On "the numbers of hair-breadths arise from measures ... and the form is then regulated"] Though hair-breadths are small, if they are accumulated without stopping and multiplied by the named numbers, they reach the standards of the foot-measure and the peck-measure; multiplied by thousands and tens of thousands, they can also be increased to the great number of a *zai*. Extending them to their greatness, they correspond to the system of the human form.

## Notes

The doctrine of the twelve offices. The heart is the sovereign, and the lung, liver, gallbladder, *danzhong*, spleen and stomach, large intestine, small intestine, kidney, triple burner and bladder are likened to state officials. If the sovereign (the heart) is enlightened, those below are at peace — in nourishing life there is longevity, in governing the state great prosperity — but if not, the twelve offices are endangered. Finally it says that the utmost Way is subtle and hard to grasp, yet the accumulation of the minute gives form its regulation.
- The Song editors point out that there are in fact eleven offices, since the spleen and stomach share one.
- *Danzhong*: Wang Bing places it in the chest between the breasts and calls it the sea of qi.
- "How essential! Those who seek it are diligent" (窘乎哉，消者瞿瞿) is a difficult phrase. Wang Bing glosses 窘 as essential and 瞿瞿 as diligent. According to the Song editors, the *Taisu* reads 肖者濯濯, and the same phrases appear in the *Qijiaobian dalun* (chapter 69).
- 𫞐 in Wang Bing's note is a variant of 権 (Kanripo writes 権). 巳 in 受巳 and 積而不巳 is the base text's form, translated as 已.
