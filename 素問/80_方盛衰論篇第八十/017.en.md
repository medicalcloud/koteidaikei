# 素問 方盛衰論篇第八十 §17 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Thus diagnosis has a great method: sitting and rising have constancy, going out and coming in have order, so as to turn the spirit illumination; one must be clear and pure; observe above and observe below, attend to the eight proper [winds] and pathogens, distinguish the regions of the five centers, press the pulse for movement and stillness, follow the meaning of slipperiness and roughness, cold and warmth at the cubit, look at its size, match it with the manifestations of illness, obtain counterflow and compliance from it, and further know the name of the illness; then diagnosis can be completely successful without losing the human situation. Hence in diagnosing one looks at the breath and at the intent, and so does not lose the principles. The Way is examined very clearly, so it can last long. Not knowing this way, one loses the classic and cuts off principle, speaks wildly and predicts recklessly; this is called losing the Way."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Thus diagnosis has a great method: sitting and rising have constancy"] When sitting and rising have constancy, breath and strength are regulated; hence the method of diagnosis must use this first.

[On "sitting and rising have constancy, going out and coming in have order, so as to turn the spirit illumination"] Why is constancy in sitting and rising valued? Because in going out, coming in, moving and acting, the spirit illumination turns along with them.

[On "observe above and observe below, attend to the eight proper [winds] and pathogens, distinguish the regions of the five centers, press the pulse for movement and stillness"] Observing above means complexion; observing below means form-qi. The eight proper means the proper signs of the eight periods; the five centers means the regions of the five viscera. After that, press the movement and stillness of the inch and cubit to decide death and life.

[On "Hence in diagnosing one looks at the breath and at the intent, and so does not lose the principles"] Count the length of the breaths to gauge the rate of the pulse; hence the method of diagnosis sometimes looks at the breathing. Knowing the breath and matching it with the pulse, one surely knows where the illness is; the principles the sages examine all fit this.

[On "Not knowing this way ... this is called losing the Way"] It means losing the way of the subtle and wondrous.

## Notes

Concluding the chapter with the great method of diagnosis. The Yellow Emperor's speech ends here.
