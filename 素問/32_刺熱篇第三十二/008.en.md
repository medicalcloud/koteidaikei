# 素問 刺熱篇第三十二 §8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The qi points for febrile illness: the space below the third vertebra governs heat in the chest; the space below the fourth vertebra governs heat in the diaphragm; the space below the fifth vertebra governs heat of the liver; the space below the sixth vertebra governs heat of the spleen; the space below the seventh vertebra governs heat of the kidney; the flourishing is at the sacrum. [Counting from] the third vertebra on the nape, in the depression. [Red colour] going up from below the cheek to the cheekbone indicates great conglomeration; going down to the jaw indicates abdominal fullness; behind the cheekbone indicates pain in the flanks; above the cheek indicates above the diaphragm.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The qi points for febrile illness ... the flourishing is at the sacrum"] The joints of the spine are called vertebrae; the end of the spine is called the sacrum. It says the hot qi of the kidney communicates outwardly with the coccyx. Examining this text, the heat of the spirit-storing zang governed by the spaces between the vertebrae does not correspond exactly to the transport points of those zang, yet it says they govern treatment; the reasoning is unclear.

[On "[Counting from] the third vertebra on the nape, in the depression"] This gives the general method of counting the vertebrae. How does one count "the space below the third vertebra governs heat in the chest"? It says that in every case the depression should be taken as where the qi issues.

[On "[Red colour] going up from below the cheek ... above the diaphragm"] This observes the colour of the facial regions to clarify the diagnosis of illness within the abdomen.

## Notes

The points on the back used for febrile illness and diagnosis of illness by the location of colour on the face; this ends the chapter.
- 栄在骶也 and 項上三椎陥者中也 are obscure and read in various ways; the translation follows the word order.
- Wang Bing questions the fact that these intervertebral points do not coincide with the back transport points of the respective zang.
