# 素問 刺熱篇第三十二 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In spleen febrile illness, first the head is heavy, the cheeks ache, the heart is vexed, the forehead is green-blue, one wants to vomit, and the body is hot. When the heat contends, the lumbus aches so that one cannot bend forward or back, the abdomen is full with diarrhoea, and both jaws ache. It worsens on *jia* and *yi* and there is great sweating on *wu* and *ji*; if the qi counterflows, one dies on *jia* and *yi*. Needle the foot greater yin and *yangming*.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In spleen febrile illness, first the head is heavy ... the body is hot"] The stomach vessel arises at the nose, crosses at the bridge, descends along the outside of the nose, enters the upper teeth, comes out again alongside the mouth and around the lips, crosses below at Chengjiang, turns back along the lower edge behind the chin, emerges at Daying, follows Jiache, rises in front of the ear, passes Kezhuren, and follows the hairline to the forehead. Therefore first the head is heavy, the cheeks ache and the forehead is green-blue. A branch of the spleen vessel goes again from the stomach up through the diaphragm and pours into the heart; its straight course rises through the diaphragm alongside the throat. Therefore the heart is vexed, one wants to vomit, and the body is hot.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* and *Taisu* say "In spleen febrile illness, first the head is heavy and the forehead aches", without the two characters 顔青.

[On "When the heat contends ... both jaws ache"] A branch of the stomach vessel arises at the lower opening of the stomach, descends inside the abdomen to Qijie where it joins, and so goes down to the thigh. Qijie is in front of the lumbus; therefore the lumbus aches. The spleen vessel enters the abdomen, belongs to the spleen and connects with the stomach. Also, the stomach vessel, after crossing at Chengjiang, turns back along the lower edge behind the chin, emerges at Daying and follows Jiache. Therefore the abdomen is full with diarrhoea and both jaws ache.

[On "It worsens on *jia* and *yi* ... one dies on *jia* and *yi*"] The spleen governs earth; *jia* and *yi* are wood, and wood fells earth; therefore it worsens and one dies on *jia* and *yi*. *Wu* and *ji* are earth; therefore there is great sweating on *wu* and *ji*. The signs of counterflowing qi are not yet discussed in the classic.

[On "Needle the foot greater yin and *yangming*"] The greater yin is the spleen vessel and the *yangming* the stomach vessel.
Song editors: The second part of the chapter on febrile illness in the *Jiayi jing* says, "The illness first has heaviness of the head, pain in the forehead, vexation of the heart and heat in the body. When the heat contends, the lumbus aches so that one cannot bend forward or back, the abdomen is full and both jaws ache. There are sudden diarrhoea, frequent hunger without desire to eat, frequent belching, heat in the centre with cold feet, abdominal distension with undigested food, frequent vomiting, diarrhoea with pus and blood, and painful retching with nothing coming up. First take Sanli, then Taibai and Zhangmen."

## Notes

The symptoms, prognosis and needling of spleen febrile illness.
- 顔 means the forehead. According to the Song editors, the *Jiayi jing* and *Taisu* read 顔痛 in place of 頰痛 and 顔青.
