# 素問 刺熱篇第三十二 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In liver febrile illness, the urine first turns yellow, the abdomen aches, one lies down much, and the body is hot. When the heat contends, there are wild speech and fright, fullness and pain in the flanks, agitation of the hands and feet, and one cannot lie down in peace. It worsens on *geng* and *xin* [days] and there is great sweating on *jia* and *yi*; if the qi counterflows, one dies on *geng* and *xin*. Needle the foot *jueyin* and lesser yang. If it counterflows, the head aches and throbs, the vessels pulling and surging into the head.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In liver febrile illness, the urine first turns yellow ... the body is hot"] The liver vessel encircles the genitals, reaches the lower abdomen and rises; therefore the urine does not flow and first turns yellow, the abdomen aches, and one lies down much. Cold presses and produces heat in the body; hence it is hot.

[On "When the heat contends ... one cannot lie down in peace"] The channels and network vessels have received heat, but the spirit-storing zang has not yet admitted the evil; evil and upright press on each other; hence "contend". "Contend" elsewhere is the same. Also, the liver vessel rises from the lower abdomen alongside the stomach, passes through the diaphragm, spreads over the flanks, follows the back of the throat and connects with the root of the tongue; therefore there are wild speech and fullness and pain in the flanks. The liver is by nature still and governs fright; therefore in illness there is fright, the hands and feet are agitated, and one cannot lie in peace.

[On "It worsens on *geng* and *xin* ... one dies on *geng* and *xin*"] The liver governs wood; *geng* and *xin* are metal, and metal controls wood; therefore it worsens and one dies on *geng* and *xin*. *Jia* and *yi* are wood; therefore there is great sweating on *jia* and *yi*.

[On "Needle the foot *jueyin* and lesser yang"] The *jueyin* is the liver vessel and the lesser yang the gallbladder vessel.

[On "If it counterflows ... surging into the head"] The liver vessel runs from the root of the tongue along the back of the throat, rises to emerge at the forehead and meets the *du* vessel at the vertex; therefore the head aches and throbs and the vessels pull and surge into the head. 員員 means seeming tense.

## Notes

This chapter describes the symptoms, prognosis and needling of febrile illnesses of the five zang; it contains no dialogue. This unit concerns liver febrile illness.
- "The heat contends": Wang Bing takes it as the hot evil in the channels contending with the upright qi of the zang, which has not yet received the evil.
