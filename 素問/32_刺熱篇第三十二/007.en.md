# 素問 刺熱篇第三十二 §7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In febrile illness that begins with pain in the chest and flanks and agitation of the hands and feet, needle the foot lesser yang and supplement the foot greater yin; when the illness is severe, perform the fifty-nine needlings. In febrile illness beginning with pain in the hands and arms, needle the hand *yangming* and greater yin, and stop when sweat comes. In febrile illness beginning in the head, needle the greater yang at the nape, and stop when sweat comes. In febrile illness beginning in the feet and shins, needle the foot *yangming*, and stop when sweat comes. In febrile illness that begins with heaviness of the body, aching bones, deafness and a tendency to close the eyes, needle the foot lesser yin; when the illness is severe, perform the fifty-nine needlings. In febrile illness that begins with dizziness and heat and fullness of the chest and flanks, needle the foot lesser yin and lesser yang. When, in the greater yang vessel, colour flourishes on the cheekbones, it is febrile illness; if the flourishing has not yet crossed, it is said, "Sweat will soon be obtained; wait for the time and it will end." If it appears contending with the *jueyin* pulse, death will come within three days. When febrile illness connects inwardly with the kidney, it is the colour of the lesser yang vessel. When, in the lesser yang vessel, colour flourishes in front of the cheeks, it is febrile illness; if the flourishing has not yet crossed, it is said, "Sweat will soon be obtained; wait for the time and it will end." If it appears contending with the lesser yin pulse, death will come within three days.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In febrile illness that begins with pain in the chest and flanks ... supplement the foot greater yin"] This gives an example of proper selection. When the wood of the foot lesser yang is ill, one drains the wood qi of the foot lesser yang and supplements the earth qi of the foot greater yin, for fear that wood will transmit to earth. Pain in the chest and flanks is governed by Qiuxu. Qiuxu is in the depression below and slightly in front of the outer ankle, where the foot lesser yang vessel passes. One may needle five body-*fen* and retain for seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. For agitation of the hands and feet in febrile illness, the classic gives no indication of what governs its treatment; but to supplement the foot greater yin vessel, one should take the well and spring points.
Song editors: For 足太陰, Quan Yuanqi's edition and the *Taisu* write 手太陰 "hand greater yin". Yang Shangshan says, "The hand greater yin rises to belong to the lung and emerges from the lung below the armpit; therefore the chest and flanks ache." Moreover, the *Lingshu jing* says, "In febrile illness with pain in the chest and flanks and agitation of the hands and feet, take it between the sinews, using the fourth needle to seek the sinews in the liver; one cannot seek it in metal; metal is the lung." From this it is certain that "hand greater yin" is correct.

[On "when the illness is severe, perform the fifty-nine needlings"] The fifty-nine needlings: on the head, five lines with five in each line, to disperse the counterflow of heat of all the yang; Dazhu, Yingshu, Quepen and Beishu, these eight, to drain the heat in the chest; Qijie, Sanli, and the upper and lower Juxu, these eight, to drain the heat in the stomach; Yunmen, Yugu, Weizhong and Suikong, these eight, to drain the heat of the four limbs; the five beside the transport points of the five zang, these ten, to drain the heat of the five zang. All these fifty-nine points are on the left and right of the heat; therefore when the illness is severe one needles them so. The five lines on the head: the central line is Shangxing, Xinhui, Qianding, Baihui and Houding; on both sides of it, Wuchu, Chengguang, Tongtian, Luoque and Yuzhen; and further on both sides, Linqi, Muchuang, Zhengying, Chengling and Naokong. Shangxing is on the skull directly above the centre of the nose, in the depression one body-*cun* inside the hairline that admits a bean; one may needle four body-*fen*. [Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes three *fen* for four *fen*, and the note to the *Shuire xue lun* also writes three *fen*. On examination, the following text of this note says "needle as for Shangxing" and also "needle as for Xinhui"; since there are two methods, following the *Jiayi jing* and the note to the *Shuire xue lun*, Shangxing should be needled three *fen* and Xinhui four *fen*.] Xinhui is in the depression one body-*cun* behind Shangxing; needle as for Shangxing. Qianding is in the depression between the bones one body-*cun* five *fen* behind Xinhui; needle as for Xinhui. Baihui is one body-*cun* five *fen* behind Qianding, in the depression admitting a finger in the whorl at the centre of the crown, the meeting of the *du* vessel and the foot greater yang vessel; needle as for Shangxing. Houding is one body-*cun* five *fen* behind Baihui, above the occipital bone; needle as for Xinhui. All five are where the qi of the *du* vessel issues. Retain Shangxing for six breaths; for moxibustion, five cones each. The points on both sides next: Wuchu is one body-*cun* five *fen* to each side of Shangxing; Chengguang one body-*cun* behind Wuchu; Tongtian one body-*cun* five *fen* behind Chengguang; Luoque one body-*cun* five *fen* behind Tongtian; Yuzhen seven body-*fen* behind Luoque. All five are where the qi of the foot greater yang vessel issues. One may needle three body-*fen*; retain Wuchu and Tongtian seven breaths each, Luoque five breaths, Yuzhen three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. [Song editors: According to the *Jiayi jing*, Chengguang must not receive moxibustion, and Yuzhen is needled two *fen*.] Further on both sides: Linqi is directly above the eye, five body-*fen* inside the hairline, the meeting of the foot greater yang, lesser yang and *yangwei* vessels; Muchuang and Zhengying are successively one body-*cun* apart; Chengling and Naokong successively one body-*cun* five *fen* apart. All five are meetings of the foot lesser yang and *yangwei* vessels. Naokong may be needled four body-*fen*, the others three body-*fen*. Retain Linqi seven breaths; for moxibustion, five cones. Dazhu is below the first vertebra of the nape, in the depressions one and a half body-*cun* to each side, the meeting of the qi of three vessels: the branch of the *du* vessel, the foot greater yang and the hand greater yang. One may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, five cones. [Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes seven cones; the note to the *Qixue lun* also writes seven cones; the notes to the *Cinüe lun* and to the heat points write five cones.] Yingshu is the transport point in the breast; its proper name is Zhongfu, six body-*cun* apart on both sides of the central line of the chest, one *cun* below Yunmen, in the depression where the artery is felt between the ribs three ribs above the nipple; take it with the patient face up. It is the meeting of the hand and foot greater yin vessels. One may needle three body-*fen* and retain five breaths; for moxibustion, five cones. Quepen is in the depression in the transverse bone above the shoulder, where the qi of the hand *yangming* vessel issues. One may needle two body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Beishu is probably Fengmen Refu, one and a half body-*cun* to each side below the second vertebra, the meeting of the *du* vessel and foot greater yang. One may needle five body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, five cones. Checking the present *Mingtang* and *Zhonggao tujing*, they do not mention Beishu, and where exactly it is remains unclear. [Song editors: In his note to the *Shuire xue lun* Wang takes Fengmen Refu as Beishu, and in his note to the *Qixue lun* takes Dazhu as Beishu, while this note says it is unclear. The three notes differ, presumably because he was in doubt.] Qijie is at both ends of the transverse bone below the navel, one body-*cun* above the groin, where the artery is felt; it is where the qi of the foot *yangming* vessel issues. One may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, five cones. Sanli is three body-*cun* below the knee, between the two sinews on the outer edge of the shin, where the foot *yangming* vessel enters. One may needle one body-*cun* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. The upper Juxu is the uniting point of the foot *yangming* with the large intestine, three body-*cun* below Sanli, where the qi of the foot *yangming* vessel issues. One may needle eight body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones. The lower Juxu is the uniting point of the foot *yangming* with the small intestine, three body-*cun* below the upper one, where the qi of the foot *yangming* vessel issues. One may needle three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones. Yunmen is below the clavicle, on both sides of the central line of the chest [Song editors: The note to the *Qixue lun* writes "beside the *ren* vessel, transversely away from the *ren* vessel" for "on both sides of the central line of the chest"; the wording differs but the location of the point is the same], six body-*cun* apart, where the artery is felt; Zhongfu lies one body-*cun* below it. Yunmen is where the qi of the hand greater yin vessel issues; take it with the arm raised. One may needle seven body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones. Checking the present *Mingtang* and *Zhonggao tujing*, they do not record a Yugu point. Considering that this point drains the heat of the four limbs, it is probably Jianyu, at the tip of the shoulder between the two bones, the meeting of the hand *yangming* and the *qiao* vessel. One may needle six body-*fen* and retain six breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Weizhong is at the bend behind the knee, at the artery in the transverse crease at the centre of the popliteal fossa [Song editors: Mr Wang annotates the Weizhong point in four places — the notes to the *Qixue lun*, *Gukong lun* and *Cinüe lun*, and this one. The other three lack the five characters "the bend behind the knee". The difference from this note is not a real difference; the notes are simply more or less detailed], where the foot greater yang vessel enters. One may needle five body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Suikong's proper name is Yaoshu, in the space below the twenty-first vertebra in the middle of the spine, where the qi of the *du* vessel issues. One may needle two body-*fen* [Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes two *cun*; the note to the *Shuire xue lun* also writes two *cun*; the notes to the *Qifu lun* and *Gukong lun* write one *fen*] and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. The five beside the transport points of the five zang are the five points Pohu, Shentang, Hunmen, Yishe and Zhishi, three body-*cun* apart on both sides of the spine, all where the qi of the foot greater yang vessel issues. Pohu is on both sides below the third vertebra; take it sitting upright; one may needle five body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones. Shentang is on both sides below the fifth vertebra; one may needle three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones. Hunmen is on both sides below the ninth vertebra; take it sitting upright; one may needle five body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones. Yishe is on both sides below the eleventh vertebra; take it sitting upright; one may needle five body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones. Zhishi is on both sides below the fourteenth vertebra; take it sitting upright; one may needle five body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones. This is the method of the fifty-nine needlings of this classic. The fifty-nine needlings indicated in the *Zhenjing* differ entirely from this classic; although both are key points for treating febrile illness, the principles of their combined use run quite contrary. One should still follow the classic's method corresponding to the signs and form of the illness, and needle according to the signs.

[On "In febrile illness beginning with pain in the hands and arms ... stop when sweat comes"] Pain in the hands and arms is governed by Lieque. Lieque is the network point of the hand greater yin, one and a half body-*cun* above the wrist, branching to the *yangming*. One may needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, five cones. To bring out sweat, Shangyang governs it. Shangyang is the well point of the hand *yangming* vessel, on the inner side of the index finger, a chive-leaf's breadth from the corner of the nail, where the hand *yangming* vessel emerges. One may needle one body-*fen* and retain one breath; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "In febrile illness beginning in the head ... stop when sweat comes"] Tianzhu governs it. Tianzhu is beside the back of the nape, in the depression on the outer edge of the great sinew at the hairline, where the qi of the foot greater yang vessel issues. One may needle two body-*fen* and retain six breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "In febrile illness beginning in the feet and shins ... stop when sweat comes"] Song editors: This item was originally absent from the *Suwen* and is also absent from the *Taisu*; it has now been added on the basis of the *Jiayi jing*.

[On "In febrile illness that begins with heaviness of the body ... needle the foot lesser yin"] According to the classic there is no point that properly governs it; one should supplement or drain the well and spring points.
Song editors: The *Lingshu jing* says, "In febrile illness with heaviness of the body, aching bones, deafness and a tendency to close the eyes, take it at the bones, using the fourth needle to seek the bones in the kidney; one cannot seek it in earth; earth is the spleen."

[On "when the illness is severe, perform the fifty-nine needlings"] As in the ancient method.

[On "In febrile illness that begins with dizziness ... needle the foot lesser yin and lesser yang"] Again the well and spring points.

[On "When, in the greater yang vessel, colour flourishes on the cheekbones, it is febrile illness"] 栄 means adorn: red colour appears on the cheekbones as if adorning them. The cheekbone means below the eye in line with the outer canthus. The greater yang corresponds to fire; hence the red colour appears.
Song editors: Yang Shangshan says, "When red colour flourishes on the cheekbones, it is febrile illness of the bones," which differs from Wang's note.

[On "if the flourishing has not yet crossed"] Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* and *Taisu* write 栄未夭 "the flourishing has not yet withered"; the later 栄未交 is also written 夭.

[On "if the flourishing has not yet crossed, it is said ... it will end"] 栄 is written 営 in one version, a scribal error. 曰 introduces the grounds of an ancient classic's method. It says that although the colour is bright and abundant, if the qi of yin and yang have not crossed and become confused, the method says, "Sweat it now and it will end." "Wait for the time" means that liver illness waits for *jia* and *yi*, heart illness for *bing* and *ding*, spleen illness for *wu* and *ji*, lung illness for *geng* and *xin*, and kidney illness for *ren* and *gui*; this is "wait for the time and it will end". What "crossing" means is as in the next sentence.

[On "If it appears contending with the *jueyin* pulse, death will come within three days"] Outwardly the red colour of the greater yang appears, while inwardly the wiry pulse of the *jueyin* responds. When the greater yang receives illness it should transmit to the *yangming*; now instead the *jueyin* pulse appears, which means earth is ruined and wood injures it; therefore one dies. Earth qi is already ruined and wood runs wild; the generating number of wood is three; therefore the date is not beyond three days.

[On "When febrile illness connects inwardly with the kidney, it is the colour of the lesser yang vessel"] 病 is perhaps 気, a scribal error. If the qi of red colour connects inwardly to both sides of the nose, it is the colour of the lesser yang vessel, not of the *jueyin*. Why? Because the region of the kidney is near the nose.
Song editors: On examination, some want to change 腎 to 鼻 "nose". The *Jiayi jing* and *Taisu* both write 腎. Yang Shangshan says, "The greater yang is water and the *jueyin* wood. Water generates wood; when wood is abundant, water declines. Therefore when the colour of greater yang water appears and wood appears contending, water dies, because the febrile illness connects inwardly with the kidney and the kidney is harmed by heat; therefore one dies." Originally the six characters 少陽之脈色也 were absent; Mr Wang added them. Wang's note is wrong; Shangshan's interpretation should be followed.

[On "When, in the lesser yang vessel, colour flourishes in front of the cheeks, it is febrile illness"] "In front of the cheeks" means below the cheekbones near both sides of the nose.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* and *Taisu* write 筋 "sinew" for 前. Yang Shangshan says, "The region of the foot lesser yang is on the cheeks; when red colour flourishes there, one knows it is febrile illness of the sinews."

[On "If it appears contending with the lesser yin pulse, death will come within three days"] When the lesser yang receives illness it should transmit to the greater yin; now instead the lesser yin pulse appears, which again means earth is ruined and wood injures it; therefore one dies. Not beyond three days is again because of the number of wood.
Song editors: On examination, some want to change 少陰 to 厥陰. The *Jiayi jing* and *Taisu* write 少陰. Yang Shangshan says, "The lesser yang is wood and the lesser yin water. When the colour of the lesser yang appears and the lesser yin appears contending, the mother overcomes the child; therefore wood dies." Wang's note here is also wrong. The old edition and the *Jiayi jing* and *Taisu* all lack the six characters 死期不過三日; Mr Wang supplied this text.

## Notes

Needling according to where febrile illness begins, the fifty-nine needlings, and the prognosis of febrile illness by facial colour and pulse.
- Wang Bing's note describes in detail the location, needling depth, number of breaths of retention and number of moxa cones for each of the fifty-nine points. Song editors' remarks inserted within the note are given in square brackets.
- "Supplement the foot greater yin": the Song editors hold, on the basis of Quan Yuanqi's edition, the *Taisu* and the *Lingshu*, that it should be "hand greater yin".
- The item on febrile illness beginning in the feet and shins was added by the Song editors from the *Jiayi jing*.
- 栄未交: according to the Song editors, the *Jiayi jing* and *Taisu* read 栄未夭.
- 其熱病内連腎，少陽之脈色也: according to the Song editors, 少陽之脈色也 was added by Wang Bing, and the later 死期不過三日 was also supplied by him. This passage is read in various ways; the translation keeps the characters as they are.
- 𩕄会, 目䆫 and 列欠 in Wang Bing's note are Xinhui (顖会), Muchuang (目窓) and Lieque (列缺).
