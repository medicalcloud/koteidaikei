# 素問 刺熱篇第三十二 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In heart febrile illness, one is first unhappy, and only after several days is there heat. When the heat contends, there are sudden heart pain, vexation and oppression, frequent vomiting, headache, a red face, and no sweating. It worsens on *ren* and *gui* and there is great sweating on *bing* and *ding*; if the qi counterflows, one dies on *ren* and *gui*. Needle the hand lesser yin and greater yang.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In heart febrile illness, one is first unhappy ... is there heat"] That which takes charge of and governs things is called the heart. When the qi of heart illness enters the channels and network vessels, the spirit is not settled and governed; therefore one is first unhappy, and only after several days is there heat.

[On "When the heat contends ... no sweating"] The heart hand lesser yin vessel arises in the heart; a branch rises from the heart connector alongside the throat. The straight course of the small intestine vessel follows the throat, descends through the diaphragm and reaches the stomach; a branch goes from the supraclavicular fossa along the neck, up the cheek to the outer canthus. Therefore there are sudden heart pain, vexation and oppression, frequent vomiting, headache and a red face. Among the fluids, the heart's is sweat; now there is febrile illness; therefore no sweat comes out.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 兌眥 for 外眥 "outer canthus", and Wang's note to the *Jue lun* also writes 兌眥; 外 should be 兌.

[On "It worsens on *ren* and *gui* ... one dies on *ren* and *gui*"] The heart governs fire; *ren* and *gui* are water, and water extinguishes fire; therefore it worsens and one dies on *ren* and *gui*. *Bing* and *ding* are fire; therefore there is great sweating on *bing* and *ding*. The signs of counterflowing qi are missing from the classic's text.

[On "Needle the hand lesser yin and greater yang"] The lesser yin is the heart vessel and the greater yang the small intestine vessel.

## Notes

The symptoms, prognosis and needling of heart febrile illness.
- Wang Bing notes that, unlike liver febrile illness, the text for heart febrile illness lacks a passage on counterflowing qi ("if it counterflows ...").
