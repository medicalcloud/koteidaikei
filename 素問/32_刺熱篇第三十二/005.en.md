# 素問 刺熱篇第三十二 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In kidney febrile illness, first the lumbus aches and the shins are sore, one suffers from thirst and drinks often, and the body is hot. When the heat contends, the nape aches and is stiff, the shins are cold and sore, the soles are hot, and one does not want to speak. If it counterflows, the nape aches and throbs, unsteady. It worsens on *wu* and *ji* and there is great sweating on *ren* and *gui*; if the qi counterflows, one dies on *wu* and *ji*. Needle the foot lesser yin and greater yang. All sweating comes out on the day of what it overcomes.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In kidney febrile illness, first the lumbus aches ... the body is hot"] The bladder vessel runs from the inside of the shoulder blade alongside the spine to the lumbus; and the lumbus is the house of the kidney; therefore first the lumbus aches. Also, the kidney vessel follows behind the inner ankle, rises inside the calf and emerges at the inner edge of the knee crease; its straight course rises from the kidney through the liver and diaphragm, enters the lung, follows the throat and runs alongside the root of the tongue. Therefore the shins are sore, one suffers from thirst and drinks often, and the body is hot.

[On "When the heat contends ... one does not want to speak"] The bladder vessel emerges from the brain and branches down the nape. The kidney vessel arises below the little toe, runs obliquely to the sole, emerges below the Rangu bone, follows behind the inner ankle, branches into the heel, and so rises inside the shin; its straight course rises from the kidney through the liver and diaphragm, enters the lung, follows the throat and runs alongside the root of the tongue. Therefore the nape aches and is stiff, the shins are cold and sore, the soles are hot, and one does not want to speak.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 然谷 for 然骨.

[On "If it counterflows, the nape aches and throbs, unsteady"] The sinew of the kidney follows the inside of the spine, runs alongside the spinal muscles up to the nape, knots at the occipital bone, and joins the sinew of the bladder; the bladder vessel also runs down the nape alongside it. Therefore the nape aches and throbs. 澹澹 means seeming unsettled.

[On "It worsens on *wu* and *ji* ... one dies on *wu* and *ji*"] The kidney governs water; *wu* and *ji* are earth, and earth punishes water; therefore it worsens and one dies on *wu* and *ji*. *Ren* and *gui* are water; therefore there is great sweating on *ren* and *gui*.

[On "Needle the foot lesser yin and greater yang"] The lesser yin is the kidney vessel and the greater yang the bladder vessel.

[On "All sweating comes out on the day of what it overcomes"] The day on which its qi dominates is the day of what it overcomes; when it dominates, it overcomes the evil; therefore each sweats on its dominant day.

## Notes

The symptoms, prognosis and needling of kidney febrile illness, and the principle of the day on which sweating occurs.
- The base text writes 巳 for the 己 in 気逆則戊己死 (the preceding 戊己甚 in the same unit has 己). The translation and quotations use 戊己.
- "The day of what it overcomes": Wang Bing takes it as the day on which the zang's qi dominates (for the liver, *jia* and *yi*).
