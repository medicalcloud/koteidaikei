# 素問 瘧論篇第三十五 §28 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Warm malaria is contracted by being struck by wind in winter. The cold qi is stored in the bones and marrow; in spring the yang qi breaks out greatly, but the evil qi cannot come out by itself. Then, on meeting great summer heat, the brain and marrow melt, the flesh wastes, the interstices open and leak, or when one exerts oneself, the evil qi comes out entirely with the sweat. This illness is stored in the kidney, and its qi first comes out from inside to the outside. In such cases yin is depleted and yang abundant; when yang is abundant there is heat. When it declines, the qi returns inward; when it enters, yang is depleted; when yang is depleted there is cold. Therefore there is first heat and then cold. It is called warm malaria."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Warm malaria ... its qi first comes out from inside to the outside"] The kidney governs winter, and winter governs the bones and marrow; the brain is the sea of marrow. Above and below correspond, and the reversal heat rises and fumigates; therefore the brain and marrow melt. When they melt, the hot qi presses outward; therefore the flesh diminishes, and the illness is stored in the kidney.

[On "In such cases yin is depleted and yang abundant; when yang is abundant there is heat"] Yin depletion means depletion of the qi of the kidney zang; yang abundance means abundance of the qi of the bladder greater yang.

[On "When it declines, the qi returns inward ... called warm malaria"] "Declines" means the illness declines and recedes. "Returns inward" means it enters the yin vessel of the kidney.

## Notes

In warm malaria, cold received in winter lies hidden in the kidney (bones and marrow) and breaks out from inside to outside, for example in the great heat of summer.
