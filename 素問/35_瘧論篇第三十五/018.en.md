# 素問 瘧論篇第三十五 §18 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The classic says, 'Do not needle blazing heat, do not needle a turbid pulse, do not needle dripping sweat,' because the illness is adverse and cannot yet be treated. When malaria first breaks out, yang qi combines into yin; at this time yang is depleted and yin abundant, there is no qi outside, and so first there is shivering with cold. When the yin qi has counterflowed to its extreme, it comes out again to yang; when yang and yin again combine outside, yin is depleted and yang replete, and so first there is heat and thirst. Malarial qi, when it combines into yang, makes yang prevail; when it combines into yin, makes yin prevail; when yin prevails there is cold, when yang prevails there is heat. Malaria is the irregularity of the qi of wind and cold; when the illness reaches its extreme, it comes again. When the illness breaks out, it is like the heat of fire, like wind and rain, and cannot be withstood. Therefore the classic says, 'Just at its height, [needling] will surely do damage; following its decline, the matter will surely prosper greatly' — this is what is meant. Before malaria breaks out, yin has not yet combined into yang and yang has not yet combined into yin; if one regulates it then, the true qi will be at peace and the evil qi will perish. Therefore the practitioner cannot treat it once it has broken out, because its qi is adverse."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The classic says, 'Do not needle blazing heat'"] Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition and the *Taisu* write 気 for 熱.

[On "do not needle a turbid pulse ... cannot yet be treated"] 熇熇 means intense heat; 渾渾 means without a thread to follow; 漉漉 means profuse sweating.

[On "When malaria first breaks out ... first there is heat and thirst"] When yin is abundant, the stomach is cold; therefore first there is shivering with cold. When yang is abundant, the stomach is hot; therefore first there is heat and the desire to drink.

[On "Malarial qi ... when the illness reaches its extreme, it comes again"] 復 means return to the former state: when its qi breaks out and reaches its extreme, it returns as before.

[On "Malaria is the irregularity of the qi of wind and cold ... it comes again"] Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* reads "Malaria is the irregular violent qi of wind and cold; when the illness reaches its extreme it comes again." Quan Yuanqi's edition and the *Taisu* read "Malaria is the qi of wind and cold; irregular; when the illness reaches its extreme it comes again," with 至 joined to the preceding phrase, differing from Mr Wang's understanding.

[On "When the illness breaks out ... cannot be withstood"] Because of its blazing intensity, it cannot be withstood.

[On "Therefore the classic says, 'Just at its height, [needling] will surely do damage'"] Song editors: The *Taisu* reads 勿敢必毀.

[On "following its decline, the matter will surely prosper greatly — this is what is meant"] 方 means just. Draining at the very height may harm the true qi; therefore it surely does damage. When the qi of the illness has declined, supplementing the channel qi makes the evil qi subside and the upright qi settle; therefore it surely prospers greatly.

[On "Before malaria breaks out ... the evil qi will perish"] What is drained is surely hit and what is supplemented surely suits; therefore the true qi is at peace and the evil qi perishes.

[On "Therefore the practitioner cannot treat it ... because its qi is adverse"] The true qi gradually ceases and the evil qi runs rampant; the true does not overcome the evil; this is adversity.

## Notes

During a malarial attack the qi is adverse, so one should not needle but regulate before or after the attack.
- 己 in Wang Bing's 病気衰己 means 已.
- On the division at 病極則復: Wang Bing ends his note at 病極則復, but according to the Song editors Quan Yuanqi's edition and the *Taisu* join 至 to the preceding phrase. The punctuation reads 病極則復至 while following the placement of the notes in the base text.
