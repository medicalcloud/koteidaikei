# 素問 瘧論篇第三十五 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Yin and yang contend above and below, deficiency and excess arise in turn, and yin and yang shift into each other. When yang combines into yin, yin is in excess and yang deficient. When the *Yangming* is deficient, there are shivering with cold and chattering jaw; when the greater yang is deficient, the lumbus, back, head and nape ache; when all three yang are deficient, yin qi prevails; when yin qi prevails, the bones are cold and ache; cold arises within, so inside and outside are all cold. When yang is abundant, the outside is hot; when yin is deficient, the inside is hot; when outside and inside are both hot, there are dyspnea and thirst, so one wants cold drinks. All this is contracted through harm from summer heat: the hot qi is abundant and stored inside the skin and outside the intestines and stomach, where the ying-nutrient qi resides. This makes the sweat pores open and the interstices loose. Then, meeting the autumn qi, sweating and encountering wind, or contracting it through bathing, water qi resides inside the skin and dwells together with the wei-defensive qi. The wei-defensive qi travels in yang by day and in yin by night. This qi comes out when it meets yang and presses inward when it meets yin; inside and outside press on each other; therefore it occurs daily."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Yin and yang contend above and below ... shift into each other"] Yang qi descends to its extreme and then rises; yin qi rises to its extreme and then descends; hence "yin and yang contend above and below". When yang is deficient the outside is cold; when yin is deficient the inside is hot; when yang is abundant the outside is hot; when yin is abundant the inside is cold. Thus as cold departs and heat arises, deficiency and excess arise in turn, and the qi of yin and yang shift and change.

[On "When yang combines into yin ... shivering with cold and chattering jaw"] "Yang combines into yin" means yang qi enters the yin division. The *Yangming* is the stomach vessel. The stomach vessel, after crossing at Chengjiang, divides back along the lower edge behind the chin and emerges at Daying; a branch descends from in front of Daying to Renying. Therefore when its qi is insufficient, one dislikes cold and shivers, and the chin and jaw shake.

[On "when the greater yang is deficient, the lumbus, back, head and nape ache"] The greater yang is the bladder vessel; it branches from the head down the nape, follows the inside of the shoulder blade, runs alongside the back and reaches the lumbus. Therefore when its qi is insufficient, the lumbus, back, head and nape ache.

[On "when all three yang are deficient ... so one wants cold drinks"] Heat harms qi; therefore when inside and outside are both hot, there are dyspnea and thirst.

[On "All this is contracted ... where the ying-nutrient qi resides"] The region outside the intestines and stomach is governed by the ying-nutrient qi; hence "where the ying-nutrient qi resides". 舎 means reside.

[On "This makes the sweat pores open"] Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition reads 汗出空疏; the *Jiayi jing* and *Taisu* are the same.

[On "Then, meeting the autumn qi ... it occurs daily"] 作 means break out.

## Notes

Explains the mechanism of malarial attacks through the contention of yin and yang and the circulation of the wei-defensive qi. Hot qi lodged from harm by summer heat and water qi (cold) entering in autumn provoke daily attacks in accordance with the day-and-night circulation of the wei-defensive qi.
