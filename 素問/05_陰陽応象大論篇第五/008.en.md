# 素問 陰陽応象大論篇第五 §8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Heaven has the four seasons and the five phases, by which there are generation, growth, gathering and storage, and by which cold, heat, dryness, dampness and wind arise. Humans have the five zang, which transform the five qi, by which joy, anger, grief, worry and fear arise.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Heaven has the four seasons and the five phases ... cold, heat, dryness, dampness and wind arise"] Generation in spring, growth in summer, gathering in autumn and storage in winter are the generation, growth, gathering and storage of the four seasons. The water of winter is cold, the fire of summer hot, the metal of autumn dry, the wood of spring wind, and the earth of long summer damp; these are the cold, heat, dampness, dryness and wind of the five phases. Among the qi of the four seasons, although earth flourishes as a lodger, if one traces what it governs, dampness belongs to the centre. Therefore it says that by the five phases the five qi of cold, heat, dryness, dampness and wind arise.

[On "Humans have the five zang ... joy, anger, grief, worry and fear arise"] The five zang are the liver, heart, spleen, lung and kidney; the five qi are joy, anger, grief, worry and fear. These five qi in turn harm the harmonious qi of the five zang.
Song editors: The *Tianyuan jidalun* (chapter 66) writes "thought" for "grief". Further, in the text below in this chapter, the emotion of the liver is anger, of the heart joy, of the spleen thought, of the lung worry, and of the kidney fear, while the *Yuji zhenzang lun* (chapter 19) writes "grief". The treatises differ. Huangfu Shi'an's *Jiayi jing*, chapter "Spirit and the Five Zang", sets out the explanation fully. Mentioning "grief" is because grief can overcome anger, taking the five emotions as overcoming one another in turn; mentioning "thought" is because thought is the emotion of the spleen. If only one is taken, the meaning is insufficient in either case; seen together, they complete each other's meaning.

## Notes

The correspondence of heaven and humans: the four seasons and five phases of heaven produce generation, growth, gathering and storage and the five qi (cold, heat, dryness, dampness, wind); the five zang of humans transform the five qi and produce the five emotions (joy, anger, grief, worry, fear).
- The Song editors discuss why one of the five emotions is "grief" in some chapters and "thought" in others, concluding that the two readings complete each other.
- 甲已経 in the Song editors' note is the base text's form (an error for 甲乙経).
