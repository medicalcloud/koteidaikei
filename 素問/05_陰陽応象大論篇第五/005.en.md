# 素問 陰陽応象大論篇第五 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Yin flavour goes out through the lower orifices; yang qi goes out through the upper orifices. What is thick in flavour is yin; what is thin [in flavour] is the yang within yin. What is thick in qi is yang; what is thin [in qi] is the yin within yang. If the flavour is thick, it causes discharge; if thin, it makes things pass. If the qi is thin, it causes release; if thick, it produces heat. The qi of vigorous fire declines; the qi of lesser fire grows vigorous. Vigorous fire feeds on qi; qi feeds on lesser fire. Vigorous fire scatters qi; lesser fire generates qi. Of qi and flavours, the acrid and sweet that disperse are yang; the sour and bitter that cause upwelling and discharge are yin.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Yin flavour goes out through the lower orifices; yang qi goes out through the upper orifices"] Flavour has substance, so it flows down to the orifices of evacuation; qi has no form, so it goes out above through the gates of breathing.

[On "What is thick in flavour is yin ... the yin within yang"] Yang is qi, and what is thick in qi is pure yang; yin is flavour, and what is thick in flavour is pure yin. Therefore what is thin in flavour is the yang within yin, and what is thin in qi is the yin within yang.

[On "If the flavour is thick, it causes discharge ... if thick, it produces heat"] Yin qi moistens and descends, so if the flavour is thick, there is discharge; yang qi blazes upward, so if the qi is thick, heat is produced. What is thin in flavour has little yin and therefore passes and drains; what is thin in qi has little yang and therefore produces sweating and release. "Release" means sweating.

[On "The qi of vigorous fire declines; the qi of lesser fire grows vigorous"] A fire that is vigorous must decline once its vigour is spent; a fire that is small grows vigorous once its smallness is past.

[On "Vigorous fire feeds on qi ... lesser fire generates qi"] Qi produces vigorous fire, hence "vigorous fire feeds on qi"; lesser fire nourishes qi, hence "qi feeds on lesser fire". Because vigorous fire feeds on qi, when qi meets vigorous fire it is consumed and scattered; because lesser fire benefits qi, when qi meets lesser fire it is generated and grows. The vigour and smallness of a person's yang qi are likewise.

[On "Of qi and flavours, the acrid and sweet that disperse are yang; the sour and bitter that cause upwelling and discharge are yin"] It is not only that qi and flavour divide yin and yang correctly; among acrid, sweet, sour and bitter there are also different qi of yin and yang. Why? Acrid disperses and sweet relaxes, so what disperses is yang; sour contracts and bitter drains, so what causes upwelling and discharge is yin.

## Notes

The yin-yang distinctions of the qi and flavour of drugs and foods by their thickness and thinness, and their actions. The doctrines of "vigorous fire" and "lesser fire" and the assignment of acrid and sweet to yang and sour and bitter to yin became fundamental to later materia medica.
- "The qi of vigorous fire declines; the qi of lesser fire grows vigorous": Wang Bing explains it as the principle of the rise and fall of fire itself, and says a person's yang qi is likewise.
- In Wang Bing's note the base text writes 巳 in 壮巳 and 少巳; it is translated as 已.
- Daizhige (*Huangdi neijing suwen*) writes 濃 for 厚 in some places.
