# 素問 陰陽応象大論篇第五 §9 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Therefore joy and anger harm qi; cold and heat harm form. Sudden anger harms yin; sudden joy harms yang. Reversed qi moves upward, fills the vessels and departs from the form. If joy and anger are without measure and cold and heat exceed their degree, life is not secure. Therefore redoubled yin must become yang, and redoubled yang must become yin.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Therefore joy and anger harm qi; cold and heat harm form"] Joy and anger all arise from qi, hence "joy and anger harm qi"; what cold and heat overcome is always the form, hence "cold and heat harm form". Taking it broadly, this is so; in finer terms, heat harms qi and cold harms form.

[On "Sudden anger harms yin; sudden joy harms yang"] In anger the qi rises; in joy the qi descends. Therefore a sudden rise of qi harms yin, and a sudden descent of qi harms yang.

[On "Reversed qi moves upward, fills the vessels and departs from the form"] 厥 means counterflow. When counterflowing qi moves upward and fills the channels and network vessels, the spirit qi floats away and departs from the body.

[On "If joy and anger are without measure ... life is not secure"] The *Lingshu jing* says: "The wise in nourishing life always follow the four seasons and adapt to cold and heat, harmonize joy and anger and keep their dwelling at peace." If joy and anger are inconstant and cold and heat exceed their degree, how can the qi of heavenly truth last long?

[On "Therefore redoubled yin must become yang, and redoubled yang must become yin"] It says that harm from cold and harm from heat are likewise.

## Notes

Joy and anger harm qi, cold and heat harm form, and sudden emotions harm yin and yang. If emotions and cold and heat lack measure, life is not secure; yin and yang at their extremes turn into each other.
- "Redoubled yin must become yang": Wang Bing says harm from cold and from heat follow the same pattern; this leads into the next unit ("If harmed by cold in winter, there will surely be a warm disease in spring", etc.).
