# 素問 陰陽応象大論篇第五 §15 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The west generates dryness; dryness generates metal; metal generates acridity; acridity generates the lung; the lung generates the skin and body hair; the skin and body hair generate the kidney. The lung governs the nose. In heaven it is dryness; on earth, metal; in the body, the skin and body hair; among the zang, the lung; among colours, white; among notes, *shang*; among voices, wailing; among changes, coughing; among orifices, the nose; among flavours, acrid; among emotions, worry. Worry harms the lung; joy overcomes worry. Heat harms the skin and body hair; cold overcomes heat. Acridity harms the skin and body hair; bitterness overcomes acridity.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The west generates dryness"] The qi of heaven is brisk and sharp; therefore it generates dryness.

[On "dryness generates metal"] Metal is dry and resonant; this is [dryness] generating metal.

[On "metal generates acridity"] All things acrid in flavour are produced by the qi of metal. The "Hongfan" of the *Shangshu* says: "Yielding and changing makes acridity."

[On "acridity generates the lung"] All that is acrid first produces and grows in the lung.

[On "the lung generates the skin and body hair"] The essential qi of the lung produces and nourishes the skin and body hair.

[On "the skin and body hair generate the kidney"] The book of yin and yang says, "Metal generates water." When the metal qi of the lung has nourished the skin and body hair, it then generates kidney-water.

[On "The lung governs the nose"] The lung stores qi and the nose lets breath through; therefore it governs the nose.

[On "In heaven it is dryness"] Light, brisk, vigorous and strong — these are the functions of dryness.

[On "on earth, metal"] Hard and vigorous, yielding and changing — this is the nature of metal.

[On "in the body, the skin and body hair"] They wrap and store the skin and interstices and ward off evil.

[On "among the zang, the lung"] Its spirit is the *po*. The *Daojing yi* says: "The *po* is in the lung; when the *po* is at peace, virtue is cultivated and life is extended."

[On "among colours, white"] It resembles the colour of metal.

[On "among notes, *shang*"] *Shang* is the sound of metal, light and vigorous. The "Yueji" says: "When *shang* is disordered there is perversity, and its officials are corrupt."

[On "among voices, wailing"] Wailing is the voice of sorrow.

[On "among changes, coughing"] 咳 means coughing, by which the throat is cleared.

[On "among orifices, the nose"] The nose governs smelling and breathing.

[On "among flavours, acrid"] Acrid can be used to disperse and moisten.

[On "among emotions, worry"] Worry is deep concern.

[On "Worry harms the lung"] Though its emotion is worry, in excess it does damage.

[On "joy overcomes worry"] In joy, the fire of the heart combines with the metal of the lung; therefore it overcomes worry. The *Xuanming wuqi* (chapter 23) says: "When the essential qi combines in the heart, there is joy."

[On "Heat harms the skin and body hair"] Because heat arises from fire and consumes the fluids.

[On "cold overcomes heat"] Yin restrains yang.
Song editors: The *Taisu* reads "Dryness harms the skin and body hair; heat overcomes dryness." Further, Wang Bing's note to the *Wuyunxing dalun* (chapter 67) says, "Fire has two kinds"; therefore the signs of harm by heat are mentioned again here.

[On "Acridity harms the skin and body hair"] Excess invites damage.

[On "bitterness overcomes acridity"] Bitter is the flavour of fire; therefore it overcomes the acridity of metal.

## Notes

The correspondences of the west: west, dryness, metal, acrid, lung, skin and body hair; the colour white, the note *shang*, the voice of wailing, the change of coughing, the orifice of the nose, the emotion of worry; and harm and victory.
- "Heat harms the skin and body hair; cold overcomes heat": by the pattern of the other directions one would expect "dryness harms the skin and body hair; heat overcomes dryness", and according to the Song editors the *Taisu* reads so. The Song editors explain the mention of heat by Wang Bing's statement in his note to the *Wuyunxing dalun* that "fire has two kinds".
- Daizhige (the plain text) writes 在 for 生 in "the skin and body hair generate the kidney".
