# 素問 陰陽応象大論篇第五 §26 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Therefore evil wind arrives as swiftly as wind and rain. Therefore those good at treatment treat the skin and body hair; next, the flesh and skin; next, the sinews and vessels; next, the six fu; next, the five zang. When one treats the five zang, half die and half live. Therefore the evil qi of heaven, when one is affected by it, harms the five zang; the cold and heat of water and grain, when one is affected by them, harm the six fu; the damp qi of earth, when one is affected by it, harms the skin, flesh, sinews and vessels.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Therefore evil wind arrives as swiftly as wind and rain"] "Arrives" means reaches the body.

[On "Therefore those good at treatment treat the skin and body hair"] They stop it at the sprouting.

[On "next, the flesh and skin"] They rescue what has already arisen.

[On "next, the sinews and vessels"] They attack what has already become ill.

[On "next, the six fu"] They treat what is already severe.

[On "next, the five zang. When one treats the five zang, half die and half live"] They treat what is already formed. Shennong says: "When the momentum of an illness has formed, half can be cured." What has just formed can be cured; what has long been fixed cuts down the body. Therefore when one treats the five zang, half live and half die.

[On "Therefore the evil qi of heaven ... harms the five zang"] The qi of the four seasons and the winds of the eight directions are all evils of heaven. The *Jingui zhenyan lun* (chapter 4) says: "The eight winds issue evils which become channel winds; they touch the five zang, and the evil qi produces illness." Therefore the evil qi of heaven, when one is affected by it, harms the five zang.

[On "the cold and heat of water and grain ... harm the six fu"] Heat harms the stomach and bladder; cold harms the qi of the intestines and gallbladder.

[On "the damp qi of earth ... harms the skin, flesh, sinews and vessels"] When damp qi prevails, the nutritive and defensive qi do not circulate; therefore when one is affected by it, it harms the skin, flesh, sinews and vessels.

## Notes

Evil wind penetrates swiftly and deeply, so those good at treatment treat it while it is shallow, in the skin and body hair; once it reaches the five zang, half die. Further, the evil qi of heaven harms the five zang, the cold and heat of food and drink harm the six fu, and the damp qi of earth harms the skin, flesh, sinews and vessels.
- Wang Bing paraphrases each stage from the skin to the five zang ("stop it at the sprouting", "rescue what has already arisen", and so on).
- In Wang Bing's notes the base text writes 巳 in 巳甚 and 巳成; it is translated as 已.
