# 素問 陰陽応象大論篇第五 §16 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The north generates cold; cold generates water; water generates saltiness; saltiness generates the kidney; the kidney generates the bones and marrow; the marrow generates the liver. The kidney governs the ears. In heaven it is cold; on earth, water; in the body, the bones; among the zang, the kidney; among colours, black; among notes, *yu*; among voices, groaning; among changes, shivering; among orifices, the ears; among flavours, salty; among emotions, fear. Fear harms the kidney; thought overcomes fear. Cold harms the blood; dryness overcomes cold. Saltiness harms the blood; sweetness overcomes saltiness.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The north generates cold"] The yin qi congeals and is icy; therefore it generates cold.

[On "cold generates water"] When cold qi is abundant and congeals, it changes into water.

[On "water generates saltiness"] All things salty in flavour are produced by the qi of water. The "Hongfan" of the *Shangshu* says: "Moistening and descending makes saltiness."

[On "saltiness generates the kidney"] All that is salty produces and grows in the kidney.

[On "the kidney generates the bones and marrow"] The essential qi of the kidney produces and nourishes the bones and marrow.

[On "the marrow generates the liver"] The book of yin and yang says, "Water generates wood." When the water qi of the kidney has nourished the bones and marrow, it then generates liver-wood.

[On "The kidney governs the ears"] The kidney belongs to the north and is situated in the dark and hidden; because sound enters [there], it governs the ears.

[On "In heaven it is cold"] Congealing, clear, harsh and icy — these are the functions of cold.

[On "on earth, water"] Clear, pure, moistening and descending — these are the functions of water.

[On "in the body, the bones"] Upright, straight and firm, they form the trunk by which the body stands.

[On "among the zang, the kidney"] Its spirit is the will. The *Daojing yi* says: "The will is stored in the kidney; when the will is active, the bones and marrow are full and replete."

[On "among colours, black"] It resembles the colour of water.

[On "among notes, *yu*"] *Yu* is the note of water, deep and profound. The "Yueji" says: "When *yu* is disordered there is danger, and its wealth is exhausted."

[On "among voices, groaning"] It is the voice of moaning.

[On "among changes, shivering"] 慄 means trembling; severe cold and great fear both have it.

[On "among orifices, the ears"] The ears govern hearing the five notes.
Song editors: The *Jingui zhenyan lun* (chapter 4) says it "opens its orifice in the two yin"; this differs because the heart lodges its orifice in the ears.

[On "among flavours, salty"] Salty can be used to soften.

[On "among emotions, fear"] Fear is that by which one dreads evil.

[On "Fear harms the kidney"] If fear does not stop, it affects the kidney within; therefore it harms. The *Lingshu jing* says, "Fear and dread that are not resolved harm the essence," which makes clear that it affects the kidney.

[On "thought overcomes fear"] When thought is deep and consideration far-reaching, one sees the source of things; therefore it overcomes fear.

[On "Cold harms the blood"] With cold the blood congeals; that it harms is clear.
Song editors: The *Taisu* writes "bones" for "blood".

[On "dryness overcomes cold"] Dryness arises from heat; therefore it overcomes cold.
Song editors: The *Taisu* writes "dampness" for "dryness".

[On "Saltiness harms the blood"] Eating salty things causes thirst; that it harms the blood is clear.
Song editors: The *Taisu* writes "bones" for "blood".

[On "sweetness overcomes saltiness"] Sweet is [the flavour of] earth; therefore it overcomes the saltiness of water.
Song editors: From "Qibo replied" above to here is the same as the *Wuyunxing dalun* (chapter 67). The two sets of notes differ considerably and should be used together.

## Notes

The correspondences of the north: north, cold, water, salty, kidney, bones and marrow; the colour black, the note *yu*, the voice of groaning, the change of shivering, the orifice of the ears, the emotion of fear; and harm and victory. This completes the correspondences of the five directions.
- "Among orifices, the ears": the *Jingui zhenyan lun* (chapter 4) gives the two yin as the orifices of the kidney. The Song editors explain the difference by the heart lodging its orifice in the ears (the view that the ears are the orifice of the heart).
- "Cold harms the blood", "saltiness harms the blood": according to the Song editors, the *Taisu* writes "bones" for "blood" and "dampness" for "dryness" in "dryness overcomes cold".
- In Wang Bing's note the base text writes 巳 in 養骨髄巳; it is translated as 已. His phrase 甘土土 is translated as it stands.
- According to the Song editors, the text from unit 012 to here is the same as in the *Wuyunxing dalun* (chapter 67).
