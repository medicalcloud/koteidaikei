# 素問 陰陽応象大論篇第五 §14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The centre generates dampness; dampness generates earth; earth generates sweetness; sweetness generates the spleen; the spleen generates the flesh; the flesh generates the lung. The spleen governs the mouth. In heaven it is dampness; on earth, earth; in the body, the flesh; among the zang, the spleen; among colours, yellow; among notes, *gong*; among voices, singing; among changes, retching; among orifices, the mouth; among flavours, sweet; among emotions, thought. Thought harms the spleen; anger overcomes thought. Dampness harms the flesh; wind overcomes dampness. Sweetness harms the flesh; sourness overcomes sweetness.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The centre generates dampness"] The yang qi presses abundantly and the yin qi rises firmly; rising and pressing combine, and so dampness arises. The *Yiyi* says: "Yang rises and presses on yin; yin is able to hold it firm, and then it steams and becomes rain." This makes clear that dampness arises from the qi of firm yin.
Song editors: Yang Shangshan says: "In the sixth month, four yang and two yin combine and steam, producing damp qi."

[On "dampness generates earth"] When earth is damp it becomes firm; this clarifies how dampness arises.
Song editors: Yang Shangshan says: "Four yang and two yin combine to make dampness, which steams and rots the myriad things and forms earth."

[On "earth generates sweetness"] All things sweet in flavour are produced by the qi of earth. The "Hongfan" of the *Shangshu* says: "Sowing and reaping make sweetness."

[On "sweetness generates the spleen"] All that is sweet first produces and grows in the spleen.

[On "the spleen generates the flesh"] The essential qi of the spleen produces and nourishes the flesh.

[On "the flesh generates the lung"] The book of yin and yang says, "Earth generates metal." When the earth qi of the spleen has nourished the flesh within, it then generates lung-metal.

[On "The spleen governs the mouth"] The spleen receives water and grain, and the mouth takes in the five flavours; therefore it governs the mouth.

[On "In heaven it is dampness"] Fog, dew, cloud and rain are the functions of dampness.

[On "on earth, earth"] Calmness, stillness, sowing and reaping are the virtue of earth.

[On "in the body, the flesh"] It covers and wraps the sinews and bones and fills out the form.

[On "among the zang, the spleen"] Its spirit is intention. The *Daojing yi* says: "Intention lodges in the spleen; when intention is at peace, wisdom does not scatter."

[On "among colours, yellow"] It resembles the colour of earth.

[On "among notes, *gong*"] *Gong* is the note of earth, large and harmonious. The "Yueji" says: "When *gong* is disordered there is waste, and the ruler is arrogant."

[On "among voices, singing"] Singing is the voice of lament.

[On "among changes, retching"] 噦 means hiccup and belching, produced by cold in the stomach.
Song editors: Wang Bing calls 噦 hiccup and belching, but belching is not 噦. Yang Shangshan says: "噦 is qi running counter."

[On "among orifices, the mouth"] The mouth governs taking in water and grain.

[On "among flavours, sweet"] Sweet can be used to relax and slow.

[On "among emotions, thought"] Thought is that by which one knows what is distant.

[On "Thought harms the spleen"] Though its emotion is thought, in excess it harms itself.

[On "anger overcomes thought"] When angry one does not think; that it overcomes is clear.

[On "Dampness harms the flesh"] The spleen governs the flesh and dislikes dampness; therefore when dampness prevails, the flesh is harmed.

[On "wind overcomes dampness"] Wind is the qi of wood; therefore it overcomes the dampness of earth.

[On "Sweetness harms the flesh"] This too is exceeding measure.
Song editors: The *Wuyunxing dalun* (chapter 67) says: "Sweetness harms the spleen."

[On "sourness overcomes sweetness"] Sour is the flavour of wood; therefore it overcomes the sweetness of earth.

## Notes

The correspondences of the centre: centre, dampness, earth, sweet, spleen, flesh; the colour yellow, the note *gong*, the voice of singing, the change of retching, the orifice of the mouth, the emotion of thought; and harm and victory by the controlling cycle.
- "Among changes, retching" (噦): Wang Bing explains it as hiccup and belching, but the Song editors say belching is not 噦 and cite Yang Shangshan: "qi running counter".
- "The centre generates dampness": Wang Bing and Yang Shangshan (cited by the Song editors) explain that dampness arises from yang and yin pressing together and steaming.
