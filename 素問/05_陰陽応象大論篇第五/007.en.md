# 素問 陰陽応象大論篇第五 §7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Therefore when there is pain first and swelling afterwards, qi has harmed form; when there is swelling first and pain afterwards, form has harmed qi. When wind prevails, there is movement; when heat prevails, there is swelling; when dryness prevails, there is drying; when cold prevails, there is puffiness; when dampness prevails, there is soggy diarrhoea.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Therefore when there is pain first ... form has harmed qi"] When there are first signs in the qi and then the form is ill, it is called qi harming form; when there are first signs in the form and then the qi is ill, it is called form harming qi.

[On "When wind prevails, there is movement"] When wind prevails, all things sway; hence there is movement.
Song editors: The *Zuozhuan* says, "When wind is excessive, there is illness of the extremities" — this is the meaning.

[On "when heat prevails, there is swelling"] When heat prevails, the yang qi stagnates within; therefore great swellings arise suddenly, and in severe cases the nutritive qi runs counter in the flesh and gathers into abscess swellings with pus.

[On "when dryness prevails, there is drying"] When dryness prevails, the fluids are exhausted and dried up; therefore the skin is dry.

[On "when cold prevails, there is puffiness"] When cold prevails, the yin qi binds in the dark mansions; the dark mansions close tightly and the yang qi attacks within; therefore there is puffiness.

[On "when dampness prevails, there is soggy diarrhoea"] When dampness prevails, it attacks the spleen and stomach within; when the spleen and stomach receive dampness, water and grain are not separated but mixed together, so the large intestine conveys them and pours them out as diarrhoea. Because dampness is abundant within and causes diarrhoea, it is called soggy diarrhoea.
Song editors: The *Zuozhuan* says, "When rain is excessive, there is illness of the abdomen" — this is the meaning. The five phrases from "when wind prevails, there is movement" to here repeat the text of the *Tianyuan jidalun* (chapter 66), where the notes are quite detailed.

## Notes

Distinguishing harm to qi and to form by whether pain or swelling comes first, and the signs when each of the five qi (wind, heat, dryness, cold, dampness) prevails.
- "When cold prevails, there is puffiness": Wang Bing explains that cold closes the dark mansions and the yang qi attacks within, causing puffiness.
- According to the Song editors, the five phrases from "when wind prevails" to "when dampness prevails" repeat the *Tianyuan jidalun* (chapter 66), where the notes are more detailed.
