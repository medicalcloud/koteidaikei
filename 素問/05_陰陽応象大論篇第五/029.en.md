# 素問 陰陽応象大論篇第五 §29 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Therefore it is said: when an illness first arises, it can be needled and ended; when it is abundant, one can wait for it to decline and then end it. Therefore, according to its lightness, raise it up; according to its heaviness, reduce it; according to its decline, make [the genuine qi] manifest. Where the form is insufficient, warm it with qi; where the essence is insufficient, supplement it with flavors. What is high, take advantage of it and let it go over; what is low, draw it out and exhaust it; fullness in the center, drain it within. Where there is pathogen, soak the body to produce sweat; what is in the skin, sweat and release it; what is swift and fierce, press and contain it; what is in excess, disperse and drain it. Examine its yin and yang so as to distinguish soft and hard; for yang illness treat yin, for yin illness treat yang. Settle the blood and qi so that each keeps to its home. When the blood is in excess, it should be let; when the qi is deficient, it should be guided."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "when an illness first arises, it can be needled and ended"] Because it is light and slight.

[On "when it is abundant, one can wait for it to decline and then end it"] If one takes it when the illness is abundant, one damages the genuine qi; therefore when it is abundant, one must wait for it to decline.

[On "Therefore, according to its lightness, raise it up"] What is light, if raised and released, the pathogen departs.

[On "according to its heaviness, reduce it"] What is heavy is moderated, reduced and removed.

[On "according to its decline, make [the genuine qi] manifest"] If one attacks according to the decline of the illness qi and makes the pathogen depart, the genuine qi becomes firm and the complexion becomes bright.

[On "Where the form is insufficient, warm it with qi; where the essence is insufficient, supplement it with flavors"] "Qi" means the wei-defensive qi; "flavors" means the flavors of the five zang. The *Lingshu jing* says: "The wei-defensive qi warms the parted flesh, fills the skin, fattens the interstices and governs opening and closing." Therefore when the wei-defensive qi is warm, the portion of the form is sufficient. The *Shanggu tianzhen lun* (chapter 1) says: "The kidney governs water; it receives the essence of the five zang and six fu and stores it, so only when the five zang are exuberant can it discharge." By this, where the essence is insufficient, one supplements the flavors of the five zang.

[On "What is high, take advantage of it and let it go over"] 越 means to go over and raise.

[On "what is low, draw it out and exhaust it"] 引 means to drain and draw.

[On "fullness in the center, drain it within"] "Within" means within the abdomen.

[On "Where there is pathogen, soak the body to produce sweat"] "Pathogen" means the qi of pathogenic wind; when wind strikes the exterior, one makes it sweat and releases it.

[On "what is in the skin, sweat and release it"] Because it is outside, it is released by sweating.

[On "what is swift and fierce, press and contain it"] 慓 means swift; 悍 means sharp. When the qi is swift and sharp, one presses it so that it is gathered in.

[On "what is in excess, disperse and drain it"] When yang is in excess, disperse; when yin is in excess, open and drain. Hence the text below.

[On "Examine its yin and yang so as to distinguish soft and hard"] Yin is called soft and yang hard.

[On "for yang illness treat yin, for yin illness treat yang"] This is what is called "drawing yang from yin and yin from yang, treating the left by the right and the right by the left".

[On "Settle the blood and qi so that each keeps to its home"] "Home" means the position of the qi of its own meridian.

[On "When the blood is in excess, it should be let"] 決 means to break open and let the blood.

[On "when the qi is deficient, it should be guided"] 𤙲 is read as 導 "guide". With guiding, the qi moves freely and smoothly.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 掣 for 𤙲.

## Notes

A summary of the principles of treatment: needle an illness at its outset, and wait for it to decline when it is abundant. Methods are listed according to the lightness or heaviness, abundance or decline of the illness, its location (high, low, center, skin) and the nature of the pathogen (letting it go over, exhausting, draining, sweating, pressing, dispersing); one must examine yin and yang, treating yin for yang illness and yang for yin illness. The chapter ends here.
- "What is high, let it go over; what is low, draw it out and exhaust it" was later cited as the basis of emetic and purgative methods.
- 𤙲 in "when the qi is deficient, it should be guided" was supplied from the Daozang edition where it was lost in the transcription (see the 【補】 note); Wang Bing reads it as 導.
- 莭 in Wang Bing's 莭減 is the base text's form, translated as 節.
- Qibo's speech, begun in unit 024, ends here.
