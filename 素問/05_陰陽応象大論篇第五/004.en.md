# 素問 陰陽応象大論篇第五 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Water is yin and fire is yang. Yang is qi and yin is flavor. Flavor returns to form; form returns to qi; qi returns to essence; essence returns to transformation. Essence feeds on qi; form feeds on flavor. Transformation generates essence; qi generates form. Flavor harms form; qi harms essence. Essence transforms into qi; qi is harmed by flavor.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Water is yin and fire is yang"] Water is cold and still, so it is yin; fire is hot and restless, so it is yang.

[On "Yang is qi and yin is flavor"] Qi only disperses and spreads, so yang is it; flavor is said to follow form, so yin is it.

[On "Flavor returns to form ... essence returns to transformation"] Form feeds on flavor, so flavor returns to form; qi nourishes form, so form returns to qi; essence feeds on qi, so qi returns to essence; transformation generates essence, so essence returns to transformation. Hence the text below says:

[On "Essence feeds on qi; form feeds on flavor"] When qi transforms, essence arises; when flavor is harmonious, form grows. Therefore it says "feeds on" them.

[On "Transformation generates essence; qi generates form"] The fluid of subtle essence is formed only by the transformation of blood; that there is form and substance depends on the movement of qi and the establishment of the ying-nutrient. Thus these two each serve life.

[On "Flavor harms form; qi harms essence"] [This is when] they exceed their measure.

[On "Essence transforms into qi; qi is harmed by flavor"] When essence receives transformation and is nourished, it feeds on qi; if essence transforms to generate [new life], it does not feed on qi. When essence and blood bind within, stagnate and become foul and rotten and attack the stomach, the five flavors are refused and cannot enter. When a woman is pregnant, for a hundred days after the essence transforms, she is always harmed by flavors.

## Notes

The mutual relations of qi and flavor, form and essence: flavor nourishes form and qi nourishes essence, but in excess they harm them.
- This passage is difficult and read in various ways; the translation renders it literally in order and follows Wang Bing.
- The end of Wang Bing's note refers to nausea in early pregnancy ("for a hundred days after the essence transforms, she is always harmed by flavors").
- Wang Bing's phrase 味曰従形 is translated as it stands; the Siku quanshu edition (Daizhige *Huangdi neijing suwen*) has the same characters. 曰 may be an error (e.g. for 由), but this has not been verified.
