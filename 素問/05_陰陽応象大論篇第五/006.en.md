# 素問 陰陽応象大論篇第五 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When yin prevails, yang is ill; when yang prevails, yin is ill. When yang prevails, there is heat; when yin prevails, there is cold. Redoubled cold becomes heat; redoubled heat becomes cold. Cold harms form; heat harms qi. When qi is harmed, there is pain; when form is harmed, there is swelling.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When yin prevails, yang is ill; when yang prevails, yin is ill"] What prevails is not ill; what does not prevail is ill.

[On "When yang prevails, there is heat; when yin prevails, there is cold"] This comes from excess.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* reads "When yin is ill there is heat; when yang is ill there is cold." The wording differs but the meaning is the same.

[On "Redoubled cold becomes heat; redoubled heat becomes cold"] Things at their extreme turn back, just as the qi of vigorous fire declines and the qi of lesser fire grows vigorous.

[On "Cold harms form; heat harms qi"] With cold, the defensive qi does not flow freely, so form is harmed; with heat, the nutritive qi is consumed within, so qi is harmed. Although yin completes form and yang transforms qi, once they exceed their measure, form and qi are harmed.

[On "When qi is harmed, there is pain; when form is harmed, there is swelling"] When qi is harmed, heat binds in the divisions of the flesh, so there is pain; when form is harmed, cold presses on the skin and interstices, so there is swelling.

## Notes

The prevailing of yin or yang, cold and heat, the harm they do to form and qi, and the resulting signs (pain and swelling).
- "When yang prevails, there is heat; when yin prevails, there is cold": according to the Song editors, the *Jiayi jing* reads "when yin is ill there is heat; when yang is ill there is cold" (same meaning).
