# 素問 陰陽応象大論篇第五 §22 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "If one can know the seven losses and eight gains, the two can be regulated; if one does not know how to use this, it is the point of early decline. At forty, the yin qi is naturally halved and one's daily activity declines. At fifty, the body is heavy and the ears and eyes are no longer keen. At sixty, there is impotence, the qi greatly declines, the nine orifices do not function well, there is depletion below and repletion above, and snivel and tears come out together. Therefore it is said: knowing it, one is strong; not knowing it, one grows old. Thus they come from the same source but have different names. The wise examine what is the same; the foolish examine what is different. The foolish have insufficiency; the wise have surplus. With surplus, the ears and eyes are keen, the body light and strong, the old become vigorous again and the vigorous are better ordered still. Therefore the sages do the work of non-action, delight in the capacity of tranquillity, and follow their desires and please their will within the guard of emptiness; thus their life span is without end and finishes only with heaven and earth. This is how the sages govern the body. Heaven is insufficient in the north-west, so the north-west is yin, and in people the right ear and eye are not as clear as the left. Earth is not full in the south-east, so the south-east is yang, and in people the left hand and foot are not as strong as the right."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "If one can know the seven losses and eight gains ... the point of early decline"] "Use" means the bedchamber. For women seven times seven is the end of the *tiangui*; for men eight times eight is its limit. If one knows that eight can be increased and seven can be decreased, each can cultivate the heavenly truth according to the portion of qi, live out the heavenly years and pass a hundred. The *Shanggu tianzhen lun* (chapter 1) says: "In a girl, at twice seven the *tiangui* arrives and the menses descend at their times; in a man, at twice eight the *tiangui* arrives and the essential qi overflows and is discharged." If the yin seven is to be decreased, the sea fills and blood descends of itself; if the yang eight is to be increased, there is union and the essence is discharged. From this the principle of the seven losses and eight gains can be known.

[On "At forty, the yin qi is naturally halved and one's daily activity declines"] Because of inner depletion the yin decreases, and because of inner dryness strength begins to decline. The *Lingshu jing* says: "At forty a person's interstices begin to loosen, the bloom gradually fades and the hair turns grey." Speaking of this stage, it too is the sequence of the decline of daily activity.

[On "At fifty, the body is heavy and the ears and eyes are no longer keen"] This is the gradual progress of decline.

[On "At sixty ... snivel and tears come out together"] This is severe decline.

[On "knowing it, one is strong; not knowing it, one grows old"] "Knowing" means knowing the way of preserving the body and keeping the nature intact through the seven losses and eight gains.

[On "Thus they come from the same source but have different names"] "The same" means the same in liking and desire; "different" means the different names of old and vigorous.

[On "The wise examine what is the same; the foolish examine what is different"] The wise examine the realm of shared desire and can attend to nature and the Way; the foolish imitate only after seeing differences in form and appearance. Following one's own nature, the Way grows and there is surplus; imitating, there is insufficiency in governing life. Hence the text below says:

[On "The foolish have insufficiency; the wise have surplus"] Acting first, there is surplus; learning afterwards, there is insufficiency.

[On "With surplus ... the vigorous are better ordered still"] Preserving the nature and keeping the body whole come about through knowing the Way. Therefore it is said: "The Way cannot be departed from for a moment; what can be departed from is not the Way." This is what is meant.

[On "Therefore the sages do the work of non-action ... This is how the sages govern the body"] The sages do not do what is useless to harm what is useful, and do not harm their nature but follow it; therefore their life span is long and ends only with heaven and earth. Gengsang Chu says: "As to sounds, colours and flavours, the sage takes them if they benefit his nature and discards them if they harm it. This is the way of keeping the nature whole." The *Shu* says: "Do not do what is useless to harm what is useful."

[On "Heaven is insufficient in the north-west ... not as clear as the left"] They are above, so they model themselves on heaven.

[On "Earth is not full in the south-east ... not as strong as the right"] They are below, so they model themselves on earth.

## Notes

The key to regulating yin and yang lies in knowing the "seven losses and eight gains"; without it one declines at forty, fifty and sixty. The sages live out their span through non-action and tranquillity. The passage also correlates the deficiency of heaven in the north-west and of earth in the south-east with the relative strength of the left and right sides of the body.
- "Seven losses and eight gains": Wang Bing relates it to sexual practice, linking it with the numbers seven for women and eight for men (chapter 1). The phrase has been interpreted in many ways since antiquity.
- "Come from the same source but have different names": Wang Bing explains that liking and desire are the same, while the names "old" and "vigorous" differ.
- "Heaven is insufficient in the north-west ... earth is not full in the south-east": the tilt of heaven and earth is matched with the strength of the left and right sides of the body.
- 嗞味 in Wang Bing's note is the base text's form, translated as 滋味.
