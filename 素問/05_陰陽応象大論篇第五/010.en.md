# 素問 陰陽応象大論篇第五 §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Therefore it is said: if harmed by cold in winter, there will surely be warm disease in spring; if harmed by wind in spring, undigested diarrhoea arises in summer; if harmed by summer heat in summer, there will surely be intermittent fever in autumn; if harmed by dampness in autumn, coughing arises in winter."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "if harmed by cold in winter, there will surely be warm disease in spring"] Harm from the qi of any of the four seasons can cause illness. That which is harmed by cold and becomes toxic is the most deadly and severe qi; those struck by it fall ill at once, and this is called cold damage. In those who do not fall ill at once, the cold toxin is stored in the flesh and skin; in spring it changes into warm disease, and in summer into summer-heat disease. Therefore those who nourish life must be careful not to be harmed by evils.

[On "if harmed by wind in spring, undigested diarrhoea arises in summer"] When wind strikes the exterior, it corresponds within to the liver, and the liver qi encroaches on the spleen; therefore there is undigested diarrhoea.
Song editors: The *Shengqi tongtian lun* (chapter 3) says: "If one is harmed by wind in spring and the evil qi lingers, there is outpouring diarrhoea."

[On "if harmed by summer heat in summer, there will surely be intermittent fever in autumn"] When the summer heat has already been severe and the autumn heat is again vigorous, the two heats attack each other; therefore there is intermittent fever. 痎 means emaciated.

[On "if harmed by dampness in autumn, coughing arises in winter"] When the autumn dampness has already been plentiful and the winter water is dominant again, water and dampness combine and the lung qi also weakens; therefore when the winter cold is severe, there is coughing.
Song editors: The *Shengqi tongtian lun* (chapter 3) says: "If harmed by dampness in autumn, [qi] rebels upward with coughing and breaks out as limpness and reversal."

## Notes

Being harmed by the evil of one season leads to illness in the next. Huangdi's speech, begun in unit 001, ends here.
- Wang Bing's note on "harmed by cold in winter" distinguishes cold damage, which falls ill at once, from cases in which the cold toxin lies hidden and becomes warm disease in spring or summer-heat disease in summer; this is close to the view in the preface-examples of the *Shanghan lun*.
- Almost the same text appears in the *Shengqi tongtian lun* (chapter 3, unit 015).
