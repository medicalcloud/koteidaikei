# 素問 陰陽応象大論篇第五 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Therefore clear yang becomes heaven and turbid yin becomes earth. The earth qi rises to become clouds, and the heavenly qi descends to become rain; rain comes from the earth qi, and clouds come from the heavenly qi. Therefore clear yang goes out through the upper orifices and turbid yin through the lower orifices; clear yang issues through the interstices and turbid yin goes to the five zang; clear yang fills the four limbs and turbid yin returns to the six fu.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The earth qi rises to become clouds ... clouds come from the heavenly qi"] When yin congeals and binds above, it combines to form clouds; when yang disperses and flows downward, it pours out as rain. Because rain is bestowed and transformed through clouds, it says rain comes from the earth; because clouds rely on qi to intermingle, it says clouds come from heaven. The principle of heaven and earth is so, and the clear and turbid in the human body are likewise.

[On "Therefore clear yang goes out through the upper orifices and turbid yin through the lower orifices"] What is based in heaven draws near to what is above; what is based in earth draws near to what is below; each follows its kind. The upper orifices are the ears, eyes, nose and mouth; the lower orifices are the front and rear yin.

[On "clear yang issues through the interstices and turbid yin goes to the five zang"] The interstices are the gates of seepage and discharge; therefore clear yang can disperse through them. The five zang are places of enclosing and storing; therefore turbid yin can go to them.

[On "clear yang fills the four limbs and turbid yin returns to the six fu"] The four limbs move outwardly, so clear yang fills them; the six fu transform inwardly, so turbid yin returns to them.

## Notes

The workings of clear yang and turbid yin, set against clouds and rain in heaven and earth, and in the body against the upper and lower orifices, the interstices and the five zang, and the four limbs and the six fu.
- "Rain comes from the earth qi, clouds come from the heavenly qi": Wang Bing explains that rain is bestowed through clouds, hence "from the earth", and that clouds rely on qi, hence "from heaven".
- Daizhige writes 四肢 for 四支.
