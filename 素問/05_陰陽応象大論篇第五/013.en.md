# 素問 陰陽応象大論篇第五 §13 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The south generates heat; heat generates fire; fire generates bitterness; bitterness generates the heart; the heart generates blood; blood generates the spleen. The heart governs the tongue. In heaven it is heat; on earth, fire; in the body, the vessels; among the zang, the heart; among colours, red; among notes, *zhi*; among voices, laughing; among changes, worry; among orifices, the tongue; among flavours, bitter; among emotions, joy. Joy harms the heart; fear overcomes joy. Heat harms qi; cold overcomes heat. Bitterness harms qi; saltiness overcomes bitterness.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The south generates heat"] The yang qi blazes and dries; therefore it generates heat.

[On "heat generates fire"] When fire is renewed by drilling with the fire-drill, it is heat alone that produces it.

[On "fire generates bitterness"] All things bitter in flavour are produced by the qi of fire. The "Hongfan" of the *Shangshu* says: "Blazing upward makes bitterness."

[On "bitterness generates the heart"] All that is bitter first produces and grows in the heart.

[On "the heart generates blood"] The essential qi of the heart produces and nourishes the blood.

[On "blood generates the spleen"] The book of yin and yang says, "Fire generates earth." When the fire qi of the heart has nourished the blood within, it then generates spleen-earth.
Song editors: The *Taisu* writes "vessels" for "blood".

[On "The heart governs the tongue"] The heart distinguishes right and wrong, and the tongue speaks of affairs; therefore it governs the tongue.

[On "In heaven it is heat"] Warmth, heat, blazing and sultriness are the functions of heat.

[On "on earth, fire"] Blazing upward, glowing red — this is the nature of fire.

[On "in the body, the vessels"] They circulate the nutritive and defensive qi and nourish the blood.

[On "among the zang, the heart"] Its spirit is the spirit. The *Daojing yi* says: "The spirit dwells in the heart; when the spirit keeps guard, blood and qi flow freely."

[On "among colours, red"] It resembles the colour of fire.

[On "among notes, *zhi*"] *Zhi* is the note of fire, harmonious and beautiful. The "Yueji" says: "When *zhi* is disordered there is sorrow, and its affairs are toilsome."

[On "among voices, laughing"] Laughing is the voice of joy.

[On "among changes, worry"] Worry can accomplish tasks.
Song editors: Yang Shangshan says: "The worry of the heart lies in the change of the heart; the worry of the lung lies in the emotion of the lung. That is, the lung governs autumn and worry is its proper [emotion]; the heart governs summer and worry arises by change."

[On "among orifices, the tongue"] The tongue governs the discrimination of the five flavours. The *Jingui zhenyan lun* (chapter 4) says: "The south, red in colour, enters and communicates with the heart and opens its orifice in the ears." If one considers it as an orifice, the meaning of "tongue" does not fit; because it governs flavour, it says "tongue".

[On "among flavours, bitter"] Bitter can be used to dry and drain.

[On "among emotions, joy"] Joy is that by which one is harmonious and happy.

[On "Joy harms the heart"] Though its emotion is joy, in excess it harms itself.

[On "fear overcomes joy"] In fear, the water of the kidney combines with the fire of the heart; therefore it overcomes joy. The *Xuanming wuqi* (chapter 23) says: "When the essential qi combines in the kidney, there is fear."

[On "Heat harms qi"] When heat prevails, breathing becomes rapid and urgent.

[On "cold overcomes heat"] Cold is the qi of water; therefore it overcomes the heat of fire.

[On "Bitterness harms qi"] Because it is produced from fire.
Song editors: The point of this chapter's discussion of what harms falls into three patterns. In the east, "wind harms the sinews" and "sourness harms the sinews", and in the centre, "dampness harms the flesh" and "sweetness harms the flesh", are cases of harming oneself. In the south, "heat harms qi" and "bitterness harms qi", and in the north, "cold harms blood" and "saltiness harms blood", are cases of harming what one overcomes. In the west, "heat harms the skin and body hair" is a case of being overcome and harmed, while "acridity harms the skin and body hair" is a case of harming oneself. Thus what harms in the five directions follows three different patterns. The *Taisu* treats all of them as harming oneself.

[On "saltiness overcomes bitterness"] Salty is the flavour of water; therefore it overcomes the bitterness of fire.

## Notes

The correspondences of the south: south, heat, fire, bitter, heart, blood; the colour red, the note *zhi*, the voice of laughing, the change of worry, the orifice of the tongue, the emotion of joy; and harm and victory by the controlling cycle.
- "Among orifices, the tongue": the *Jingui zhenyan lun* (chapter 4) gives the ears as the orifice of the heart. Wang Bing explains that the tongue does not fit as an orifice but is named because it governs flavour.
- The Song editors point out that the cases of "harm" in the five directions fall into three patterns (harming oneself, harming what one overcomes, being overcome and harmed).
- 埶 in "In heaven it is heat" is the base text's form; the other sources write 熱.
