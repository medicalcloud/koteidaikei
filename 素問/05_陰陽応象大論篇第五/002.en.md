# 素問 陰陽応象大論篇第五 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Therefore accumulated yang becomes heaven and accumulated yin becomes earth. Yin is still and yang restless; yang generates and yin makes grow; yang kills and yin stores; yang transforms qi and yin completes form. Cold at its extreme generates heat; heat at its extreme generates cold. Cold qi generates turbidity; hot qi generates clarity. When clear qi is below, there is undigested diarrhoea; when turbid qi is above, there is distension. This is yin and yang acting in reverse, the counterflow and compliance of illness.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Therefore accumulated yang becomes heaven and accumulated yin becomes earth"] This is why yin and yang are said to be the Way of heaven and earth.

[On "Yin is still and yang restless"] This speaks of the standard by which they work in response to the kinds of things.

[On "yang generates and yin makes grow; yang kills and yin stores"] This clarifies the different functions of heaven and earth in killing and generating mentioned above. Shennong says: "Heaven generates by yang and makes grow by yin; earth kills by yang and stores by yin."
Song editors: Some doubt the meaning of "yin makes grow, yang kills". It can be seen from the meaning of the eight trigrams of the *Zhouyi* laid out in the four directions. *Kun* is yin; its position is the south-west corner, and its time is the turn of the sixth and seventh months, when the myriad things grow abundantly — how can one say yin has no principle of growth? *Qian* is yang; its position is the division of *xu* and *hai*, and its time is the turn of the ninth and tenth months, when the myriad things are gathered and killed — who can say yang has no principle of killing? By this the principle of yin making grow and yang killing is evident. These words also appear in the *Tianyuan jidalun* (chapter 66), where the explanation is naturally different.

[On "yang transforms qi and yin completes form"] This clarifies the warp and weft by which the myriad things are nourished and generated.

[On "Cold at its extreme generates heat; heat at its extreme generates cold"] This clarifies the general principle above.

[On "Cold qi generates turbidity; hot qi generates clarity"] This speaks of the correct qi.

[On "When clear qi is below ... there is distension"] When hot qi is below, grain is not transformed; hence undigested diarrhoea. When cold qi is above, qi does not disperse; hence distension. Why? Because yin is still and yang restless.

[On "This is yin and yang acting in reverse, the counterflow and compliance of illness"] "Reverse" means repeatedly overturning; "acting" means working. If one repeatedly overturns in one's work, illness is like this.

## Notes

The basic properties of yin and yang (heaven and earth, stillness and restlessness, generating, growing, killing and storing, qi and form, cold and heat, clear and turbid); when the clear and turbid qi are out of place, there is undigested diarrhoea or distension.
- "Yang generates and yin makes grow; yang kills and yin stores": the Song editors answer those who doubt that yin makes grow and yang kills by referring to the directions and seasons of the eight trigrams.
- "When clear qi is below, there is undigested diarrhoea": Wang Bing paraphrases clear qi as hot qi, which when below prevents the transformation of grain.
- Daizhige (the plain text) writes 燥 for 躁.
