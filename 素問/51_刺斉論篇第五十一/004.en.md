# 素問 刺斉論篇第五十一 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "'In needling the bones, do not injure the sinews' means the needle reaches the sinews and is withdrawn without reaching the bones. 'In needling the sinews, do not injure the flesh' means it reaches the flesh and is withdrawn without reaching the sinews. 'In needling the flesh, do not injure the vessels' means it reaches the vessels and is withdrawn without reaching the flesh. 'In needling the vessels, do not injure the skin' means it reaches the skin and is withdrawn without reaching the vessels. What is called 'in needling the skin, do not injure the flesh' means that when the illness is in the skin, the needle is inserted into the skin without injuring the flesh. 'In needling the flesh, do not injure the sinews' [warns against] passing the flesh and striking the sinews. 'In needling the sinews, do not injure the bones' [warns against] passing the sinews and striking the bones. These are called contrary."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "'In needling the bones, do not injure the sinews' ... without reaching the vessels"] All these concern dispelling evil. When there is cold evil in the sinews, wind evil in the flesh, damp evil in the vessels, or heat evil in the skin, it is dispelled in this way. Evil means anything that does not accord with the upright qi and invades it.
Song editors: On examination, this refers to needling too shallowly, not reaching the place to be needled; the text below warns against going too deep.

[On "What is called 'in needling the skin, do not injure the flesh' ... These are called contrary"] This warns against exceeding the limit and going too deep.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi says, "Needling like this is called injuring. All these are excesses; excess surely damages the blood and qi; this is called adverse; evil will surely enter through it."

## Notes

The first half describes the error of needling too shallowly and missing the target, the second half the error of needling too deeply and injuring the layer below; both are called "contrary".
