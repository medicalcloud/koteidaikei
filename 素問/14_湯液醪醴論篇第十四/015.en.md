# 素問 湯液醪醴論篇第十四 §15 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Emperor said: "There are [illnesses] that do not arise from the body hair: the yang of the five zang is exhausted, the fluids fill the outer wall, the *po* dwells alone, the essence is isolated within, the qi is depleted without, and the body cannot be kept within its clothes; the four extremities are tense and stir the centre; the qi is resisted within and the form is distended without. How is it treated?"

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "There are [illnesses] that do not arise from the body hair: the yang of the five zang is exhausted"] Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition and the *Taisu* write 傷 "harmed" for 陽 "yang"; the meaning also makes sense.

[On "the fluids fill the outer wall ... How is it treated?"] "Not from the body hair" means arising from within. The yin qi is abundant within and the yang qi exhausted and cut off, unable to enter the abdomen; hence "the yang of the five zang is exhausted". The fluids are water; 充 means fill; 郭 means the skin. Yin accumulates within, water qi swells and fills, rises to attack the lung, and the lung qi becomes isolated and endangered. The *po* is the spirit of the lung; the kidney causes water harm, and the child does not rescue the mother; hence "the *po* dwells alone". When the yin essence is damaged and pared within and the yang qi depleted without, the triple burner is closed and overflowing, the waterways do not pass, water fills the skin, and the body is blocked and swollen; hence "the body cannot be kept within its clothes". In all such cases the pulses of the four limbs are rapid and tense and drum within in the lung. "The lung stirring" means rapid breathing and coughing. All such cases mean that water qi is blocked within the abdominal membrane and the oedema distends outside the body; if one wishes to investigate tip and root, can it be done? "The four extremities" means the four ends, that is, the four limbs. The *Zuozhuan* says, "When wind is excessive, there is illness of the extremities," and the *Lingshu jing* says, "Yang receives qi at the four extremities."
Song editors: We suspect the character 施 in 形施於外 is an error.

## Notes

Huangdi asks how to treat an illness that arises from within rather than through the body hair: the yang of the five zang is exhausted, water fills the skin, the body is too swollen for its clothes, the limbs are tense and there is coughing (oedema).
- Wang Bing explains it as an illness in which yin is abundant and yang exhausted, water qi fills the skin and attacks the lung.
- According to the Song editors, Quan Yuanqi's edition and the *Taisu* write 五蔵傷 for 五蔵陽, and 施 in 形施於外 may be an error.
