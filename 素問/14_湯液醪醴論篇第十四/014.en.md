# 素問 湯液醪醴論篇第十四 §14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The patient is the root and the physician the tip; if tip and root are not obtained, the evil qi does not submit. This is what is meant."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On the whole sentence] It says that the physician and the patient do not suit each other. The physician, or relatives and brothers, may fully clarify the situation, but if [the patient] is doubtful he does not use [them]; though the physician is prepared and knowledgeable, [the patient] does not say he knows the method; he does not admit the subtlety of needles and moxa nor allow the attack of drugs and stones. In such a case, even if the Way is clear and every undertaking entirely sure, when the patient does not consent to treatment, how can one treat him? The *Wuzang bielun* (chapter 11) says: "With those bound by ghosts and spirits one cannot speak of the utmost virtue; with those who dislike needles and stones one cannot speak of the utmost skill; when a sick person does not consent to treatment, the illness certainly will not be cured, and treating it will have no effect." All this means that physician and patient do not suit each other and the evil qi does not submit. Is it only dislike of needles and moxa? It applies to drugs and stones too.
Song editors: The *Yijing bianqi lun* (chapter 13) says: "Once the tip and the root are obtained, the evil qi submits."

## Notes

The patient is the root and the physician the tip; if they do not suit each other the evil qi does not submit and the illness is not cured.
- Wang Bing describes the case of a patient who does not trust the physician or accept treatment, citing the *Wuzang bielun* (chapter 11). The first part of his note is somewhat obscure; the translation follows the characters.
