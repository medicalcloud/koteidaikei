# 素問 経脈別論篇第二十一 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the qi of food enters the stomach, it disperses essence to the liver and overflows qi into the sinews. When the qi of food enters the stomach, the turbid qi returns to the heart and overflows essence into the vessels; the qi of the vessels flows into the channels, and the qi of the channels returns to the lung. The lung receives the homage of the hundred vessels and transports essence to the skin and body hair. The hair and vessels combine their essence and move qi to the storehouse; the essence of the storehouse is spirit illumination, which stays in the four zang. The qi returns to the balance; the balance is level, and the qi opening forms the *cun*, by which one decides death and life. When drink enters the stomach, it lets the essential qi roam and overflow and transports it up to the spleen; the spleen qi disperses the essence, which returns up to the lung; [the lung] opens and regulates the waterways and transports down to the bladder. The essence of water spreads in all directions, and the five channels move together, according with the four seasons, the five zang and yin and yang; measuring by them is the norm.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the qi of food enters the stomach, it disperses essence to the liver and overflows qi into the sinews"] The liver nourishes the sinews; therefore when the stomach disperses the essential qi of grain into the liver, it soaks and nourishes the sinews and network vessels.

[On "When the qi of food enters the stomach, the turbid qi returns to the heart and overflows essence into the vessels"] The turbid qi is the qi of grain. The heart lies above the stomach; therefore the qi of grain returns to the heart and overflows its subtle essence into the vessels. Why? Because the heart governs the vessels.

[On "the qi of the vessels flows into the channels ... transports essence to the skin and body hair"] It says that the qi of the vessels flows and circulates to form the great channels, and the qi of the channels returns to its source and rises to pay homage to the lung. The lung is the canopy, lying high, through which regulation proceeds; therefore it receives the homage of the hundred vessels. The *Pingren qixiang lun* (chapter 18) says, "The true qi of the zang is high in the lung, by which it moves the nutritive and defensive qi and yin and yang." Thus the lung receives the homage of the hundred vessels and spreads and transforms the essential qi, transporting it to the skin and hair.

[On "The hair and vessels combine their essence and move qi to the storehouse"] "Storehouse" means where qi gathers; it is called the sea of qi, between the two breasts, named *danzhong*.

[On "the essence of the storehouse is spirit illumination ... The qi returns to the balance"] The *danzhong* spreads qi in three passages: the lower goes to Qijie, the upper to the airway, and the ancestral qi stays in the sea, accumulating in the chest, called the sea of qi. Divided and transformed thus, the four zang are settled, the triple burner balanced, and inside and outside, above and below, each obtains its place.

[On "the balance is level ... by which one decides death and life"] The pulse methods of three generations all take three *cun* as the divisions of *cun*, *guan* and *chi*. Therefore when the strands of qi inside and outside, high and low, are even and balanced, the pulse at the qi opening forms the *cun*. The qi opening is the great meeting place of the vessels, to which all the hundred vessels pay homage; therefore by its divisions one decides death and life.

[On "When drink enters the stomach ... transports it up to the spleen"] Water and drink flow down to the middle burner, where water is transformed into subtle essence and rises as clouds and mist; the clouds and mist disperse and change and pour into the spleen. The *Lingshu jing* says, "The upper burner is like a mist; the middle burner is like a soaking vat" — this is what is meant.

[On "the spleen qi disperses the essence ... transports down to the bladder"] Water and earth combine and transform, rising to moisten lung-metal; the qi of metal communicates with the kidney; therefore it regulates the waterways and turns to pour into the lower burner, where the bladder receives and transforms it into urine. The *Lingshu jing* says, "The lower burner is like a drain" — this is what is meant.

[On "The essence of water spreads in all directions ... measuring by them is the norm"] Thus the essence of water spreads, the channel qi moves, the sinews and bones are formed, the blood and qi comply, matching the four seasons and cold and heat, according with the signs of the five zang; measuring yin and yang, fullness and emptiness are used as the constant way. 度 means measure; 以 means use.
Song editors: One version reads "yin and yang, movement and stillness".

## Notes

The essential qi of food entering the stomach circulates through the liver, sinews, heart, vessels, lung, skin and hair, and the *danzhong* (sea of qi), and shows at the qi opening (*cunkou*); drink circulates through the spleen, lung and bladder and spreads throughout the body. This is regarded as a key passage in the *Suwen* on the digestion, absorption and distribution of food and drink.
- "The lung receives the homage of the hundred vessels": Wang Bing explains that the lung receives the gathering of all the vessels.
- "Storehouse": Wang Bing takes it as the *danzhong* (sea of qi).
- 盈 in Wang Bing's note was supplied where the transcription gave only a component description (see the 【補】 note).
