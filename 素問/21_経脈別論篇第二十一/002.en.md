# 素問 経脈別論篇第二十一 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "In general, a person's fright, fear, anger, toil, movement and rest all produce changes. Thus when one travels at night, panting comes from the kidney, and the excessive qi makes the lung ill. When one falls and is afraid, panting comes from the liver, and the excessive qi harms the spleen. When one is startled and afraid, panting comes from the lung, and the excessive qi harms the heart. When one wades through water and stumbles, panting comes from the kidney and bones. At such times, in the brave the qi moves and it ends, while in the timid [the evil] clings and becomes illness. Therefore it is said: the way of diagnosing illness is to observe a person's courage or timidity, bones, flesh and skin, and to be able to know their condition; this is the method of diagnosis. Thus when one eats and drinks to excess, sweat comes from the stomach; when frightened and robbed of essence, sweat comes from the heart; when carrying heavy loads on long journeys, sweat comes from the kidney; when running fast in fear, sweat comes from the liver; when shaking the body in toil, sweat comes from the spleen. Therefore in spring, autumn, winter and summer, in the yin and yang of the four seasons, illness arises from overuse; this is the norm.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In general, a person's fright ... all produce changes"] "Changes" means alteration of the usual signs.

[On "Thus when one travels at night, panting comes from the kidney"] The kidney dominates at night and its qi accords with darkness; therefore when one travels at night, panting comes from the kidney within.

[On "and the excessive qi makes the lung ill"] Travelling at night tires the kidney and so one pants; when the qi is excessive and out of order, it makes the lung ill.

[On "When one falls and is afraid, panting comes from the liver"] Fear arises in the liver; falling damages the sinews and blood, and so one pants hard; therefore it comes from the liver.

[On "and the excessive qi harms the spleen"] Liver-wood recklessly overflows and harms spleen-earth.

[On "When one is startled and afraid, panting comes from the lung"] When startled the heart has nothing to lean on, the spirit has nowhere to return, and the qi is disordered in the chest; therefore panting comes from the lung.

[On "and the excessive qi harms the heart"] When startled the spirit goes beyond itself; therefore the excessive qi instead harms the heart.

[On "When one wades through water and stumbles, panting comes from the kidney and bones"] Damp qi communicates with the kidney, and the bones are governed by the kidney; therefore wading and stumbling make panting come from the kidney and bones. 跌 means the foot slipping; 仆 means the body falling.

[On "At such times, in the brave ... becomes illness"] Qi has strength and weakness, spirit has vigour and timidity; hence the different outcomes.

[On "Therefore it is said: the way of diagnosing illness ... the method of diagnosis"] Only by penetrating the nature and disposition and grasping the condition can one understand deeply and make the diagnosis suit the case.

[On "Thus when one eats and drinks to excess, sweat comes from the stomach"] Extreme fullness fills the stomach; therefore sweat comes from the stomach.

[On "when frightened and robbed of essence, sweat comes from the heart"] Fright robs the heart of essence, the spirit qi floats and overflows, and yang presses within; therefore sweat comes from the heart.

[On "when carrying heavy loads on long journeys, sweat comes from the kidney"] The bones are tired and the qi overflows, and the kidney is overstrained; therefore sweat comes from the kidney.

[On "when running fast in fear, sweat comes from the liver"] The sinews are suddenly overworked and the liver qi exhausted; therefore sweat comes from the liver.

[On "when shaking the body in toil, sweat comes from the spleen"] "Shaking the body in toil" means exerting strength in movement, not running fast or travelling far. When one moves and exerts strength, the essence of grain spreads in all directions and the spleen transforms water and grain; therefore sweat comes from the spleen.

## Notes

Fright, fear, toil and other activities change the pulse; the organs from which panting and sweating come in various situations are described. The brave recover as their qi moves, the timid fall ill; illness arises from overuse.
