# 素問 評熱病論篇第三十三 §12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Taxation wind is in principle located below the lung. As an illness it makes one stiff above and see dimly, spit out phlegm like nasal mucus, dislike wind and shiver with cold. This is the illness of taxation wind."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Taxation wind is in principle located below the lung"] Wind arises from taxation; hence it is called taxation wind. "Taxation" means taxation of the kidney. The kidney vessel rises from the kidney through the liver and diaphragm and enters the lung; therefore when the kidney is taxed and wind arises, it rises and resides below the lung.

[On "As an illness it makes one stiff above and see dimly"] Song editors: Yang Shangshan says, "Stiff above means tending to look up. Seeing dimly means looking with the eyes closed and not seeing clearly." Moreover, the *Qianjin fang* writes 目眩 "dizzy vision" for 冥視.

[On "spit out phlegm like nasal mucus ... the illness of taxation wind"] The bladder vessel arises at the inner canthus, rises over the forehead, crosses at the vertex, enters to connect with the brain, comes out again and branches down the nape, follows the inside of the shoulder blade, runs alongside the spine to the lumbus, enters along the spinal muscles and connects with the kidney. Now the kidney essence is insufficient and draws on the bladder outside; the bladder qi cannot rise to nourish; therefore the head and nape are stiff and vision unclear. The lung is pressed by wind and the qi of taxation rises and fumigates; therefore one spits out phlegm like nasal mucus. The kidney qi is insufficient, yang qi attacks within, and taxation and heat combine; therefore one dislikes wind and shivers with cold.

## Notes

Wind arising from taxation of the kidney resides below the lung and causes stiffness of the nape, dim vision, phlegm and chills; this is taxation wind.
- 強上: Wang Bing takes it as stiffness of the head and nape; Yang Shangshan as tending to look up.
- 𤾁 in Wang Bing's note is a variant of 鼻 (Kanripo writes 鼻).
