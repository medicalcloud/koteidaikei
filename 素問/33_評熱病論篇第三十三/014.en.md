# 素問 評熱病論篇第三十三 §14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Thereby stop the bending forward and back. In those whose greater yang draws on essence, in three days; in the middle-aged, in five days; in those without essence, in seven days, one coughs up green-yellow mucus, like pus, the size of a pellet, coming out of the mouth or nose. If it does not come out, it harms the lung; if the lung is harmed, one dies."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Thereby stop the bending forward and back"] 救 means stop. 俛仰 means bending and stretching. It says to stop bending and stretching in activity so as not to let the qi of taxation spread.

[On "In those whose greater yang draws on essence, in three days ... in seven days"] Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* reads 三日中若五日; the *Qianjin fang* reads "Observe it within three or five days; those who are not clear are the ones." These differ from this text.

[On "one coughs up green-yellow mucus ... if the lung is harmed, one dies"] The greater yang is the bladder vessel; the bladder and kidney are exterior and interior; therefore the greater yang draws on essence. 巨 means great. Those in whom the greater yang vessel draws on essential qi and rises to attack the lung should cough up thick mucus in three days, the middle-aged in five days, and those who do not ordinarily use their essential qi in seven days; its colour is green-yellow and it is like pus. In ordinary, regulated coughing, it rises from the throat and comes out of the mouth; in sudden, violent coughing, the qi bursts through the *Xu* gate and comes out of the nose. All this is caused by the kidney qi being taxed and exhausted, the lung qi being depleted within, and the yang qi rushing and pressing; therefore if it does not come out, it harms the lung. When the lung is harmed, the nutritive and defensive scatter and the *po* is not governed within; therefore one dies.
Song editors: Mr Wang says, "In sudden, violent coughing, the qi bursts through the *Xu* gate and comes out of the nose." Checking the seven surging gates in the *Nanjing*, there is no gate named *Xu*; it is perhaps the *Ben* gate (cardia). Yang Cao says, "*Ben* is the diaphragm, where the stomach qi comes out. The stomach sends out grain qi to transmit it to the lung; the lung is above the diaphragm; therefore the stomach is the *Ben* gate."

## Notes

The treatment and course of taxation wind.
- 以救俛仰: Wang Bing takes it as stopping bodily bending and stretching to keep the patient at rest.
- 巨陽引精者三日，中年者五日，不精者七日 is obscure and read in various ways; the translation follows Wang Bing (three days for the young with abundant essential qi, five for the middle-aged, seven for those with declining essential qi).
