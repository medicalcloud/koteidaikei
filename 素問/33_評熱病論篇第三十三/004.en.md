# 素問 評熱病論篇第三十三 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The reason people sweat is that it all arises from grain, and grain generates essence. Now when evil qi contends in the bones and flesh and sweat is obtained, it is because the evil retreats and the essence prevails. When the essence prevails, one should be able to eat and not become hot again. Recurrent heat is the evil qi; sweat is the essential qi. Now if after sweating one at once becomes hot again, the evil prevails. Inability to eat means the essence has nothing to employ. If one is ill and [the heat] lingers, life may collapse at once. Moreover, the *Relun* says, 'When there is sweating and the pulse is still agitated and abundant, one dies.' Now the pulse does not correspond with the sweating; this means one cannot overcome the illness, and death is clear. Wild talk is loss of the will; loss of the will means death. Now three signs of death appear and not one of life; even if one seems to recover, one will surely die."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The reason people sweat ... grain generates essence"] It says that grain qi transforms into essence, and only when the essential qi prevails does sweat form.

[On "Now when evil qi contends ... the essence prevails"] This refers to the first sweating.

[On "Now if after sweating one at once becomes hot again ... nothing to employ"] "Nothing to employ" means nothing that can be made into sweat. If grain is not transformed, essence is not generated; essence does not transform and flow; therefore there is nothing to employ.

[On "If one is ill and [the heat] lingers, life may collapse at once"] In such cases, if after sweating [the heat] quickly lingers and lodges without leaving, the person's life is at once in peril.
Song editors: On 病而留者, according to Wang's note 病 should be 疾; moreover the *Jiayi jing* reads 而熱留者 "and the heat lingers".

[On "Moreover, the *Relun* says ... one dies"] The *Relun* means the *Relun* of high antiquity. After sweating the pulse should be slow and calm; if instead it is agitated, tense and abundant, the true qi is exhausted and the evil abundant; therefore one knows death is certain.

[On "Now the pulse does not correspond ... death is clear"] The pulse not calming but being agitated and abundant is the lack of correspondence.

[On "Wild talk is loss of the will; loss of the will means death"] The will lodges in the essence. Now the essence has nothing to employ, so the will has nowhere to reside; when the will does not stay, it is lost.

[On "Now three signs of death appear ... one will surely die"] Sweating with an agitated, abundant pulse is the first death; inability to overcome the illness is the second; wild talk with loss of the will is the third.

## Notes

Since sweat arises from the essential qi, fever that does not fall after sweating shows that the evil prevails; three signs of death are given.
- *Relun*: Wang Bing takes it as a "*Relun* of high antiquity". A similar passage occurs in the *Rebing* chapter of the *Lingshu*.
- 精無俾也: Wang Bing takes 俾 in the sense of 使 "employ".
