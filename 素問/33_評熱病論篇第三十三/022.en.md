# 素問 評熱病論篇第三十三 §22 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Water is yin, and below the eyes is also yin; the abdomen is where the utmost yin resides; therefore when water is in the abdomen, it surely makes the area below the eyes swell. The true qi counterflows upward; therefore the mouth is bitter and the tongue dry, and one cannot lie flat on the back when lying down; lying flat on the back, one coughs up clear water. All with water illness therefore cannot lie down; lying down they are startled, and being startled the coughing is severe. Rumbling in the abdomen means the illness is rooted in the stomach. When it presses on the spleen, there is vexation and inability to eat. Food not going down means the stomach duct is blocked. Heaviness of the body making walking difficult is because the stomach vessel is in the feet. Absence of menses means the uterine vessel is closed. The uterine vessel belongs to the heart and connects within the uterus; now the qi rises and presses on the lung, and the heart qi cannot communicate downward; therefore the menses do not come."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Water is yin ... therefore the menses do not come"] Considering the meanings explained in the text above, the meaning of "the heat rises from the chest and back to the head; there are sweating, hot hands, dry mouth with bitterness and thirst" has not been explained. Presumably slips of the old text were lost and it became confused here. What is the explanation? The kidney lesser yin vessel rises from the kidney through the liver and diaphragm, enters the lung, follows the throat and runs alongside the root of the tongue. The bladder greater yang vessel rises from the inner canthus over the forehead and crosses at the vertex; a branch goes from the vertex to the upper corner of the ear; the straight course enters from the vertex to connect with the brain, comes out again, branches down the nape, follows the inside of the shoulder blade, runs alongside the spine to the lumbus and enters along the spinal muscles. Now yin is insufficient and yang in surplus; therefore the heat rises from the chest and back to the head with sweating, and the mouth is dry, bitter and thirsty. The heart is a yang zang whose vessel runs through the arm and hand; the kidney is a yin zang whose vessel follows the chest and foot. When the kidney is insufficient, the heart qi is in surplus; therefore the hands are hot. Moreover, the vessels of the heart and kidney are both lesser yin vessels.

## Notes

Explains the reasons for other symptoms of wind water (swelling below the eyes, coughing, borborygmus, loss of appetite, heaviness of the body, absence of menses).
- Wang Bing notes that the explanation of "the heat rises from the chest and back to the head; sweating, hot hands, dry mouth with bitterness and thirst" from unit 018 is missing here, and supplies one himself.
