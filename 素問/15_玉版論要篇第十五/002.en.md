# 素問 玉版論要篇第十五 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "*Kuiduo* means measuring the depth of an illness; *qiheng* refers to extraordinary illnesses. Let me speak of the utmost numbers of the Way: by the changes of the five colors and the pulse one measures the extraordinary and the constant; the Way lies in the One. The spirit turns and does not go back; if it goes back it does not turn, and then it loses its mechanism. The key of the utmost numbers is close at hand yet subtle. It was inscribed on jade tablets and called 'matching the jade mechanism'. The complexion appears above, below, left or right, each in its key position. If the color appears shallow, decoctions are the main treatment, and it ends in ten days; if it appears deep, compounded drugs are necessarily the main treatment, and it ends in twenty-one days; if it appears very deep, wines are the main treatment, and it ends in a hundred days. If the color is dull and the facial flesh wasted, it cannot be treated; it ends when a hundred days are over. A short pulse with exhausted qi means death; warm illness with extreme deficiency means death. The complexion appears above, below, left or right, each in its key position. Above is adverse, below is favorable. In women the right is adverse and the left favorable; in men the left is adverse and the right favorable. If they are interchanged, doubled yang means death and doubled yin means death. When yin and yang are reversed, treatment lies in the mutual seizing of the steelyard and beam; this is the matter of the extraordinary and the constant, the matter of measuring. A beating pulse with impediment and lameness is the intermingling of cold and heat. A solitary pulse means wasted qi; deficiency with discharge means loss of blood. Solitary is adverse; deficiency is favorable. In carrying out the method of the extraordinary and constant, one begins with the greater yin. Moving to what it cannot overcome is called adverse, and adverse means death; moving to what it overcomes is called favorable, and favorable means life. The victories of the eight winds and four seasons end and begin again; if counterflow goes the round once, it can no longer be counted. The key of the discussion is complete."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Let me speak of the utmost numbers ... the Way lies in the One"] "The One" means the correspondence of complexion and pulse. If one knows the correspondence of complexion and pulse, one can measure the extraordinary and the constant.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 謂 for 請.

[On "The spirit turns and does not go back ... it loses its mechanism"] Blood and qi are the spirit qi. The *Bazheng shenming lun* (chapter 26) says: "Blood and qi are a person's spirit; they must be carefully nourished." Blood and qi follow the four seasons, shifting in turn; confinement and dominance circulate, and the five qi do not usurp one another's order. This is "the spirit turns and does not go back". "Going back" means moving backward. Blood and qi follow what is dominant and should not move backward; if they move backward they go against the norm; going against the norm, they go back and do not turn; going back and not turning, they lose the mechanism of vital qi. How is this shown? When wood declines, fire dominates; when fire declines, earth dominates; when earth declines, metal dominates; when metal declines, water dominates; when water declines, wood dominates — ending and beginning again in a circle. This is "the spirit turns and does not go back". If when wood declines water dominates, when water declines metal dominates, when metal declines earth dominates, when earth declines fire dominates, and when fire declines wood dominates, this is "going back and not turning". If the constant track of heaven is reversed, how can there be life?

[On "The key of the utmost numbers is close at hand yet subtle"] It says the essential way of the changes of the five colors and five pulses is close to the constancy of heaven and yet subtle.

[On "It was inscribed on jade tablets and called 'matching the jade mechanism'"] "Jade mechanism" is the name of a chapter. It says that the essential meaning of this turning was inscribed on jade tablets and matches the text of the *Yuji* [*zhenzang*] *lun*.
Song editors: From "the utmost numbers of the Way" to here overlaps with the text of the *Yuji zhenzang lun* (chapter 19), but the notes differ considerably.

[On "The complexion appears above, below, left or right, each in its key position"] The complexion here is a foreign qi. For example, a red, yellow, white or black color appearing within the region of liver-wood is in each case a foreign qi; the other zang follow the same pattern. Where it appears is always above, below, left or right in the *mingtang* (the nose and face), at the key places for observation; hence "each in its key position".
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 客 "guest" for 容. The method of inspecting color is set out in the *Jiayi jing*.

[On "If the color appears shallow ... it ends in ten days"] When the color is shallow the illness is light; therefore it ends in ten days.

[On "if it appears deep ... it ends in twenty-one days"] When the color is deep the illness is severe; therefore it ends only when the compounded drugs are completed.

[On "if it appears very deep ... it ends in a hundred days"] Because the illness is deep and severe, the days are many.

[On "If the color is dull and the facial flesh wasted, it cannot be treated"] When the color appears very deep, together with a dull, bad color, and the flesh of the face is also wasted, it cannot be treated.

[On "it ends when a hundred days are over"] If the color is not dull and the face not wasted, treating it for a hundred days can end it completely.
Song editors: It means that although a dull color and wasted face cannot be treated, the term ends only when a hundred days are over.

[On "A short pulse with exhausted qi means death"] A short pulse is already deficient; if gradual exhaustion is added, the genuine qi is about to be exhausted; therefore one must die.

[On "warm illness with extreme deficiency means death"] When one is extremely deficient and has warm illness, the warm qi dries up the essence and blood within; therefore one dies.

[On "Above is adverse, below is favorable"] Color appearing below is the qi of illness arising [lightly]; hence favorable. Color appearing above is a sign of harm to the spirit; hence adverse.

[On "In women the right is adverse ... in men the left adverse and the right favorable"] The left is yang; therefore for men the right is favorable and the left adverse. The right is yin; therefore for women the right is adverse and the left favorable.

[On "If they are interchanged, doubled yang means death and doubled yin means death"] When a woman's color appears on the left and a man's on the right, this is interchange. A man's color appearing on the left is called doubled yang; a woman's color appearing on the right is called doubled yin. When qi reaches its extreme it reverses; therefore both mean death.

[On "When yin and yang are reversed"] Song editors: The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* (chapter 5) says "yin and yang acting in reverse".

[On "treatment lies in the mutual seizing ... the matter of measuring"] "The mutual seizing of the steelyard and beam" means the two qi of yin and yang do not obtain what suits high and low. This is a matter extraordinary to the constant; one should measure the qi and treat it as appropriate.

[On "A beating pulse with impediment and lameness is the intermingling of cold and heat"] When the pulse strikes and beats against the hand and one suffers numbness, cramps and lameness, these are all produced by the intermingling of the qi of cold and heat, not by the deficiency or excess of pathogenic qi.

[On "A solitary pulse means wasted qi; deficiency with discharge means loss of blood"] When the pulse has exterior without interior or interior without exterior, it is called solitary, lost qi. If it has both exterior and interior but the qi is insufficient, it is called deficient, declining qi.

[On "Solitary is adverse; deficiency is favorable"] The solitary has nothing to rely on; hence adverse. Deficiency and decline can be restored; hence favorable.

[On "In carrying out the method ... one begins with the greater yin"] In the method of measuring the extraordinary and constant, one first fixes the healthy qi of the four seasons by the greater yin pulse at the qi opening, and then measures the extraordinary and constant qi.

[On "Moving to what it cannot overcome is called adverse, and adverse means death"] Wood showing a metal pulse, metal a fire pulse, fire a water pulse, water an earth pulse, earth a wood pulse — these all move to what they cannot overcome; hence adverse. The injury and victory do not cease; therefore adverse means death.

[On "moving to what it overcomes is called favorable, and favorable means life"] Wood showing water, fire or earth pulses; fire showing metal, earth or wood; earth showing metal, water or fire; metal showing earth, wood or water; water showing metal, fire or wood — these are all pulses it can overcome; hence favorable. Being favorable, there is no controlling, killing, harming or ruining; therefore favorable means life.

[On "The victories of the eight winds and four seasons end and begin again"] Because they do not go beyond the five phases, although they overcome one another, they still circulate, ending and beginning again.

[On "if counterflow goes the round once ... The key of the discussion is complete"] 過 means "throughout". If counterflow goes once throughout the five qi, it can no longer be counted as balanced and harmonious.

## Notes

*Kuiduo* means measuring the depth of illness and *qiheng* refers to extraordinary illnesses; the passage gives the key to diagnosis by the changes of the five colors and the pulse. Normally the spirit (blood and qi) turns in the order of the five phases and does not go back. It sets out treatment and prognosis by the depth of facial color, favorable and adverse positions of color (above and below, left and right, for men and women), solitary and deficient pulses, beginning the method of the extraordinary and constant with the greater yin (qi opening), and favorable and adverse outcomes by the controlling relations of the five phases.
- According to the Song editors, the passage from "the utmost numbers of the Way" to "matching the jade mechanism" overlaps with the *Yuji zhenzang lun* (chapter 19).
- "The spirit turns and does not go back": Wang Bing explains that the five phases dominating in their generating order is "turning without going back", and the reverse order is "going back without turning".
- "Complexion" (容色): according to the Song editors, Quan Yuanqi's edition reads 客色 "guest color".
- Wang Bing's explanation of "if counterflow goes the round once" is somewhat hard to follow.
- The chapter title "Key Discussion of the Jade Tablets" comes from "inscribed on jade tablets" and the closing "the key of the discussion is complete".
- In the base, 2 instance(s) of 巳 meaning 已 "already/stop" to 已 are emended (graphic confusion; the base often does not distinguish 巳, 已 and 己, while Wikisource's plain text and other sources mostly have the correct character).
