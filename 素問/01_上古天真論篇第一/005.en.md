# 素問 上古天真論篇第一 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Therefore their aims were at ease and their desires few; their hearts were at peace and without fear; their bodies laboured but did not tire; their qi followed and was in accord; each followed his wishes, and all obtained what they desired.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "their aims were at ease ... their bodies laboured but did not tire"] Because the inner workings [of desire] cease, desires are few; because outer turmoil is stilled, the heart is at peace. Then feeling and desire both vanish, right and wrong are threaded into one, and rising and resting are all fitting; therefore they do not tire.

[On "their qi followed and was in accord ... all obtained what they desired"] Because their aims are not greedy, all that they wish goes smoothly; because their hearts are easily satisfied, what they desire is surely fulfilled. Since they seek nothing out of the ordinary, nothing is hard to obtain. The *Laozi* says: "Know contentment and you will not be disgraced; know when to stop and you will not be imperilled; thus you can last long."

## Notes

The condition of those who are tranquil and empty: since their desires are few, what they wish for comes to them as a matter of course.
- Wang Bing reads the passage through the *Laozi*'s idea of knowing contentment.
