# 素問 上古天真論篇第一 §11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

"In a man, at eight years the kidney qi is replete, the hair grows long and the teeth are replaced. At twice eight the kidney qi is exuberant, the *tiangui* arrives, the essential qi overflows and is discharged, and yin and yang are in harmony; therefore he can have children. At three times eight the kidney qi is evened out and the sinews and bones are vigorous and strong; therefore the wisdom teeth come in and growth reaches its peak. At four times eight the sinews and bones are at their fullest and the flesh is full and strong. At five times eight the kidney qi declines, the hair falls and the teeth wither. At six times eight the yang qi declines and is exhausted in the upper body, the face withers, and the hair and temples turn grey. At seven times eight the liver qi declines and the sinews cannot move; the *tiangui* is exhausted, the essence is scant, the kidney organ declines, and the whole body is worn out. At eight times eight the teeth and hair are gone. The kidney governs water; it receives the essence of the five zang and six fu and stores it, so only when the five zang are exuberant can it discharge. Now the five zang have all declined, the sinews and bones are slack, and the *tiangui* is used up; therefore the hair and temples are white, the body is heavy, the gait is unsteady, and he has no children."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In a man, at eight years ... the teeth are replaced"] The number of old yin culminates in ten; the number of young yin comes next at eight. A man is of young yang qi, so the number of young yin is matched with him. The "Xici" of the *Yi* says: "Heaven nine, earth ten" — these are the numbers.

[On "At twice eight ... therefore he can have children"] Men and women differ in their yin and yang substance; [when] the *tiangui* [arrives], the forms of essence and blood also differ. Yin is still: the sea fills and the blood is let go. Yang moves: it responds and joins, and the essence is discharged. Because the two communicate and harmonize, they can have children. The "Xici" of the *Yi* says: "Man and woman join their essences, and the myriad things are generated." This is what is meant.

[On "At three times eight ... growth reaches its peak"] It is so because they are put to good use.

[On "At four times eight ... the flesh is full and strong"] A man's *tiangui* ends at eight times eight; the age of four times eight is likewise half of his capacity.

[On "At five times eight ... the teeth wither"] The kidney governs the bones, and the teeth are the surplus of the bones. Once the kidney qi has declined, the essence has nothing to nourish; therefore the hair falls and the teeth also dry and wither.

[On "At six times eight ... the hair and temples turn grey"] The yang qi is also the qi of *yangming*. The *Lingshu jing* says: "The foot *yangming* vessel starts at the nose, meets at the root of the nose, descends along the outside of the nose, enters the upper gums, comes out again to skirt the mouth and circle the lips, meets below at Chengjiang, then runs back along the lower edge behind the chin, emerges at Daying, follows Jiache, rises in front of the ear, passes Kezhuren, and follows the hairline to the forehead." Therefore when it declines in the upper body, the face withers and the hair and temples turn white.

[On "At seven times eight ... the whole body is worn out"] The liver qi nourishes the sinews; because the liver declines, the sinews cannot move. The kidney qi nourishes the bones; because the kidney declines, the body is exhausted. Because the *tiangui* is already exhausted, the essence is scant. It is not merely that the capacity declines; it is indeed the numbers of heaven that make it so.

[On "At eight times eight the teeth and hair are gone"] Because the yang qi is exhausted and the essential qi has declined, the teeth and hair are not firm and leave the body. "Gone" means fallen out.

[On "The kidney governs water ... only when the five zang are exuberant can it discharge"] The essential qi of the five zang and six fu overflows and seeps into the kidney, and the kidney organ receives and stores it. How is this made clear? The *Lingshu jing* says: "The five zang govern the storing of essence; that which stores essence must not be harmed." According to this, each of the five zang has essence, which is poured into the kidney as it is used. The kidney is the metropolis where it gathers and the place that controls the gate; it is not that the kidney alone has essence. Therefore it says "only when the five zang are exuberant can it discharge."

[On "Now the five zang have all declined ... he has no children"] This is what is meant by "when things reach their prime they grow old; this is called the way of heaven."

## Notes

The life of a man described in eight-year stages: at sixteen the *tiangui* arrives and he can have children; at thirty-two he is in his prime; at fifty-six the *tiangui* is exhausted; at sixty-four the teeth and hair fall out. The second half explains that the kidney receives and stores the essence of all the five zang and six fu, so that when the five zang decline, he can no longer have children.
- On the number eight, Wang Bing explains it as the number of young yin (a yin number matched with the yang of the male).
- In the division of the base text and Wang Bing's notes, "the *tiangui* is exhausted, the essence is scant, the kidney organ declines, and the whole body is worn out" belongs to seven times eight (fifty-six), and eight times eight (sixty-four) has only "the teeth and hair are gone". Daizhige (*Chongguang buzhu Huangdi neijing suwen*) has "eight times eight" after "the sinews cannot move", assigning the following phrases to sixty-four.
- Wikisource (the plain text) writes 懈惰 for 解堕 (same meaning).
- In Wang Bing's note, the base text writes 巳 in 天癸巳竭; it is translated as 已 "already".
- Qibo's speech, begun in unit 010, ends here.
