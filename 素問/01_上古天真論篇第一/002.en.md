# 素問 上古天真論篇第一 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "Among the people of high antiquity, those who knew the Way modelled themselves on yin and yang and harmonized with the arts and numbers. Their eating and drinking had measure, their rising and resting had regularity, and they did not toil recklessly. Therefore body and spirit could remain together, they lived out the full span allotted by heaven, and only after passing a hundred years did they depart.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "modelled themselves on yin and yang and harmonized with the arts and numbers"] "High antiquity" means remote antiquity. "Knowing the Way" means knowing the way of self-cultivation. Yin and yang are the constant way of heaven and earth; the arts and numbers are the great principles of preserving life. Therefore those who cultivate themselves must attend to these first. The *Laozi* says: "The myriad things carry yin on their backs and embrace yang, and through the blending qi they attain harmony." The *Siqi tiaoshen dalun* (chapter 2) says: "Yin and yang and the four seasons are the end and beginning of the myriad things and the root of death and life. Go against them and calamity arises; follow them and grave illness does not occur. This is called attaining the Way." This is what is meant here.

[On "eating and drinking had measure, ... did not toil recklessly"] Food and drink are the flavours that fill emptiness; rising and resting are the guiding rules of movement and rest. Therefore those who cultivate themselves practise them with care. The *Bilun* (chapter 43) says: "If food and drink are doubled, the intestines and stomach are harmed," and the *Shengqi tongtian lun* (chapter 3) says: "If rising and resting are as if startled, the spirit qi floats." These warn against reckless movement. Guangchengzi said: "Be still, be pure; do not toil your body, do not agitate your essence, and you can live long." Therefore the sages attend to these first.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's annotated edition reads "eating and drinking had constant measure, rising and resting had constant degree, they were neither reckless nor [over-]active"; the *Taisu* is the same. Yang Shangshan says: "They take sounds, sights, fragrance and flavour according to principle and do not look or listen recklessly; they move according to principle and do nothing beyond their station."

[On "body and spirit could remain together ... did they depart"] Body and spirit together reach their allotted span. Because they attend to self-cultivation and serve the heavenly true [qi], they can live out their heavenly years. "Depart" means leaving the bodily frame. The *Lingshu jing* says: "At a hundred years of age the five zang are all empty, the spirit qi has all departed, and the bodily frame alone remains and comes to its end." Because they knew the Way, their years were long and their lives extended. "Passing a hundred years" means reaching a hundred and twenty. The "Hongfan" chapter of the *Shangshu* says: "The first is longevity" — [that is,] a hundred and twenty years.

## Notes

The first half of Qibo's answer: the self-cultivation of those in antiquity who knew the Way, and why they lived out their full span.
- "Arts and numbers" (*shushu*): Wang Bing explains it as "the great principles of preserving life"; others take it to mean specific practices of nourishing life such as daoyin and massage.
- "Passing a hundred years": Wang Bing takes this as a hundred and twenty.
- According to the Song editors, Quan Yuanqi's edition and the *Taisu* read the phrase on eating, rising and toil differently (see the commentary).
- Qibo's speech continues into unit 004 and beyond, so the quotation mark is left open here.
- The base text writes the name Qibo with the character 歧 (kept as in the base).
