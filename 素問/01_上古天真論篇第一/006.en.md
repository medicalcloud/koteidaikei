# 素問 上古天真論篇第一 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Therefore they found their food delicious, let their clothing be as it was, and took joy in their customs; high and low did not envy one another. Their people were therefore called simple.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "they found their food delicious"] Accepting it whether fine or coarse.
Song editors: Another edition writes 甘 *gan* (sweet) for 美 *mei* (delicious).

[On "let their clothing be as it was"] Accepting it whether fine or poor.

[On "took joy in their customs"] Doing away with longing for [other things].

[On "high and low did not envy one another. Their people were therefore called simple"] Reaching the point of seeking nothing; this is what is called contentment of the heart. The *Laozi* says: "No calamity is greater than not knowing contentment; no fault is greater than desiring to get. Therefore the contentment of knowing contentment is lasting contentment." That is, knowing contentment does not mean having enough things; it is contentment of the heart that is knowing contentment. If one does not give rein to desire, one is the same as the simple. Therefore the sage says: "I am without desire, and the people of themselves become simple."
Song editors: Another edition writes 日 *ri* (day) for 曰 *yue* (called).

## Notes

The way of life of people with few desires: content with their food, clothing and customs, high and low did not envy one another, and so their people are called simple.
- Wang Bing takes "found their food delicious" as accepting food whether fine or coarse.
- According to the Song editors, another edition reads 甘 for 美 and 日 for 曰; with 日 the sentence would mean "their people therefore became simpler day by day".
