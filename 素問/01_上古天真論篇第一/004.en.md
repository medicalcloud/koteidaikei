# 素問 上古天真論篇第一 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the sages of high antiquity taught those below them, they all said: "Avoid the depleting evil and the thieving wind at the proper times. Be tranquil, content and empty, and the true qi will follow; the essence and spirit will keep to the interior. From where then could illness come?"

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Avoid the depleting evil and the thieving wind at the proper times"] Evil that enters by taking advantage of depletion is called depleting evil; [wind] that secretly harms the central harmony is called thieving wind. "Avoid them at the proper times" refers to the days of the eight nodal terms, and to the days when Taiyi enters the central palace and the eight winds pay court to it. The *Lingshu jing* says: "Unless evil qi meets with depletion, it cannot by itself harm a person." This makes clear that only when a person is depleted does evil prevail.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's annotated edition reads "In the teaching of the sages of high antiquity, those below all acted accordingly"; the *Taisu* and the *Qianjin* are the same. Yang Shangshan says: "What the sages of high antiquity had people do, they first did themselves. This is teaching without words, and teaching without words surpasses teaching with words. Therefore many of the common people below imitated them; hence it says 'those below all acted accordingly'." The meaning of Taiyi entering the central palace and the eight winds paying court is given in full in the *Tianyuan yuce*.

[On "Be tranquil, content and empty ... From where then could illness come?"] Being tranquil, content and empty means stillness. If one models oneself on the clarity and stillness of the Way, the essential qi is held within; therefore evil qi cannot do harm.

## Notes

Qibo continues: the sages of antiquity taught people to avoid external evils at the proper times and to be tranquil and empty within, and so they did not fall ill.
- "Depleting evil and thieving wind": Wang Bing explains depleting evil as evil that enters through depletion, and thieving wind as wind that secretly harms the central harmony.
- "At the proper times": Wang Bing refers to the eight nodal terms (the beginnings of the four seasons, the equinoxes and the solstices) and the days when Taiyi moves.
- According to the Song editors, Quan Yuanqi's edition, the *Taisu* and the *Qianjin* read the opening as "In the teaching of the sages of high antiquity, those below all acted accordingly."
- Wikisource (the plain text) writes 恬淡 for 恬惔 (same meaning).
