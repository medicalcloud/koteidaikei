# 素問 上古天真論篇第一 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Of old there was Huangdi. From birth he was spiritually gifted; while still an infant he could speak; as a child he was quick and orderly; growing up he was sincere and perceptive; and when grown he ascended to heaven. He then asked the Heavenly Master: "I have heard that the people of high antiquity all passed a hundred years of age and yet their movements did not decline, whereas people today are all declining in their movements at the age of fifty. Is it that the times are different? Or is it that people have lost [the way]?"

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "as a child he was quick and orderly ... ascended to heaven"] [Huangdi was] the son of Shaodian, ruler of the state of Youxiong; his surname was Gongsun. *Xun* means quick, *dun* means sincere, *min* means penetrating. Skilled in the use of shield and halberd, he punished those who did not pay tribute, pacified the realm, destroyed Chiyou, reigned by the virtue of Earth, and made his capital at the hill of Xuanyuan; hence he is called Xuanyuan Huangdi. Later he cast a tripod at Mount Dinghu, and when the tripod was finished he rose to heaven in broad daylight. His ministers buried his robes and cap at Mount Qiao; the tomb still exists today.

[On "the Heavenly Master"] The Heavenly Master is Qibo.

## Notes

The opening of the chapter. After describing Huangdi's innate gifts, it has him ask Qibo why the people of antiquity lived long while people today decline early.
- "Ascended to heaven": Wang Bing reads this according to the legend that Huangdi cast a tripod at Dinghu and rose to heaven; the translation follows him. Another reading takes it as "ascended the throne".
- *Xunqi*: Wang Bing glosses *xun* as "quick".
- "Heavenly Master" is an honorific for Qibo.
- The two alternatives in the question ("the times are different" / "people have lost the way") frame Qibo's answer in units 002 and 003.
