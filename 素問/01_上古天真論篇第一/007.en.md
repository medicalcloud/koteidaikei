# 素問 上古天真論篇第一 §7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Therefore cravings could not weary their eyes, and lewdness and evil could not delude their hearts. The foolish and the wise, the worthy and the unworthy, were not afraid of things; thus they accorded with the Way.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "cravings could not weary their eyes, and lewdness and evil could not delude their hearts"] Because the eyes do not look recklessly, cravings cannot weary them; because the heart is one with the mysterious, lewdness and evil cannot delude it. The *Laozi* says: "Not displaying what is desirable keeps the heart from disorder," and also: "The sage acts for the belly, not for the eyes."

[On "The foolish and the wise ... thus they accorded with the Way"] Feeling and calculation both vanish, and one does not become a storehouse of schemes. Darkening the heart and seeing all as one, one casts aside both victory and defeat; therefore the heart and will are kept at peace and accord with the Way. Gengsang Chu says: "Keep your body whole, embrace your life, and do not let your thoughts be anxious and restless."
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's annotated edition reads "accorded with the Way and its numbers".

## Notes

Since they were not caught by desire, people of every kind of endowment were not afraid of things, and so accorded with the Way.
- According to the Song editors, Quan Yuanqi's edition reads "accorded with the Way and its numbers".
