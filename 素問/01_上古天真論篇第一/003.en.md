# 素問 上古天真論篇第一 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

People today are not so. They take wine as their ordinary drink and recklessness as their norm; drunk, they enter the bedchamber; through desire they exhaust their essence, and through waste they scatter their true [qi]. They do not know how to keep what is full, they do not govern the spirit according to the season, they strive to please their hearts, they go against the joy of life, and their rising and resting have no measure. Therefore they decline at fifty.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "People today are not so"] They move toward the ground of death and depart from the Way.

[On "take wine as their ordinary drink"] They are sunk in drink.

[On "recklessness as their norm"] They are lacking in trustworthiness.

[On "drunk, they enter the bedchamber"] They go to excess in sex.

[On "through desire they exhaust their essence, and through waste they scatter their true [qi]"] Delighting in sex is called desire; using lightly is called waste. If one delights in sex without measure, the essence is exhausted; if one uses lightly without stopping, the true [qi] scatters. Therefore the sages cherish their essence and are grave about emitting it, so that their marrow is full and their bones are firm. The *Laozi* says: "Weaken their ambitions and strengthen their bones." Heshang Gong says: "Those who have desires destroy themselves." The "Quli" says: "Desire must not be indulged."
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 好 *hao* (fondness) for 耗 *hao* (waste).

[On "do not know how to keep what is full, do not govern the spirit according to the season"] This speaks of using lightly and indulging desire. The *Laozi* says: "To hold a vessel and fill it to the brim is not as good as stopping in time." It means that cherishing essence and preserving spirit is like holding a vessel filled to the brim: if one moves carelessly, it tips over and the heavenly true [qi] is exhausted. The *Zhengao* says: "If one cannot always be careful in affairs, one brings a hundred illnesses on oneself; how can one then blame the spirits?" This is what is meant.
Song editors: Another edition writes 解 *jie* (understand) for 時 *shi* (season).

[On "strive to please their hearts, go against the joy of life"] To use [oneself] for the pleasure of the heart's desires is to go against the joy of nourishing life. The *Laozi* says: "Excessive love must lead to great expense" — this is of that kind. To love excessively and be unable to save oneself, to discuss the Way yet think [the harm] has not yet come — this is the great calamity that cuts down life.

[On "their rising and resting have no measure. Therefore they decline at fifty"] This too comes from waste and scattering. The Way must not be departed from even for a moment; [if one departs from it,] one's life cannot run to the end of its heavenly years. The *Laozi* says: "When things reach their prime they grow old; this is called contrary to the Way; what is contrary to the Way perishes early." This is what is called departing from the Way.

## Notes

The second half of Qibo's answer: people today decline at fifty because they deplete their essence and true qi through drink, sex and desire, and keep no measure in rising and resting. In terms of the question in unit 001, the answer is that people have lost the Way.
- Wang Bing comments phrase by phrase.
- "Do not govern the spirit according to the season" (*bu shi yu shen*): according to the Song editors, another edition reads *jie* for *shi*, giving "do not understand how to govern the spirit".
- "Through waste they scatter their true [qi]": according to the Song editors, the *Jiayi jing* reads 好 *hao* "fondness" for 耗 *hao* "waste".
- *Zhen* ("true") is taken here as the true qi (heavenly true).
