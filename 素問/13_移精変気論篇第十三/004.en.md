# 素問 移精変気論篇第十三 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Complexion and pulse are what the Lord on High valued and what the former masters transmitted. In high antiquity Jiudaiji was made to regulate complexion and pulse and communicate with the spirit illumination, matching them with metal, wood, water, fire and earth, the four seasons, the eight winds and the six unions, without departing from their norms; in their changes and shifts, he observed their subtlety and knew their key. If one wishes to know their key, it is complexion and pulse. The complexion corresponds to the sun and the pulse to the moon; to seek their key constantly — that is the key. The changes of the complexion correspond to the pulses of the four seasons; this is what the Lord on High valued, matching it with the spirit illumination, and by it one keeps far from death and close to life. The way of life is thereby lengthened, and this is called the sage king. In middle antiquity, illness was treated when it arrived: with decoctions for ten days they removed the illnesses of the eight winds and five impediments; if not ended in ten days, they treated with herbal decoctions and the branches of roots, the root and the tips serving as aids; once the tip and the root were obtained, the evil qi submitted. In later ages the treatment of illness is not so. Treatment is not based on the four seasons, does not know the sun and moon, and does not examine counterflow and compliance; only when the illness has taken form do they want to treat its exterior with fine needles and its interior with decoctions. Crude practitioners, heedless, think it can be attacked; and so before the illness has ended, a new illness arises."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Complexion and pulse are what the Lord on High valued and what the former masters transmitted"] The Lord on High means the sovereign of high antiquity; the former master means Jiudaiji, teacher in the generation of Qibo's ancestors.

[On "In high antiquity Jiudaiji ... without departing from their norms"] The former master matched a white complexion and hair-like pulse with metal, corresponding to autumn; a green complexion and wiry pulse with wood, corresponding to spring; a black complexion and stony pulse with water, corresponding to winter; a red complexion and surging pulse with fire, corresponding to summer; and a yellow complexion and intermittent pulse with earth, corresponding to long summer and the four seasonal ends. By this complexion and pulse, below he matched the rest and dominance of the five phases and above he accorded with the coming and going of the four seasons. Therefore, within the six unions, though the eight winds blow and break, the usual signs are not departed from and all can be predicted. Why? Because one sees their changes and knows them. Hence the text below says:

[On "in their changes and shifts ... it is complexion and pulse"] It says that what lets one know the key subtlety of the changes and shifts of the qi of the four seasons and five phases is complexion and pulse.

[On "The complexion corresponds to the sun and the pulse to the moon ... that is the key"] That the pulse corresponds to the moon and the complexion to the sun is the standard of the times of divination and observation. Constantly seeking discrepancies of complexion and pulse is the key to diagnosing normal people.

[On "The changes of the complexion ... close to life"] Observing whether complexion and pulse are good or bad, one understands the signs of death and life; therefore one can always keep far from death and close to life.

[On "The way of life is thereby lengthened, and this is called the sage king"] The Lord on High heard the Way and diligently practised it, and the way of life was lengthened; only the sage kings did so and constantly used it.

[On "In middle antiquity ... the illnesses of the eight winds and five impediments"] The eight winds are the winds of the eight directions; the five impediments are the impediments of the skin, flesh, sinews, bones and vessels. The *Lingshu jing* says: "Wind from the east is called the infant wind; when it harms a person, it is outside in the sinews and lodges inside in the liver. Wind from the south-east is called the weak wind; it is outside in the flesh and lodges inside in the stomach. Wind from the south is called the great weak wind; it is outside in the vessels and lodges inside in the heart. Wind from the south-west is called the scheming wind; it is outside in the flesh and lodges inside in the spleen. Wind from the west is called the hard wind; it is outside in the skin and lodges inside in the lung. Wind from the north-west is called the breaking wind; it is outside in the hand greater yang vessel and lodges inside in the small intestine. Wind from the north is called the great hard wind; it is outside in the bones and lodges inside in the kidney. Wind from the north-east is called the baleful wind; it is outside in the armpits and flanks and lodges inside in the large intestine." Further, the *Bilun* says: "Those harmed by wind on *jia* and *yi* days in spring have sinew impediment; on *bing* and *ding* in summer, vessel impediment; on *geng* and *xin* in autumn, skin impediment; those harmed by evil on *ren* and *gui* in winter have bone impediment; those who meet it in the utmost yin have flesh impediment." These are the so-called illnesses of the eight winds and five impediments.
Song editors: This note cites the *Bilun*, but the present *Bilun* in the classic is not like this. It should say: the *Fenglun* (chapter 42) says, "Those harmed by wind on *jia* and *yi* in spring have liver wind; on *bing* and *ding* in summer, heart wind; those harmed by evil on *wu* and *ji* in late summer, spleen wind; those struck by evil on *geng* and *xin* in autumn, lung wind; on *ren* and *gui* in winter, kidney wind." And the *Bilun* (chapter 43) says, "When the three qi of wind, cold and damp arrive mixed and combine, there is impediment. Meeting them in winter, bone impediment; in spring, sinew impediment; in summer, vessel impediment; in the utmost yin, flesh impediment; in autumn, skin impediment."

[On "if not ended in ten days ... the evil qi submitted"] 草蘇 means a drug decoction; 草荄 means grass roots; 枝 means stems. It says that roots and shoots of various drugs are combined into a decoction so that they assist one another, and this is taken. Of drugs, some use the root, some the stem, some the branches, some the flowers and fruits, and some the root, stem, branches, flowers and fruits together; if the decoction does not remove [the illness], all are used; hence "the root and the tips serving as aids". "Once the tip and the root were obtained, the evil qi submitted" means that when the physician and the patient correspond in treatment, the evil qi all submits and follows the season in compliance. The *Tangye laoli lun* (chapter 14) says, "The illness is the root and the physician the tip; if tip and root are not obtained, the evil qi does not submit" — this refers to physician and patient not corresponding. Some say this takes 鍼 "needle" from the end of the *Biaoben lun*.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition also reads: "If its tip and root are obtained, the evil qi then disperses."

[On "In later ages ... does not examine counterflow and compliance"] The qi of the four seasons each have their location; to attack recklessly without basing oneself on that location is contrary to the ancients. The *Sishi ci nicong lun* (chapter 64) says: "The qi of spring is in the channels, of summer in the minute network vessels, of long summer in the flesh, of autumn in the skin, of winter in the bones and marrow." The practitioner should drive out and subdue the evil according to where it is. "Does not know the sun and moon" means the sun has cold and warmth, brightness and darkness, and the moon has emptiness and fullness, waning and waxing. The *Bazheng shenming lun* (chapter 26) says: "In the method of needling, one must observe the sun, moon, stars and constellations and the qi of the four seasons and eight nodes, and needle when the qi is settled. Therefore when the weather is warm and the sun bright, a person's blood is moist and flowing and the defensive qi floats, so the blood is easily drained and the qi easily moved. When the weather is cold and the sun dim, the blood congeals and is rough and the defensive qi sinks. When the moon begins to grow, the blood and qi begin to be refined and the defensive qi begins to move. When the moon is full, the blood and qi are abundant and the flesh firm. When the moon is empty, the flesh diminishes, the channels and network vessels are depleted, the defensive qi departs and the body is left alone. Therefore one regulates the blood and qi according to the season of heaven. Thus when it is cold, do not needle; when it is warm, there is no congealing; when the moon is growing, do not drain; when it is full, do not supplement; when it is empty, do not treat. This is called regulating at the right time. Following the order of heaven and the times of abundance and depletion, shifting with the light to fix the positions, stand correctly and wait for it. Therefore draining when the moon is growing is called depleting the zang; supplementing when the moon is full makes the blood and qi overflow and leaves stagnant blood in the network vessels, called double repletion; treating when the moon is empty is called disordering the channels: yin and yang become confused, the true and the evil are not distinguished, they sink and linger, the outside is depleted and the inside disordered, and licentious evils arise." This is what is meant. "Does not examine counterflow and compliance" means not carefully assessing whether the illness can be treated or not. Hence the text below says:

[On "only when the illness has taken form ... its interior with decoctions"] It speaks of a mind that is rough and careless and not precise.

[On "Crude practitioners, heedless ... a new illness arises"] 粗 means rough and careless; 兇兇 means not considering whether something is fitting. Why so? Suppose a hungry person's body and qi are emaciated and weak, and one feeds him till he is utterly full — will he not suffer cholera? It is not that giving food is an evil; it is that the time is missed and measure is exceeded. It is not that the illness resists needles, stones and decoctions; if the time is missed and measure exceeded, the harm instead increases.
Song editors: In another edition 霍 is written 害 "harm".

## Notes

Complexion and pulse are the key to judging death and life; in high antiquity Jiudaiji diagnosed by matching them with the five phases, four seasons, eight winds and six unions. In middle antiquity illness was treated with decoctions and herbal drugs once it arrived. Physicians of later ages ignore the seasons, sun and moon and prognosis, and attack with needles and decoctions only after the illness has formed, producing new illnesses.
- Wang Bing takes the eight winds as the winds of the eight directions of the *Lingshu jing* ("Jiugong bafeng") and the five impediments as those of skin, flesh, sinews, bones and vessels. The Song editors point out that the passage he cites as the *Bilun* does not match the present *Bilun* and conflates the *Fenglun* and *Bilun*.
- Wang Bing quotes at length from the *Bazheng shenming lun* (chapter 26) on the phases of the moon and needling.
- 草蘇 and 草荄: Wang Bing explains them as a drug decoction and grass roots.
