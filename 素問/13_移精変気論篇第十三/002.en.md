# 素問 移精変気論篇第十三 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "People of old lived among birds and beasts; they moved about to avoid the cold and stayed in the shade to avoid the heat. Within they had no burden of longing, and without they had no form of striving for office. It was an age of tranquility, and pathogen could not enter deeply. Therefore toxic drugs did not [need to] treat the interior nor needles and stones the exterior, and so they could end illness by shifting the essence and incantation. The present age is not so. Worry and trouble entangle the interior, and toilsome bodies harm the exterior; moreover, they lose the compliance with the four seasons and go against what suits cold and heat; thieving winds come repeatedly and depleting pathogens morning and evening; within they reach the five zang and the bones and marrow, without they harm the orifices and skin. Thus minor illnesses become severe and major illnesses are fatal, and so incantation cannot end them."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Within they had no burden of longing, and without they had no form of striving for office"] Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 臾 for 伸.

[On "It was an age of tranquility ... end illness by shifting the essence and incantation"] In ancient times they lived in nests and caves, hiding in the evening and roaming in the morning; that they lived among birds and beasts is clear. When one moves restlessly, yang becomes abundant; therefore the body is hot enough to withstand cold. Shade produces coolness; therefore staying in the shade avoids the heat. When the will discards thoughts and imaginings, there is no burden of longing within; when the heart is without wishes and desires, there is no form of striving for office without. Calmly preserving the heavenly truth, they were naturally not overcome by pathogen. Therefore by shifting the essence and changing the qi, without resorting to toxic drugs, by announcing in prayer the origin of the illness, without troubling needles and stones, it was ended.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi says: "*Zhuyou* is a spirit of the south."

[On "The present age is not so"] Their feelings long and they act on [desire], far from the Way.

## Notes

People of old lived tranquilly in nature without desires or ambitions, so pathogen did not penetrate deeply and incantation alone cured illness. People today harm themselves within and without by worry and toil and go against the seasons and cold and heat, so pathogen penetrates deeply and incantation cannot cure them.
- The Song editors' note after Wang Bing's note is written in the base text as 新校正本按全元起云祝由南方神 (with 本 for 云).
- The base's 伸官 is emended to 伸宦. The *Taisu*, the *Leijing*, Wikisource (the plain text) and the Daizhige *Chongguang buzhu* text read 宦 "official service"; this agrees with Wang Bing's explanation (pushing oneself forward to seek office), and 官 is taken as a graphic error for 宦. According to the Song editors, Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 臾 for 伸.
