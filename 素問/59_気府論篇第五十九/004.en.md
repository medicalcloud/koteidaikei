# 素問 気府論篇第五十九 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The points where the vessel qi of the hand greater yang issues are thirty-six: one each at the inner canthus; one each outside the eye; one each below the cheekbone; one each above the auricle; one each in the ear; one each at the Jugu point; one each at the point on the bone above the armpit fold; one each in the depression above the pillar bone; one each four *cun* above Tianchuang; one each at Jianjie; one each three *cun* below Jianjie; from the elbow down to the base of the little finger, six transport points each.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "thirty-six: one each at the inner canthus"] This means the two Jingming points, at the inner canthus, the meeting of five vessels: the hand and foot greater yang, foot *yangming*, and yin and yang *qiao*; one may needle one body-*fen* and retain six breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Where points are said to be meetings of several vessels yet are not mentioned under the vessels that meet there, they are listed under their principal vessel.

[On "one each outside the eye"] This means the two Tongziliao points, five body-*fen* outside the canthus, the meeting of the hand greater yang and hand and foot lesser yang; one may needle three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "one each below the cheekbone"] This means the two Quanliao points. 鼽 is 頄, the cheekbone of the face. In the depression below the cheekbone, the meeting of the hand greater yang and lesser yang; one may needle three body-*fen*.

[On "one each above the auricle"] This means the two Jiaosun points, in the middle of the outer rim above the ear, below the upper hairline, where there is a space when the mouth is open, the meeting of the hand greater yang and hand and foot lesser yang; one may needle three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes "hand *yangming*" for "hand greater yang".

[On "one each in the ear"] This means the two Tinggong points; location and needling as in the *Qixue lun*.

[On "one each at the Jugu point"] Jugu is the name of a point, in the depression between the two forked bones rising from the tip of the shoulder, the meeting of the hand *yangming* and *qiao* vessel; one may needle one and a half body-*cun*; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes five cones.

[On "one each at the point on the bone above the armpit fold"] This means the two Naoshu points, behind the shoulder and upper arm, below the great bone, in the depression on the upper edge of the shoulder blade, the meeting of the hand greater yang, *yangwei* and *qiao* vessels; take it with the arm raised; one may needle eight body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes "hand and foot greater yang".

[On "one each in the depression above the pillar bone"] This means the two Jianjing points, in the depression in the cleft on the shoulder, above the supraclavicular fossa and in front of the great bone, the meeting of the hand and foot lesser yang and *yangwei*; one may needle five body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "one each four *cun* above Tianchuang"] This means Tianchuang and Qiaoyin, four points; location and needling as in the *Qixue lun*.

[On "one each at Jianjie"] This means the two Bingfeng points, on the shoulder behind the small shoulder bone, where there is a space when the arm is raised, the meeting of four vessels: the hand greater yang and *yangming* and the hand and foot lesser yang; take it with the arm raised; one may needle five body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* gives five cones.

[On "one each three *cun* below Jianjie"] This means the two Tianzong points, in the depression below the great bone behind Bingfeng, where the vessel qi of the hand greater yang issues; one may needle five body-*fen* and retain six breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "from the elbow down to the base of the little finger, six transport points each"] The six transport points start at the tip of the finger; when the classic says "to the base of the little finger", it takes the tip as the base, speaking of the base from above. The same applies to the *yangming* and lesser yang below. The six transport points are Xiaohai, Yanggu, Wangu, Houxi, Qiangu and Shaoze; counting left and right, twelve. Their location and needling follow the *Qixue lun*.
Song editors: For the three channels of the hand greater yang, *yangming* and lesser yang below, each says "to the base of such-and-such finger"; Wang's note taking the tip as the base is wrong. On examination, the well points of the three hand yang all emerge at the lower edge of the nail at the tip of the finger; "base" here means the base of the fingernail. How could the tip be taken as the base?

## Notes

The points where the vessel qi of the hand greater yang issues.
- 晴明, 去㫮, 脾上廉, 両义骨 and 六宂 in Wang Bing's notes are presumably errors for 睛明, 眥, 胛, 叉 and 穴; the translation follows these readings.
