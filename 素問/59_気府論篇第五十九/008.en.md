# 素問 気府論篇第五十九 §8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The points where the qi of the *ren* vessel issues are twenty-eight: two in the centre of the throat; one each in the bony depressions of the breast; three *cun* below Jiuwei, the stomach duct five *cun*, and from the stomach duct down to the pubic bone six and a half *cun*, one [each *cun*] — this is the rule of the abdominal vessel. One where it branches below at the genitals; one each below the eyes; one below the lower lip; one at Yinjiao.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The points where the qi of the *ren* vessel issues are twenty-eight"] Now one point is missing.

[On "two in the centre of the throat"] This means Lianquan and Tiantu. Lianquan is below the chin, above the laryngeal prominence and below the root of the tongue, the meeting of the *yinwei* and *ren* vessels; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Tiantu is four body-*cun* below the laryngeal prominence, in the central hollow, the meeting of the *yinwei* and *ren* vessels; take it with the needle held low; one may needle one body-*cun* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "one each in the bony depressions of the breast"] This means the six points Xuanji, Huagai, Zigong, Yutang, Danzhong and Zhongting. Xuanji is one body-*cun* below Tiantu, Huagai one body-*cun* below Xuanji; Zigong, Yutang, Danzhong and Zhongting are each one body-*cun* six *fen* apart in depressions. All are where the qi of the *ren* vessel issues; take them face up; each may be needled three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones.

[On "from the stomach duct down to the pubic bone six and a half *cun*, one"] Song editors: On examination, the character 一 is suspected to be an error.

[On "three *cun* below Jiuwei ... the rule of the abdominal vessel"] Jiuwei is the name of a point in front of the heart, exactly at the end of the xiphoid process; the bone hangs down like the tail of a turtledove, hence the name. Below Jiuwei are fourteen transport points: Jiuwei, Juque, Shangwan, Zhongwan, Jianli, Xiawan, Shuifen, Qizhong, Yinjiao, Boyang, Dantian, Guanyuan, Zhongji and Qugu. Jiuwei is five body-*fen* below the xiphoid process in front of the chest, a branch of the *ren* vessel; moxibustion and needling are not allowed. In people without a xiphoid process, [Jiuwei is] one body-*cun* down from the edge of the forked bones [Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* says one and a half *cun* is the place of Jiuwei]. Next, Juque, Shangwan, Zhongwan, Jianli, Xiawan and Shuifen are successively one body-*cun* apart. Shangwan is the meeting of the foot *yangming* and hand greater yang; Zhongwan is where the three vessels of the hand greater yang, lesser yang and foot *yangming* arise. Needling Qizhong is prohibited; needling it causes a malignant ulcer in the navel, and if stool comes out, one dies and cannot be treated. Yinjiao is one body-*cun* below the navel, the meeting of the *ren* vessel and the yin thoroughfare. Boyang is one body-*cun* [and a half] below the navel. Dantian is the alarm point of the triple burner, two body-*cun* below the navel. Guanyuan is the alarm point of the small intestine, three body-*cun* below the navel, the meeting of the three foot yin and the *ren* vessel. Zhongji is one *cun* below Guanyuan, the meeting of the three foot yin. Qugu is above the pubic bone, one body-*cun* below Zhongji, a meeting of the foot *jueyin*. All fourteen are where the qi of the *ren* vessel issues. Jianli and Dantian may be needled six body-*fen* and retained seven breaths [Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes five *fen* and ten breaths]. Shangwan and Yinjiao may be needled eight body-*fen*; Xiawan and Shuifen one body-*cun*; Zhongwan and Boyang one body-*cun* two *fen*; Qugu one and a half body-*cun*, retaining seven breaths; the others one body-*cun* two *fen*. For moxibustion, Guanyuan and Zhongwan may receive seven cones each; Qizhong, Zhongji and Qugu three each; the others five. From Jiuwei down to the genitals, all are governed by the *ren* vessel; this is the rule of the abdominal vessel.
Song editors: According to this note's "the others may be needled one *cun* one [two] *fen*", Guanyuan is among them, which differs from the *Jiayi jing* and the notes to the *Qixue lun* and *Gukong lun*, which give two *cun*; the *Jiayi jing*'s measure should be followed.

[On "One where it branches below at the genitals"] This means the Huiyin point. From Qugu down to the genitals, between the two lower orifices, is this point. It is the branch network vessel of the *ren* vessel running alongside the *du* vessel, a meeting with the thoroughfare vessel; hence "one where it branches below at the genitals". One may needle two body-*cun* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes three breaths for seven.

[On "one each below the eyes"] This means the two Chengqi points, seven body-*fen* below the eye, directly below the pupil, the meeting of the yang *qiao*, *ren* vessel and foot *yangming*; one may needle three body-*fen*; moxibustion is not allowed.

[On "one below the lower lip"] This means the Chengjiang point, in front of the chin below the lower lip, the meeting of the foot *yangming* vessel and *ren* vessel; take it with the mouth open; one may needle two body-*fen* and retain five breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes six breaths.

[On "one at Yinjiao"] Yinjiao is the name of a point; location and needling as for the [*du*] vessel.

## Notes

The points where the qi of the *ren* vessel issues.
- 開元 and 逓相云 in Wang Bing's note are presumably errors for 関元 (Guanyuan) and 逓相去 "successively apart"; the translation follows these readings.
- Wang Bing's note places Boyang one *cun* below the navel (so also the Siku quanshu edition), which coincides with Yinjiao. Since fascicle 3 of the *Jiayi jing* says "Qihai, also called Boyang ... one and a half *cun* below the navel", the translation supplies [and a half].
