# 素問 気府論篇第五十九 §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Below the tongue of the foot lesser yin and the tense vessel in the pubic hair of the *jueyin*, one each; the hand lesser yin, one each; the yin and yang *qiao*, one each; where the vessel qi of the fish-margins of the hands and feet issues — in all, three hundred sixty-five points.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Below the tongue of the foot lesser yin and the tense vessel in the pubic hair of the *jueyin*, one each"] The two points of the foot lesser yin below the tongue are in front of the artery in the depression in front of Renying; this is the root of sun and moon, two left and right; where the vessel qi of the foot lesser yin issues; one may needle four body-*fen*. The tense vessel is in the pubic hair, two and a half body-*cun* apart on both sides above the genitals; when pressed it is hard under the finger, and when pressed hard the pain draws up and down. The one on the left, when struck by cold, draws up to the lower abdomen and down to the testicles, readily causing pain and tension of the lower abdomen; this is cold strike. Through both these vessels runs the great network vessel of the *jueyin*; hence "the tense vessel of the *jueyin*". It is the connector of the testicles. Moxibustion is allowed but not needling; for hernia with lower abdominal pain, moxibustion may be applied.
Song editors: On examination, the points below the tongue and in the pubic hair are not in the *Jiayi jing*.

[On "the hand lesser yin, one each"] This means the cleft point of the hand lesser yin, half a body-*cun* behind the wrist, the cleft of the hand lesser yin; one may needle three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones; two, left and right.

[On "the yin and yang *qiao*, one each"] The one of the yin *qiao* is the Jiaoxin point, two body-*cun* above the inner ankle, in front of the lesser yin and behind the greater yin, between sinew and bone, the cleft of the yin *qiao*; one may needle four body-*fen* and retain five breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. The one of the yang *qiao* is the Fuyang point, three body-*cun* above the outer ankle, in front of the greater yang and behind the lesser yang, between sinew and bone; take it carefully; the cleft of the yang *qiao*; one may needle six body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Four, left and right.

[On "where the vessel qi of the fish-margins of the hands and feet issues — in all, three hundred sixty-five points"] What is given in the classic is more, by nineteen points in all. These are the so-called qi residences. But scattered points exist in the regions of all the channels; therefore the classic sometimes does not mention them, and the numbers in the channel courses of the *Jiayi jing* differ — this is the reason.

## Notes

Lists the remaining points and states that there are three hundred sixty-five in all; this ends the chapter.
- The meaning of 日月本 "root of sun and moon" in Wang Bing's note is unclear; the translation keeps the characters.
- Wang Bing's final sentence presumably states that the points listed in the text exceed three hundred sixty-five.
- Daizhige (重广补注黄帝内经素问) has 巻十六 at the end of the chapter; this marks the juan division in that source.
