# 素問 気府論篇第五十九 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The points where the vessel qi of the foot *yangming* issues are sixty-eight: three each beside the hairline of the forehead; one each in the hollow of the cheekbone; one each in the bone hollow of Daying; one each at Renying; one each in the bone hollow outside Quepen; one each between the bones of the breast; beside and outside Jiuwei, three *cun* below the nipples, alongside the stomach duct, five each; alongside the navel three *cun* wide, three each; two *cun* below the navel alongside it, three each; one each at the artery of Qijie; one each above Futu; from Sanli down to the middle toe, eight transport points each — the point hollows where it divides.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "sixty-eight: three each beside the hairline of the forehead"] This means Xuanlu, Yangbai and Touwei, six points left and right, counted transversely along the frontal hairline. Xuanlu is above the curved corner, in the middle of the temple, where the vessel qi of the foot *yangming* issues; needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Yangbai is one body-*cun* above the eyebrow, directly above the pupil, the meeting of the foot *yangming* and *yinwei*; one may needle three body-*fen*, moxa three cones. Touwei is at the corner of the forehead at the hairline, one and a half body-*cun* to each side of Benshen, the meeting of the foot lesser yang and *yangming*; one may needle five body-*fen*; moxibustion is prohibited.
Song editors: In the *Jiayi jing*, Yangbai is the meeting of the foot lesser yang and *yangwei*; Mr Wang's note says the meeting of the foot *yangming* and *yinwei*. On examination, since it is among the points where the foot *yangming* vessel qi issues, "foot *yangming*" is close, but the *yangming* channel does not reach here and does not meet the *yinwei*. Wang's note is suspected to be wrong and the *Jiayi jing* right.

[On "one each in the hollow of the cheekbone"] This means the Sibai point, one body-*cun* below the eye, where the vessel qi of the foot *yangming* issues; one may needle four body-*fen*; moxibustion is not allowed.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* gives needling three *fen* and moxibustion seven cones.

[On "one each in the bone hollow of Daying"] Daying is the name of a point, at the artery in the bone depression one body-*cun* three *fen* in front of the angle of the jaw, where the vessel qi of the foot *yangming* issues; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "one each at Renying"] Renying is the name of a point, beside the laryngeal prominence on the neck where the great pulse is felt, where the vessel qi of the foot *yangming* issues; one may needle four body-*fen*; too deep, it kills; moxibustion is prohibited.

[On "one each in the bone hollow outside Quepen"] This means the two Tianliao points, in the depression at the corner of the upper hidden bone in the supraclavicular fossa of the shoulder, the meeting of the hand and foot lesser yang and *yangwei*; one may needle eight body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 毖骨 for 伏骨.

[On "one each between the bones of the breast"] This means Yingchuang and others, six points. Yingchuang is on both sides of the chest, four body-*cun* from the central line on each side, in the depression four body-*cun* eight *fen* below the clavicle, where the vessel qi of the foot *yangming* issues; take it face up; one may needle four body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones. Above this point are Qihu, Kufang and Wuyi, and below are Ruzhong and Rugen. Qihu is below the clavicle, directly above Yingchuang, four body-*cun* eight *fen* above it; Kufang is one body-*cun* six *fen* below Qihu; Wuyi is three body-*cun* two *fen* below Qihu; below that is Yingchuang; below Yingchuang is Ruzhong; the depression one body-*cun* six *fen* below Ruzhong is Rugen. All are where the vessel qi of the foot *yangming* issues; take them face up. Needling and moxibustion of Ruzhong are prohibited; if applied, an eroding sore may unfortunately arise: if the sore contains clear fluid or pus and blood, it can be treated; if it contains a fleshy growth or becomes an eroding sore, one dies. The other five points may all be needled four body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* gives five cones.

[On "beside and outside Jiuwei, three *cun* below the nipples, alongside the stomach duct, five each"] This means the five points Burong, Chengman, Liangmen, Guanmen and Taiyi, ten in all left and right, four body-*cun* to each side of the central line of the abdomen [Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* says two *cun* each; this note is suspected to have an extra 各]. Burong is at the end of the fourth rib, and down to Taiyi each is one body-*cun* apart above and below; all are where the vessel qi of the foot *yangming* issues; one may needle eight body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones.
Song editors: In the *Jiayi jing* Burong is needled five *fen*; this note's "all eight *fen*" is suspected to be an error.

[On "alongside the navel three *cun* wide, three each"] 広 means the transverse distance from the navel. "Three *cun* wide" means each at the same distance as Taiyi. "Three each" means Huaroumen, Tianshu and Wailing. Huaroumen is one body-*cun* below Taiyi; Tianshu is one body-*cun* below Huaroumen, exactly level with the navel; Wailing is one body-*cun* below Tianshu. All are where the vessel qi of the foot *yangming* issues. Tianshu may be needled five body-*fen*, retaining seven breaths; Huaroumen and Wailing may each be needled eight body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones each.
Song editors: In the *Jiayi jing*, Tianshu is two *cun* to each side of the navel, with Huaroumen above and Wailing below; these three points are each two *cun* from the navel. Here the classic and note say three *cun* wide, so the *Suwen* and *Jiayi jing* differ; but the *Jiayi jing*'s measurements agree with other books, and only this classic differs.

[On "two *cun* below the navel alongside it, three each"] Two *cun* below the navel is one body-*cun* below Wailing, the Daju point. "Three each" means Daju, Shuidao and Guilai. Daju is one body-*cun* below Wailing, where the vessel qi of the foot *yangming* issues; one may needle eight body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones. Shuidao is three body-*cun* below Daju, where the vessel qi of the foot *yangming* issues; one may needle two and a half body-*cun*; for moxibustion, five cones. Guilai is two body-*cun* below Shuidao; one may needle eight body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones.

[On "one each at the artery of Qijie"] Qijie is the name of a point, below Guilai and one body-*cun* above the groin, where the pulse is felt, where the vessel qi of the foot *yangming* issues; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: On examination, this note agrees with the *Jiayi jing*. The notes to *Cire* and the heat points say "at both ends of the transverse bone below the navel, above the groin"; the note to the *Cijin lun* says "below the abdomen, four *cun* apart on both sides of the navel, above the groin"; the note to *Gukong* says "on both sides of the hairline, above the groin". The notes differ; all are recorded here.

[On "one each above Futu"] This means the two Biguan points, above the knee, in the crossing division behind Futu; one may needle six body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "from Sanli down to the middle toe ... where it divides"] This means the eight points Sanli, Shanglian, Xialian, Jiexi, Chongyang, Xiangu, Neiting and Lidui; counting left and right, sixteen transport points. Shanglian is the uniting point of the foot *yangming* with the large intestine, and Xialian with the small intestine. Their location and needling follow the *Qixue lun*. "The point hollows where it divides" means that the foot *yangming* vessel divides at the Sanli point and runs down: its straight course follows the shin, passes the instep, enters the middle toe and emerges at its tip, which is Lidui; its branch runs together with the straight course to the instep and enters the space next to the middle toe; hence "the point hollows where it divides". 之 means go: dividing, each goes to the point hollows between the toes.

## Notes

The points where the vessel qi of the foot *yangming* issues.
- 左右共一寸 in Wang Bing's note is presumably an error for 左右共十穴 "ten points left and right"; the translation gives "ten".
- 入分, 朣 and 炙 in Wang Bing's notes are presumably errors for 八分, 瞳 and 灸.
- 伏莬 is Futu (伏兎).
