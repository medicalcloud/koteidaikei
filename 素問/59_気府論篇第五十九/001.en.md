# 素問 気府論篇第五十九 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The points where the vessel qi of the foot greater yang issues are seventy-eight: one each at the heads of both eyebrows; entering the hairline to the nape three and a half *cun*, five beside, three *cun* apart; those where its floating qi is in the skin, five lines in all with five in each line, five times five, twenty-five; one each on both sides of the great sinew in the nape; one each on both sides of Fengfu; alongside the back down to the tailbone, twenty-one vertebrae, one each in fifteen spaces; the transport points of the five zang, five each; the transport points of the six fu, six each; from Weizhong down to beside the little toe, six transport points each

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The points where the vessel qi of the foot greater yang issues are seventy-eight"] Including those where the qi floats thinly and communicates, it should be ninety-three, not seventy-eight: the points where the main vessel meets and issues are seventy-eight, and those where it floats thinly and communicates are fifteen; that is the count.

[On "one each at the heads of both eyebrows"] This means the Cuanzhu point; its location and needling depth and moxa cones follow the same method as in the *Qixue lun*.

[On "entering the hairline to the nape three and a half *cun*, five beside, three *cun* apart"] This means Dazhu and Fengmen, two points each; location and needling depth and moxa cones as in the *Qixue lun*.
Song editors: Another edition says "entering the hairline to the vertex three *cun*", and the note says "*cun* means body-*cun*; all *cun* follow the same method", which is entirely different from this note. This note says Dazhu and Fengmen, two each, with location and needling as in the *Qixue lun*; but the present *Qixue lun* has no Fengmen point, yet the note says "the same method". That this note is wrong is evident; the error is not Mr Wang's but a later person's. On examination, "entering the hairline to the nape three and a half *cun*, five beside, three *cun* apart" presumably describes the points of the following "floating qi in the skin, five lines with five in each". Hence Wang gave no explanation and only said "*cun* means body-*cun*". It is simply that 頂 "vertex" was miswritten 項 "nape" and the character 半 is superfluous. "Entering the hairline to the vertex" means from the Xinhui point inside the hairline to Baihui on the vertex is three *cun*, and from Baihui back to Houding again three *cun*; hence "entering the hairline to the vertex three *cun*". "Five beside" means counting the four lines on the sides together there are five lines. "Three *cun* apart" presumably means counting three *cun* each to left, right, front and back from Baihui at the vertex, five lines with five in each, twenty-five points in all. Later people mistook 頂 for 項 and took it as Dazhu and Fengmen, which is a serious error. Moreover, Dazhu is on both sides below the first vertebra and Fengmen below the second; from the hairline above, the distance is not just three and a half *cun*; the error is very clear.

[On "those where its floating qi is in the skin, five lines ... twenty-five"] Floating qi means qi that floats and passes through them and can remove heat. The five lines run on the head from two body-*cun* inside the hairline back to behind the vertex. Of the twenty-five, the central line is Xinhui, Qianding, Baihui, Houding and Qiangjian, five, the qi of the *du* vessel; the two lines beside it are Wuchu, Chengguang, Tongtian, Luoque and Yuzhen, five each, the qi of this channel; the next two lines beside are Linqi, Muchuang, Zhengying, Chengling and Naokong, five each, the qi of the foot lesser yang. The four side lines with five each make twenty points, and the central line's five make twenty-five. Their needling and moxibustion follow the same method as in the *Shuire xue lun*.

[On "one each on both sides of the great sinew in the nape"] This means the two Tianzhu points; location and needling as in the *Qixue lun*.

[On "one each on both sides of Fengfu"] This means the two Fengchi points; needling as in the *Qixue lun*.
Song editors: According to the *Jiayi jing*, Fengchi is the meeting of the foot lesser yang and *yangwei*, not where the greater yang issues. The classic's "both sides of Fengfu" is the position of the Tianzhu points, again restating the points "on both sides of the great sinew in the nape" above. This note adds the two Fengchi points beyond the count of ninety-three, and together with Dazhu and Fengmen above adds six points in excess.

[On "alongside the back down to the tailbone ... one each in fifteen spaces"] Of "one each in fifteen spaces", thirteen points survive in the present *Zhonggao kongxue tujing*, twenty-six left and right: Fufen, Pohu, Shentang, Yixi, Geguan, Hunmen, Yanggang, Yishe, Weicang, Huangmen, Zhishi, Baohuang and Zhibian. Fufen is below the second vertebra on both sides of the inner edge attached to the nape, three body-*cun* from the spine on each side, a meeting of the foot greater yang; one may needle eight body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones. Pohu is on both sides below the third vertebra, directly below Fufen, where the vessel qi of the foot greater yang issues — the twelve points below are the same; take it sitting upright; one may needle five body-*fen*; moxibustion as for Fufen. Shentang is on both sides below the fifth vertebra, directly below Pohu; one may needle three body-*fen*; moxibustion as for Fufen. Yixi is on both sides below the sixth vertebra, directly below Shentang [Song editors: The note to the *Gukong lun* says, "Press it with the hand and have the patient call out 'yixi'; then it moves under the finger"]; one may needle six body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; moxibustion as for Fufen. Geguan is on both sides below the seventh vertebra, directly below Yixi; take it sitting upright with the shoulders opened; one may needle five body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones [Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* gives five cones]. Hunmen is on both sides below the ninth vertebra, directly below Geguan; take it sitting upright; needling and moxibustion as for Geguan. Yanggang is on both sides below the tenth vertebra, directly below Hunmen; take it sitting upright; needling and moxibustion as for Hunmen. Yishe is on both sides below the eleventh vertebra, directly below Yanggang; take it sitting upright; needling and moxibustion as for Yanggang. Weicang is on both sides below the twelfth vertebra, directly below Yishe; needling and moxibustion as for Yishe. Huangmen is on both sides below the thirteenth vertebra, directly below Weicang; needling as for Weicang; moxibustion up to thirty cones [Song editors: Thirty cones for Huangmen agrees with the *Jiayi jing*; the note on the water points writes three cones]. Zhishi is on both sides below the fourteenth vertebra, directly below Huangmen; take it sitting upright; needling and moxibustion as for Pohu. Baohuang is on both sides below the nineteenth vertebra, directly below Zhishi; take it lying prone; needling and moxibustion as for Pohu [Song editors: Five cones for Zhishi and Baohuang as for Pohu is written three cones in the *Jiayi jing*; the note on the water points also writes three, and the heat-point method also gives three for Zhishi]. Zhibian is on both sides below the twenty-first vertebra, directly below Baohuang; take it lying prone; needling and moxibustion as for Pohu.

[On "the transport points of the five zang, five each; the transport points of the six fu, six each"] The lung transport point is on both sides below the third vertebra, one and a half body-*cun* from the spine on each side; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. The heart transport point is on both sides below the fifth vertebra, distance and needling as for the lung point, retaining seven breaths. The liver point is on both sides below the ninth vertebra, distance and needling as for the heart point, retaining six breaths. The spleen point is on both sides below the eleventh vertebra, distance and needling as for the liver point, retaining seven breaths. The kidney point is on both sides below the fourteenth vertebra, distance and needling as for the spleen point, retaining seven breaths. The gallbladder point is on both sides below the tenth vertebra, distance as for the lung point; take it sitting upright; one may needle five body-*fen* and retain seven breaths. The stomach point is on both sides below the twelfth vertebra, distance and needling as for the spleen point, retaining seven breaths. The triple burner point is on both sides below the thirteenth vertebra, distance and needling as for the gallbladder point. The large intestine point is on both sides below the sixteenth vertebra, distance and needling as for the lung point, retaining six breaths. The small intestine point is on both sides below the eighteenth vertebra, distance and needling as for the heart point, retaining six breaths. The bladder point is on both sides below the nineteenth vertebra, distance and needling as for the kidney point, retaining six breaths. For moxibustion, all the transport points of the five zang and six fu may receive three cones.
Song editors: On examination, some doubt 各五 and 各六 in the classic and take 各 as an error, but this is not so. 各 is used because it means five each and six each on left and right, not five or six for every zang or fu.

[On "from Weizhong down to beside the little toe, six transport points each"] This means the six points Weizhong, Kunlun, Jinggu, Shugu, Tonggu and Zhiyin; counting left and right, twelve transport points. Their location and needling follow the method of the *Qixue lun*. The classic says the points where the vessel qi issues are seventy-eight, but what is here amounts in all to ninety-three. Thus the total is in error; there are copying mistakes.
Song editors: On examination, Mr Wang says ninety-three in all, but including Dazhu, Fengmen and Fengchi there are now ninety-nine. Calculating from Mr Wang's total, it is clear these three points were wrongly added later.

## Notes

This chapter lists the points where the vessel qi of each channel issues (the "qi residences"); it contains no dialogue. This unit concerns the foot greater yang. Wang Bing's notes give the location and needling of each point; Song editors' remarks inserted within the notes are given in square brackets.
- The final 也 of this sentence is placed at the beginning of unit 002 in the base text's division into units.
- For "entering the hairline to the nape three and a half *cun*, five beside, three *cun* apart", Wang Bing's note gives Dazhu and Fengmen, but the Song editors regard this as a later error and take it as the five lines of points on the head, reading 頂 "vertex" for 項.
- 魄門 in Wang Bing's note, as a point on both sides below the ninth vertebra, is presumably an error for 魂門 (Hunmen); the translation gives Hunmen.
- 祑辺 in Wang Bing's note is Zhibian (秩辺).
