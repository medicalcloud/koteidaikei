# 素問 陰陽離合論篇第六 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "What is outside is yang and what is inside is yin. Thus the centre is yin. Its thoroughfare is below, [and above it] is named greater yin. The greater yin has its root at Yinbai; it is named the yin within yin. Behind the greater yin is named lesser yin. The lesser yin has its root at Yongquan; it is named the lesser yin within yin. In front of the lesser yin is named *jueyin*. *Jueyin* has its root at Dadun; it is the yin cut off from yang, named the cut-off yin of yin. Thus, in the separation and union of the three yin, greater yin is the opening, *jueyin* the closing and lesser yin the pivot. The three channels must not lose one another; when [the pulse] beats without sinking, it is named one yin.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "What is outside is yang and what is inside is yin"] This says that the three yang are the separation and union that circulate outside, and the three yin are the separation and union that function inside.

[On "Thus the centre is yin. Its thoroughfare is below, [and above it] is named greater yin"] The *chong* vessel is below the spleen, hence "its thoroughfare is below". The *Lingshu jing* says: "The *chong* vessel and the network vessel of the foot lesser yin both start below the kidney; the branch that ascends passes through the womb." By this, above the thoroughfare is the position of the greater yin.

[On "The greater yin has its root at Yinbai; it is named the yin within yin"] Yinbai is the name of a point at the tip of the big toe. Because the greater yin dwells in yin, it is called the yin within yin.

[On "Behind the greater yin is named lesser yin"] This is the order of the positions of the zang and of the channels. The greater yin is the spleen and the lesser yin the kidney; below the spleen and near the back is the position of the kidney. The *Lingshu jing* says: "The foot greater yin vessel starts at the tip of the big toe, follows the inner side of the toe, rises along the front edge of the inner ankle, ascends inside the calf and follows behind the shin bone. The foot lesser yin vessel starts below the little toe, runs obliquely to the sole, emerges below the navicular bone, follows behind the inner ankle and rises inside the calf." By this, below the greater yin is named lesser yin.

[On "The lesser yin has its root at Yongquan; it is named the lesser yin within yin"] Yongquan is the name of a point on the sole, in the hollow formed when the toes are curled.

[On "In front of the lesser yin is named *jueyin*"] This too is the order of the positions of the zang and of the channels. The lesser yin is the kidney and *jueyin* the liver; in front of the kidney and near the top is the position of the liver. The *Lingshu jing* says: "The foot lesser yin vessel follows behind the inner ankle and rises along the inner edge of the calf. The foot *jueyin* vessel follows the upper edge of the instep, one *cun* from the inner ankle, and eight *cun* above the ankle crosses and emerges behind the greater yin, rising inside the knee." For this reason, in front of the lesser yin is named *jueyin*.

[On "*Jueyin* has its root at Dadun; it is the yin cut off from yang, named the cut-off yin of yin"] Dadun is the name of a point at the tip of the big toe, among the three hairs. Because two yin combine, it is called "the yin cut off from yang". 厥 means exhausted; the yin qi is exhausted here, hence it is named "the cut-off yin of yin".

[On "Thus, in the separation and union of the three yin, greater yin is the opening, *jueyin* the closing and lesser yin the pivot"] This too is because the qi are unequal.
Song editors: The *Jiuxu* says: "When the barrier breaks, the granary has nothing to transport; those with blockage and outpouring diarrhoea take the greater yin. When the closing breaks, the qi slackens and one is prone to grief; those with grief take the *jueyin*. When the pivot breaks, the vessels have places that knot and do not pass; those with obstruction take the lesser yin." The *Jiayi jing* is the same.

[On "The three channels must not lose one another; when [the pulse] beats without sinking, it is named one yin"] "Sinking" means appearing differently; the floating of yang is likewise. If the channel qi arrives in response without difference of sinking and floating, it can all be called the qi of one yin, and there is no longer the different function of the three yin descending in gradation.

## Notes

The separation and union of the three yin. The positions of greater yin, lesser yin and *jueyin* and their roots (the well points Yinbai, Yongquan and Dadun) are given; greater yin is the opening, *jueyin* the closing and lesser yin the pivot. When the three channels are in harmony and the pulse does not sink, it is called one yin.
- "The yin cut off from yang, named the cut-off yin of yin": Wang Bing explains that two yin combine and the yin qi is exhausted here.
- According to the Song editors, the *Jiuxu* and the *Jiayi jing* also write "barrier" for "opening" for the three yin and describe the illnesses when each breaks.
- In Wang Bing's note the base text has a blank in 陰　之絶陰.
- Qibo's speech continues into the next unit (the end of the chapter).
