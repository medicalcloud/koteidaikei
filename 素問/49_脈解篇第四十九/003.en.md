# 素問 脈解篇第四十九 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In the *yangming*, what is called shivering with cold: the *yangming* is *wu*; the fifth month is the yin within abundant yang. Yang is abundant and yin qi is added to it; therefore there is shivering with cold. What is called swollen shins and thighs that cannot be drawn in: the fifth month is the yin within abundant yang; yang declines in the fifth month, and one yin qi rises and begins to contend with yang; therefore the shins swell and the thighs cannot be drawn in. What is called panting above and water: yin qi descends and rises again; when it rises, evil lodges between the zang and fu; therefore there is water. What is called chest pain and shortness of breath: water qi is in the zang and fu; water is yin qi; yin qi is within; therefore there are chest pain and shortness of breath. What is called reversal when severe, dislike of people and fire, and fright on hearing the sound of wood: yang qi and yin qi press on each other, water and fire dislike each other; therefore one is startled with fright. What is called the wish to stay alone with doors and windows closed: yin and yang press on each other; yang is exhausted and yin abundant; therefore one wishes to stay alone with doors and windows closed. What is called the urge, when the illness comes, to climb high and sing, throw off clothes and run: yin and yang contend again and combine outwardly in yang; therefore one throws off clothes and runs. What is called headache, nasal congestion and abdominal swelling when [evil] lodges in the minute vessels: the *yangming* combines above; above means its minute network vessels and the greater yin; therefore there are headache, nasal congestion and abdominal swelling.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In the *yangming*, what is called shivering with cold ... the yin within abundant yang"] Yang is abundant and bright; hence *wu*. At the summer solstice in the fifth month one yin qi rises and the yang qi descends; hence "the yin within abundant yang".

[On "Yang is abundant and yin qi is added to it ... shivering with cold"] Yang qi descends and yin qi rises; hence "yang is abundant and yin qi is added to it".

[On "What is called swollen shins ... cannot be drawn in"] Its vessel descends to the thigh, reaches Futu, enters the kneecap, descends along the outer edge of the shin to the instep and enters the inner side of the middle toe; a branch separates three *cun* below the knee and enters the outer side of the middle toe; hence this.

[On "What is called panting above and water ... there is water"] The zang is the spleen and the fu the stomach. The foot greater yin vessel runs from the foot to the abdomen; the foot *yangming* vessel runs from the head to the foot. Now yin qi descends slightly and the greater yin rises; hence "yin qi descends and rises again". When it rises again, the yin qi that descended does not disperse but lodges between spleen and stomach and transforms into water.

[On "What is called chest pain and shortness of breath ... shortness of breath"] When water stops below, qi stagnates above; when qi stagnates above, the lung is full; therefore there are chest pain and shortness of breath.

[On "What is called the wish to stay alone ... doors and windows closed"] One dislikes noise; hence this.

[On "What is called the urge ... throws off clothes and runs"] Song editors: On examination, from "reversal when severe" to here corresponds to the earlier *Yangming maijie lun*.

## Notes

The symptoms of *yangming* illness explained by the state of yin and yang in the fifth month (*wu*). The latter part overlaps with the *Yangming maijie* (chapter 30).
