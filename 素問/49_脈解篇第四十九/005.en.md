# 素問 脈解篇第四十九 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In the lesser yin, what is called lumbar pain: the lesser yin is the kidney; in the tenth month the yang qi of all things is harmed; therefore the lumbus aches. What is called vomiting, coughing, rising qi and panting: yin qi is below, yang qi above, and all the yang qi floats with nothing to rely on; therefore there are vomiting, coughing, rising qi and panting. What is called *sese*, inability to stand or sit for long, and on rising the eyes blurred and seeing nothing: the yin and yang of all things are unsettled and have no master yet; the autumn qi has just arrived, light frost has just begun to fall, and it is about to kill all things; yin and yang are robbed within; therefore the eyes are blurred and see nothing. What is called shortness of breath and proneness to anger: the yang qi is not in order; when it is not in order, the yang qi cannot come out; the liver qi should govern but has not yet attained it; therefore one is prone to anger. Proneness to anger is called boiling reversal. What is called fear as if someone were about to seize one: in the autumn qi all things have not yet entirely gone; yin qi is scant and yang qi enters; yin and yang press on each other; therefore there is fear. What is called aversion to the smell of food: the stomach has no qi; therefore one dislikes the smell of food. What is called a black face like the colour of earth: the autumn qi robs within; therefore the colour changes. What is called blood with coughing: the yang vessels are harmed; the yang qi is not yet abundant above but the vessels are full; when full there is coughing; therefore blood appears in the nose.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In the lesser yin, what is called lumbar pain ... the lumbus aches"] The lesser yin is the kidney vessel; the lumbus is the house of the kidney; therefore the lumbus aches.

[On "What is called vomiting, coughing, rising qi and panting ... panting"] Because its vessel rises from the kidney through the liver and diaphragm and enters the lung; hence such illness.

[On "What is called *sese*"] Song editors: On examination, the characters 色色 are suspected to be an error.

## Notes

The symptoms of lesser yin illness explained by the state of yin and yang in the tenth month and autumn.
- 色色 is suspected by the Song editors to be an error; its meaning is unclear, and the translation keeps the characters.
- The term "boiling reversal" (煎厥) also appears in the *Shengqi tongtian lun* (chapter 3).
- The base text writes 𤾁 for 鼻 "nose" (other sources write 鼻); the translation gives "nose".
