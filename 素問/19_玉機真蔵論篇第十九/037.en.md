# 素問 玉機真蔵論篇第十九 §37 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Therefore wind is the chief of all illnesses. Now when wind and cold lodge in a person, making the body hair stand on end and the skin close up and become hot, at this time one can release it by sweating. Or there may be impediment and numbness with swelling and pain; at this time one can remove it with hot compresses, fire, moxa and needling. If untreated, the illness enters and lodges in the lung; it is called lung impediment, with coughing and rising qi. If untreated, the lung then transmits it on to the liver; the illness is called liver impediment, also called reversal, with pain in the flanks and vomiting of food; at this time one can massage or needle. If untreated, the liver transmits it to the spleen; the illness is called spleen wind, with jaundice, heat in the abdomen, vexation of the heart and passing of yellow; at this time one can massage, give drugs or bathe. If untreated, the spleen transmits it to the kidney; the illness is called conglomeration, with pent-up heat and pain in the lower abdomen and passing of white, also called *gu* (worm illness); at this time one can massage or give drugs. If untreated, the kidney transmits it to the heart; the illness is the sinews and vessels pulling against each other and becoming tense, called convulsion; at this time one can apply moxa or give drugs. If untreated, when ten days are complete, as a rule there will be death. If the kidney thus transmits it to the heart, and the heart then turns back and passes it on to the lung, cold and heat break out, and as a rule death comes in three years. This is the order of illness.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Therefore wind is the chief of all illnesses"] It means it comes before all illnesses.
Song editors: The *Shengqi tongtian lun* (chapter 3) says, "Wind is the beginning of all illnesses."

[On "Now when wind and cold lodge in a person ... become hot"] 客 means lodging and stopping in the body. Wind strikes the skin and cold prevails in the interstices; therefore the body hair stands on end, the sweat pores close tightly and heat arises.

[On "at this time one can release it by sweating"] The evil is in the skin and hair; therefore it can be released by sweating. The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* (chapter 5) says, "Those good at treatment treat the skin and body hair" — this is what is meant.

[On "Or there may be impediment and numbness with swelling and pain"] The illness arises and changes; therefore it is like this. When heat strikes the blood and qi there is numbness; when cold qi harms the form there is swelling and pain. The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* says: "Cold harms form; heat harms qi; when qi is harmed there is pain; when form is harmed there is swelling."

[On "at this time one can remove it with hot compresses, fire, moxa and needling"] All mean releasing and dispersing the cold evil and spreading the correct qi.

[On "If untreated, the illness enters and lodges in the lung ... with coughing and rising qi"] When evil enters the yin, there is illness of impediment; therefore entering the lung is called impediment. The *Xuanming wuqi lun* (chapter 23) says, "When evil enters the yang there is mania; when evil enters the yin there is impediment." The change of the lung is coughing; when one coughs the qi rises; hence rising qi.

[On "If untreated, the lung then transmits it on to the liver ... vomiting of food"] Lung-metal cuts down wood, and the qi descends into the liver; hence "if untreated, it goes to the liver". The liver qi communicates with the gallbladder, and the gallbladder is prone to anger; anger is counterflow of qi; hence it is also called reversal. The liver's *jueyin* vessel goes from the lower abdomen to belong to the liver and connect with the gallbladder, rises through the diaphragm, spreads over the flanks and ribs, follows behind the throat and enters the nasopharynx; therefore the flanks ache and food entering the abdomen comes back out; hence "vomiting of food".

[On "If untreated, the liver transmits it to the spleen ... passing of yellow"] The liver qi corresponds to wind, and wood overcomes spleen-earth; earth receives the qi of wind; hence it is called spleen wind — named because the qi of wind communicates with the liver. Illness of the spleen readily produces jaundice; hence jaundice breaks out. The spleen's greater yin vessel enters the abdomen, belongs to the spleen, connects with the stomach, rises through the diaphragm, runs alongside the throat, connects with the root of the tongue and spreads under the tongue; its branch separates from the stomach, rises through the diaphragm and pours into the heart. Therefore the abdomen is hot, the heart vexed, and yellow colour is passed at the place of evacuation.

[On "If untreated, the spleen transmits it to the kidney ... also called *gu*"] The kidney's lesser yin vessel pierces the spine from the back inner edge of the thigh, belongs to the kidney and connects with the bladder; therefore there is pent-up heat and pain in the lower abdomen and white fluid is passed in the urine. The pent-up heat binds within, consuming the fat and flesh, wearing it away from within day by day as if worms were eating it; hence it is also called *gu*.

[On "If untreated, the kidney transmits it to the heart ... called convulsion"] When the kidney is insufficient, water is not produced; when water is not produced, the sinews dry and become tense; therefore they pull against each other. The yin qi is weak within and the yang qi burns without; the sinews and vessels receive heat and twitch of themselves; hence it is called convulsion.

[On "at this time one can apply moxa or give drugs. If untreated ... there will be death"] When it reaches the heart and the qi is at its extreme, it is like this. If it is transmitted further, it is as described below.

[On "If the kidney thus transmits it to the heart ... death comes in three years"] When it is passed from the kidney to the heart and the heart does not accept the illness, it turns back at once and passes it to lung-metal; the lung is harmed a second time; therefore there is cold and heat. Three years: from lung to kidney one year, from kidney to liver one year, from liver to heart one year, and then fire again encroaches on the lung; hence death in three years.

[On "This is the order of illness"] It means the order of transmission to what is overcome.

## Notes

Wind and cold enter through the skin and, if untreated, are transmitted in the controlling order — lung, liver, spleen, kidney, heart — until death; the treatment suitable at each stage is given.
- Wang Bing explains the signs of each stage by the courses of the channels.
- Daizhige (the plain text) has some differences, such as 盛痺 for 或痺 and 痺 for 癉.
