# 素問 玉機真蔵論篇第十九 §19 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Emperor said: "Good. The winter pulse is like a camp; what makes it camp-like?"

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On the question] It means the pulse is sunken and deep, like a camp in motion.
Song editors: 深 "deep" is written 濡 "soft" in one version and also 搏 "beating". Since the text below says "its qi comes sunken and beating", 深 should be 搏. But the *Jiayi jing* has 濡 for 搏, and one should follow the *Jiayi jing* and read 濡. Why? A pulse sunken and soft — 濡 is the ancient form of 軟 — is the normal, regulated winter pulse; if it is sunken and beats against the hand, it is an excessive winter pulse. Therefore one should follow the *Jiayi jing*'s 濡.

## Notes

Huangdi asks what it means for the winter pulse to be "camp-like".
- "Camp" (営): Wang Bing takes it as the pulse being sunken and deep, like a camp in motion.
- The Song editors argue that 搏 in "sunken and beating" below should be read as 濡 "soft", following the *Jiayi jing*.
