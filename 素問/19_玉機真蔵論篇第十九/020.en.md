# 素問 玉機真蔵論篇第十九 §20 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The winter pulse is the kidney, the north, water, by which the myriad things are gathered and stored. Therefore its qi comes sunken and beating; hence it is called camp-like. What goes against this is illness."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Therefore its qi comes sunken and beating; hence it is called camp-like"] It means sunken and beating against the hand.
Song editors: According to the *Jiayi jing*, 搏 should be 濡, as explained above. Further, Yueren says: "The winter pulse is stony; it is the north, water, where the myriad things are stored. In deep winter water congeals like stone; therefore its pulse comes sunken, soft and slippery; hence it is called stony."

## Notes

The winter pulse corresponds to the kidney, the north and water; it is sunken and beating, hence called camp-like. Elsewhere it is called stony.
- The Song editors say 搏 should be 濡 (see the previous unit).
- 滑 in Yueren's text quoted by the Song editors was supplied where the transcription gave only a component description (see the 【補】 note).
- Daizhige (the plain text) writes 含蔵 for 合蔵.
