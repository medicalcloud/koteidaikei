# 素問 玉機真蔵論篇第十九 §18 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Excess makes a person's qi counterflow, with back pain and gloomy oppression; insufficiency makes a person pant, short of breath and coughing, with rising qi and blood appearing, and sounds of illness heard below."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On the whole passage] The lung's greater yin vessel starts in the middle burner, descends to connect with the large intestine, returns along the stomach opening, rises through the diaphragm, belongs to the lung, and from the lung connector emerges crosswise below the armpit. Also [the lung] stores qi, which becomes cough, and governs breathing. Therefore when the qi is abundant, the shoulders and back ache and the qi counterflows; when insufficient, the breathing changes, one is short of breath and coughs, with rising qi and blood appearing. "Sounds of illness heard below" means that when panting there is a sound in the lung.

## Notes

Excess of the autumn pulse brings counterflow of qi and back pain; insufficiency brings panting, shortness of breath, cough, rising qi with blood, and sounds in the lung.
