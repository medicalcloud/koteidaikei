# 素問 玉機真蔵論篇第十九 §14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The autumn pulse is the lung, the west, metal, by which the myriad things are gathered and completed. Therefore its qi comes light, empty and floating, coming urgent and going scattered; hence it is called floating. What goes against this is illness."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Therefore its qi comes light, empty and floating ... hence it is called floating"] Because the pulse comes light and empty, it is called floating. It comes urgent because yang has not yet sunk down; it goes scattered because the yin qi rises.
Song editors: Yueren says: "The autumn pulse is hair-like; it is the west, metal, where the myriad things end. The flowers and leaves of grasses and trees all fall in autumn, and only the branches remain, like fine hairs; therefore its pulse comes light, empty and floating; hence it is called hair-like."

## Notes

The autumn pulse corresponds to the lung, the west and metal; it is light, empty and floating, hence called floating. Elsewhere it is called hair-like.
