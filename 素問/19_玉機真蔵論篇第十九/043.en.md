# 素問 玉機真蔵論篇第十九 §43 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Huangdi said: "In all treatment of illness, examine the form and qi, the lustre of the complexion, the abundance or decline of the pulse and whether the illness is new or old, and then treat it, without missing its time. When form and qi match, it is called treatable; when the complexion is lustrous and floating, it is called easily ended; when the pulse follows the four seasons, it is called treatable; when the pulse is weak and slippery, there is stomach qi, and it is termed easily treated — take it at the proper time. When form and qi do not match, it is called hard to treat; when the complexion is dull and lustreless, it is called hard to end; when the pulse is replete and hard, it is called worsening; when the pulse goes against the four seasons, it is untreatable. One must examine these four difficulties and announce them clearly. What is called going against the four seasons is getting a lung pulse in spring, a kidney pulse in summer, a heart pulse in autumn, or a spleen pulse in winter, all arriving suspended, cut off, sunken and rough; this is termed going against the four seasons. When there is not yet any form of the zang, [yet] in spring and summer the pulse is sunken and rough and in autumn and winter floating and large, this is called going against the four seasons. Heat illness with a quiet pulse, diarrhoea with a large pulse, loss of blood with a replete pulse, illness in the centre with a replete, hard pulse, illness outside with a pulse that is not replete and hard — all are hard to treat."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In all treatment of illness ... without missing its time"] It means one should always take it before its time.

[On "When form and qi match, it is called treatable"] Abundant qi with abundant form, depleted qi with depleted form — this is matching.

[On "when the complexion is lustrous and floating, it is called easily ended"] The complexion is floating and moist, and blood and qi nourish each other; therefore it is easily ended.

[On "when the pulse follows the four seasons, it is called treatable"] The pulse wiry in spring, hooked in summer, floating in autumn and camp-like in winter is called following the four seasons; 従 means comply.

[On "when the pulse is weak and slippery ... take it at the proper time"] If one waits for the time when it can be taken and takes it, every undertaking will be entirely sure; one should treat according to where the blood and qi are in the four seasons.
Song editors: For 取之以時 the *Jiayi jing* reads 治之趨之，旡後其時 "treat it promptly without missing its time"; both it and Mr Wang's reading make sense.

[On "When form and qi do not match, it is called hard to treat"] Abundant form with depleted qi, or abundant qi with depleted form, are all mismatches.

[On "when the complexion is dull and lustreless, it is called hard to end"] 夭 means not bright and bad; "lustreless" means withered and dry.

[On "when the pulse is replete and hard, it is called worsening"] A replete, hard pulse means abundant evil qi; therefore it worsens.

[On "when the pulse goes against the four seasons, it is untreatable"] Because the qi counterflows, there is illness. The four phrases above are called the four difficulties. Hence the text below says:

[On "One must examine these four difficulties and announce them clearly"] These four are what the crude talk about easily and what the skilled find hard to do.

[On "What is called going against the four seasons ... termed going against the four seasons"] Getting a lung pulse in spring is the autumn [pulse] coming to appear; a kidney pulse in summer is the winter [pulse] appearing; a heart pulse in autumn is the summer [pulse] appearing; a spleen pulse in winter is the spring [pulse] appearing. "Suspended and cut off" means like a hanging thing snapping off.

[On "When there is not yet any form of the zang, in spring and summer the pulse is sunken and rough"] Song editors: The *Pingren qixiang lun* has "the pulse is thin", with the same meaning.

[On "and in autumn and winter floating and large, this is called going against the four seasons"] "Not yet" means there is not yet the form of the zang pulse.

[On "Heat illness with a quiet pulse ... all are hard to treat"] All are hard to treat because they do not correspond to the signs.
Song editors: The *Pingren qixiang lun* says "illness in the centre with a depleted pulse, illness outside with a rough, hard pulse", the opposite of this. This classic is wrong and that treatise right. From "not yet any form of the zang, in spring and summer" to here overlaps with the *Pingren qixiang lun*, where the notes are fuller.

## Notes

In treatment one must examine form and qi, complexion, pulse and the age of the illness and treat without delay. It lists the easily treated (form and qi matching, lustrous complexion, pulse following the seasons, weak slippery pulse) and the four difficulties, and describes pulses that go against the seasons and pulses contradicting the illness.
- According to the Song editors, the second half overlaps with the *Pingren qixiang lun* (chapter 18), and the pulses for "illness in the centre/outside" are wrong here and right there.
- The base text writes 巳 for 已 in 易已 and 難已; translated as 已.
