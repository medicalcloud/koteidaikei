# 素問 玉機真蔵論篇第十九 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Excess makes a person prone to anger, dazed, dizzy and clouded, with illness of the vertex; insufficiency makes a person's chest ache, pulling to the back, and below both flanks are full."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On the whole passage] 忽忽 means not clear-headed; 眩 means dizziness in which things seen seem to spin; 冒 means clouding and oppression; 胠 means the flanks below the armpits. 忘 "forget" should be 怒 "anger"; it is a scribal error. The *Lingshu jing* says: "When the liver qi is in excess, there is anger." The liver's *Jueyin* vessel rises from the foot into the pubic hair, rises again through the diaphragm, spreads over the flanks and ribs, follows behind the throat, enters the nasopharynx, and emerges at the forehead to meet the *Du* meridian at the vertex; therefore the illness is like this.
Song editors: The *Qijiaobian dalun* (chapter 69) says: "When wood is excessive, in severe cases one is dazed, prone to anger, dizzy and clouded, with illness of the vertex." So 忘 should be 怒.

## Notes

Excess of the spring pulse brings dizziness and illness of the vertex; insufficiency brings pain of the chest and back and fullness of the flanks.
- Wikisource (the plain text) writes 肋 for 脅.
- The base's 善忘 "forgetful" is emended to 善怒 "prone to anger". Wang Bing says 忘 should be 怒, a scribal error, and the Song editors, citing the *Qijiaobian dalun* ("when wood is in excess, in severe cases one is dazed and prone to anger, dizzy and clouded, with illness of the vertex"), also say 忘 should be 怒. That passage in the *Leijing* and the *Taisu* also reads 善怒, and anger fits an illness of liver excess. Although Wikisource (the plain text) and the Daizhige texts all read 忘, and Zhang Zhicong's *Jizhu* explains it as 忘, 怒 is adopted following the base's own Wang Bing note and the Song editors.
