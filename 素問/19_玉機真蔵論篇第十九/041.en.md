# 素問 玉機真蔵論篇第十九 §41 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The five zang all receive qi from the stomach; the stomach is the root of the five zang. The qi of the zang cannot reach the hand greater yin by itself; it must rely on the stomach qi to reach the hand greater yin. Thus each of the five zang, in its own season, shows itself and arrives at the hand greater yin. Therefore when evil qi prevails, the essential qi declines. Therefore when the illness is severe, the stomach qi cannot reach the hand greater yin together with it, and so the qi of the true zang appears alone. Appearing alone means the illness has overcome the zang; hence it is said there is death."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The five zang all receive qi from the stomach; the stomach is the root of the five zang"] The stomach is the sea of water and grain; therefore the five zang receive from it.

[On "The qi of the zang cannot reach the hand greater yin by itself ..."] The normal person constantly receives qi from the stomach; the stomach qi is the constant qi of a normal person; therefore the qi of the zang can reach the hand greater yin only through the stomach.
Song editors: From "the normal person constantly" to "the constant qi of a normal person" is originally text from the *Pingren qixiang lun*, which Mr Wang cites here as his note. The *Jiayi jing* says, "People constantly receive qi from the stomach; the pulse takes the stomach qi as its root," which differs slightly; the meaning of the *Jiayi jing* is better.

[On "Thus each of the five zang ... arrives at the hand greater yin"] It shows its own form at the hand greater yin.

[On "and so the qi of the true zang appears alone ... hence it is said there is death"] This is what is called a pulse without stomach qi. The *Pingren qixiang lun* (chapter 18) says: "When a person has no stomach qi, it is called adverse, and the adverse die."

## Notes

The qi of the five zang reaches the hand greater yin (*cunkou*) by means of the stomach qi. When illness is severe and the stomach qi no longer accompanies it, the qi of the true zang appears alone, meaning the illness has overcome the zang, and death follows.
