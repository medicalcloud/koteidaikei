# 素問 玉機真蔵論篇第十九 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "The spring pulse is the liver, the east, wood, by which the myriad things begin to be born. Therefore its qi comes soft, weak, light, empty and slippery, straight and long; hence it is called string-like. What goes against this is illness."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Therefore its qi comes ... hence it is called string-like"] It means straight and long, shaped like a string.
Song editors: Yueren says: "The spring pulse is wiry; it is the east, wood. The myriad things begin to be born and do not yet have branches and leaves; therefore its pulse comes moist, weak and long." The *Sishi jing* writes 寛 "relaxed" for 軽 "light".

[On "What goes against this is illness"] "Going against" means going against the constant, normal signs.

## Notes

The spring pulse corresponds to the liver, the east and wood; it is soft, weak, light, empty, slippery, straight and long, hence called wiry.
- Daizhige writes 軟 for 耎.
