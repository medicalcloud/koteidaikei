# 素問 玉機真蔵論篇第十九 §35 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Each of the five zang receives [illness] qi from what it generates, transmits it to what it overcomes; the qi lodges in what generates it, and it dies at what it cannot overcome. When an illness is about to be fatal, it must first be transmitted and reach what the organ cannot overcome; then the patient dies. This speaks of the counterflow of qi; therefore death. The liver receives qi from the heart, transmits it to the spleen; the qi lodges in the kidney, and on reaching the lung there is death. The heart receives qi from the spleen, transmits it to the lung; the qi lodges in the liver, and on reaching the kidney there is death. The spleen receives qi from the lung, transmits it to the kidney; the qi lodges in the heart, and on reaching the liver there is death. The lung receives qi from the kidney, transmits it to the liver; the qi lodges in the spleen, and on reaching the heart there is death. The kidney receives qi from the liver, transmits it to the heart; the qi lodges in the lung, and on reaching the spleen there is death. All these are deaths by counterflow. Divide one day and night into five; this is the means of divining the early or late hour of death and life.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Each of the five zang receives qi from what it generates ... then the patient dies"] "Receives qi from what it generates" means receiving the qi of illness from the organ it generates. "Transmits it to what it overcomes" means transmitting it to the organ it controls. "The qi lodges in what generates it" means lodging in the organ that generates it. "Dies at what it cannot overcome" means dying at the position of the organ that controls it. The transmission is not in order; therefore death is certain.

[On "This speaks of the counterflow of qi; therefore death"] What is counterflow is as described below.

[On "Divide one day and night into five ... the early or late hour of death and life"] The liver dies at the position of the lung, on *geng* and *xin* in autumn; the other four follow this pattern. Further, morning is governed by *jia* and *yi*, midday by *bing* and *ding*, the seasonal ends above by *wu* and *ji*, late afternoon by *geng* and *xin*, and night by *ren* and *gui*. By this the early or late hour of death and life can be known.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 者 for 生, reading "divining the early or late hour of the one who dies". This text speaks solely of the counterflow of qi, and so of death, not of the hour of life. Mr Wang's change of 者 to 生 is inferior in meaning to the *Suwen* text preserved in the *Jiayi jing*.

## Notes

In illness each of the five zang receives qi from the organ it generates, transmits it to the organ it controls, lodges it in the organ that generates it, and dies on reaching the organ that controls it (counterflow of qi); examples are given for each. The day is divided into five to divine the hour of death.
- No speaker is indicated for this passage; it is punctuated as it stands without quotation marks.
- The Song editors say 生 in "the hour of death and life" is Wang Bing's alteration and the *Jiayi jing*'s 者 is better.
- 戊巳 in Wang Bing's note is the base text's form, translated as 戊己.
