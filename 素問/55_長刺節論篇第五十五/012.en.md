# 素問 長刺節論篇第五十五 §12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In great wind illness, the joints are heavy and the beard and eyebrows fall out; it is called great wind. Take needling the flesh as the criterion, sweating for a hundred days; needle the bones and marrow, sweating for a hundred days; after two hundred days in all, when the beard and eyebrows grow, stop needling.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In great wind illness ... sweating for a hundred days"] It drains the pent-up heat of the defensive qi.

[On "needle the bones and marrow, sweating for a hundred days"] It drains the pent-up heat of the nutritive qi.

[On "after two hundred days in all, when the beard and eyebrows grow, stop needling"] The pent-up heat is driven out and yin qi recovers within; therefore there is much sweating and the beard and eyebrows grow.

## Notes

The needling of great wind (understood as a leprosy-like illness); this ends the chapter.
- Daizhige (重广补注黄帝内经素问) has 巻十五 at the end of the chapter; this marks the juan division in that source.
