# 素問 長刺節論篇第五十五 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the illness is in the lower abdomen, with abdominal pain and inability to pass urine or stool, it is called hernia; it is contracted from cold. Needle the lower abdomen and between the thighs, needle between the lumbar crest bones; needle many times, and when it is all hot the illness ends.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Needle the lower abdomen and between the thighs ... the illness ends"] The *jueyin* vessel encircles the genitals and reaches the lower abdomen. The thoroughfare vessel and the lesser yin network vessel both arise below the kidney, emerge at Qijie and follow the inner thigh. The branch that runs behind goes from the lower abdomen down to the centre of the bone; in women it enters and is tied to the urinary orifice; its end follows the genitals and joins at the perineum, winds behind the perineum, branches to wind around the buttocks, and reaches and joins the lesser yin and the central network vessel of the greater yang. The lesser yin rises along the posterior inner thigh, passes through the spine and belongs to the kidney. In men it follows the penis down to the perineum, the same as in women. Therefore needle the lower abdomen and between the thighs, and also between the lumbar crest bones. The lumbar crest bone is in the depression standing level alongside the spine at the lumbar region, where a bone is felt on pressing. Hernia arises from cold; therefore needle it many times and stop when the lower abdomen is all hot. 炅 means heat.
Song editors: Another edition writes 基 for 篡.

## Notes

The needling of hernia due to cold.
- Wang Bing's description of the course of the thoroughfare vessel and lesser yin network vessel resembles the description of the *du* vessel in the *Gukong lun* (chapter 60).
