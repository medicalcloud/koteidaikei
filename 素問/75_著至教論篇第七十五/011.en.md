# 素問 著至教論篇第七十五 §11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Emperor said, "The three yang are the utmost yang. When they accumulate and combine there is fright; the illness rises like a gale and arrives like a thunderclap; the nine orifices are all blocked, yang qi overflows, the throat is dry and blocked. When they combine with yin, above and below have no constancy, and pressing [on the viscera] they cause intestinal flux. This is called the three yang striking straight at the heart: sitting, one cannot rise; lying down, the body is at ease. As for the illness of the three yang, and knowing the world besides — how does one distinguish yin and yang, respond to the four seasons, and match them with the five phases?"

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The three yang are the utmost yang"] The six yang combine, hence called the most abundant yang.

[On "When they accumulate and combine there is fright ... the throat is dry and blocked"] 積 means doubled. The six yang doubled and combined are vastly abundant and cannot be withstood; yang is angry and pent up, and when it is simply abundant it overflows without bounds, hence the dryness and blocked orifices.

[On "When they combine with yin, above and below have no constancy, and pressing they cause intestinal flux"] Yin means the viscera. When yang presses on the viscera and causes illness, the diagnosis again has no fixed above and below; if the illness is below, there is frequent stool, red and white.

[On "This is called the three yang striking straight at the heart: sitting, one cannot rise; lying down, the body is at ease"] The foot greater yang vessel follows the shoulder down to the lower back, so sitting one cannot rise and lying the body is at ease. The reason is that on rising yang drums abundantly, so one always wants to lie down; lying down, the channel qi is even and the body is at peace.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* has 身重 ("body heavy") for 便身全.

[On "how does one distinguish yin and yang, respond to the four seasons, and match them with the five phases"] It means his knowledge is not yet complete.

## Notes

The Emperor describes the illness of the three yang and indicates that Leigong does not yet know their relation to yin and yang, the seasons and the five phases.
- Wang Bing's note does not explain 直心; the translation reads it as "striking straight at the heart", but this has not been verified.
