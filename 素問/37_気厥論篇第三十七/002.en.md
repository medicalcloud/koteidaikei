# 素問 気厥論篇第三十七 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "When the kidney transfers cold to the liver, there are abscess swelling and shortness of breath. When the spleen transfers cold to the liver, there are abscess swelling and contracted sinews. When the liver transfers cold to the heart, there are mania and blockage within. When the heart transfers cold to the lung, there is lung wasting; in lung wasting one drinks one and urinates two; one dies and it cannot be treated. When the lung transfers cold to the kidney, there is surging water; in surging water the abdomen is not hard when pressed, water qi lodges in the large intestine, and on walking fast it gurgles, like fluid wrapped in a bag; it is an illness of water.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the kidney transfers cold to the liver, there are abscess swelling and shortness of breath"] The liver stores blood. When cold enters, the yang qi does not disperse; when the yang qi does not disperse, the blood gathers and the qi is rough; therefore there are abscess swelling and shortness of breath.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition reads "the kidney transfers cold to the spleen". Yuanqi's note says, "The kidney is harmed by cold and transmits it to the spleen. The spleen governs the flesh; when cold arises in the flesh it knots and becomes hard, and the hardness turns to pus; therefore there is an abscess. The blood is harmed and the qi diminished; hence shortness of breath." The *Jiayi jing* also reads "transfers cold to the spleen". Mr Wang followed a corrupt edition and so explained it as the liver; this too is one of a wise man's lapses.

[On "When the spleen transfers cold to the liver, there are abscess swelling and contracted sinews"] The spleen zang governs the flesh and the liver zang the sinews. When the flesh is warm the sinews are relaxed; when the flesh is cold the sinews are tense; therefore the sinews contract. When the flesh is cold the defensive qi knots and gathers; therefore there is abscess swelling.

[On "When the liver transfers cold to the heart, there are mania and blockage within"] The heart is a yang zang, and the spirit dwells in it; when cold presses on it, the spirit is disordered and departs; therefore there is mania. Yang qi and cold press on each other; therefore there is blockage and the interior does not communicate.

[On "When the heart transfers cold to the lung ... it cannot be treated"] The heart is a yang zang, yet it receives all sorts of cold; the cold qi does not dissipate and is transferred to the lung. The cold, following heart-fire, melts the essence of metal within; metal receives the evil of fire; therefore there is inner wasting. When the lung zang wastes and melts, the qi has nothing to hold it; therefore one drinks one and urinates two. Metal and fire injure each other; therefore one dies and it cannot be treated.

[On "When the lung transfers cold to the kidney ... it is an illness of water"] The lung stores qi and the kidney governs water. When lung cold enters the kidney, the kidney qi is in surplus; when the kidney qi is in surplus it rushes up to the lung; hence "surging water". The large intestine is the fu of the lung. Since both lung and kidney are pressed by cold and there is nowhere to go above or below, water qi lodges in the large intestine. The kidney receives congealed cold and cannot transform fluids, and water accumulates in the large intestine without flowing; therefore on walking fast the intestines gurgle with a sloshing sound like fluid in a bag, making an illness of water.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 治主肺者 "treatment governs the lung" for 水之病也.

## Notes

Illnesses arising from the transfer of cold between zang (abscess swelling, contracted sinews, mania, lung wasting, surging water). Qibo's speech continues to unit 004.
- "The kidney transfers cold to the liver": according to the Song editors, Quan Yuanqi's edition and the *Jiayi jing* read "to the spleen", which the Song editors consider correct.
