# 素問 気厥論篇第三十七 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the uterus transfers heat to the bladder, there are urinary retention and blood in the urine. When the bladder transfers heat to the small intestine, the intestines are blocked and stool does not pass, and above there is rotting of the mouth. When the small intestine transfers heat to the large intestine, there are hidden conglomerations and sinking. When the large intestine transfers heat to the stomach, one eats well yet grows thin as [food] enters; this is called food passing. When the stomach transfers heat to the gallbladder, it is also called food passing. When the gallbladder transfers heat to the brain, the bridge of the nose smarts and there is nasal deep-source; in nasal deep-source turbid mucus flows down without stopping; it is transmitted to become nosebleed and dim vision. Therefore it is contracted through qi reversal."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the uterus transfers heat to the bladder ... blood in the urine"] The bladder is the fu of fluids, and the uterus is the office of receiving. Therefore when heat enters the bladder and uterus, it is hot outside and the yin network vessels overflow within; therefore one cannot urinate and passes blood. The *Zhengli lun* says, "When heat is in the lower burner, there is blood in the urine" — this is what is meant.

[On "When the bladder transfers heat to the small intestine ... rotting of the mouth"] The small intestine vessel connects with the heart, follows the throat, descends through the diaphragm, reaches the stomach and belongs to the small intestine. Therefore when heat is received and descends, it blocks the intestines so that stool does not pass; above, sores arise in the mouth and rot. 麋 means rotting.

[On "When the small intestine transfers heat to the large intestine ... sinking"] The heat of the small intestine has moved into the large intestine; the two heats press on each other, the blood overflows and becomes hidden conglomeration. When the blood is rough and does not flow, the menses sink and stagnate and do not come; hence "hidden conglomerations and sinking". 虙 is the same as 伏 "hidden". One version writes 疝 for 瘕, a copying error.

[On "When the large intestine transfers heat to the stomach ... food passing"] The stomach is the sea of water and grain, and its qi nourishes the flesh outwardly. Heat consumes water and grain and also melts the flesh; therefore one eats well yet grows thin as [food] enters. "Food passing" means that food enters but shifts and passes through without producing flesh and skin. 亦 means 易 "change".
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 又 for 入. Mr Wang's note "one eats well yet grows thin as [food] enters" makes no sense at all; it is not as good as the *Jiayi jing*, which writes 又 and reads it with the following text.

[On "When the stomach transfers heat to the gallbladder, it is also called food passing"] The meaning is the same as above.

[On "When the gallbladder transfers heat to the brain ... without stopping"] When the fluid of the brain seeps down, it becomes turbid mucus; the mucus flows without stopping like a spring; hence "nasal deep-source". 頞 means the bridge of the nose. The foot greater yang vessel arises at the inner canthus, rises over the forehead, crosses at the vertex and enters to connect with the brain. The foot *yangming* vessel arises at the nose, crosses at the bridge, and binds the greater yang vessel beside it. Now when the brain is hot, the foot greater yang counterflows and, together with the *yangming* vessel, becomes abundant and presses on the bridge; therefore the bridge of the nose smarts. 辛 means sore pain. Hence the text below says:

[On "it is transmitted to become nosebleed and dim vision"] Because the foot *yangming* vessel crosses at the bridge and binds the greater yang vessel beside it, when [its] heat is abundant the yang network vessels overflow; when they overflow, there is nosebleed and blood-sweat. 衊 means blood-sweat. When the bleeding is severe, the *yangming* and greater yang vessels decline and cannot nourish the eyes; therefore the eyes are dim. 瞑 means dark.

[On "Therefore it is contracted through qi reversal"] 厥 means counterflow of qi; all these are contracted through counterflow of qi.

## Notes

Illnesses arising from the transfer of heat between fu (retention, rotting of the mouth, hidden conglomeration, food passing, nasal deep-source, etc.); this ends the chapter. Qibo's speech, begun in unit 002, ends here.
- 善食而痩入: according to the Song editors, the *Jiayi jing* writes 又 for 入, reading 善食而痩，又謂之食亦. The translation keeps the characters as they are.
- The *Zhengli lun* passage quoted by Wang Bing has not been checked.
- 耳 in Wang Bing's 故耳熱盛 is translated from the context as an error for 其 or the like.
