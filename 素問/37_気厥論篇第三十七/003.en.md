# 素問 気厥論篇第三十七 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the spleen transfers heat to the liver, there are fright and nosebleed. When the liver transfers heat to the heart, one dies. When the heart transfers heat to the lung, it is transmitted and becomes diaphragm wasting. When the lung transfers heat to the kidney, it is transmitted and becomes soft spasm. When the kidney transfers heat to the spleen, it is transmitted and becomes depletion; with intestinal flux, one dies and it cannot be treated.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the spleen transfers heat to the liver, there are fright and nosebleed"] The liver stores blood and also governs fright; therefore when heat presses on it, there are fright and bleeding from the nose.

[On "When the liver transfers heat to the heart, one dies"] Two yang combine and fire and wood burn each other; therefore when liver heat enters the heart one should die. The *Yinyang bielun* says, "The liver going to the heart is called generating yang; those of generating yang die within four days."
Song editors: The meaning of the *Yinyang bielun* passage differs from this; Mr Wang should not have cited that erroneous text to force it onto this meaning.

[On "When the heart transfers heat to the lung ... diaphragm wasting"] Between the heart and lung there is an oblique diaphragm membrane whose lower edge connects inwardly with the transverse diaphragm. Therefore when heart heat enters the lung, over time it is transmitted and transformed into heat of the diaphragm within, with wasting-thirst and much drinking.

[On "When the lung transfers heat to the kidney ... soft spasm"] 柔 means the sinews are soft and powerless; 痓 means the bones are stiff and do not obey. The qi and bones are all hot and the marrow is not full within; therefore the bones are stiff and cannot be raised, and the sinews soft, slack and powerless.

[On "When the kidney transfers heat to the spleen ... it cannot be treated"] Spleen-earth controls water. That the kidney instead transfers heat to it means spleen-earth cannot control water and receives the illness; therefore over time it is transmitted as depletion and damage. "Death with intestinal flux" is because the kidney governs the lower burner and, resembling water, is cold; now it transfers heat, which means the essential qi wastes within and the lower burner has nothing to guard it; therefore the intestinal flux [does not cease] and the qi is not held.

## Notes

Illnesses arising from the transfer of heat between zang.
- 除 in Wang Bing's 腸澼除而気不禁止 is translated from the context as "does not cease"; taken literally the sentence is hard to construe.
