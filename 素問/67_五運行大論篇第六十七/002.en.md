# 素問 五運行大論篇第六十七 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Guiyuqu said: 'Earth governs *jia* and *ji*; metal governs *yi* and *geng*; water governs *bing* and *xin*; wood governs *ding* and *ren*; fire governs *wu* and *gui*. Above *zi* and *wu*, the lesser yin governs; above *chou* and *wei*, the greater yin governs; above *yin* and *shen*, the lesser yang governs; above *mao* and *you*, the *yangming* governs; above *chen* and *xu*, the greater yang governs; above *si* and *hai*, the *jueyin* governs.' This does not accord with yin and yang. What is the reason?"

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "This does not accord with yin and yang. What is the reason?"] *Jia* at the head means the first of the six *jia*, that is, the *jiazi* year.

## Notes

The Emperor cites Guiyuqu's account (the stems' transformation into movements and the branches' controllers of heaven) and asks why it does not accord with the allocation of yin and yang.
- The unit begins with 鬼臾区曰 in the base text, but from the context it is read as the Emperor quoting Guiyuqu, and is marked with single quotation marks as a continuation of the Emperor's speech.
- Wang Bing's note concerns 首甲 in the previous unit.
- 己亥 is read as 巳亥 (*si* and *hai*).
