# 素問 五運行大論篇第六十七 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "This is a clear way; this is the yin and yang of heaven and earth. What can be counted is the yin and yang within humans, and what corresponds can be obtained by counting. Yin and yang can be counted to ten and extended to a hundred, counted to a thousand and extended to ten thousand. The yin and yang of heaven and earth are not reckoned by number but spoken of by image."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "This is a clear way; this is the yin and yang of heaven and earth"] The sages of high antiquity looked up at the images of heaven to set yin and yang right. The way of yin and yang is not unclear, but people are ignorant of its source; when its root and beginning are stated, a hundred doubts arise. Huangdi feared that the supreme principle and true doctrine would easily be slandered and abandoned, and pitied the common people; hence he opened the question. The Heavenly Teacher knows the way, and what he says comes from truth and is surely not erroneous; hence he answers his superior: "This is a clear way; this is the yin and yang of heaven and earth." The method of yin and yang says: "*Jia* and 巳 [*ji*] combine, *yi* and *geng* combine, *bing* and *xin* combine, *ding* and *ren* combine, *wu* and *gui* combine." This presumably takes the meaning of the sages' looking up at the images of heaven. Otherwise, the positions of the ten stems are each in one direction, and verifying their separation and combination would be remote. Alas, how far it is! The common people use it daily without knowing it. Hence the Most High established the saying: "My words are very easy to understand and very easy to practice, yet no one under heaven can understand them or practice them." This is of that kind.
Song editors: On examination, "metal governs *yi* and *geng*": *yi* is the soft of *geng*, *geng* the hard of *yi*. Broadly speaking they are yin and yang; narrowly, husband and wife; this is the matter of hard and soft. The rest are all like this.

[On "The yin and yang of heaven and earth are not reckoned by number but spoken of by image"] It means that when knowledge is partial and shallow, one does not see the origin; the farther what is pointed to, the nearer one's understanding. If one grasps the origin, [the response, like] drumstick and drum, is not far.

## Notes

The yin and yang of heaven and earth are known by image, not by number.
- 巳 in Wang Bing's 甲巳 is read as 己. "The Most High" (太上) presumably refers to Laozi.
