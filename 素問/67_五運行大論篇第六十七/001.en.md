# 素問 五運行大論篇第六十七 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Huangdi sat in the Bright Hall, began to rectify the guiding lines of heaven, surveyed the eight extremities, and examined and established the five constants. He invited the Heavenly Teacher and asked: "The treatise says: 'In the movement and stillness of heaven and earth, spirit brightness is their guiding principle; in the rising and falling of yin and yang, cold and heat make manifest their signs.' I have heard the numbers of the five movements from you, Master. What you said was to set right that each of the five qi governs a year, with *jia* at the head to fix the movement. On that basis I have considered it.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Huangdi sat in the Bright Hall ... established the five constants"] The Bright Hall is the palace where government is promulgated. The eight extremities are the farthest reaches of sight in the eight directions. 考 means examining and comparing; 建 means establishing. The five constants are the five qi moving within heaven and earth. Sitting upright and correcting his qi, he observes the harmony of heaven.

[On "in the rising and falling of yin and yang, cold and heat make manifest their signs"] Song editors: On examination, "the treatise" refers to the text of the *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* and the *Qijiaobian dalun*, which say "in the coming and going of yin and yang, cold and heat make manifest their signs".

## Notes

The opening of the chapter. Huangdi asks the Heavenly Teacher (Qibo) how the five movements are combined with the three yin and three yang. The Emperor's speech continues into unit 002.
