# 素問 四時刺逆従論篇第六十四 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the lesser yang is in surplus, there is sinew impediment and fullness of the flanks; when insufficient, liver impediment. If slippery, there is liver wind *shan*; if rough, accumulation, with tight sinews and eye pain at times.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the lesser yang is in surplus ... liver impediment"] The lesser yang and *jueyin* are exterior and interior; hence the illness returns to the 脾 [liver].

[On "If slippery, there is liver wind *shan* ... eye pain at times"] The liver governs the sinews; hence the sinews are at times tight. The *jueyin* vessel goes up to emerge at the forehead and meets the *du* vessel at the vertex; its branch descends from the eye connector into the cheek; hence eye pain.

## Notes

Illnesses of the lesser yang from surplus, insufficiency, and slippery and rough pulses.
- 脾 in Wang Bing's 病帰於脾 is presumably an error for 肝, by analogy with 皆病帰於腎 in the previous unit.
