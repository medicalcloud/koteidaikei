# 素問 四時刺逆従論篇第六十四 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the greater yin is in surplus, there is flesh impediment and cold in the center; when insufficient, spleen impediment. If slippery, there is spleen wind *shan*; if rough, accumulation, with the heart and abdomen at times full.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the greater yin is in surplus ... spleen impediment"] The spleen governs the flesh; hence this.

[On "If slippery, there is spleen wind *shan* ... at times full"] The greater yin vessel enters the abdomen, connects to the spleen and networks the stomach; its branch again divides from the stomach, goes up through the diaphragm and 疰 [pours] into the heart. Hence spleen *shan* and the heart and abdomen at times full.

## Notes

Illnesses of the greater yin from surplus, insufficiency, and slippery and rough pulses.
