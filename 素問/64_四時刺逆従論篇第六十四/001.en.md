# 素問 四時刺逆従論篇第六十四 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the *jueyin* is in surplus, there is yin impediment; when insufficient, heat impediment arises. If [the pulse] is slippery, there is fox *shan* wind; if rough, there is accumulated qi in the lower abdomen.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the *jueyin* is in surplus, there is yin impediment"] 痺 means pain; 陰 means cold. Surplus means that *jueyin* qi is abundant and full; so yin issues outward and becomes cold impediment.
Song editors: On examination, Wang's taking 痺 as pain does not work.

[On "when insufficient, heat impediment arises"] When yin is insufficient, yang is in surplus; hence heat impediment.

[On "If [the pulse] is slippery, there is fox *shan* wind; if rough, there is accumulated qi in the lower abdomen"] The *jueyin* vessel follows the yin side of the thigh into the pubic hair, encircles the genitals and reaches the lower abdomen. Also its branching network vessel follows the shin up to the testicles and binds at the penis. Hence fox *shan* and accumulated qi in the lower abdomen.
Song editors: Yang Shangshan says: "The fox cannot urinate at night and can do so only when the sun rises. A person's illness is like the fox's; hence it is called fox *shan*. One version says 孤 (solitary) *shan*: the solitary bowel, the triple burner, becomes *shan*; hence solitary *shan*."

## Notes

The opening of the chapter, listing illnesses from surplus and insufficiency of the six channels and from slippery and rough pulses. The chapter does not begin with a question from Huangdi.
- 滑 and 濇 are read as pulse qualities.
