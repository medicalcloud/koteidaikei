# 素問 大奇論篇第四十八 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the liver is full, the kidney full, the lung full, and all are replete, there is swelling. Lung congestion: panting and fullness of both sides. Liver congestion: fullness of both sides, fright on lying down, inability to urinate. Kidney congestion: fullness from below the feet to the lower abdomen, the shins of unequal size, the thighs and shins greatly lame, turning into hemiplegia.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the liver is full ... there is swelling"] 満 means the vessel qi is full and replete. 腫 means abscess swelling. When the zang qi is full, it is so.

[On "Lung congestion: panting and fullness of both sides"] The lung stores qi and outwardly governs breathing; a branch of its vessel emerges transversely from the lung connector below the armpit; therefore there are panting and fullness of both sides.
Song editors: On examination, the *Jiayi jing* writes 癰 "abscess" for 雍 in lung, liver and kidney congestion.

[On "Liver congestion ... inability to urinate"] The liver vessel follows the inner thigh into the pubic hair, encircles the genitals, reaches the lower abdomen, rises through the liver and diaphragm and spreads over the flanks; therefore the sides are full and one cannot urinate. The liver governs fright; therefore one is startled on lying down.

[On "Kidney congestion: fullness from below the feet to the lower abdomen"] Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 胠下 "below the sides" for 脚下; 脚 should be 胠; one cannot say "from below the feet to the lower abdomen".

[On "the shins of unequal size ... turning into hemiplegia"] The thoroughfare vessel is the sea of the channel vessels; with the network vessel of the lesser yin it arises below the kidney, emerges at Qijie, follows the inner edge of the inner thigh, enters obliquely into the popliteal fossa, follows the inner edge of the shin bone, descends alongside the lesser yin channel behind the inner ankle and enters the sole; its ascending part emerges three body-*cun* below the navel; hence this. If the blood and qi change, there is hemiplegia.

## Notes

This chapter describes the relation of various pulses to illnesses; it contains no dialogue. This unit describes the symptoms of congestion (abscess) of the liver, kidney and lung.
- 雍: according to the Song editors, the *Jiayi jing* writes 癰; Wang Bing also says "swelling means abscess swelling".
