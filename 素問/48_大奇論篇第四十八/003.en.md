# 素問 大奇論篇第四十八 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the kidney pulse is large, tense and sunken, or the liver pulse large, tense and sunken, all indicate hernia. When the heart pulse is beating, slippery and tense, there is heart hernia; when the lung pulse is sunken and beating, there is lung hernia. When the three yang are tense, there is conglomeration; when the three yin are tense, hernia; when the two yin are tense, epileptic reversal; when the two yang are tense, fright.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the kidney pulse is large, tense and sunken ... all indicate hernia"] Hernia is produced by cold qi knotting and gathering. A sunken pulse indicates repletion; a tense pulse indicates pain. The qi is replete and cold presses and gathers; therefore there are colicky pain and hernia.

[On "When the heart pulse is beating ... lung hernia"] All because cold presses on the zang.

[On "When the three yang are tense ... hernia"] When the greater yang receives cold, the blood congeals into conglomeration; when the greater yin receives cold, the qi gathers into hernia.

[On "when the two yin are tense ... fright"] The two yin is the lesser yin; the two yang is the *yangming*.
Song editors: On examination, from "when the two yang are tense, there is conglomeration" to here was in the *Jue lun* in Quan Yuanqi's edition; Mr Wang moved it here.

## Notes

The relation of pulse states to hernia, conglomeration, epileptic reversal and fright.
- The Song editors' "two yang tense, conglomeration" presumably refers to "three yang tense, conglomeration" in the text.
