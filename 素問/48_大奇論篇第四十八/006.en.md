# 素問 大奇論篇第四十八 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the pulse arrives beating, with nosebleed and fever, one dies. A pulse that comes suspended, hooked and floating is the usual pulse [in nosebleed]. When the pulse arrives as if panting, it is called sudden reversal; in sudden reversal one does not know how to converse with people. When the pulse arrives as if rapid, it causes sudden fright, which ends by itself in three or four days. When the pulse arrives floating and merging — floating and merging as if rapid, more than ten beats per breath — the channel qi is already insufficient; if it appears faintly, death comes in ninety days. When the pulse arrives like fire burning on firewood, the essence of the heart has already been seized; one dies when the grasses dry. When the pulse arrives like scattered leaves, the liver qi is already depleted; one dies when the leaves fall. When the pulse arrives like a visiting guest — that is, the pulse is blocked and then drums — the kidney qi is already insufficient; one dies when the jujube flowers hang and fall. When the pulse arrives like a ball of mud, the essence of the stomach is already insufficient; one dies when the elm pods fall. When the pulse arrives like a crossbar, the gallbladder qi is already insufficient; one dies when the grain ripens. When the pulse arrives like a thread of string, the essence of the uterus is already insufficient; if the patient talks much, death comes with the first frost; if not talking, it can be treated. When the pulse arrives like crossing lacquer — that is, arriving from left and right sides — if it appears faintly, death comes in thirty days. When the pulse arrives like a gushing spring, floating and drumming in the flesh, the greater yang qi is already insufficient; there is shortness of breath, and one dies when the chive flowers are tasted. When the pulse arrives like crumbling earth and cannot be found on pressing, the qi of the flesh is already insufficient; if among the five colours black appears first, one dies when the white *lei* blooms. When the pulse arrives like the uvula — that is, floating and probed it grows larger on pressing — the twelve transport points are already insufficient; one dies when water freezes. When the pulse arrives like an upturned knife — that is, floating it is small and tense, pressed it is hard, large and tense — the five zang are stagnant and hot, and cold and heat combine solely in the kidney; such a person cannot sit and dies at the beginning of spring. When the pulse arrives slippery like a ball and does not meet the hand — that is, it cannot be found on pressing — the large intestine qi is already insufficient; one dies when the jujube leaves sprout. When the pulse arrives like a flower, it makes one prone to fear, unwilling to sit or lie, and always listening while walking or standing; the small intestine qi is already insufficient; one dies in the last month of autumn.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the pulse arrives beating, with nosebleed and fever, one dies"] Nosebleed indicates depletion, and the pulse should not beat; now that it beats, the qi is at its extreme; therefore one dies.

[On "A pulse that comes suspended, hooked and floating is the usual pulse"] Because it is the usual pulse of those with nosebleed.

[On "When the pulse arrives as if panting, it is called sudden reversal"] 喘 means it comes suddenly, abundant and tense, and goes immediately declining, like a person panting.

[On "in sudden reversal one does not know how to converse with people"] Such are the signs of sudden reversal.

[On "When the pulse arrives as if rapid, it causes sudden fright"] A rapid pulse indicates heat; heat stirs the liver and heart within; therefore there is fright.

[On "which ends by itself in three or four days"] Rapid is the heart pulse; wood is invaded by fire. The illness does not arise from the liver and does not combine with evil; therefore after three days, on the fourth day it disappears by itself. This is because the generating number of wood is three.

[On "When the pulse arrives floating and merging"] Like floating waves merging, the later ones overtaking the earlier, moving fast and swift without regular signs.

[On "When the pulse arrives like fire burning on firewood ... when the grasses dry"] Like the flames of burning firewood, flickering, never settled in form, and then suddenly extinguished.

[On "When the pulse arrives like scattered leaves ... when the leaves fall"] Like scattered leaves following the wind, irregular in form.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 叢棘 "thick thorns" for 散葉.

[On "When the pulse arrives like a visiting guest ... when the jujube flowers hang and fall"] "Blocked and then drums" means it barely appears without moving and soon leaves again. 懸 means like a hanging object that moves and then is cut off and goes.

[On "When the pulse arrives like a ball of mud ... when the elm pods fall"] Like a rolling pearl; this is called a ball of mud.

[On "When the pulse arrives like a crossbar ... when the grain ripens"] The pulse is long and hard, like a crossbar under the fingers.

[On "When the pulse arrives like a thread of string ... if not talking, it can be treated"] The uterine vessel is tied to the kidney, and the kidney vessel runs alongside the root of the tongue. One whose qi is insufficient should be unable to speak; now that one instead talks much, the true qi is cut off within, leaves the kidney and returns outward to the tongue; therefore one dies.

[On "When the pulse arrives like crossing lacquer ... death comes in thirty days"] "Arriving from left and right sides" means like dripping lacquer crossing and bending back left and right.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 交棘 "crossing thorns" for 交漆.

[On "When the pulse arrives like a gushing spring ... when the chive flowers are tasted"] Like the movement of a spring: it only comes out and does not go in.

[On "When the pulse arrives like crumbling earth ... when the white *lei* blooms"] The form of crumbling earth means floating it is large, empty and soft, but on pressing it is absent.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 委土 for 頽土.

[On "When the pulse arrives like the uvula ... when water freezes"] Like the uvula in the throat.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 懸離 for 懸雍; Yuanqi's note says, "懸離 means the pulse and the flesh do not accord."

[On "When the pulse arrives like an upturned knife ... at the beginning of spring"] 菀 means accumulate; 熟 means heat.

[On "When the pulse arrives like a flower ... in the last month of autumn"] "Like a flower" means weak like a blossom and impossible to take properly. The small intestine vessel rises into the ear; therefore one is always listening.

## Notes

Lists abnormal pulses leading to death (the so-called death pulses), likening them to objects, with the time of death for each; this ends the chapter.
- 予 in 予不足 is taken as "already, beforehand".
- The base text writes 己 for the 已 of 自已 (as in units 002 and 004).
- 白塁発死 is often understood as "dies when the white *lei* plant blooms" (Daizhige [重广补注] writes 藟); the translation keeps the characters.
- The punctuation and interpretation of phrases such as 少気，味韭英而死 and 懸去棗華而死 vary; the translation follows the word order.
- The Song editors' note introduced as 新校王云 is reproduced as in the base material (presumably an error for 新校正云).
