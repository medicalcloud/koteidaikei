# 素問 大奇論篇第四十八 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the heart pulse is full and large, there are epilepsy with convulsions and contracted sinews. When the liver pulse is small and tense, there are epilepsy with convulsions and contracted sinews. When the liver pulse is galloping and violent, there has been fright; if the pulse does not arrive or there is loss of voice, it will end by itself without treatment. When the kidney pulse is small and tense, the liver pulse small and tense, or the heart pulse small, tense and not drumming, all indicate conglomeration. When the kidney and liver pulses are both sunken, there is stone water; both floating, wind water; both depleted, death; both small and stringlike, a tendency to fright.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the heart pulse is full and large ... contracted sinews"] When the heart pulse is full and large, the liver qi flows down, hot qi presses within, the sinews dry and the blood is exhausted; therefore there are epilepsy with convulsions and contracted sinews.

[On "When the liver pulse is small and tense ... contracted sinews"] The liver nourishes the sinews and stores blood within; the liver qi receives cold; therefore there are epilepsy with convulsions and contracted sinews. A small, tense pulse indicates cold.

[On "When the liver pulse is galloping and violent, there has been fright"] 騖 means galloping, expressing its swiftness. Yang qi presses within; therefore fright breaks out.

[On "if the pulse does not arrive or there is loss of voice, it will end by itself without treatment"] If the liver qi reverses, the pulse does not pass; when the reversal recedes, the pulse passes again. Also, its vessel spreads over the flanks and follows the back of the throat; therefore, even if the pulse does not arrive or there is loss of voice, it ends by itself without treatment.

[On "When the kidney pulse is small and tense ... all indicate conglomeration"] Small and tense indicates severe cold; not drumming means the blood does not flow; when the blood does not flow and cold presses, the blood congeals within and forms conglomeration.

[On "When the kidney and liver pulses are both sunken, there is stone water"] The liver vessel enters the genitals and passes through the lower abdomen within; the kidney vessel passes through the spine and connects with the bladder. When both zang [pulses] combine, the zang qi fumigates, and the thoroughfare vessel connects from below the kidney to the uterus. Now the water does not move and transform; therefore it hardens and knots. The kidney governs water; water freezes in winter; water has the kidney as its source; the kidney resembles water and sinks. Therefore when the qi combine and sink, it is called stone water.
Song editors: On examination, from "When the kidney and liver pulses are both sunken" to "both small and stringlike, a tendency to fright" below was in the *Jue lun* in Quan Yuanqi's edition; Mr Wang moved it here.

[On "both floating, wind water"] A floating pulse indicates wind; the lower burner governs water; wind presses below; hence it is called wind water.

[On "both depleted, death"] The kidney is the root of the five zang and the liver the master of generation; when both are insufficient, the masters of life are both faint; therefore death.

[On "both small and stringlike, a tendency to fright"] A small, stringlike pulse is due to insufficiency of the liver and kidney; hence this.

## Notes

The relation of abnormal pulses of the heart, liver and kidney to illnesses such as epilepsy with convulsions, fright, conglomeration, stone water and wind water.
- The base text writes 己 for the 已 of 自已 (also in Wang Bing's note); the translation takes it as 已.
- 𤺛瘛 in Wang Bing's note is presumably a variant writing of 癇瘛.
