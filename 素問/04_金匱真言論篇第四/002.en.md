# 素問 金匱真言論篇第四 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "The eight winds issue evils which become channel winds; they touch the five zang, and the evil qi produces illness. What is called gaining the victory of the four seasons: spring overcomes long summer, long summer overcomes winter, winter overcomes summer, summer overcomes autumn, and autumn overcomes spring. This is what is called the victory of the four seasons. The east wind arises in spring; the illness is in the liver, and the transport point is in the neck and nape. The south wind arises in summer; the illness is in the heart, and the transport point is in the chest and flanks. The west wind arises in autumn; the illness is in the lung, and the transport point is in the shoulders and back. The north wind arises in winter; the illness is in the kidney, and the transport point is in the lower back and thighs. The centre is earth; the illness is in the spleen, and the transport point is in the spine. Therefore with the qi of spring, illness is in the head; with the qi of summer, illness is in the zang; with the qi of autumn, illness is in the shoulders and back; with the qi of winter, illness is in the four limbs. Therefore in spring one is prone to nasal congestion and nosebleeds; in midsummer, to illness of the chest and flanks; in long summer, to outpouring diarrhoea and cold in the centre; in autumn, to wind malaria; in winter, to impediment and reversal. Therefore if in winter one does not practise massage and *qiao* exercises, in spring there will be no nasal congestion or nosebleeds, in spring no illness of the neck and nape, in midsummer no illness of the chest and flanks, in long summer no outpouring diarrhoea or cold in the centre, in autumn no wind malaria, and in winter no impediment and reversal, [nor] undigested diarrhoea with sweating. Essence is the basis of the body. Therefore those who store essence do not suffer warm disease in spring. Those who do not sweat in the summer heat develop wind malaria in autumn. This is the pulse method for ordinary people.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The eight winds issue evils ... the evil qi produces illness"] Tracing where it arises: the eight winds issue evils; when the channel vessels receive them, they follow the channels and touch the five zang; because the evil offends the correct, illness arises.

[On "What is called gaining the victory of the four seasons ... the victory of the four seasons"] Spring is wood, summer fire, long summer earth, autumn metal, winter water; each takes as its victory what it controls and kills. The mutual victory of the five seasons does not mean that a person struck by the eight winds falls ill; it means that each falls ill according to what it cannot overcome. "Overcome" means to control and restrain.

[On "The east wind arises in spring ... the transport point is in the neck and nape"] The qi of spring issues and flourishes above the myriad things; therefore the transport point is in the neck and nape. The *Calendar of Prohibitions* (歴忌) says, "On *jia* and *yi* days, do not treat the neck" — this is what is meant.

[On "The south wind arises in summer ... in the chest and flanks"] The heart's lesser yin vessel follows the chest and emerges at the flanks; therefore the transport point is there.

[On "The west wind arises in autumn ... in the shoulders and back"] The lung is in the upper burner; the back is the storehouse of the chest, and the shoulders and back adjoin; therefore the transport point is there.

[On "The north wind arises in winter ... in the lower back and thighs"] The lower back is the storehouse of the kidney, and the thighs adjoin it; because they are connected by qi, they are mentioned together.

[On "The centre is earth ... in the spine"] The spine is made to correspond to earth because it lies in the centre.

[On "Therefore with the qi of spring, illness is in the head"] The qi of spring means the liver qi; each follows what its zang qi corresponds to.
Song editors: The *Zhouli* says: "In spring there is illness of headache."

[On "with the qi of summer, illness is in the zang"] This is the correspondence of the heart.

[On "with the qi of autumn, illness is in the shoulders and back"] This is the correspondence of the lung.

[On "with the qi of winter, illness is in the four limbs"] The four limbs have little qi, and cold toxin readily harms them; wherever the evil is received becomes the place of illness.

[On "Therefore in spring one is prone to nasal congestion and nosebleeds"] Because the qi is in the head. The "Yueling" of the *Liji* says: "If in late autumn the decree of summer is carried out, the people suffer much nasal congestion and sneezing."

[On "in midsummer, to illness of the chest and flanks"] Because the heart vessel follows the chest and flanks.

[On "in long summer, to outpouring diarrhoea and cold in the centre"] Earth governs the centre; it is the granary for dregs, water and grain; therefore there is outpouring diarrhoea and cold in the centre.

[On "in autumn, to wind malaria"] When coolness breaks the summer heat, this illness arises. The *Shengqi tongtian lun* (chapter 3) says, "If, before the *po*-sweat has finished, the body is weak and the qi melted away and the acupoints close, it breaks out as wind malaria" — this is the meaning of coolness breaking the summer heat. The "Yueling" of the *Liji* says: "If in early autumn the decree of summer is carried out, the people suffer much malaria."

[On "in winter, to impediment and reversal"] Blood resembles water; with cold, water congeals; because it flows pressed by qi, there is impediment and reversal.

[On "Therefore if in winter one does not practise massage and *qiao* exercises, in spring there will be no nasal congestion or nosebleeds"] 按 means massage; 蹻 means moving the hands and feet like a nimble person — this is what is called *daoyin*. If one disturbs and moves the sinews and bones, the yang qi is not stored. In spring the yang qi rises, the heat is doubled and fumigates the lung; the lung communicates with the nose, so when it is ill it shows there. Therefore if in winter one does not practise massage and *qiao*, in spring there will be no nasal congestion or nosebleeds. 鼽 means water coming out of the nose; 衄 means blood coming out of the nose.

[On "... in winter no impediment and reversal, [nor] undigested diarrhoea with sweating"] The five phrases above are all brought about by not practising massage and *qiao* in winter.
Song editors: We suspect that the six characters 飱泄而汗出也 are superfluous in view of the text above.

[On "Essence is the basis of the body. Therefore those who store essence do not suffer warm disease in spring"] This means precisely that if one does not practise massage and *qiao* in winter, the essential qi lies hidden and stored, and since the yang does not rise recklessly, there is no warm disease in spring.

[On "Those who do not sweat in the summer heat develop wind malaria in autumn"] This means precisely that the wind-cool qi breaks the summer-heat sweating.
Song editors: The meaning from here on does not connect with the text above.

[On "This is the pulse method for ordinary people"] It means the method of assessing the pulse of sick people.

## Notes

Qibo's answer: the eight winds offend the five zang through the channels; the mutual victory of the four seasons (the controlling cycle of the five phases); the zang and the transport-point regions affected by the wind of each direction; and the illnesses to which each season is prone. If in winter one does not stir the yang qi by massage and *daoyin* and keeps the essence stored, the next year's illnesses can be prevented.
- "Victory of the four seasons": Wang Bing takes it as the controlling cycle of the five phases; each falls ill in the season it cannot overcome.
- "Not practising massage and *qiao* in winter": Wang Bing takes 按蹻 as massage and *daoyin*; stirring the sinews and bones in winter prevents the yang qi from being stored and leads to nosebleeds in spring.
- "Undigested diarrhoea with sweating": the Song editors suspect these six characters are superfluous; the translation renders them as they stand.
- "This is the pulse method for ordinary people": the Song editors say what follows does not connect with what precedes. Wang Bing takes it as "the method of assessing the pulse of sick people".
- 𭼠 in 秋不病風𭼠 is a variant of 瘧 (the other sources write 瘧).
- The "illness of headache" in spring cited by the Song editors from the *Zhouli* is from the section on the physician for illnesses (*jiyi*).
- Daizhige (the plain text) writes 肋 for 脅 and 肢 for 支.
