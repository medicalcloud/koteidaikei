# 素問 金匱真言論篇第四 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Therefore it is said: within yin there is yin, and within yang there is yang. From dawn to midday is the yang of heaven, the yang within yang. From midday to dusk is the yang of heaven, the yin within yang. From nightfall to cockcrow is the yin of heaven, the yin within yin. From cockcrow to dawn is the yin of heaven, the yang within yin. Therefore people also correspond to this.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "within yin there is yin, and within yang there is yang"] This refers to their first arising and their flourishing.

[On "From dawn to midday ... From midday to dusk is the yang of heaven, the yin within yang"] At midday yang is abundant; hence "the yang within yang". At dusk yin is abundant; hence "the yin within yang". The yang qi governs the daytime; therefore from dawn to dusk is all the yang of heaven, but within it there is again the distinction of yin and yang.

[On "From nightfall to cockcrow ... From cockcrow to dawn is the yin of heaven, the yang within yin"] At cockcrow the yang qi has not yet come out; therefore it is the yin of heaven. At dawn the yang qi has already risen; hence "the yang within yin".

## Notes

The day is divided into four parts to show that within yin and yang there is again yin and yang; people correspond to this.
- "Nightfall" (合夜) means the coming on of night after sunset.
- Wang Bing explains "the yin within yang" by saying "at dusk yin is abundant" (the period from midday to dusk in which yin increases).
