# 素問 金匱真言論篇第四 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Speaking of the yin and yang of a person, the outside is yang and the inside yin. Speaking of the yin and yang of the human body, the back is yang and the abdomen yin. Speaking of the yin and yang among the zang and fu of the human body, the zang are yin and the fu yang: the five zang — liver, heart, spleen, lung and kidney — are all yin; the six fu — gallbladder, stomach, large intestine, small intestine, bladder and triple burner — are all yang. Why should one wish to know the yin within yin and the yang within yang? Because illnesses of winter are in the yin, of summer in the yang, of spring in the yin and of autumn in the yang, one must in every case look at where it lies in order to apply needles and stones. Therefore the back is yang, and the yang within yang is the heart; the back is yang, and the yin within yang is the lung; the abdomen is yin, and the yin within yin is the kidney; the abdomen is yin, and the yang within yin is the liver; the abdomen is yin, and the utmost yin within yin is the spleen. All these are yin and yang, exterior and interior, inside and outside, female and male, transporting to and corresponding with one another; therefore they correspond to the yin and yang of heaven."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "the zang are yin and the fu yang"] The zang are the five spirit storehouses; the fu are the six transforming storehouses.

[On "the six fu — gallbladder ... triple burner — are all yang"] The *Lingshu jing* says, "The triple burner joins above with the hand heart-master," and also, "The foot triple burner is another name for the greater yang." The *Zhengli lun* says: "The triple burner has a name but no form; above it joins with the hand heart-master, below it joins with the right kidney; it governs the guiding of all qi and is called the envoy."

[On "the back is yang, and the yang within yang is the heart"] The heart is a yang zang located in the upper burner; being yang residing in yang, it is the yang within yang. The *Lingshu jing* says: "The heart is a male zang." Male is yang.

[On "the back is yang, and the yin within yang is the lung"] The lung is a yin zang located in the upper burner; being yin residing in yang, it is called the yin within yang. The *Lingshu jing* says: "The lung is a female zang." Female is yin.

[On "the abdomen is yin, and the yin within yin is the kidney"] The kidney is a yin zang located in the lower burner; being yin residing in yin, it is called the yin within yin. The *Lingshu jing* says: "The kidney is a female zang." Female is yin.

[On "the abdomen is yin, and the yang within yin is the liver"] The liver is a yang zang located in the middle burner; being yang residing in yin, it is called the yang within yin. The *Lingshu jing* says: "The liver is a male zang." Male is yang.

[On "the abdomen is yin, and the utmost yin within yin is the spleen"] The spleen is a yin zang located in the middle burner; being greater yin residing in yin, it is called the utmost yin within yin. The *Lingshu jing* says: "The spleen is a female zang." Female is yin.

[On "All these are yin and yang ... they correspond to the yin and yang of heaven"] Because their qi and images match, they can correspond above to heaven.

## Notes

The yin and yang of the body (outside and inside, back and abdomen, zang and fu) and the yin-yang position of each of the five zang, which one needs in order to locate illness and apply needles and stones. Qibo's speech, begun in unit 002, ends here.
- The heart is the yang within yang, the lung the yin within yang, the kidney the yin within yin, the liver the yang within yin, and the spleen the utmost yin within yin. Wang Bing explains each by the yin or yang nature of the organ and the burner (upper, middle, lower) in which it lies.
- "Illnesses of winter are in the yin ... of autumn in the yang": assigning spring to yin and autumn to yang may look reversed, but Wang Bing does not comment.
- The view of the triple burner in the *Zhengli lun* cited by Wang Bing ("a name but no form") is close to that of the *Nanjing*.
