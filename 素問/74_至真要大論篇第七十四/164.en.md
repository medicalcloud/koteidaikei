# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §164 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Emperor said, "The treatise says, 'Treat cold with heat, treat heat with cold,' and practitioners cannot abandon this rule and change the way. Some ill with heat are cooled but remain hot; some ill with cold are heated but remain cold. Both [illnesses] remain and new illness arises again. How are these treated?"

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Both [illnesses] remain and new illness arises again. How are these treated"] This refers to new illness where treatment does not make the illness recede but instead produces cold and heat following the cold and heat of the medicine. Some stop and recur; some clear while the medicine is present and break out when it is gone; some never cease at all. If practitioners abandon this rule, they have no method of renewal; if they rely on the standard, the force of the illness is not removed. Abandoning it goes against ordinary feeling; treating by it, the medicine has no effect. The mind is confused and the intent bewildered, with no way to understanding. Not knowing the way, on what can they rely? When illness arises from medicine and new and old confront each other, how can one hope for a cure?

## Notes

A question about treatment when regular treatment of cold and heat fails and instead produces new illness.
