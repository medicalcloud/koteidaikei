# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §56 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "For the qi controlling heaven: when wind runs rampant and prevails, calm it with acrid and cool, assist with bitter and sweet, moderate it with sweet, drain it with sour. When heat runs rampant and prevails, calm it with salty and cold, assist with bitter and sweet, contract it with sour. When damp runs rampant and prevails, calm it with bitter and hot, assist with sour and acrid, dry it with bitter, drain it with bland. When damp is severe above with heat, treat with bitter and warm, assist with sweet and acrid, and stop once sweating has occurred. When fire runs rampant and prevails, calm it with sour and cold, assist with bitter and sweet, contract it with sour, release it with bitter, restore with sour; rampant heat is the same. When dryness runs rampant and prevails, calm it with bitter and 湿 [warm], assist with sour and acrid, purge it with bitter. When cold runs rampant and prevails, calm it with acrid and hot, assist with sweet and bitter, drain it with salty."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "when wind runs rampant and prevails, calm it with acrid and cool ... drain it with sour"] The qi of *jueyin* is not yet abundant heat, hence it speaks of calming it with cool medicine. In the functioning of qi, accumulated coolness becomes cold, accumulated warmth becomes heat; heat reduced becomes warmth, cold reduced becomes coolness; warmth increased becomes heat, coolness increased becomes cold. When each matches its portion, cold is cold, warmth warmth, heat heat, coolness coolness. In using formula books, can one fail to attend to this? Hence, as to 商降 and the amount of cold, heat, warmth and coolness, a good maker of formulas must understand them thoroughly. The other qi are all likewise and follow these rules.
Song editors: According to the text above in this treatise, "When the upper runs rampant over the lower, calm it with what overcomes it; when the outer runs rampant within, treat it with what overcomes it." Hence for the spring in earth it says "treat", for the controller of heaven "calm".

[On "When heat runs rampant and prevails, calm it with salty and cold, assist with bitter and sweet, contract it with sour"] When heat qi has already receded yet flares up from time to time, the heart is depleted and its qi scattered and not gathered; contract it with sour. Though contracting with sour, one must combine the help of cold to eradicate its source. When heat appears too severe, release it with bitter. If after sweating it becomes cool, the pathogenic qi is exhausted; do not [use] cold water. If after sweating it is still hot, the pathogenic qi is not exhausted; contract it with sour. If after that it is hot again, induce sweating again. If after sweating it is hot again, the viscera are depleted; supplement the heart. The rules fit thus. Those treating heat need not release it more than two or three times; how much less would there be cases changing back and forth four times?

[On "When damp runs rampant and prevails, calm it with bitter and hot ... drain it with bland"] Wherever damp qi runs rampant there is swelling and fullness; just remove the damp and the swelling and fullness subside of themselves. Illness caused by damp without swelling or fullness is the same. In treating it, when damp qi is above, induce vomiting with bitter; when below, drain it with bitter and percolate it with bland; then all is dried. Draining means percolation, with promoting the waterways and passing urine as the rule. Although sour is hot, it is also used to promote urination and remove hidden water. Treating damp illness without passing urine is not the proper method.
Song editors: For "damp running rampant within" it says "assist with sour and bland"; here it says "sour and acrid", so 辛 should perhaps be 淡 ("bland").

[On "When damp is severe above with heat, treat with bitter and warm ... stop once sweating has occurred"] When damp qi is in excess above the middle of the body and fire qi is again pent up, and the constraint and damp press on each other, use bitter-warm and sweet-acrid medicines to release the exterior and make sweat flow, expelling it. Hence "take sweating as the sign of removal of illness, and 巳 [stop]".

[On "release it with bitter, restore with sour; rampant heat is the same"] Same as the meaning for rampant heat; heat too follows this method. Restore its native qi with sour; if its qi is not restored, the rampant qi becomes empty and invites damage.

[On "When dryness runs rampant and prevails, calm it with bitter and 湿, assist with sour and acrid, purge it with bitter"] To restrain the prevalence of dryness one must use bitter and 湿 [warm]; this is the qi and flavor of fire. When purging is needed, use bitter; when supplementing, sour; when draining, acrid. When coolness is severe and produces cold that lingers, purge it with bitter and 湿 [warm]; when qi is in surplus, drain it with acrid. The other qi are the same.
Song editors: According to the text above, "when dryness runs rampant within, treat with bitter and warm"; here it says "bitter and 湿", so 湿 should be 温. The three occurrences of 湿 in the classic and the note should all be 温. Also the *Tianyuan* [*Liuyuan*] *zhengji dalun* has "bitter and slightly warm".

[On "When cold runs rampant and prevails, calm it with acrid and hot, assist with sweet and bitter, drain it with salty"] Once the rampancy is dispersed, stop; do not go too far.
Song editors: According to the text above, "when cold runs rampant within, treat with sweet and hot, assist with bitter and acrid"; here "calm with acrid and hot, assist with sweet and bitter" is an error in the text. Also the *Tianyuan* [*Liuyuan*] *zhengji dalun* says "under the governance of greater yang, the year calls for drying with bitter".

## Notes

The answer giving treatments when the six qi controlling heaven run rampant and prevail; for the controller of heaven the word is "calm".
- 苦湿 in the main text is read as 苦温 ("bitter and warm") following the Song editors.
- The latter part of the note on heat and 商降 in the note on wind are hard to construe; the translation is tentative (not verified).
