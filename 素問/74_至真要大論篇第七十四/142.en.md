# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §142 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Emperor said, "You say that the qi of spring and autumn begin earlier and the qi of winter and summer begin later; this I already know. But the six qi come and go, and the governors of the year are not constant. How are they supplemented and drained?"

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "But the six qi come and go ... How are they supplemented and drained"] If the divisions and positions of the six qi are clarified by the equinoxes and solstices, the first and fourth qi take as their rule beginning fifteen days before the Beginning of Spring and the Beginning of Autumn; the third and sixth qi take as their rule beginning fifteen days after the Beginning of Summer and the Beginning of Winter. Reckoning the four qi before and after thus, the middle of the third and sixth qi falls exactly on the two solstices. Hence "the qi of spring and autumn begin earlier and the qi of winter and summer begin later". But every three hundred sixty-five days one qi [year] changes; once a year has passed, the qi is renewed; when the new qi comes, the old departs. The suitable flavors differ for heaven and earth, and in formulas for supplementing and draining one must know what comes first and after; hence he asks again.

## Notes

A question about supplementing and draining when the governing qi changes year by year.
