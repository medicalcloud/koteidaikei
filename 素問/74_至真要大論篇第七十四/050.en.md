# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §50 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When lesser yin controls heaven and heat runs rampant and prevails, oppressive heat arrives and fire carries out its governance. People suffer vexing heat in the chest, dry throat, fullness of the right flank, skin pain, cold and heat, coughing and panting. Heavy rain is about to arrive. Spitting blood, bloody discharge, stuffy nose and nosebleed, sneezing and retching, change in the color of urine, and in severe cases sores and puffy swelling, pain in the shoulders, back, forearms, upper arms and supraclavicular fossa, heart pain, lung distension, great abdominal fullness, bloating with panting and coughing. The illness is rooted in the lungs. If Chize is cut off, it is death and cannot be treated.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "heart pain, lung distension, great abdominal fullness, bloating with panting and coughing. The illness is rooted in the lungs"] This refers to the *jiazi*, *bingzi*, *wuzi*, *gengzi*, *renzi*, *jiawu*, *bingwu*, *wuwu*, *gengwu* and *renwu* years. Oppressive heat arrives because fire carries out its governance. In these years people's illnesses gather on the right, presumably because the small intestine connects with the heart. Illness arises from the lungs, hence "rooted in the lungs".
Song editors: According to the *Jiayi jing*, "change in the color of urine, pain in the shoulders, back, forearms, upper arms and supraclavicular fossa, lung distension and fullness, bloating with panting and coughing" is illness of the lungs; "stuffy nose and nosebleed" is illness of the large intestine. Presumably in years when lesser yin controls heaven, fire overcomes metal, hence such illnesses. Also, Wang Bing's note says people's illnesses gather on the right because the small intestine connects with the heart; but according to the *Jiayi jing*, the small intestine attaches to the spine and circles to the left, while the winding intestine attaches to the spine and circles to the 在 [right]; this does not fit what he says. Might it not be that fire prevails and overcomes metal, so that the large intestine falls ill?

[On "The illness is rooted in the lungs. If Chize is cut off, it is death and cannot be treated"] Chize is in the large crease on the inner edge of the elbow, where the pulsing vessel meets the hand; it is the qi of the lungs. Fire melts metal; receiving the decree of the great [fire], metal qi is cut off within, hence certain danger and death. If Chize does not arrive, lung qi is already cut off; though the nutritive and defensive qi spread and move, they have no master, and the true qi is exhausted within. How could there be life?

## Notes

The case of lesser yin controlling heaven (*zi* and *wu* years).
