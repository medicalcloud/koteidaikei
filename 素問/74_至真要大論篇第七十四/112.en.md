# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §112 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Hence the saying 'For what is near use odd, for what is far use even; for sweating do not use odd, for purging do not use even; to supplement and treat the upper, make the formula moderate; to supplement and treat the lower, make it urgent; urgent means thick qi and flavor, moderate means thin qi and flavor; fit it to reach its place' means this. When the site of illness is far and the qi and flavor [would be spent] midway, use food to carry it past, without exceeding the rules. Thus in the way of balancing qi, when near, whether odd or even, make the dose small; when far, whether odd or even, make the dose large. Large means few in number, small means many. Many is nine, few is two. If odd does not remove it, use even; this is called a double formula. If even does not remove it, use contrary assistance to take it; this is what is called cold, heat, warmth and coolness contrarily following the illness."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "urgent means thick qi and flavor, moderate means thin qi and flavor; fit it to reach its place' means this"] Sweating medicines do not use even formulas because the qi is insufficient to release outward; purging medicines do not use the odd rule because the toxin of the medicine attacks to excess. In treating and supplementing the upper, if the formula is swift and urgent, it does not stay but presses downward; in treating and supplementing the lower, if the formula is slow, it only moistens the way and its strength is weak. If an urgent formula has thin qi and flavor, its strength equals a moderate one; if a moderate formula has thick qi and flavor, its force equals an urgent one. Then moderation is not moderate and urgency not urgent, thickness not thick and thinness not thin; size does not fit the rule and weight has no measure, so deficiency and excess, cold and heat, viscera and bowels are thrown into confusion, and there is no way to bring order. How could one hope for the spirit to be at peace?

[On "When the site of illness is far ... without exceeding the rules"] For example, when illness is in the kidneys, if one feeds the qi and flavor of the heart and they are cold and insufficient, still hurry them past. If one does not feed qi and flavor, the kidney medicine overrides the heart and the heart grows weaker still. The other cases of upper and lower, near and far are the same.

[On "Large means few in number, small means many. Many is nine, few is two"] The amounts of decoctions and pills are generally thus. Near and far refer to the positions of the bowels and viscera: heart and lungs are near, kidneys and liver far, spleen and stomach in the middle; the three yang [intestines], womb, bladder and gallbladder also have their near and far. The body is divided into three: the upper is near, the lower far. Or, with lofty insight, one weighs what is suitable: if the formula is odd, make the weights even; if the formula is even, make the weights odd. In such cases, near with an even rule, take it many times; far with an odd rule, take it few times. Thus lungs nine doses, heart seven, spleen five, liver three, kidneys two is the constant rule; hence "small means many in number, large means few".
Song editors: On examination, "three yang, womb, bladder and gallbladder" in the note is written "three intestines, womb, bladder and gallbladder" in one edition. On further examination, "three yang" makes no sense, and "three intestines" is not right either: the intestines are large and small, and with the bladder-intestine make three, but since "womb and bladder" are already mentioned, one cannot say "three intestines"; 三 should be 二 ("two").

[On "If even does not remove it, use contrary assistance to take it ... contrarily following the illness"] For a formula, better light than heavy, better benign than toxic, better small than large. Hence if an odd formula does not remove it, an even formula takes charge; if the illness remains with an even formula, contrarily add one assistant with qi like the illness's to take it. Heat turns its back on cold, and cold opposes heat. Slight heat is broken by cold, slight cold is dispelled by heat. Very great cold or heat can contend for supremacy with what opposes its nature and resist alien qi. Sounds that are not alike do not respond; qi that are not alike do not combine. Then [the illness qi] shuns [the medicine] and will not let it attack; if it does attack, the illness qi resists the [medicine's] sound and qi, makes its own cold or heat, and shuts itself up in firm defense. Hence the sages reversed the assistant to make its qi the same, so that sound and qi respond and combine, and made cold and heat blend, so that they differ in the end but are alike at the start. When dryness and moisture defeat the hard, the rigid must break and the soft and brittle disperse of themselves.

## Notes

The answer on the nearness or farness of illness and the oddness, evenness, size and number of doses of formulas, and on double formulas and contrary assistance. Qibo's speech ends here.
- The first note of Wang Bing says "sweating medicines do not use even formulas" and "purging medicines do not use the odd rule", the reverse of the main text (for sweating do not use odd, for purging do not use even). One of them may be in error, but this has not been verified.
- The second note of Wang Bing is hard to construe; the translation is tentative (not verified).
