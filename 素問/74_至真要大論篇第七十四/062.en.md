# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §62 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "In the prevalence of *jueyin*, there is ringing in the ears and dizziness, confused nausea, and the stomach and diaphragm feel as if cold. Great winds rise frequently and naked creatures do not thrive. Qi combines in the flanks and rib-sides and transforms into heat; urine is yellow-red; there is pain in the stomach duct right at the heart, propping up both rib-sides, rumbling intestines and swill diarrhea, lower abdominal pain, pouring diarrhea red and white, and in severe cases vomiting and blockage of the diaphragm and throat.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "lower abdominal pain, pouring diarrhea red and white, and in severe cases vomiting and blockage of the diaphragm and throat"] The five *si* and five *hai* years. Below the heart and above the navel is the domain of the stomach. Stomach and diaphragm means above the stomach duct and below the great diaphragm; wind and cold qi arise [there, hence it feels cold]. Qi combining means attaching to one side. Blockage of the diaphragm and throat means food and drink go in and come back out.
Song editors: According to the *Jiayi jing*, stomach illness is "pain in the stomach duct right at the heart, propping up both rib-sides, blockage of the diaphragm and throat".

## Notes

Among the prevalences of the six qi, the prevalence of *jueyin*. Qibo's speech continues through unit 67.
