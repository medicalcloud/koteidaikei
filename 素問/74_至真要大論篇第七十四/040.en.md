# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §40 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "In years when *jueyin* is in the spring and wind runs rampant and prevails, the earth's qi is not bright, the plains are dim, and grasses bloom early. People suffer shivering with chills, frequent stretching and yawning, heart pain with propping fullness, internal tension of both rib-sides, food and drink not going down, blockage of the diaphragm and throat, vomiting on eating, abdominal distension with frequent belching, relief as if [the illness] were waning after passing stool or gas, and heaviness of the whole body.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "relief as if [the illness] were waning after passing stool or gas, and heaviness of the whole body"] This refers to the *jiayin*, *bingyin*, *wuyin*, *gengyin*, *renyin*, *jiashen*, *bingshen*, *wushen*, *gengshen* and *renshen* years. The qi not being bright means the color of the qi at the edge of heaven is dark and dim; since wind moves over the earth, the plains are all so. 昧 means dark. 脅 means below both breasts and outside the flanks. 伸 means wanting to stretch and exert the sinews and bones.
Song editors: According to the *Jiayi jing*, "shivering with chills, frequent stretching and yawning" is illness of the stomach; "vomiting on eating, abdominal distension with frequent belching, relief as if waning after passing stool or gas, and heaviness of the whole body" is illness of the spleen; "food and drink not going down, blockage of the diaphragm and throat" is pathogen in the stomach duct. Presumably in years when *jueyin* is in the spring, wood flourishes and overcomes the spleen and stomach, hence such illnesses. Also, according to the *Maijie*: "What is meant by vomiting on eating is that things are abundant and full and overflow upward, hence vomiting. What is meant by relief as if waning after passing stool or gas is that in the twelfth month yin qi declines below and yang qi is about to emerge; hence 'relief as if waning after passing stool or gas'."

## Notes

The first of the six cases in which the qi in the spring runs rampant and prevails: *jueyin* (*yin* and *shen* years). Qibo's speech continues through unit 45.
