# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §126 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "The pulse arrives compliantly, but on pressing it drums strongly and abundantly. Hence, of the hundred illnesses, some arise from the root, some from the 摽 [tip], some from the middle qi; some are cured by taking the root, some by taking the tip, some by taking the middle qi, some by taking both tip and root, some by contrary taking, some by compliant taking. The contrary is truly compliant; what seems compliant is contrary. Hence the saying 'Knowing tip and root, one uses them without peril; clearly knowing contrary and compliant, one acts rightly without needing to ask' means this. One who does not know this is not fit to speak of diagnosis but fit only to disorder the classic. Hence the *Great Essentials* says, 'The crude practitioner beams with delight, thinking he knows; before he has finished speaking of heat, cold illness begins again; the same qi has different forms; he is confused in diagnosis and disorders the classic'; this is what it means. The way of tip and root is concise yet broad, small yet great; by speaking of one thing one can know the harms of the hundred illnesses. Speaking of tip and root is easy, but do not damage [it]; examine root and tip, and the qi can be regulated; clearly know prevalence and retaliation, and one becomes a model for the myriad people. The way of heaven is complete."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The pulse arrives compliantly, but on pressing it drums strongly and abundantly"] The form and pattern are cold, but on pressing the pulse qi drums and strikes abundantly under the hand; this is abundant heat rejecting yin and producing illness; it is not cold.

[On "some by contrary taking, some by compliant taking"] Taking with contrary assistance is contrary taking; taking with odd and even [formulas] is compliant taking. Treating cold illness with cold and hot illness with heat is contrary taking. 従 means compliance.

[On "The contrary is truly compliant; what seems compliant is contrary"] Treating heat with heat when abundant cold blocks yang, treating cold with cold when abundant heat rejects yin, and the like, are at times called contrary; though contrary is used outwardly, inwardly it is compliant; this contrariness is truly compliance. If one treats with cold when cold blocks yang, or with heat when heat rejects cold, it is compliant outwardly but the middle qi is contrary; hence a formula that seems compliant is contrary.

[On "the same qi has different forms; he is confused in diagnosis and disorders the classic'; this is what it means"] 嘻嘻 means delighted: being pleased in mind and thinking one has completely understood the Way. In the functioning of the six qi, the crude and the skilled each get only half. The crude take the transformation of *jueyin* to be cold, but it is warm; they take the transformation of greater yang to be heat, but it is cold. Thus they err and in use lose the Way; hence their learning, knowledge and application do not reach half of the skilled practitioner's way. Greater yang and lesser yin each have a transformation of cold and of heat; weighing their tip and root and applying them accordingly, there is regular and contrary. Why? Greater yang has cold as root and heat as tip; lesser yin has heat as root and cold as tip; the use of formulas is likewise. So too the middle qi of *jueyin* and *yangming*: the middle qi of *jueyin* is heat, that of *yangming* damp; these two qi too are contrary to their kind, like greater yang and lesser yin. But greater yang and lesser yin have tip and root whose functions differ from the other qi; hence "the same qi has different forms". Since the tip and root of one channel already differ in cold and heat, when speaking of the root one should investigate its tip, and when discussing the tip one should seek its root. If one speaks of qi without exhausting tip and root, and discusses illness without distinguishing yin and yang, then even though arising from the same qi, one is blocked as to the signs of cold and warmth. Hence the mind is confused about the right principle and treatment further disorders the classic. Calling such a one a crude practitioner truly fits.

[On "clearly know prevalence and retaliation, and one becomes a model for the myriad people. The way of heaven is complete"] Even the changes of heaven and earth can be fully known; how could the diagnosis of one person be called obscure? Grasping the essentials of the classic and holding to the source of the method, one may become teacher of the world and still regard one's way as humble. How great it is to be a model for the myriad people!
Song editors: According to the *Biaoben bingchuan lun*: "Some are in the tip and sought in the tip; some in the root and sought in the root; some in the root and sought in the tip; some in the tip and sought in the root. Hence in treatment some are cured by taking the tip, some by taking the root, some by contrary taking, some by compliant taking. Hence one who knows contrary and compliant acts rightly without asking; one who knows tip and root succeeds ten thousand times in ten thousand attempts; one who does not know tip and root acts recklessly. The way of yin and yang, contrary and compliant, tip and root, is small yet great; by speaking of one thing one knows the harms of the hundred illnesses; little yet much, shallow yet broad, by speaking of one one can know a hundred. Through the shallow one knows the deep; examining the near one knows the far. Speaking of tip and root is easy, but none can reach it. If treatment goes against, it is contrary; if treatment succeeds, it is compliant. When illness comes first and counterflow later, treat the root; when counterflow comes first and illness later, treat the root; when cold comes first and illness arises later, treat the root; when heat comes first and illness arises later, treat the root; when heat comes first and central fullness arises later, treat the tip; when illness comes first and diarrhea later, treat the root; when diarrhea comes first and other illness arises later, treat the root, being sure to regulate it first and then treat the other illness; when illness comes first and central fullness arises later, treat the tip; when central fullness comes first and heart vexation later, treat the root. People have guest qi and 同 [fixed] qi. When stool and urine are obstructed, treat the tip; when they flow, treat the root. When illness breaks out in surplus, it is root then tip: treat the root first and the tip after. When illness breaks out in insufficiency, it is tip then root: treat the tip first and the root after. Carefully examine whether it is mild or severe and regulate it with discernment: for the mild, treat together; for the severe, treat singly. When stool and urine are obstructed first and illness arises later, treat the root." That classic discusses tip and root in particular detail.

## Notes

The answer that what looks like yin illness, with a pulse drumming strongly on pressing, is really heat; and on the importance of knowing tip, root and middle qi, and contrary and compliant taking. Qibo's speech ends here.
- 摽 in the main text is read as 標 ("tip").
- The Song editors' quotation of the *Biaoben bingchuan lun* differs slightly in wording from the text of chapter 65; it is translated as quoted (同気 is supplemented as 固気).
