# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §118 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "When *jueyin* arrives, its pulse is string-like; when lesser yin arrives, its pulse is hook-like; when greater yin arrives, its pulse is sunken; when lesser yang arrives, it is large and floating; when *yangming* arrives, it is short and rough; when greater yang arrives, it is large and long. If it arrives in harmony, it is balanced; if it arrives excessively, there is illness; if it arrives contrary, there is illness; if it should arrive but does not, there is illness; if it arrives before it should, there is illness; if yin and yang are exchanged, it is dangerous."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When *jueyin* arrives, its pulse is string-like"] Soft, empty and slippery, straight and long, is called string-like. If replete and strong, illness; if not replete but faint, also illness; if not straight and long, also illness; if not in its position, also illness; if in its position but unable to be string-like, also illness.

[On "when lesser yin arrives, its pulse is hook-like"] Abundant in coming and declining in going, like a belt hook lying down, is called hook-like. If not abundant in coming but abundant instead in going, illness; if abundant both coming and going, also illness; if abundant neither coming nor going, also illness; if not like a lying belt hook, also illness; if not in its position, also illness; if in its position but unable to be hook-like, also illness.

[On "when greater yin arrives, its pulse is sunken"] 沈 means low: obtained only on pressing, lower than the pulses of the other positions. If very sunken, illness; if not sunken, also illness; if not in its position, also illness; if in its position but unable to be sunken, also illness.

[On "when lesser yang arrives, it is large and floating"] 浮 means high; large means somewhat larger than the pulses of the other positions. If very large and floating, illness; floating but not large, also illness; large but not floating, also illness; neither large nor floating, also illness; not in its position, also illness; in its position but unable to be large and floating, also illness.

[On "when *yangming* arrives, it is short and rough"] Coming and going without fluency is called rough; coming and going not far is called short. If very short, illness; if very rough, illness; if neither short nor rough, also illness; if not in its position, also illness; if in its position but unable to be short and rough, also illness.

[On "when greater yang arrives, it is large and long"] Coming and going far is called long. If very large, illness; if very long, illness; long but not large, also illness; large but not long, also illness; not in its position, also illness; in its position but unable to be long and large, also illness.

[On "If it arrives in harmony, it is balanced"] Without excess it is balanced; regulated, neither weak nor strong, is harmony.

[On "if it arrives excessively, there is illness"] String-like like a drawn bow, slippery like strung pearls, sunken and attached to the bone, floating higher than the skin, rough and halting, short like hemp seeds or millet, large like a hat-pin, long like a pulled cord: all mean arriving excessively.

[On "if it arrives contrary, there is illness"] Rough instead of string-like, thin instead of large, floating instead of sunken, sunken instead of floating, long and slippery instead of short and rough, strong and replete instead of soft and empty, large instead of thin: all are signs of qi contrary to its constant balance; when there is illness, it appears thus.

[On "if it should arrive but does not, there is illness"] The position of the qi has already arrived but the pulse qi does not respond.

[On "if it arrives before it should, there is illness"] Divining by the calendar, whenever one reaches a solar period that corresponds to a division of the six positions of the year, the pulse image should change in response, as in years of southern or northern governance. If the pulse changes first before the sequence of qi has moved, it arrives ahead of heaven, hence illness.

[On "if yin and yang are exchanged, it is dangerous"] Not responding to heaven's constancy, the qi appear crossed, lose their constant positions and appear exchanged: a yang pulse appears in a yin position, a yin pulse in a yang position. This is appearing in exchanged positions. The two qi are disordered, hence the qi is endangered.
Song editors: According to the *Liuwei zhi dalun*: The Emperor said, "There are arrivals that arrive, arrivals that do not arrive, and arrivals in excess; why?" Qibo said, "Arriving when it should is harmony; failing to arrive when it should means the coming qi is insufficient; arriving before it should means the coming qi is in surplus." The Emperor said, "What of failing to arrive when it should, and arriving before it should?" Qibo said, "Responding is compliance; otherwise it is counterflow. Counterflow produces change; change produces illness." The Emperor said, "Please speak of the response." Qibo said, "The birth of things is its response; qi and pulse are its response." What is called pulse response is this pulse response.

## Notes

The answer giving the pulses when the six qi arrive and how to tell balance, illness and danger.
