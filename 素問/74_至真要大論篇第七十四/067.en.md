# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §67 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In the prevalence of greater yang, congealing chill arrives, water freezes out of season, and feathered creatures transform late. Hemorrhoids and malaria break out; when cold reversal enters the stomach, heart pain arises within; the genitals develop sores, the private functions are impaired, pulling on the genitals and thighs; the sinews and flesh are tense and heavy, blood vessels congeal and become rough, network vessels fill and change color, or there is bloody discharge; the skin is blocked and swollen, the abdomen full and appetite reduced; heat instead travels upward, with pain in the head, nape, fontanel, vertex and Naohu, and eyes as if they would fall out; when cold enters the lower burner, it passes on to become soggy diarrhea."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "pain in the head, nape, fontanel, vertex and Naohu ... soggy diarrhea"] The five *chen* and five *xu* years. Cold qi overrides and presses, and yang cannot overcome it, so standing water freezes even outside the cold season. Water qi greatly prevails and yang fire does not move, so the generation and transformation of feathered creatures comes later. 拘 means tense; 苛 means heavy; 絡 means network vessels. The tip of greater yang qi is at the vertex, so heat instead travels up to the head. Its vessel starts at the inner canthus of the eye, rises across the forehead, crosses at the vertex, enters to network the brain, and comes back out to descend the nape separately; hence pain in the fontanel, vertex and Naohu, and eyes as if they would fall out. 濡 means watery diarrhea.
Song editors: According to the *Jiayi jing*, "hemorrhoids and malaria, pain in the head, nape, fontanel, vertex and Naohu, eyes as if they would fall out" is illness of the greater yang channel.

## Notes

The prevalence of greater yang. Qibo's speech ends here.
