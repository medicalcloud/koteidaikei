# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §150 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Emperor said, "How are illnesses treated that are not cured by regulating the qi? Of toxic and non-toxic [medicines], which comes first and which after? I wish to hear the way."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Of toxic and non-toxic, which comes first and which after? I wish to hear the way"] The kinds of illness arising are four: first, those that begin with movement of qi and form something within; second, those that do not arise from movement of qi and form something outside; third, those that begin with movement of qi and arise as illness within; fourth, those that do not arise from movement of qi and arise as illness outside. Those formed within through movement of qi are accumulations and gatherings, concretions and conglomerations, tumors, goiters, nodules, epilepsy and the like. Those formed outside are abscesses and swellings, sores, scabs and scabies, ulcers and hemorrhoids, tremors and spasms, edema, red eyes, rashes, puffy swelling, pain and itching and the like. Those arising within not through movement of qi are lodged fluids, food stagnation, hunger and satiety, exhaustion, retained food, cholera, sorrow, fear, joy and anger, longing and pent-up worry and the like. Those arising outside are miasma, malignant goblins, insects and snakes, *gu* poison, flying corpse, ghost strike, collisions, falls, wind, cold, summer heat and damp, cuts, arrow wounds, stabs and slashes, beatings and the like. Among these four kinds, some are cured by treating the inside alone, some by treating the inside as well, some by treating the outside alone, some by treating the outside as well, some by treating inside first and outside after, some by treating outside first and inside after; some must be attacked with assembled toxic drugs, some must be regulated and guided with non-toxic drugs. In 几 [all] these kinds, the methods applied may be heavy or light, moderate or urgent, contracting or dispersing, moistening or drying, softening or hardening; practitioners differ in understanding, each indulges his own 已 [mind], liking red and rejecting white. Hence he asks again.

## Notes

A question about treating illnesses not cured by regulating qi alone, and the order of toxic and non-toxic medicines.
