# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §44 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In years when *yangming* is in the spring and dryness runs rampant and prevails, haze and mist make it cool and dark. People suffer frequent vomiting with bitterness, frequent deep sighing, pain of the heart and rib-sides with inability to turn over, and in severe cases dry throat, a dusty face, a body without luster, and heat instead on the outside of the feet.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "in severe cases dry throat, a dusty face, a body without luster, and heat instead on the outside of the feet"] This refers to the *jiazi*, *bingzi*, *wuzi*, *gengzi*, *renzi*, *jiawu*, *bingwu*, *wuwu*, *gengwu* and *renwu* years. 霿霧 means dark and indistinct like mist. 清 is a thin cold. It means mist arises, dark and hazy, so that the shapes of things cannot be distinguished, and it is thinly cold. Pain of the heart and rib-sides means pain in the rib-side beside the heart. A dusty face means the face has a color as if covered with dust.
Song editors: According to the *Jiayi jing*, "frequent vomiting with bitterness, frequent deep sighing, pain of the heart and rib-sides with inability to turn over, and in severe cases a dusty face, a body without luster, and heat instead on the outside of the feet" is illness of the gallbladder; "dry throat, dusty face" is illness of the liver. Presumably in years when *yangming* is in the spring, metal flourishes and overcomes wood, hence such illnesses. Also, according to the *Maijie*: "What is called pain of the heart and rib-sides in lesser yang means lesser yang is abundant; the abundant is what the heart displays. In the ninth month yang qi is exhausted and yin qi abundant, hence pain of the heart and rib-sides. What is called inability to turn over is because yin qi stores things; when things are stored they do not move, hence one cannot turn over."

## Notes

The case of *yangming* in the spring (*zi* and *wu* years).
