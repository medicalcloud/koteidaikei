# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §16 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "Wind moving on the earth is what is called the root; the other qi follow the same rule. What is rooted in heaven is the qi of heaven; what is rooted in earth is the qi of earth. Heaven and earth combine their qi, the six periods divide, and the myriad things are transformed and born.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Wind moving on the earth is what is called the root; the other qi follow the same rule"] When *jueyin* is in the spring, wind moves on the earth; when lesser yin, heat; when greater yin, damp; when lesser yang, fire; when *yangming*, dryness; when greater yang, cold. Hence "the other qi follow the same rule". The root means the primordial qi above the six qi.

[On "What is rooted in heaven is the qi of heaven; what is rooted in earth is the qi of earth"] What transforms in heaven is heaven's qi; what transforms in earth is earth's qi.
Song editors: According to the *Changes*, "What is rooted in heaven draws close to what is above; what is rooted in earth draws close to what is below"; this is what it means.

[On "Heaven and earth combine their qi, the six periods divide, and the myriad things are transformed and born"] The myriad things dwell between heaven and earth and are all generated and transformed by the six qi. From the workings of yin and yang, nothing has ever escaped generation and transformation to go outside yin and yang.

## Notes

The answer that the qi of the spring moving on the earth is the root, and that heaven's and earth's qi combine to generate all things. Qibo's speech continues into the next unit.
