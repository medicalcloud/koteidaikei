# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §144 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Emperor said, "Good. The hundred illnesses all arise from wind, cold, summer heat, damp, dryness and fire, through their transformations and changes. The classic says, 'Drain what is abundant, supplement what is depleted.' I have bestowed this on the practitioners, yet when they use it they still cannot achieve complete success. I wish to make the essential way certain to work, responding like drum to drumstick, like pulling out a thorn or washing off sweat. May I hear of the skill of the practitioner, the craftsman, the spirit and the sage?"

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "all arise from wind, cold, summer heat, damp, dryness and fire, through their transformations and changes"] Wind, cold, summer heat, damp, dryness and fire are the six qi of heaven. Those that are quiet and compliant are transformations; those that move and alter are changes. Hence "their transformations and changes".

[On "like pulling out a thorn or washing off sweat. May I hear of the skill of the practitioner, the craftsman, the spirit and the sage"] Needling is called the skill of the practitioner and craftsman; medicine is called that of the spirit and sage.
Song editors: According to the *Nanjing*, "Knowing by looking is called spirit; knowing by listening is called sage; knowing by asking is called practitioner; knowing by feeling the pulse is called craftsman. Knowing by the outside is called sage; knowing by the inside is called spirit."

## Notes

Since practitioners do not achieve complete success with the classic's supplementing and draining, he asks for a sure way of treatment.
