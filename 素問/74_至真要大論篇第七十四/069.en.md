# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §69 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "The prevalence of *jueyin* is treated with sweet and cool, assisted with bitter and acrid, drained with sour. The prevalence of lesser yin is treated with acrid and cold, assisted with bitter and salty, drained with sweet. The prevalence of greater yin is treated with salty and hot, assisted with acrid and sweet, drained with bitter. The prevalence of lesser yang is treated with acrid and cold, assisted with sweet and salty, drained with sweet. The prevalence of *yangming* is treated with sour and warm, assisted with acrid and sweet, drained with bitter. The prevalence of greater yang is treated with sweet and hot, assisted with acrid and sour, drained with salty."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The prevalence of greater yang is treated with sweet and hot, assisted with acrid and sour, drained with salty"] When the six prevalences arrive, each first goes to what cannot overcome it. Hence what cannot overcome should first be drained to open its way, and then the prevailing qi is drained so that it withdraws and dissolves. If in treating the prevalences one does not drain and dispel them, the prevailing qi grows ever stronger and produces various illnesses within.
Song editors: On examination, all these treatments first drain what cannot overcome and then drain what comes to prevail; only for the prevalence of greater yang does "treat with sweet and hot" differ. 甘 is perhaps an error for 苦; if it said "treat with bitter and hot", the treatments of all six prevalences would be consistent.

## Notes

The answer giving treatments for the prevalences of the six qi.
