# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §78 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "The retaliation of *jueyin* is treated with sour and cold, assisted with sweet and acrid, drained with sour, moderated with sweet. The retaliation of lesser yin is treated with salty and cold, assisted with bitter and acrid, drained with sweet, contracted with sour, released with acrid and bitter, softened with salty. The retaliation of greater yin is treated with bitter and hot, assisted with sour and acrid, drained, dried and percolated with bitter. The retaliation of lesser yang is treated with salty and cold, assisted with bitter and acrid, softened with salty, contracted with sour, released with acrid and bitter; in releasing do not keep away from heat, and do not offend warmth and coolness; the same rule as for lesser yin. The retaliation of *yangming* is treated with acrid and warm, assisted with bitter and sweet, drained with bitter, purged with bitter, supplemented with sour. The retaliation of greater yang is treated with salty and hot, assisted with sweet and acrid, hardened with bitter. In treating all prevalences and retaliations, heat what is cold, cool what is hot, make cool what is warm, warm what is cool, contract what is scattered, disperse what is repressed, moisten what is dry, moderate what is tense, soften what is hard, harden what is brittle, supplement what is weak, drain what is strong; settle each qi, be sure it is clear and quiet, and the qi of illness will decline and depart, returning to where it belongs. This is the general outline of treatment."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The retaliation of *jueyin* is treated with sour and cold ... moderated with sweet"] If it is not greatly moderated, it does not end even in summer. Retaliation is heavier than prevalence, so it is treated with acrid and cold.
Song editors: Another edition has "treated with acrid and cold" for "treated with sour and cold".

[On "contracted with sour, released with acrid and bitter, softened with salty"] If one does not induce strong sweating but attacks with cold, it holds on until mid-autumn, the heat lies hidden and knots within, becoming heart heat, shortage of qi and of strength so that one cannot rise. If the hidden heat does not disperse, it returns to the bones.

[On "The retaliation of greater yin is treated with bitter and hot ... drained, dried and percolated with bitter"] If it is not dried and percolated, over time it becomes swelling of the body, abdominal fullness, stiffness of the joints, oppressive fullness arising within the calves and Futu, and puffy swelling on the inner side of the knees, lower back and shins.

[On "in releasing do not keep away from heat, and do not offend warmth and coolness; the same rule as for lesser yin"] If one does not induce sweating to take away the abundant yang, heat runs rampant within into the four limbs, causing *jieyi*, which cannot be named: one cannot call it heat, for it is not severe; nor cold, nor strong, nor weak, for none is severe; it cannot be named in words, hence it is called *jieyi*. Crude doctors call it ghost qi or evil illness. If it does not end over a long time, the bones are hot, the marrow dries and the teeth are parched; this is bone-heat illness. Sweating takes away the yang so that no heat lingers. Hence for sweating, even in summer months when heat causes illness, or in convalescence, hot medicines are used to release it. In spring and autumn, even if fire heat prevails, one must not induce sweating with hot medicine; if sweat does not come, the medicine's heat grows severe within, aids the illness to become malaria, and in turn assails the spirit. Hence "do not offend warmth and coolness". The qi of lesser yin is hot and its treatment is the same, hence "the same rule as for lesser yin". If its sweat is repeatedly taken, the fluids are exhausted, so contract with sour and moisten with salty.
Song editors: The *Tianyuan* [*Liuyuan*] *zhengji dalun* says "in releasing the exterior do not keep away from heat".

[On "The retaliation of *yangming* is treated with acrid and warm ... supplemented with sour"] Draining means percolation: sweat, urine, and hot baths are all this. Around the autumn equinox one may also release it. If there is prevalence in spring, follow the method for prevalence, or if it does not end, also soak in hot water to harmonize inside and outside. After angry retaliation the qi is all depleted, so supplement it to settle and preserve the qi. The treatment of the other retaliations is the same.

[On "The retaliation of greater yang is treated with salty and hot, assisted with sweet and acrid, hardened with bitter"] If it is not hardened, cold qi changes within, stopping and recurring, recurring and stopping, dragging on for years and producing great cold disease.

[On "the qi of illness will decline and depart, returning to where it belongs. This is the general outline of treatment"] The qi of greater yang is cold, those of lesser yin and lesser yang hot, that of *jueyin* warm, that of *yangming* cool, that of greater yin damp. When there is prevalence and retaliation, double each [opposing] qi to regulate it, so that it can be balanced. 宗 means belonging. When regulation does not lose its principle, excess qi returns of itself to where it belongs and deficient qi settles of itself where it dwells. When prevalence and retaliation have declined and ended, supplement, nourish and stabilize each. Be sure it is clear and quiet, without disturbing it recklessly, and the six qi circulate and the five spirits are at peace. Treating and calming the cold and heat of the movements and qi likewise returns each to the qi controlling heaven and earth.

## Notes

The answer giving treatments for the retaliations of the six qi and the general outline of treating prevalence and retaliation.
- Wang Bing's note on the retaliation of *jueyin* says "treated with acrid and cold", differing from "sour and cold" in the main text (the Song editors say another edition has "acrid and cold").
