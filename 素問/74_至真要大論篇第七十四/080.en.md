# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §80 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "Above the middle of the body there are three qi; this is heaven's domain, governed by heaven's qi. Below the middle of the body there are three qi; this is earth's domain, governed by earth's qi. Name the qi by the names, name the places by the qi, and speak of the illnesses. The middle is what is called Tianshu. Hence when the upper prevails and the lower is ill as well, name it by earth; when the lower prevails and the upper is ill as well, name it by heaven. What is called the arrival of prevalence is when the requiting qi is bent and hidden and has not yet broken out; when retaliation arrives, the names do not differ by heaven and earth, and all take the retaliating qi as the rule."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Name the qi by the names ... The middle is what is called Tianshu"] The middle of the body means precisely the navel. Some take the waist as the middle of the body, taking it as the center; this goes beyond the center of heaven. The people of the Central Plain are all like this. If one stretches the arms to point at heaven and the feet to point at earth and measures with a cord, the middle falls exactly at the navel. Hence it also 日 [says], "The middle is what is called Tianshu." Tianshu lies exactly two inches on either side of the navel. "Three qi": for example, when lesser yin controls heaven, there is heat above and greater yang in the middle combined with it, making three. All six qi are so. The controller of heaven has three qi, and so does the controller of earth; hence three qi above the middle of the body and three below. Name the qi by the names, name the places by the qi, and speak of the forms and patterns of illness by the cold and heat of the qi and places. Thus, the qi of foot *jueyin* lies on the inner side of the foot, thigh and shin, rises to the lower abdomen and follows the rib-sides. The qi of foot *yangming* is on the top of the foot, the outside of the shin and the front of the thigh, rises beside the navel, follows the chest and breasts up to the face. The qi of foot greater yang starts at the eye, rises across the forehead, networks the head, descends the nape and back, passes the lower back, crosses the hip pivot, runs down the back of the thigh into the back of the knee, threads the calf, emerges behind the outer ankle, and reaches the outer side of the little toe. The qi of foot greater yin follows the inner side of the foot, thigh and shin and rises in front of the abdomen and rib-sides; foot lesser yin is the same. The qi of foot lesser yang follows the outer side of the shin, rises along the sides of the abdomen and rib-sides, follows the cheek and ear to the outer canthus, at the side of the head. These are the domains governed by the six qi of the foot. The qi of hand *jueyin*, lesser yin and greater yin emerge crosswise from the heart and chest, follow the inner side of the arm and reach the tips of the middle finger, little finger and thumb. The qi of hand *yangming*, lesser yang and greater yang all start on the back of the hand, follow the outer side of the arm, rise to the shoulder and shoulder blade and up to the head. These are the domains governed by the six qi of the hand. To know the diagnosis of an illness, one should speak of it according to where the qi is: illnesses of 令 [cold] return to the yin domains, illnesses of heat to the yang domains. Hence when prevalence and retaliation arise, speaking first of illnesses producing cold or heat always follows this principle of things.
Song editors: According to the *Liuwei zhi dalun*, "above Tianshu, heaven's qi governs; below Tianshu, earth's qi governs; in the domain where the qi meet, human qi follows them".

[On "Hence when the upper prevails and the lower is ill as well, name it by earth; when the lower prevails and the upper is ill as well, name it by heaven"] When that qi has already prevailed and this one cannot yet retaliate, it is repressed and pent up, unable to extend, with nowhere to go: advancing, it is troubled by its enemy; retreating, it is trapped in obstruction. Hence when upper prevalence arrives, the lower is ill as well; when lower prevalence arrives, the upper is ill as well. The upper prevailing and the lower ill means earth's qi is pent up; hence from earth's constraint it is named earth illness. The lower prevailing and the upper ill means heaven's qi is blocked; hence from heaven's blockage it is named heaven illness. For what is named by heaven, the formula follows heaven's qi as the rule and attacks against earth's qi; for what is named by earth, the formula may follow heaven's qi as the rule. For example, with *yangming* controlling heaven and lesser yin in the spring, when the upper prevails and the lower is ill as well, it arises from oppression below. Heaven's qi is properly prevailing, and heaven can be opposed; hence following heaven's qi, the formula is the same as for coolness. When lesser yin and the others control heaven, the rule for upper and lower prevalence is the same.
Song editors: According to the *Liuyuan zhengji dalun*, "when the upper prevails, heaven's qi descends downward; when the lower prevails, earth's qi shifts upward"; this is what it means.

[On "when retaliation arrives, the names do not differ by heaven and earth, and all take the retaliating qi as the rule"] When prevalence arrives and illness arises before retaliation, differing names by heaven and earth is the pattern. When the retaliating qi breaks out, whatever arises, regardless of upper or lower prevalence, all follows the retaliating qi as the master of the cold or heat of the illness.

## Notes

The answer: above the middle of the body (Tianshu, the navel) heaven's qi governs, below it earth's qi; in prevalence illnesses are named by heaven and earth, in retaliation by the retaliating qi.
- In the first note of Wang Bing, 中原之人悉如此矣, and the latter half of the second note (on treating what is named by heaven or by earth) are hard to construe; the translation is tentative (not verified). In the first note, 日半 is read as 曰半, as in the Siku quanshu edition (Daizhige *Huangdi neijing suwen*), and 令病 as 冷病.
