# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §88 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "It dwells in a position not its own, where it does not suit. When it retaliates greatly against the prevalence, the host overcomes it, hence it falls ill instead. This is what is called fire, dryness and heat."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "It dwells in a position not its own ... hence it falls ill instead"] It is like leaving one's own palace to go to another state: one's strength is already declining, one does not get on with the host, resentment follows behind, and one only seeks advantage. Hence when its strength is spent [in retaliation], the host attacks it in turn, and it falls ill itself.

[On "This is what is called fire, dryness and heat"] Lesser yang is fire, *yangming* dryness, lesser yin heat. When lesser yin or lesser yang is in the spring, fire dwells in the position of water; when *yangming* controls heaven, metal dwells in the position of fire. When metal retaliates against the prevalence, the fire host overcomes it; when fire retaliates against the prevalence, the water host overcomes it. In the prevalence and retaliation of the other qi, there is no illness qi of the host overcoming. Hence it also says "what is called fire, dryness and heat".

## Notes

The answer that qi dwelling outside its own position, when it retaliates greatly, is overcome by the host qi and falls ill.
