# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §32 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Emperor said, "You say to examine where yin and yang are and regulate them. The treatise says, 'When *renying* and the inch opening respond to each other, equal in size like a pulled cord, it is called balanced.' Where yin is, what of the inch opening?"

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The treatise says, 'When *renying* and the inch opening respond to each other ... it is called balanced'"] Song editors: On examination, the passage from "the treatise says" to "called balanced" is originally text of the *Lingshu jing*. It now appears in the *Jiayi jing*: "The inch opening governs the interior, *renying* the exterior. The two respond to each other, going and coming together, equal in size like a pulled cord. In spring and summer *renying* is slightly larger; in autumn and winter the inch opening is slightly larger. Hence it is called balanced."

[On "Where yin is, what of the inch opening"] Where yin is, the pulse is sunken and does not respond, and is not equal like a pulled cord; its sign is rather drooping. Hence he asks to clarify it.

## Notes

A question about the inch-opening pulse where yin is.
