# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "When *jueyin* controls heaven, its transformation is by wind; when lesser yin controls heaven, by heat; when greater yin controls heaven, by damp; when lesser yang controls heaven, by fire; when *yangming* controls heaven, by dryness; when greater yang controls heaven, by cold. The illnesses are named by the positions of the viscera on which they preside."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When *jueyin* controls heaven, its transformation is by wind"] Flying and rising, drumming and splitting, harmonious qi comes forth; the flourishing and withering of the myriad things all transform, change, succeed and fail by it.

[On "when lesser yin controls heaven, by heat"] Blazing, steaming, oppressive warmth, so the multitudes of things grow luxuriant.

[On "when greater yin controls heaven, by damp"] Clouds and rain moisten and enrich, and fluids are produced.

[On "when lesser yang controls heaven, by fire"] Blazing, fierce and brilliant, it melts the disaster of cold.

[On "when *yangming* controls heaven, by dryness"] The transformation of drying proceeds, so things do not rot with damp.

[On "when greater yang controls heaven, by cold"] The transformation opposing yang.
Song editors: On examination, 陽 in "opposing yang" in the note is perhaps an error.

[On "The illnesses are named by the positions of the viscera on which they preside"] Liver wood is positioned in the east, heart fire in the south, spleen earth in the southwest and the four corners, lung metal in the west, kidney water in the north; these are the fixed positions of the five viscera. But the six qi drive the five movements, and where they arrive, if the qi do not suit each other there is illness; if they suit, there is harmony. Hence it first [speaks of] where the six qi preside and then of the illnesses of the five viscera.

## Notes

The answer giving the transformations of the six qi controlling heaven, and naming illnesses by the positions of the viscera they preside over.
