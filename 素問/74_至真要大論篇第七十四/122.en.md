# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §122 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "Lesser yang and greater yin follow the root; lesser yin and greater yang follow both root and tip; *yangming* and *jueyin* follow neither tip nor root but the middle. Hence for those following the root, transformation arises from the root; for those following tip and root, there are transformations of both; for those following the middle, the middle qi is their transformation."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Lesser yang and greater yin follow the root ... follow neither tip nor root but the middle"] The root of lesser yang is fire and that of greater yin damp; root and end are the same, so they follow the root. The root of lesser yin is heat and its tip yin; the root of greater yang is cold and its tip yang; root and end differ, so they follow both root and tip. The middle of *yangming* is greater yin, the middle of *jueyin* lesser yang; root and end differ from the middle, so they follow neither tip nor root but the middle. Following the root, the tip or the middle is in each case taking that as the governing function of transformation.

[On "Hence for those following the root ... the middle qi is their transformation"] Transformation means the original master of qi transformation. When there is illness, treat it with cold or heat by the function of the original master qi.
Song editors: According to the *Liuwei zhi dalun*: "Above lesser yang, fire qi governs, and in the middle *yangming* [*jueyin*] appears. Above *jueyin* [*yangming*], dry qi governs, and in the middle greater yin appears. Above greater yang, cold qi governs, and in the middle lesser yin appears. Above *jueyin*, wind qi governs, and in the middle lesser yang appears. Above lesser yin, heat qi governs, and in the middle greater yang appears. Above greater yin, damp qi governs, and in the middle *yangming* appears. These are the roots. Below the root is what appears in the middle; below what appears is the tip of the qi. Root and tip differ, and the responses of the qi have different images." This is what it means.

## Notes

The answer on what the six qi follow among root, tip and middle qi.
- In the Song editors' quotation of the *Liuwei zhi dalun*, "in the middle *yangming* appears" (for lesser yang) and "above *jueyin*, dry qi governs" read in the text of chapter 68 "in the middle *jueyin* appears" and "above *yangming*, dry qi governs". The translation supplies the chapter 68 readings in brackets.
