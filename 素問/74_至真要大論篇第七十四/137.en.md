# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §137 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Hence the saying 'The qi guard and control one another like the weights of a balance, which must not lose each other. The qi of yin and yang, when clear and quiet, keep generation and transformation in order; when they stir, severe diseases arise' means this."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The qi guard and control one another like the weights of a balance, which must not lose each other"] 権衡 is a steelyard. The qi of heaven and earth, cold and heat facing each other, warmth and coolness looking across at each other, are like holding a steelyard: if high, not right; if low, not right; when the two are equal and do not usurp each other's order, there is clarity and quiet, and generation and transformation each get their share.

[On "when clear and quiet, keep generation and transformation in order; when they stir, severe diseases arise' means this"] Stirring means altering the constant balanced signs and becoming calamity. 苛 means severe.
Song editors: According to the *Liuwei zhi dalun*, "Success and failure lean on and lie hidden in each other, arising from movement; when movement does not 巳 [cease], change arises."

## Notes

Concluding that when the qi of yin and yang are balanced and quiet like a steelyard there is order, and when they stir illness arises. Qibo's speech ends here.
