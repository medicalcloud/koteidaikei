# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §147 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "All wind with trembling and dizziness belongs to the liver. All cold with contraction and tension belongs to the kidneys. All qi with fullness and oppression belongs to the lungs. All damp with swelling and fullness belongs to the spleen. All heat with clouding and spasms belongs to fire. All pain, itching and sores belong to the heart. All reversal, retention and discharge belong to the lower. All wilting, panting and retching belong to the upper. All lockjaw and drum-like shivering, as if the guard of the spirit were lost, belong to fire. All tetany with stiff neck belongs to damp. All counterflow surging upward belongs to fire. All distension with enlarged abdomen belongs to heat. All agitation and mania belong to fire. All sudden rigidity belongs to wind. All illnesses with sound that drum like a drum when tapped belong to heat. All illnesses with puffy swelling, aching soreness and fright belong to fire. All twisting cramps and turbid fluids belong to heat. All illnesses with fluids clear, transparent and cold belong to cold. All retching of sour fluid and sudden pouring diarrhea with urgency belong to heat. Hence the *Great Essentials* says, 'Carefully keep to the mechanisms of illness, attend to what each belongs to; seek out what is present, seek out what is absent; hold responsible what is abundant, hold responsible what is depleted; first [consider] the five prevalences, free the blood and qi, make them regulated and flowing, and bring about harmony and balance.' This is what it means."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "All wind with trembling and dizziness belongs to the liver"] The nature of wind is to move; wood qi is the same.

[On "All cold with contraction and tension belongs to the kidneys"] 収 means drawing in; 引 means tension. Cold things contract; water qi is the same.

[On "All qi with fullness and oppression belongs to the lungs"] In high autumn the qi is cool and mist gathers like smoke. When coolness arrives the qi is hot, and when it is severe again the qi is exhausted; by examining the images of things, what they belong to can be known. 膹 means swelling fullness; 鬱 means rushing pressure. In the functioning of qi, metal qi is the same.

[On "All damp with swelling and fullness belongs to the spleen"] Where soil is thin, water is shallow; where soil is thick, water is deep; where the land is level it is dry, where it is high it is damp. In having damp qi, earth qi is the same.

[On "All heat with clouding and spasms belongs to fire"] Signs of the image of fire.

[On "All pain, itching and sores belong to the heart"] When the heart is calm, pain is slight; when the heart is agitated, pain is severe. All things arise from the heart; pain, itching and sores arise from the heart.

[On "All reversal, retention and discharge belong to the lower"] The lower means the lower burner, the qi of the liver and kidneys. Guarding and controlling below is the qi of the kidneys; binding the gates is the qi of the liver. Hence reversal, retention and discharge all belong to the lower. 厥 means qi counterflow; 固 means locked retention. All cases of qi counterflowing upward, or retention that cannot be released, intake and output without measure, and dryness and damp without constancy, depend on the governance and guarding of the lower burner.

[On "All wilting, panting and retching belong to the upper"] The upper means the upper burner, the qi of the heart and lungs. The pressing and melting of blazing heat is the qi of the heart; receiving the heat and dividing the transformation is the qi of the lungs. Heat is pent up and transforms above, so the illness belongs to the upper burner.
Song editors: On examination, wilting seems not to be an illness of the upper. Wang Bing's note does not explain why it belongs to the upper, leaving later readers in doubt. Now according to the *Wei lun*, "When the five viscera cause wilting, it is because the lungs are hot and their lobes scorched, breaking out as wilting and lameness"; hence "belongs to the upper". Wilting also means lung wilting.

[On "All lockjaw and drum-like shivering, as if the guard of the spirit were lost, belong to fire"] Heat arising within.

[On "All tetany with stiff neck belongs to damp"] Greater yang injured by damp.

[On "All counterflow surging upward belongs to fire"] The functioning of the flaring-up nature.

[On "All distension with enlarged abdomen belongs to heat"] Heat pent up within, arising from lung distension.

[On "All agitation and mania belong to fire"] Heat abundant in the stomach and the four 未 [extremities].

[On "All sudden rigidity belongs to wind"] Yang is pent up within and yin moves outside.

[On "All illnesses with sound that drum like a drum when tapped belong to heat"] This means there is sound.

[On "All illnesses with puffy swelling, aching soreness and fright belong to fire"] Heat qi is abundant.

[On "All twisting cramps and turbid fluids belong to heat"] 反戻 means cramping sinews; fluids means urine.

[On "All illnesses with fluids clear, transparent and cold belong to cold"] What comes out above and below, as vomit and as urine.

[On "All retching of sour fluid and sudden pouring diarrhea with urgency belong to heat"] Sour means sour water and its taste.

[On "first [consider] the five prevalences ... bring about harmony and balance.' This is what it means"] Profound are the words of the sage; in principle it must be so. Seeking out what is present or absent and holding responsible what is depleted or abundant means everything has its cause. For example, when there is great cold and one heats it strongly but it does not become hot, there is no fire; when heat comes and goes, appearing by day and hiding by night, or breaking out at night and stopping by day, moving with the seasons, there is no fire: one should aid the heart. Again, when there is great heat and one cools it strongly but it does not become cool, there is no water; when heat stirs and stops, coming and going suddenly, now moving and now stopping, there is no water: one should aid the kidneys. Internal blockage with retching counterflow so that food cannot enter means there is fire. Illness with retching and vomiting, food coming back up long after eating, means there is no fire. Sudden rapid pouring diarrhea with food not digested means there is no water. Long-lasting sloppy diarrhea, stopping and starting without regularity, means there is no water. Hence when the heart is abundant it produces heat; when the kidneys are abundant they produce cold; when the kidneys are depleted, cold moves within; when the heart is depleted, heat withdraws inward. Also, heat not getting cold means there is no fire; cold not getting heat means there is no water. When cooling does not cool, hold responsible the absence of water; when heating does not heat, hold responsible the absence of fire; when heating does not last, hold responsible the depletion of the heart; when cooling does not last, hold responsible the deficiency of the kidneys. Drain what is present, supplement what is absent; supplement the depleted, drain the abundant; for what lies between, open what is blocked so that there is no obstruction above or below. When qi and blood flow and are regulated, cold and heat harmonize of themselves and yin and yang are regulated and flowing. Thus among formulas there are cases where heat is treated with cold and after cooling neither water nor food can enter, and cold is attacked with heat and after heating clouding and agitation arise; this is because qi does not flow and becomes blocked. Taking water and fire as the guide, the other qi can be known. Hence "seek out what is present, seek out what is absent; hold responsible what is abundant, hold responsible what is depleted". Making the qi flow and be regulated is the wondrous way. The five prevalences means the five phases prevailing in turn: first take as the rule the mutual prevalence of the five phases, of cold, heat, warmth, coolness and damp, and of sour, salty, sweet, acrid and bitter.

## Notes

The so-called nineteen articles on the mechanisms of illness: the viscera, regions and qi to which various symptoms belong, followed by a quotation from the *Great Essentials* on keeping to the mechanisms of illness and seeking out presence, absence, depletion and abundance. Qibo's speech ends here.
- 胘 in the main text is read as 眩 ("dizziness"), and 四未 in Wang Bing's note as 四末 ("four extremities").
- The first part of the note on the lungs ("when coolness arrives the qi is hot, and when it is severe again the qi is exhausted") is hard to construe; the translation is tentative (not verified).
