# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §47 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "When the various qi are in the spring: when wind runs rampant within, treat with acrid and cool, assist with bitter, moderate it with sweet, disperse it with acrid. When heat runs rampant within, treat with salty and cold, assist with sweet and bitter, contract it with sour, release it with bitter. When damp runs rampant within, treat with bitter and hot, assist with sour and bland, dry it with bitter, drain it with bland. When fire runs rampant within, treat with salty and cold, assist with bitter and acrid, contract it with sour, release it with bitter. When dryness runs rampant within, treat with bitter and warm, assist with sweet and acrid, purge it with bitter. When cold runs rampant within, treat with sweet and hot, assist with bitter and acrid, drain it with salty, moisten it with acrid, harden it with bitter."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "when wind runs rampant within, treat with acrid and cool, assist with bitter, moderate it with sweet, disperse it with acrid"] The nature of wind likes warmth and dislikes coolness, hence it is treated with coolness; this is treating with the overcoming qi. Assisting with bitter follows what benefits it. When wood suffers from tension, moderate it with sweet; when it suffers repression, disperse it with acrid. The *Zangqi fa shi lun* says, "The liver suffers from tension; quickly feed it sweet to moderate it. The liver desires to disperse; quickly feed it acrid to disperse it"; this is what it means. 食 is also pronounced like 飼: eating oneself is 食, feeding another is 飼. The proper flavors of the great rule are thus, but those who make formulas need not use them all; with one or two assistants, stop when the illness is gone. The other qi are all the same.

[On "When heat runs rampant within, treat with salty and cold ... release it with bitter"] The nature of heat dislikes cold, hence it is treated with cold. When heat is greatly abundant and severe in the exterior, release it with bitter; if not exhausted, restrain it again with cold; if restraint by cold does not exhaust it, release it again with bitter, and contract it with sour. For severe cases, repeat the formula; for slight cases, one formula will surely end it. For those that break out and stop intermittently, also contract with sour.

[On "When damp runs rampant within, treat with bitter and hot ... drain it with bland"] Damp is the opposite of dryness, hence it is treated with bitter and hot and assisted with sour and bland. Dryness removes damp, so bitter dries the damp; bland opens the orifices, so bland percolates and drains. The *Zangqi fa shi lun* says, "The spleen suffers from damp; quickly feed it bitter to dry it"; the *Lingshu jing* says, "Bland opens the orifices"; the *Shengqi tongtian lun* says, "When the flavor is excessively bitter, spleen qi is not moistened and stomach qi becomes thick"; these make clear that bitter dries.
Song editors: According to the *Tianyuan* [*Liuyuan*] *zhengji dalun*, "below is greater yin; its transformation below is sweet and warm".

[On "When fire runs rampant within, treat with salty and cold ... release it with bitter"] The great prevalence of fire qi arises from the anger of the heart [fire] in the heart and abdomen. Salty is soft and softening by nature, so it treats this, and sour contracts it. As a general rule, when one judges that sweating is needed, assist with acrid; one need not rely on bitter flavor to induce sweating. What should be softened is treated with salty. The *Zangqi fa shi lun* says, "The heart desires softness; quickly feed it salty to soften it. The heart suffers from slackness; quickly feed it sour to contract it"; this is what it means.

[On "When dryness runs rampant within, treat with bitter and warm, assist with sweet and acrid, purge it with bitter"] Warmth benefits [what is] cool by nature, hence it is treated with bitter. Purging means promoting evacuation so that [dryness] cannot take hold.
Song editors: According to the *Zangqi fa shi lun*, "The lungs suffer from qi counterflowing upward; quickly feed them bitter to drain it; drain with acrid, supplement with sour." Also, below, for dryness running rampant when controlling heaven, it says "assist with sour and acrid"; here it says "sweet and acrid", so 甘 ("sweet") should perhaps be 酸 ("sour"). The *Liuyuan zhengji dalun* has "below, sour and hot", which again differs from the treatment with bitter and warm; it also says "contract it with sour to settle the lower, and in severe cases drain it with bitter".

[On "When cold runs rampant within, treat with sweet and hot ... harden it with bitter"] Treating cold with heat crushes the prevailing and breaks the function of its qi so that it does not proliferate. The assistance of bitter and acrid is to carry the matter through.
Song editors: According to the *Zangqi fa shi lun*, "The kidneys suffer from dryness; quickly feed them acrid to moisten them. The kidneys desire firmness; quickly feed them bitter to firm them. Supplement with bitter, drain with salty." The old note quoted this under "when damp runs rampant within", where it makes no sense; we now move it here.

## Notes

The answer giving the treatments (main and assisting flavors) when the six qi in the spring run rampant within.
- The *Tianyuan zhengji dalun* cited by the Song editors presumably refers to chapter 71, the *Liuyuan zhengji dalun*.
