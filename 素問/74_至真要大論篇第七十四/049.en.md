# 素問 至真要大論篇第七十四 §49 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "When *jueyin* controls heaven and wind runs rampant and prevails, the great void is darkened with dust, clouds and things are thrown into turmoil, spring qi arises in the cold [season], and flowing water does not freeze. People suffer pain in the stomach duct right at the heart, propping up both rib-sides, blockage of the diaphragm and throat, food and drink not going down, stiffness of the root of the tongue, vomiting on eating, cold diarrhea and abdominal distension, sloppy diarrhea, conglomerations, and water blockage. Hibernating creatures do not depart. The illness is rooted in the spleen. If Chongyang is cut off, it is death and cannot be treated.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "cold diarrhea and abdominal distension ... The illness is rooted in the spleen"] This refers to the *yisi*, *dingsi*, *jisi*, *xinsi*, *guisi*, *yihai*, *dinghai*, 巳 [*ji*] *hai*, *xinhai* and *guihai* years. In these years people's illnesses gather in the center. Wind moves from heaven, so dust rises in the great void; wind stirs and swirls, so clouds and things are thrown into turmoil. Dust is blue-green dirt; when distant things cannot be distinguished, it is dust darkness. Earth illnesses readily cause diarrhea. If there is water illness, urine is blocked and does not come down; if there is great diarrhea, the menses too are often blocked and cut off.
Song editors: According to the *Jiayi jing*, "stiffness of the root of the tongue, vomiting on eating, abdominal distension with sloppy diarrhea, conglomerations, water blockage" is illness of the spleen; and illness of the stomach is "abdominal 脾 distension, pain in the stomach duct right at the heart, propping up both rib-sides, blockage of the diaphragm and throat, food and drink not going down". Presumably in years when *jueyin* controls heaven, wood overcomes earth, hence such illnesses.

[On "The illness is rooted in the spleen. If Chongyang is cut off, it is death and cannot be treated"] Chongyang is on the instep, where the pulsing vessel meets the hand; it is the qi of the stomach. If the Chongyang pulse is faint, food and drink diminish; if cut off, neither medicine nor food enters, and what goes down the throat comes back up. Attacking it, [medicine] does not enter; nourishing it, [life] does not arise; pathogenic qi grows stronger day by day and true qi is cut off within. Hence death is certain and recovery impossible.

## Notes

The first of the six cases in which the qi controlling heaven runs rampant and prevails: *jueyin* (*si* and *hai* years). It gives the viscus at the root of the illness and the pulse of that viscus's qi whose cutting off means death. Qibo's speech continues through unit 54.
- The reading of 脾 in 腹脾脹 in the Song editors' note is uncertain.
