# 素問 三部九候論篇第二十 §18 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "When the pulses of the nine indicators are all sunken, fine, suspended and cut off, it is yin, governing winter; therefore death comes at midnight. When they are abundant, agitated, panting and rapid, it is yang, governing summer; therefore death comes at midday. Therefore those with cold-and-heat illness die at dawn; those with heat in the centre or heat illness die at midday; those with wind illness die at dusk; those with water illness die at midnight; those whose pulse is now sparse and now rapid, now slow and now fast, die when the day rides on the seasonal ends. When the body and flesh are already wasted, even if the nine indicators are in harmony, one still dies. Even if the seven diagnostic signs appear, if the nine indicators are all compliant, one does not die. What is called not dying refers to illnesses of wind qi and of menstruation, which resemble the illnesses of the seven signs but are not; hence it says one does not die. If there is an illness of the seven signs and the pulse signs are also ruined, one dies, and there will surely be hiccup and belching. One must carefully ask how the illness began and what the present illness is, and then feel along each pulse, look at whether the channels and network vessels are floating or sunken, and follow them above and below, against and with. If the pulse is fast, there is no illness; if slow, there is illness; if the pulse does not come and go, death; if the skin clings [to the bones], death."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the pulses of the nine indicators ... death comes at midday"] Positions have no constant abode, and things at their extreme reverse. In the sense of *Qian* and *Kun*: when yin is at its extreme, the dragons fight in the fields; when yang is at its extreme, the arrogant dragon has regret. Therefore with pulses at the extreme of yin or yang, death comes at midnight or midday.

[On "Therefore those with cold-and-heat illness die at dawn"] This too is change at the extreme. At dawn wood dominates, and the qi of wood is wind; therefore at the time of dominant wood the cold-and-heat illness ends in death. The *Shengqi tongtian lun* (chapter 3) says, "When due to dew and wind, cold and heat arise"; by this, cold-and-heat illness is caused by the pressing of wind.

[On "those with heat in the centre or heat illness die at midday"] It is the extreme of yang.

[On "those with wind illness die at dusk"] *Mao* and *you* are opposed.

[On "those with water illness die at midnight"] Because water dominates.

[On "those whose pulse is now sparse and now rapid ... die when the day rides on the seasonal ends"] *Chen*, *xu*, *chou* and *wei* are where earth lodges and dominates; the spleen qi is cut off within; therefore they die when the day rides on the seasonal ends.

[On "When the body and flesh are already wasted ... one still dies"] This too means form and qi not matching. With the signs of "flesh wasted and the body cannot move" above, one dies even if the nine indicators are balanced.

[On "Even if the seven diagnostic signs appear ... one does not die"] If only the nine indicators comply with the decrees of the four seasons, one lives even though the seven signs appear together. 従 means comply.

[On "What is called not dying ... hence it says one does not die"] In wind illness the pulse is large and rapid; in menstrual illness the pulse is small and faint. Although the signs are roughly like those of the seven signs, the signs of death and life differ; therefore one does not die.

[On "If there is an illness of the seven signs and the pulse signs are also ruined, one dies"] It says that although with the seven signs one does not die if the nine indicators comply, if the illness has the form of the seven signs and the pulse response is ruined and disordered, then even if all nine indicators comply one still cannot live.

[On "there will surely be hiccup and belching"] The essence of the stomach is exhausted within and the spirit does not guard the heart; therefore at death hiccup and belching break out. The *Xuanming wuqi* (chapter 23) says: "The heart produces belching; the stomach produces hiccup."

[On "One must carefully ask how the illness began and what the present illness is"] 方 means precisely. It says one must trace the beginning and grasp the end.

[On "If the pulse is fast, there is no illness"] Because the qi is strong and abundant.

[On "if slow, there is illness"] Because the qi is insufficient.

[On "if the pulse does not come and go, death"] The essence and spirit have gone.

[On "if the skin clings [to the bones], death"] The bones are dry and withered.

## Notes

If the pulses of the nine indicators are yin (sunken, fine, suspended, cut off), death comes at midnight; if yang (abundant, agitated, panting, rapid), at midday. The hour of death differs by illness (dawn, midday, dusk, midnight, the seasonal ends). If the flesh is wasted, death comes even if the nine indicators are in harmony; even with the seven signs, if the nine indicators comply one does not die. One should ask about the beginning and present state of the illness before feeling the pulse.
- Wang Bing explains the death at the extremes of yin and yang by the *Yi* images of *Qian* and *Kun*: "dragons fighting in the fields" and "the arrogant dragon has regret".
- Daizhige (the plain text) writes 旋絶 for 懸絶.
