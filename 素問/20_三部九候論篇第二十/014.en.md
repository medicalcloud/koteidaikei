# 素問 三部九候論篇第二十 §14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "When the body is abundant but the pulse fine, and the breath short and insufficient for breathing, it is dangerous. When the body is thin but the pulse large, with much qi in the chest, it is fatal. When form and qi match, one lives; when comparison shows disharmony, one is ill; when the three regions and nine indicators all fail one another, one dies. When the pulses above and below, left and right, respond to each other like a pestle pounding, the illness is severe; when above and below, left and right, fail one another and cannot be counted, one dies. Even if the indicators of the middle region alone are in harmony, if they fail the other organs, one dies; if the indicators of the middle region are diminished, one dies. When the eyes are sunken inward, one dies."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the body is abundant but the pulse fine ... it is dangerous"] Form and qi are contrary; therefore the vital qi is in great danger. The *Yuji zhenzang lun* (chapter 19) says, "When form and qi match, it is called treatable." Now the qi of the pulse is insufficient and the body abundant and in surplus; the signs do not support each other; therefore it is dangerous. "Dangerous" means close to death but some still live. The *Cizhi lun* (chapter 53) says, "When the qi is replete the form is replete; when the qi is depleted the form is depleted. This is the norm; the opposite is illness." Now a fine pulse and scant qi mean weak qi; a sturdy, abundant body means abundant form. With abundant form and weak qi, the vital qi is endangered.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's annotated edition, the *Jiayi jing* and the *Maijing* write 死 "fatal" for 危.

[On "When the body is thin but the pulse large ... it is fatal"] Here the qi of the form is insufficient and the qi of the pulse in surplus; therefore death. With a thin body, large pulse and much qi in the chest, the storehouses of form are already harmed; hence fatal. All such cases are form and qi not matching.

[On "When form and qi match, one lives; when comparison shows disharmony, one is ill"] 参 means comparing; 伍 means grouping by kind. If in comparing and grouping there is disharmony, that is, not following the norm, there is illness.

[On "when the three regions and nine indicators all fail one another, one dies"] "Fail" means the signs of qi do not resemble one another. There are seven diagnostic signs of failure, as described in the text below.

[On "When the pulses above and below ... cannot be counted, one dies"] The three regions and nine indicators, above and below, left and right, make eighteen diagnoses in all. "Like a pestle pounding" means large, rapid and drumming, like the pestle going up and down. The *Maiyao jingwei lun* (chapter 17) says, "If large, the illness is advancing"; therefore the illness is severe. "Cannot be counted" means more than ten beats per breath. The *Maifa* says: "When a person breathes out once and the pulse comes twice, and breathes in once and it comes twice, it is normal; three times is called leaving the channel; four times, loss of essence; five times, death; six times, the end of life." Now failing one another and uncountable is beyond ten beats; if even five beats mean death, how much more ten.

[On "Even if the indicators of the middle region alone are in harmony ... if the indicators of the middle region are diminished, one dies"] The middle region, left and right, has six diagnoses in all. When the upper and lower regions no longer correspond, even if the middle region alone is in harmony, it cannot last long. Being diminished relative to above and below also means declining qi; therefore all die. 減 means being partially scant. We, [Lin] Yi and others, note that the old edition lacked the eight characters 中部之候相減者死; we have added them according to Quan Yuanqi's annotated edition and the *Jiayi jing*. Moreover, the note had an explanation of 減 while the classic lacked the text; this had dropped out after Wang's note.

[On "When the eyes are sunken inward, one dies"] This speaks of the greater yang. The greater yang vessel starts at the inner canthus; sunken eyes mean the greater yang is cut off; therefore death. The greater yang alone is mentioned because it governs the qi of all the yang.

## Notes

Death and life are judged from the match of form and qi, the correspondence of the three regions and nine indicators, the pulses above and below and left and right, the middle region, and sunken eyes.
- According to the Song editors, Quan Yuanqi's edition, the *Jiayi jing* and the *Maijing* write "fatal" for "dangerous".
- The eight characters "if the indicators of the middle region are diminished, one dies" were absent from the old edition and were supplied by the Song editors (Lin Yi and others) from Quan Yuanqi's edition and the *Jiayi jing* (explained within Wang Bing's note).
- Kanripo has 過十至 within Wang Bing's note (the base text has a blank).
