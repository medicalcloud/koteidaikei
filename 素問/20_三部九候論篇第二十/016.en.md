# 素問 三部九候論篇第二十 §16 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Examine the nine indicators: one alone small is ill; one alone large is ill; one alone fast is ill; one alone slow is ill; one alone hot is ill; one alone cold is ill; one alone sunken is ill. With the left hand [on] the foot, press five *cun* above the ankle, and with the right hand flick at the ankle: if the response passes beyond five *cun*, wriggling, there is no illness; if the response is fast and strikes the hand turbidly, there is illness; if it strikes the hand slowly, there is illness; if the response does not reach five *cun* upward and there is no response to flicking, death. Therefore when the flesh is wasted and the body cannot move, death; when the middle region is now sparse and now rapid, death; when the pulse is intermittent and hooked, the illness is in the network vessels. The nine indicators correspond to one another, above and below as one, and must not fail one another. If one indicator lags, there is illness; if two lag, the illness is severe; if three lag, the illness is dangerous. 'Lagging' means not responding together. Examine the fu and zang to know the time of death or life; one must first know the regular pulses and then know the morbid pulses. When the true-zang pulse appears, death comes when it is overcome. When the qi of the foot greater yang is cut off, the feet cannot bend or stretch, and at death the eyes are turned up."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Examine the nine indicators ... one alone sunken is ill"] This is what is meant by "there are seven diagnostic signs of failure". When the seven diagnostic signs appear in the pulse, it means comparison shows disharmony, and one names the illness according to what alone differs.

[On "With the left hand [on] the foot ... flick at the ankle"] Both hands and feet are taken. Above the wrist is the hand greater yin vessel; above the ankle is the foot greater yin vessel. The foot greater yin governs the flesh and corresponds to the lower region; the hand greater yin governs qi and corresponds to the middle region. Hence the text below says, "When the flesh is wasted and the body cannot move, death; when the middle region is now sparse and now rapid, death." We, [Lin] Yi and others, note that the *Jiayi jing* and Quan Yuanqi's annotated edition both read: "With the left hand press five *cun* above the ankle on the foot, and with the right hand flick at the ankle." Quan Yuanqi's note says: "Above the inner ankle, where Yinjiao emerges, it communicates with the bladder and is tied to the kidney; the kidney is the Gate of Life; therefore one takes it to make good or ill fortune clear." The present text lacks one 而 and has an extra 庶 and 足. Wang's note, explaining it as taking both hands and feet, is particularly far-fetched; one should take Quan Yuanqi's old annotated edition and the *Jiayi jing* as correct.

[On "if the response passes beyond five *cun*, wriggling, there is no illness"] Because the qi is harmonious.

[On "if the response is fast and strikes the hand turbidly ... slowly, there is illness"] 渾渾 means disordered; 徐徐 means slow.

[On "if the response does not reach five *cun* ... death"] The qi is cut off; therefore there is no response.

[On "Therefore when the flesh is wasted and the body cannot move, death"] When the qi of grain declines outside, the flesh seems wasted away, and the heavenly truth is exhausted within; therefore the body cannot move. When both the true and the grain [qi] decline, death arrives. 去 is like "move away".

[On "when the middle region is now sparse and now rapid, death"] Now sparse and now rapid means the qi is lost and disordered; therefore death.

[On "when the pulse is intermittent and hooked, the illness is in the network vessels"] Hooked is the summer pulse, and the qi of summer is in the network vessels; therefore the illness is in the network vessels. When the network vessels receive evil, the channels are obstructed; therefore it is intermittent.

[On "The nine indicators correspond ... must not fail one another"] "Above and below as one" means slow and fast, small and large are equal.

[On "'Lagging' means not responding together"] 倶 is like "same", "one".

[On "Examine the fu and zang to know the time of death or life"] When illness enters the fu one recovers; when it enters the zang one dies. Therefore the standard for the time of death and life is known by examination.

[On "one must first know the regular pulses and then know the morbid pulses"] The regular pulses are the pulses of the four seasons and five zang.

[On "When the true-zang pulse appears, death comes when it is overcome"] The so-called true-zang pulses: the true liver pulse arrives tense inside and out, like running the hand along a knife blade, sharp and taut, like pressing the string of a zither; the true heart pulse arrives hard and beating, like running the hand over Job's-tears seeds, string upon string; the true spleen pulse arrives weak, now rapid, now sparse; the true lung pulse arrives large and empty, like feathers touching a person's skin; the true kidney pulse arrives beating and cut off, like flicking a stone with the finger, snapping. All five mean obtaining the true-zang pulse without stomach qi. The *Pingren qixiang lun* (chapter 18) says, "The stomach is the constant qi of a normal person; when a person has no stomach qi it is called adverse, and the adverse die" — this is what is meant. "Death when overcome" means dying at the time of what controls it. The *Pingren qixiang lun* says, "If the liver appears on *geng* and *xin* days one dies; the heart, on *ren* and *gui*; the spleen, on *jia* and *yi*; the lung, on *bing* and *ding*; the kidney, on *wu* and *ji*" — this is death when overcome.

[On "When the qi of the foot greater yang is cut off ... the eyes are turned up"] The foot greater yang vessel starts at the inner canthus, rises to the forehead, crosses at the vertex, enters from the vertex to connect with the brain, comes out again, branches down the nape, follows the inside of the shoulder blade and runs alongside the spine to the lower back; its branch separates from the shoulder blade, descends through the buttocks, passes the hip joint, joins below in the back of the knee, pierces the calf, follows the heel and reaches the outer side of the foot. When the greater yang qi is cut off, death is like this.
Song editors: The *Zhenyao jingzhong lun* (chapter 16) records the signs of termination of the three yang and three yin vessels; here only the single sign of the foot greater yang being cut off is given; the rest is probably a lacuna. Further, 貫臋 in the note is written 貫胂 in the *Jiayi jing*; Mr Wang writes 貫胂 in his notes to the *Juelun* and *Ci nüe lun*, and 貫臋 in his note to the *Ci yaotong lun*. Considering the *Jiayi jing*, 臀 in the note should be 胂.

## Notes

Among the nine indicators, the one that alone differs (small, large, fast, slow, hot, cold, sunken) shows where the illness is (the seven diagnostic signs). The method of pressing above the ankle and flicking, judging life and death by the response of the pulse, lagging of the indicators, the true-zang pulse, and the cutting off of the foot greater yang qi are described.
- "With the left hand on the foot, press five *cun* above the ankle ...": the Song editors (Lin Yi and others), following the *Jiayi jing* and Quan Yuanqi's edition, read it as "with the left hand press five *cun* above the ankle, with the right hand flick at the ankle", calling Wang Bing's explanation far-fetched. The translation follows the base text's characters.
- 意苡子 and 戊巳 in Wang Bing's note are taken as 薏苡子 and 戊己.
