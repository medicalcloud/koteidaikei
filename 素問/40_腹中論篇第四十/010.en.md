# 素問 腹中論篇第四十 §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Take four [parts] of cuttlefish bone and one of *lüru*, combine the two, make pills with sparrow eggs the size of adzuki beans, and take five pills before meals, washed down with abalone broth. It benefits the intestines and the damaged liver."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "washed down with abalone broth. It benefits the intestines"] Song editors: Another edition writes 傷中 "the damaged centre" [for 腸中].

[On "washed down with abalone broth ... the damaged liver"] Medicine first and food afterward is called 後飯. According to the ancient *Bencao jing*, cuttlefish bone, *lüru* and the others do not treat blood desiccation; yet the classic's method uses them to attack what produced and started it. When one is drunk, toils and enters the bedchamber, the essential qi in the kidney is exhausted; when the menses diminish and do not come, bad blood lingers within. When the essential qi is exhausted, the genitals wilt and do not rise and there is no semen; when bad blood lingers, blood impediment clings within and does not disperse. Hence these four drugs are first used in the prescription. The ancient *Bencao jing* says, "Cuttlefish bone: salty, cold and neutral, non-toxic; governs blocked menses in women. *Lüru*: acrid, cold and neutral, slightly toxic; governs the dispersal of bad blood. Sparrow egg: sweet, warm and neutral, non-toxic; governs impotence in men, strengthening and warming, increasing semen and producing children. Abalone: acrid and pungent, warm and neutral, non-toxic; governs static blood and blood impediment in the four limbs that does not disperse." Seeking the text and grasping its meaning, the purport of the prescription is thus, and treatment is given accordingly.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* and *Taisu* write 䕡茹 for 藘茹. On examination, the properties in Wang's note are those of 䕡茹, so 藘 should be changed to 䕡. Moreover, the *Bencao* gives cuttlefish bone as "slightly warm" instead of "cold", and sparrow egg as "sour" instead of "sweet", differing from Wang's note.

## Notes

The prescription for blood desiccation using cuttlefish bone, *lüru* (taken as madder), sparrow eggs and abalone broth; one of the few drug prescriptions in the *Suwen*.
- 後飯: Wang Bing takes it as medicine first and food afterward (taking before meals).
- 藘茹: the Song editors hold that it should be changed to 䕡茹.
