# 素問 五蔵生成篇第十 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Therefore, if one eats much salty food, the vessels congeal and become rough and the complexion changes; if much bitter, the skin withers and the body hair falls out; if much acrid, the sinews become tense and the nails wither; if much sour, the flesh becomes calloused and shrunken and the lips curl up; if much sweet, the bones ache and the hair falls. This is how the five flavours harm. Therefore the heart desires bitter, the lung acrid, the liver sour, the spleen sweet, and the kidney salty. This is how the five flavours correspond.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "if one eats much salty food, the vessels congeal and become rough and the complexion changes"] The heart is joined with the vessels and its splendour is the complexion. Salty benefits the kidney, which overcomes the heart; the heart cannot prevail; therefore the vessels congeal and become rough and the complexion changes.

[On "if much bitter, the skin withers and the body hair falls out"] The lung is joined with the skin and its splendour is the body hair. Bitter benefits the heart, which overcomes the lung; the lung cannot prevail; therefore the skin withers and the hair falls out.

[On "if much acrid, the sinews become tense and the nails wither"] The liver is joined with the sinews and its splendour is the nails. Acrid benefits the lung, which overcomes the liver; the liver cannot prevail; therefore the sinews become tense and the nails dry and wither.

[On "if much sour, the flesh becomes calloused and shrunken and the lips curl up"] The spleen is joined with the flesh and its splendour is the lips. Sour benefits the liver, which overcomes the spleen; the spleen cannot prevail; therefore the flesh becomes calloused and shrunken and the skin of the lips curls up.

[On "if much sweet, the bones ache and the hair falls"] The kidney is joined with the bones and its splendour is the hair. Sweet benefits the spleen, which overcomes the kidney; the kidney cannot prevail; therefore the bones ache and the hair falls out.

[On "This is how the five flavours harm"] The five flavours enter the mouth, are conveyed to the intestines and stomach, and nourish the five zang within. Each has what it nourishes and what it desires; in desiring, they harm one another. Hence the text below says:

[On "Therefore the heart desires bitter"] Because it accords with fire.

[On "the lung acrid"] Because it accords with metal.

[On "the liver sour"] Because it accords with wood.

[On "the spleen sweet"] Because it accords with earth.

[On "the kidney salty"] Because it accords with water.

[On "This is how the five flavours correspond"] Each returns and gathers according to its desire.

## Notes

Overeating each of the five flavours harms, through the controlling cycle, the part of the body joined with a zang and its outward splendour; each zang also desires a particular flavour.
- Wang Bing explains that a flavour benefits one zang, which then overcomes another (salty benefits the kidney, which overcomes the heart, etc.).
- Wikisource (the plain text) writes 侭 for 䐢 (probably a garble).
