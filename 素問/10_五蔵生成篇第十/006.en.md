# 素問 五蔵生成篇第十 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

All the vessels belong to the eyes; all the marrow belongs to the brain; all the sinews belong to the joints; all the blood belongs to the heart; all the qi belongs to the lung. These are the morning and evening of the four limbs and the eight ravines. Therefore when a person lies down, the blood returns to the liver; the liver receives blood and can see, the feet receive blood and can walk, the palms receive blood and can grip, and the fingers receive blood and can grasp. If, on rising from lying down, one is blown on by the wind, blood that congeals in the skin causes impediment, in the vessels causes roughness, and in the feet causes reversal. In these three cases the blood moves but cannot return to its hollows; therefore there is impediment and reversal.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "All the vessels belong to the eyes"] The vessels are the storehouse of blood. The *Xuanming wuqi* (chapter 23) says, "Prolonged looking harms the blood"; by this it is clear that all the vessels belong to the eyes.
Song editors: Huangfu Shi'an says: "The *Nine Scrolls* says, 'The heart stores the vessels, and the vessels house the spirit.' The spirit illumination penetrates the body; hence it says they belong to the eyes."

[On "all the marrow belongs to the brain"] The brain is the sea of marrow; therefore all the marrow belongs to it.

[On "all the sinews belong to the joints"] The firm, knotted parts of the qi of the sinews all connect between the bones and joints. The *Xuanming wuqi* (chapter 23) says, "Prolonged walking harms the sinews"; by this it is clear that all the sinews belong to the joints.

[On "all the blood belongs to the heart"] The blood resides within the vessels and belongs to the heart. The *Bazheng shenming lun* (chapter 26) says, "Blood and qi are a person's spirit," and the spirit is the master of the heart; therefore all the blood belongs to the heart.

[On "all the qi belongs to the lung"] Because the lung governs qi.

[On "These are the morning and evening of the four limbs and the eight ravines"] "Ravine" is the name of the small meeting places of the flesh; the eight ravines are the elbows, knees and wrists. In this way qi, blood, sinews and vessels each have their rise and fall; hence there are morning and evening.

[On "Therefore when a person lies down, the blood returns to the liver"] The liver stores blood and the heart moves it. When a person moves, the blood circulates in all the channels; when at rest, it returns to the liver. Why? Because the liver governs the sea of blood.

[On "the liver receives blood and can see"] This speaks of its function. The eyes are the official of the liver; therefore the liver receives blood and can see.

[On "the feet receive blood and can walk"] When qi moves, blood flows; therefore the feet receive blood and can walk.

[On "the palms receive blood and can grip"] This corresponds to the function of grasping.

[On "the fingers receive blood and can grasp"] This corresponds to the function of taking hold. Blood and qi are a person's spirit; therefore whatever receives blood can function.

[On "If, on rising ... blood that congeals in the skin causes impediment"] This means numbness.

[On "in the vessels causes roughness"] 泣 means the blood does not flow freely.

[On "and in the feet causes reversal"] 厥 means cold, counterflowing feet.

[On "In these three cases ... therefore there is impediment and reversal"] The hollows are the paths of the blood, the great channel passages.

## Notes

The vessels, marrow, sinews, blood and qi belong respectively to the eyes, brain, joints, heart and lung. When a person lies down the blood returns to the liver, and the eyes, feet, palms and fingers each function by receiving blood. If one is struck by wind on rising, the blood congeals, causing impediment, roughness and reversal.
- "When a person lies down, the blood returns to the liver" is often cited as the basis of the doctrine that the liver stores blood.
- "Eight ravines": Wang Bing takes them as the elbows, knees and wrists.
- Daizhige writes 四肢 for 四支.
