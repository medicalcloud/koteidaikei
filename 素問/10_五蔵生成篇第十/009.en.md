# 素問 五蔵生成篇第十 §9 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The smallness, largeness, slipperiness, roughness, floating and sinking of the pulse can be distinguished with the fingers; the images of the five zang can be inferred by analogy; the appearances and sounds of the five zang can be recognized by the mind; the subtle signs of the five colours can be examined with the eyes. If one can combine pulse and colour, one can be completely sure. When a red pulse arrives panting and hard, the diagnosis is accumulated qi in the centre, at times harming eating; it is called heart impediment. It is contracted from external illness: through anxious thought the heart becomes depleted, so evil follows it. When a white pulse arrives panting and floating, with depletion above and repletion below, fright, accumulated qi in the chest, panting and depletion, it is called lung impediment, with cold and heat. It is contracted by having sex while drunk. When a green pulse arrives long and flicking left and right, with accumulated qi below the heart propping the flanks, it is called liver impediment. It is contracted from cold and damp, and follows the same rule as hernia, with lower back pain, cold feet and headache. When a yellow pulse arrives large and empty, with accumulated qi in the abdomen and reversal qi, it is called reversal hernia; women follow the same rule. It is contracted by hastily using the four limbs, sweating, and meeting the wind. When a black pulse arrives hard and large above, with accumulated qi in the lower abdomen and genitals, it is called kidney impediment. It is contracted by bathing in cold water and then lying down.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The smallness, largeness ... can be distinguished with the fingers"] A small pulse is fine and small; a large pulse is full and large; a slippery pulse flows smoothly coming and going; a rough pulse comes and goes with difficulty; a floating pulse floats under the hand; a sinking pulse is found only by pressing. Although these forms are many and different, if the hand is skilful and the mind attentive, the fingers can distinguish them.

[On "the images of the five zang can be inferred by analogy"] "Image" means qi image. It says that although the five zang are hidden and unseen, their qi images, natures and functions can still be inferred by analogy with things. How so? The liver resembles wood and is bending and straight; the heart resembles fire and blazes upward; the spleen resembles earth and is calm and still; the lung resembles metal and is firm and decisive; the kidney resembles water and moistens downward. All of these broadly set out the main signs; whatever within them changes with circumstances and whose image extends by association can be inferred by analogy with things of the same kind.

[On "the appearances and sounds of the five zang can be recognized by the mind"] "Sounds" means the five notes. The liver's note is *jue*, the heart's *zhi*, the spleen's *gong*, the lung's *shang*, the kidney's *yu*; these are their normal correspondences. As for their winning and losing against each other and the good or bad that appears in the voice, one with keen ears and a quick mind can recognize and know them by the mind.

[On "the subtle signs of the five colours can be examined with the eyes"] "Colour" means the complexion. The liver's colour is green, the heart's red, the spleen's yellow, the lung's white, the kidney's black; these are their normal colours. As for their qi images alternating and subtly showing good and ill fortune, one with clear eyes and far-reaching wisdom can divine and see them.

[On "If one can combine pulse and colour, one can be completely sure"] With a green colour the pulse is wiry; with red, hooked; with yellow, intermittent; with white, hair-like; with black, hard. These are the normal colours and pulses. If one compares their similarities and differences and pronounces on success or ruin, one is thorough and not misled, and every undertaking is entirely sure. Examples of illness by colour and pulse are as described below.

[On "When a red pulse arrives panting and hard ... it is called heart impediment"] "Panting" means the pulse arrives as if suddenly panting. When a zang that dwells high is ill, the pulse takes a panting form; therefore it is mentioned only for the heart and lung. Panting means the heart qi is insufficient; hardness means the qi of illness is in surplus. The heart vessel starts in the heart and chest; therefore accumulated qi is in the centre and at times harms eating. "Accumulation" means the qi of illness accumulating and gathering; "impediment" means the qi of the zang not spreading and moving.

[On "It is contracted from external illness: through anxious thought the heart becomes depleted, so evil follows it"] Through anxious thought the heart becomes depleted, so external evil lodges there.

[On "When a white pulse arrives ... it is called lung impediment, with cold and heat"] Panting is insufficiency; floating is depletion of the lung. Insufficiency of the lung is called depletion of the heart. When above is depleted, below must be full and replete. Because of insufficiency, one is easily frightened and qi accumulates in the chest. A pulse panting and floating means the lung itself is insufficient; panting and depleted means the heart qi rises and encroaches, the lung receives heat and the qi cannot nourish. Therefore it is called lung impediment, and outwardly there is cold and heat.

[On "It is contracted by having sex while drunk"] The flavour of wine is bitter and drying and benefits the heart within; when very drunk one enters the bedchamber; therefore the heart qi rises and overcomes the lung.

[On "When a green pulse arrives long and flicking left and right ... it is called liver impediment"] A pulse long and flicking is wiry and tight. Tight is cold qi; struck by dampness it is wiry. The liver governs the flanks and is near the heart; therefore qi accumulates below the heart and props the flanks. The "Examples of Pulse Names" in the *Zhengli lun* says: "A tight pulse is like a cut rope; it flicks against the hand left and right."

[On "It is contracted from cold and damp ... lower back pain, cold feet and headache"] A tight pulse is cold; a long pulse is damp. Hernia as an illness also arises from cold and damp; hence "the same rule as hernia". Because cold and damp are below, the lower back aches. The liver vessel starts at the foot, rises to the head, emerges at the forehead and meets the *du* vessel at the vertex; therefore when ill, the feet are cold and the head aches. 清 also means cold.

[On "When a yellow pulse arrives ... it is called reversal hernia"] A large pulse is qi; an empty pulse is depletion. Being both qi and depletion, the spleen qi accumulates in the abdomen. If the kidney qi counterflows upward, it is reversal hernia; if the kidney qi does not rise, there is only depletion and accumulation of spleen qi.

[On "women follow the same rule. It is contracted by hastily using the four limbs, sweating, and meeting the wind"] "Women follow the same rule" means their signs are the same. The qi of wind communicates with the liver; therefore if one sweats and meets the wind, the spleen qi accumulates and fills the abdomen.

[On "When a black pulse arrives hard and large above ... it is called kidney impediment"] "Above" means the *cunkou*. The kidney governs the lower burner; therefore the qi accumulates in the lower abdomen and genitals.

[On "It is contracted by bathing in cold water and then lying down"] When damp qi harms the lower part, it naturally returns to the kidney; how much more if one bathes and then lies down — how could one not fall ill? The *Lingshu jing* says: "Below the waist, it is dampness that strikes."

## Notes

Methods of examination by pulse, image, sound and colour; combining pulse and colour, one can be completely sure. Then, from the form of the pulse of each colour and the location of the accumulated qi, it describes heart impediment, lung impediment, liver impediment, reversal hernia and kidney impediment, with their causes.
- "Red pulse", "white pulse", etc. mean the pulse of the zang corresponding to that colour (or the pulse of a person with that complexion); Wang Bing explains it as diagnosis combining colour and pulse.
- Wang Bing explains "panting" as the pulse arriving as if suddenly panting.
- 尔 in Wang Bing's note is the base text's form, translated as 爾.
- The passage from the *Zhengli lun* cited by Wang Bing has not been checked in other sources.
