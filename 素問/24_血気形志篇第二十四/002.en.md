# 素問 血気形志篇第二十四 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

To find the back transport points, first measure [with a grass stalk] the distance between the two nipples and fold it in the middle; then with another stalk measure and remove half; when done, make two corners prop against each other [to form a triangle]. Then raise it to measure the back: put one corner at the top, level with the Dazhui of the spine, with the two corners below; what corresponds to the lower corners is the transport point of the lung. Move down one measure again: the transport point of the heart. Move down one measure again: the left corner is the transport point of the liver and the right corner that of the spleen. Move down one measure again: the transport point of the kidney. These are called the transport points of the five zang, the standard for moxibustion and needling.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "To find the back transport points ... the transport point of the lung"] 度 means measure. It says that one measures the distance between the nipples with a grass stalk, removes one quarter, makes the oblique sides equal to the horizontal, folds it into three corners, and puts the upper corner level with the Dazhui of the spine; then the two lower corners correspond to the lung transport points.

[On "Move down one measure again: the transport point of the heart"] It means putting the upper corner level with the third vertebra.

[On "Move down one measure again ... the standard for moxibustion and needling"] The *Lingshu jing* and the *Zhonggao* both say, "The lung transport point is beside the third vertebra, the heart transport point beside the fifth, the liver transport point beside the ninth, the spleen transport point beside the eleventh, and the kidney transport point beside the fourteenth." Examining the grass-measuring method of this classic, in a person of standard proportions, at the first measure the two lower corners correspond roughly to the lung transport points; at the second, roughly to the heart transport points; at the third, roughly to the seventh vertebra, beside which is the position of the diaphragm transport point. That this classic says "the left corner is the transport point of the liver and the right corner that of the spleen" differs entirely from the *Zhonggao* and other classics. Moreover, at the fourth measure the two lower corners correspond roughly to the ninth vertebra, beside which is the liver transport point. As to the classic's saying "kidney transport point", its source has not yet been traced.

## Notes

An old method of locating the back transport points of the five zang using grass stalks.
- Wang Bing points out that the positions obtained by this method do not agree with the back transport points in the *Lingshu jing* and *Zhonggao*.
- 更以他草度去半已 is read in various ways; the translation follows the word order, with additions in brackets based on Wang Bing's note (removing a quarter and forming a triangle).
- 脊 in Wang Bing's note was supplied where the transcription gave only a component description (see the 【補】 note).
