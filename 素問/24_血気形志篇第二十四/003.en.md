# 素問 血気形志篇第二十四 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the body is at ease and the mind suffers, illness arises in the vessels; treat it with moxibustion and needling. When the body is at ease and the mind is at ease, illness arises in the flesh; treat it with needles and stones. When the body suffers and the mind is at ease, illness arises in the sinews; treat it with hot compresses and *daoyin*. When the body suffers and the mind suffers, illness arises in the throat; treat it with the hundred drugs. When the body is repeatedly frightened and afraid and the channels and network vessels do not flow, illness arises as numbness; treat it with massage and medicinal wine. These are called the five [conditions of] body and mind.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the body is at ease and the mind suffers ... moxibustion and needling"] "Body" means the bodily form; "mind" means the heart's will. In detail, the seven spirits each keep different stations, but taken together they can be summed up as body and mind, outside and inside. "The body is at ease" means not toiling greatly; "the mind suffers" means brooding and deep thought. Without great toil the sinews and bones are balanced; with brooding and deep thought the nutritive and defensive qi are disrupted and blocked, and the qi and blood do not comply; hence illness arises in the vessels. Draining the abundant and supplementing the depleted is the way of moxibustion and needling, but one should still first remove the blood network vessels and then regulate. Hence the text above, "In treating any illness one must first remove its blood, then remove what afflicts, observe what it desires, and then drain what is in surplus and supplement what is insufficient," expresses this meaning.

[On "When the body is at ease and the mind is at ease ... needles and stones"] "The mind is at ease" means being delighted and forgetting worry. When the sinews and bones do not toil and the heart-spirit is delighted, the texture of the flesh is dense, the passages of qi are full and blocked, and the defensive qi becomes stagnant and knotted; hence illness arises in the flesh. When defensive qi lingers and fills, drain it with needles; when pus and blood knot and gather, break them with stones. "Stones" means stone needles, that is, *bian* stones; nowadays the 䤵 needle is used instead.

[On "When the body suffers and the mind is at ease ... hot compresses and *daoyin*"] "The body suffers" means pursuing one's work and labour. When one pursues work and labour and once overdoes the exertion, toil damage results; overexertion causes damage; hence illness arises in the sinews. 熨 means a drug compress; 引 means *daoyin*.

[On "When the body suffers and the mind suffers ... the hundred drugs"] One pursues work and labour and broods with deep thought. With worry the liver qi combines in the spleen; the liver is paired with the gallbladder, and the throat is its envoy; hence illness arises in the throat. The *Xuanming wuqi pian* says, "When essential qi combines in the liver, there is worry," and the *Qibing lun* says, "The liver is the general of the interior; it takes decisions from the gallbladder, and the throat is its envoy."
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes "exhaustion" for "throat" and "sweet drugs" for "the hundred drugs".

[On "When the body is repeatedly frightened ... massage and medicinal wine"] With fright the qi of the vessels gathers; with fear the spirit is not contained. The vessels gather and the spirit wanders; hence the channels and network vessels do not flow and there is the illness of numbness. Massage serves to open what is blocked and to guide yin and yang; medicinal wine serves to nourish the upright, expel evil, regulate the centre and order the qi. Hence in prescriptions these are suitable. 醪薬 means medicinal wine. 不仁 means not responding in function, that is, numbness and impediment (𤸷痺).

## Notes

Depending on the combination of ease or suffering of body (形) and mind (志), illness arises in different places, each with a suitable treatment.
- 恱 in Wang Bing's note is a variant of 悦.
- Wang Bing explains the illness of "body suffers and mind suffers" with reference to the liver and gallbladder. The *Xuanming wuqi pian* passage he quotes reads 并於肝則憂 in the previous chapter (chapter 23).
- According to the Song editors, the *Jiayi jing* reads "illness arises in exhaustion; treat it with sweet drugs".
