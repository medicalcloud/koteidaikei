# 素問 血気形志篇第二十四 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The constant numbers of a person: the greater yang constantly has much blood and little qi; the lesser yang constantly little blood and much qi; the *yangming* constantly much qi and much blood; the lesser yin constantly little blood and much qi; the *jueyin* constantly much blood and little qi; the greater yin constantly much qi and little blood. These are the constant numbers of heaven. The foot greater yang and lesser yin are exterior and interior; the lesser yang and *jueyin* are exterior and interior; the *yangming* and greater yin are exterior and interior. These are the yin and yang of the foot. The hand greater yang and lesser yin are exterior and interior; the lesser yang and the heart master are exterior and interior; the *yangming* and greater yin are exterior and interior. These are the yin and yang of the hand. Now, knowing what afflicts the yin and yang of hand and foot, in treating any illness one must first remove its blood, then remove what afflicts, observe what it desires, and then drain what is in surplus and supplement what is insufficient.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The constant numbers of a person ... the constant numbers of heaven"] The amounts of blood and qi are the constant numbers of heaven; therefore the way of using the needle is always to drain what is abundant.
Song editors: The *Shi'er jingshui* chapter of the *Jiayi jing* says, "The *yangming* has much blood and much qi: needle six *fen* deep and retain for ten breaths. The greater yang has much blood and much qi: needle five *fen* deep and retain for seven breaths. The lesser yang has little blood and much qi: needle four *fen* deep and retain for five breaths. The greater yin has much blood and little qi: needle three *fen* deep and retain for four breaths. The lesser yin has little blood and much qi: needle two *fen* deep and retain for three breaths. The *jueyin* has much blood and little qi: needle one *fen* deep and retain for two breaths." The amounts of blood and qi for the greater yang and greater yin differ from the *Suwen*. Moreover, the chapter on the differing forms, natures, blood and qi of the twenty-five types of yin and yang people agrees with the *Suwen*. Presumably Huangfu [Mi] was in doubt and kept both.

[On "The foot greater yang and lesser yin ... supplement what is insufficient"] "First remove its blood" means that when one sees a blood vessel abundantly full and unlike the usual, one removes it; it does not mean that in ordinary needling one always first removes blood.

## Notes

The amounts of blood and qi in the six channels, the exterior-interior pairings of the channels of hand and foot, and the order of treatment (first remove blood, then supplement and drain). The chapter contains no dialogue.
- As the Song editors point out, the amounts of blood and qi for the greater yang and greater yin disagree with the *Jiayi jing* and other chapters of the *Lingshu*. See also the Song editors' note in unit 004.
