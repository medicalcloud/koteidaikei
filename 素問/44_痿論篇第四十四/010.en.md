# 素問 痿論篇第四十四 §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The *Yangming* is the sea of the five zang and six fu and governs the moistening of the ancestral sinew; the ancestral sinew governs binding the bones and freeing the joints. The *Chong* meridian is the sea of the meridian vessels; it governs seeping into and irrigating the valleys and ravines, and joins the *Yangming* at the ancestral sinew. Yin and yang gather at the meeting of the ancestral sinew and meet at Qijie, with the *Yangming* as their chief; all belong to the *Dai* meridian and connect with the *Du* meridian. Therefore when the *Yangming* is deficient, the ancestral sinew slackens and the *Dai* meridian does not draw; therefore the feet wilt and cannot be used."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The *Yangming* is the sea of the five zang and six fu"] The *Yangming* is the stomach vessel; the stomach is the sea of water and grain.

[On "governs the moistening of the ancestral sinew ... freeing the joints"] The ancestral sinew means the vertical sinews above and below the pubic bone in the pubic hair. Above it connects with the chest and abdomen, below it passes through the hips and buttocks, and it also runs through the back and abdomen up to the head and nape; hence "the ancestral sinew governs binding the bones and freeing the joints". The lumbus is the great joint of the body that governs bending and stretching; hence "joints" (機関).

[On "The *Chong* meridian is the sea of the meridian vessels"] The *Lingshu jing* says, "The *Chong* meridian is the sea of the twelve meridians."

[On "it governs seeping into and irrigating the valleys and ravines, and joins the *Yangming* at the ancestral sinew"] Accordingly, the vertical sinews above and below the pubic bone on both sides of the navel are precisely the ancestral sinew. The *Chong* meridian rises along the abdomen five body-*fen* to each side of the navel, and the *Yangming* vessel rises one and a half body-*cun* to each side of the navel; the ancestral sinew pulses between them; hence "joins the *Yangming* at the ancestral sinew". Being the sea of the twelve meridians, it governs seeping into and irrigating the valleys and ravines. The great meetings of the flesh are valleys; the small meetings are ravines.
Song editors: On examination, 宗筋脈於中 is written 宗筋縦於中 in one version.

[On "Yin and yang gather at the meeting of the ancestral sinew ... connect with the *Du* meridian"] The ancestral sinews gather and meet within the pubic bone, coming from above downward; hence "yin and yang gather at the meeting of the ancestral sinew". The ancestral sinew runs down alongside the navel and joins at the pubic bone; the *Yangming* assists on its outside and the *Chong* meridian lies within; hence "meet at Qijie, with the *Yangming* as their chief". Qijie is where the pulse beats on both sides of the pubic hair. The *Dai* meridian arises at the lower ribs, circles the body once and connects with the *Du* meridian. The *Du* meridian arises at Guanyuan and runs up and down along the abdomen; hence "all belong to the *Dai* meridian and connect with the *Du* meridian". The *Du*, *Ren* and *Chong* meridians arise together but run differently; therefore the classic sometimes cites them variously.

[On "Therefore when the *Yangming* is deficient ... cannot be used"] The *Yangming* vessel descends from the supraclavicular fossa along the inner edge of the breast, down alongside the navel to Qijie; a branch arises at the lower mouth of the stomach, runs inside the abdomen down to join at Qijie, then descends to the thigh, reaches Futu, enters the kneecap, descends along the outer edge of the shin to the instep and enters the inner side of the middle toe; another branch separates three *cun* below the knee and enters the outer side of the middle toe. Therefore when the *Yangming* is deficient, the ancestral sinew slackens, the *Dai* meridian does not draw, and the feet wilt and cannot be used. 引 means draw.

## Notes

The *Yangming* (stomach), as the sea of the five zang and six fu, moistens the ancestral sinew and is linked with the *Chong*, *Dai* and *Du* meridians; therefore the *Yangming* is taken in treating wilting.
- The base's 閏 is emended to 潤 "moisten". The *Taisu*, the *Leijing*, the *Jiayi jing*, the quotation in the *Lingshu jizhu*, Wikisource (the plain text) and Daizhige all read 主潤宗筋; 閏, lacking the water radical, is taken as an error for 潤.
