# 素問 気穴論篇第五十八 §7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The transport points of the zang are fifty points; the transport points of the fu, seventy-two points; the heat transport points, fifty-nine points; the water transport points, fifty-seven points. On the head, five lines with five in each line, five times five, twenty-five points. On both sides of the central spine, five each, ten points in all. On both sides above Dazhui, one each, two points in all. Tongzi[liao] of the eye and Fubai, two points. In the divisions of both hip sockets, two points. Dubi, two points. Duosuowen in the ear, two points. The root of the eyebrow, two points. Wangu, two points. The centre of the vertex, one point. The occipital bone, two points. Shangguan, two points. Daying, two points. Xiaguan, two points. Tianzhu, two points. The upper and lower Juxu, four points. Quya, two points. Tiantu, one point. Tianfu, two points. Tianyou, two points. Futu, two points. Tianchuang, two points. Jianjie, two points. Guanyuan, one point. Weiyang, two points. Jianzhen, two points. Yinmen, one point. The navel, one point. The chest transport points, twelve points. The back transport points, two points. The breast transport points, twelve points. Fenrou, two points. Transversely above the ankles, two points. The yin and yang *qiao*, four points. The water transport points are in the divisions; the heat transport points are in the qi points; the cold-heat transport points are in the hollows of both knee bones, two points. The great prohibition, twenty-five, is five *cun* below Tianfu. In all, three hundred sixty-five points, through which the needle works."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The transport points of the zang are fifty points"] 蔵 means the five zang — liver, heart, spleen, lung and kidney — not including the four form-storing zang. 兪 means the well, spring, stream, river and uniting points, not the back transport points. Of these, for the liver: the well point is Dadun, the spring Xingjian, the stream Taichong, the river Zhongfeng, the uniting Ququan. Dadun is at the tip of the big toe, a chive-leaf's breadth from the corner of the nail, among the three hairs, where the foot *jueyin* vessel emerges; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain ten breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Xingjian is in the depression between the big toes where the pulse is felt, where the foot *jueyin* vessel flows [Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 流 for 留; other occurrences of "flows" are all written 留]; one may needle six body-*fen* and retain ten breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Taichong is in the depression two body-*cun* behind the base joint of the big toe [Song editors: The note to *Ci yaotong* says "on the inner side behind the base joint, in the depression two body-*cun* back, where the pulse is felt"], where the foot *jueyin* vessel pours; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain ten breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Zhongfeng is in the depression one and a half body-*cun* [Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* says one *cun*] in front of the inner ankle, taken with the foot turned up, found with the foot stretched, where the foot *jueyin* vessel runs; one may needle four body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Ququan is in the depression below the inner auxiliary bone of the knee, above the large sinew and below the small sinew, found with the knee bent, where the foot *jueyin* vessel enters; one may needle six body-*fen* and retain ten breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. For the pericardium: the well is Zhongchong, the spring Laogong, the stream Taling, the river Jianshi, the uniting Quze. Zhongchong is in the depression at the tip of the middle finger, a chive-leaf's breadth from the corner of the nail, where the hand heart-master vessel emerges; one may needle one body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, one cone. Laogong is at the artery in the centre of the palm, where the hand heart-master vessel flows; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain six breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Taling is in the depression between the two tendons behind the palm bone, where the hand heart-master vessel pours; one may needle six body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Jianshi is in the depression between the two tendons three body-*cun* behind the palm, where the hand heart-master vessel runs; one may needle six body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, seven cones [Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* says three cones]. Quze is in the depression below the inner edge of the elbow, found with the elbow bent, where the hand heart-master vessel enters; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. For the spleen: the well is Yinbai, the spring Dadu, the stream Taibai, the river Shangqiu, the uniting Yinlingquan. Yinbai is at the inner side of the tip of the big toe, a chive-leaf's breadth from the corner of the nail, where the foot greater yin vessel emerges; one may needle one body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Dadu is in the depression behind the base joint of the big toe, where the foot greater yin vessel flows; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Taibai is in the depression below the round bone on the inner side of the foot, where the foot greater yin vessel pours; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Shangqiu is in the depression slightly in front of and below the inner ankle, where the foot greater yin vessel runs; one may needle four body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Yinlingquan is in the depression below the auxiliary bone on the inner side below the knee, found with the foot stretched, where the foot greater yin vessel enters; one may needle five body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. For the lung: the well is Shaoshang, the spring Yuji, the stream Taiyuan, the river Jingqu, the uniting Chize. Shaoshang is at the inner side of the tip of the thumb, a chive-leaf's breadth from the corner of the nail, where the hand greater yin vessel emerges; one may needle one body-*fen* and retain one breath; for moxibustion, three cones [Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes one cone]. Yuji is at the scattered vessels on the inner side behind the base joint of the thumb, where the hand greater yin vessel flows; one may needle two body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Taiyuan is in the depression behind the palm, where the hand greater yin vessel pours; one may needle two body-*fen* and retain two breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Jingqu is in the depression at the *cunkou*, where the hand greater yin vessel runs; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; moxibustion is not allowed, as it harms a person's spirit. Chize is at the artery above the crease in the elbow, where the hand greater yin vessel enters; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. For the kidney: the well is Yongquan, the spring Rangu, the stream Taixi, the river Fuliu [Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 留 for 溜, and likewise in other occurrences of Fuliu], the uniting Yingu. Yongquan is in the depression in the sole, in the hollow formed when the foot is bent and the toes curled, where the foot lesser yin vessel emerges; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Rangu is in the depression below the large raised bone in front of the inner ankle, where the foot lesser yin vessel flows; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones; if much blood is seen on needling it, it makes one suddenly hungry and want to eat. Taixi is in the depression at the artery above the heel bone behind the inner ankle, where the foot lesser yin vessel pours; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Fuliu is in the depression two body-*cun* above the inner ankle [Song editors: The note to the *Ci yaotong pian* says "at the artery two *cun* above and behind the inner ankle"], where the foot lesser yin vessel runs; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, five cones. Yingu is behind the inner auxiliary bone below the knee, below the large sinew and above the small sinew, felt on pressing and found with the knee bent, where the foot lesser yin vessel enters; one may needle four body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones. Thus the transport points of the five zang are five per zang, twenty-five in all; counting both left and right vessels, fifty points.

[On "the transport points of the fu, seventy-two points"] 府 means the six fu, not including the nine form-storing fu. 兪 again means the well, spring, stream, source, river and uniting points, not the back transport points. The fu of the liver is the gallbladder: its well is Qiaoyin, spring Xiaxi, stream Linqi, source Qiuxu, river Yangfu, uniting Yanglingquan. Qiaoyin is at the tip of the fourth toe, a chive-leaf's breadth from the corner of the nail, where the foot lesser yang vessel emerges; one may needle one body-*fen* and retain one breath [Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes three breaths]; for moxibustion, three cones. Xiaxi is in the depression in front of the base joint in the cleft between the fourth and fifth toes, where the foot lesser yang vessel flows; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Linqi is in the depression behind the base joint of the fourth toe, one and a half body-*cun* from Xiaxi, where the foot lesser yang vessel pours; one may needle three body-*fen* [Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes two *fen*] and retain five breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Qiuxu is in the depression below and slightly in front of the outer ankle, three body-*cun* from Linqi, where the foot lesser yang vessel passes; one may needle five body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Yangfu is above the outer ankle [Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* says four *cun* above the outer ankle], in front of the auxiliary bone, about three body-*fen* in front of the end of Juegu, seven body-*cun* from Qiuxu, where the foot lesser yang vessel runs; one may needle five body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Yanglingquan is in the depression on the outer edge of the shin one body-*cun* below the knee, where the foot lesser yang vessel enters; one may needle six body-*fen* and retain ten breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. The fu of the spleen is the stomach: its well is Lidui, spring Neiting, stream Xiangu, source Chongyang, river Jiexi, uniting Sanli. Lidui is at the tip of the second toe, a chive-leaf's breadth from the corner of the nail, where the foot *yangming* vessel emerges; one may needle one body-*fen* and retain one breath; for moxibustion, one cone. Neiting is in the depression on the outer side between the big and second toes, where the foot *yangming* vessel flows; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain ten breaths [Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes twenty breaths]; for moxibustion, three cones. Xiangu is in the depression behind the base joint on the outer side between the big and second toes, two body-*cun* from Neiting, where the foot *yangming* vessel pours; one may needle five body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Chongyang is at the artery between the bones five body-*cun* up on the instep, three body-*cun* above Xiangu, where the foot *yangming* vessel passes; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain ten breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Jiexi is two and a half body-*cun* behind Chongyang [Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes one and a half *cun*, and the note to *Cinüe* three and a half *cun*; the two notes in the *Suwen* differ; the *Jiayi jing* should be followed], in the depression above the ankle, where the foot *yangming* vessel runs; one may needle five body-*fen* and retain five breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Sanli is three body-*cun* below the knee, between the two sinews on the outer edge of the shin bone, where the foot *yangming* vessel enters; one may needle one body-*cun* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. The fu of the lung is the large intestine: its well is Shangyang, spring Erjian, stream Sanjian, source Hegu, river Yangxi, uniting Quchi. Shangyang is on the inner side of the index finger, a chive-leaf's breadth from the corner of the nail, where the hand *yangming* vessel emerges; one may needle one body-*fen* and retain one breath; for moxibustion, three cones. Erjian is in the depression on the inner side in front of the base joint of the index finger, where the hand *yangming* vessel flows; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain six breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Sanjian is in the depression on the inner side behind the base joint of the index finger, where the hand *yangming* vessel pours; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Hegu is in the cleft between the thumb and index finger, where the hand *yangming* vessel passes; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain six breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Yangxi is in the depression between the two tendons on the upper side of the wrist, where the hand *yangming* vessel runs; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Quchi is at the outer auxiliary bone of the elbow, between the two bones when the elbow is bent, where the hand *yangming* vessel enters; take it with the hand held to the chest; one may needle five body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. The fu of the heart is the small intestine: its well is Shaoze, spring Qiangu, stream Houxi, source Wangu, river Yanggu, uniting Shaohai. Shaoze is in the depression at the tip of the little finger, one body-*fen* below the nail, where the hand greater yang vessel emerges; one may needle one body-*fen* and retain two breaths; for moxibustion, one cone. Qiangu is in the depression on the outer side of the little finger in front of the base joint, where the hand greater yang vessel flows; one may needle one body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Houxi is in the depression on the outer side of the little finger behind the base joint, where the hand greater yang vessel pours; one may needle one body-*fen* and retain two breaths; for moxibustion, one cone. Wangu is in the depression below the raised bone in front of the wrist on the outer side of the hand, where the hand greater yang vessel passes; one may needle two body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Yanggu is in the depression below the pointed bone in the wrist on the outer side of the hand, where the hand greater yang vessel runs; one may needle two body-*fen* and retain three breaths [Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes two breaths]; for moxibustion, three cones. Shaohai is in the depression outside the large bone on the inner side of the elbow, five body-*fen* from the tip of the elbow, found with the elbow bent, where the hand greater yang vessel enters; one may needle two body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, five cones. The fu of the pericardium is the triple burner: its well is Guanchong, spring Yemen, stream Zhongzhu, source Yangchi, river Zhigou, uniting Tianjing. Guanchong is at the tip of the fourth finger, a chive-leaf's breadth from the corner of the nail, where the hand lesser yang vessel emerges; one may needle one body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Yemen is in the depression between the fourth and fifth fingers, where the hand lesser yang vessel flows; one may needle two body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones. Zhongzhu is in the depression behind the base joint between the fourth and fifth fingers, where the hand lesser yang vessel pours; one may needle two body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Yangchi is in the depression above the wrist on the back of the hand, where the hand lesser yang vessel passes; one may needle two body-*fen* and retain six breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Zhigou is in the depression between the two bones three body-*cun* behind the wrist, where the hand lesser yang vessel runs; one may needle two body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Tianjing is in the depression between the two tendons one body-*cun* behind the large bone on the outer side of the elbow, found with the elbow bent, where the hand lesser yang vessel enters; one may needle one body-*cun* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. The fu of the kidney is the bladder: its well is Zhiyin, spring Tonggu, stream Shugu, source Jinggu, river Kunlun, uniting Weizhong. Zhiyin is on the outer side of the little toe, a chive-leaf's breadth from the corner of the nail, where the foot greater yang vessel emerges; one may needle one body-*fen* and retain five breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Tonggu is in the depression on the outer side of the little toe in front of the base joint, where the greater yang vessel flows; one may needle two body-*fen* and retain five breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Shugu is in the depression at the border of red and white flesh behind the base joint on the outer side of the little toe, where the foot greater yang vessel pours; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Jinggu is in the depression at the border of red and white flesh below the large bone on the outer side of the foot, found by pressing, where the foot greater yang vessel passes; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Kunlun is in the depression above the heel bone behind the outer ankle, where a fine pulse is felt, where the foot greater yang vessel runs; one may needle five body-*fen* and retain ten breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Weizhong is at the artery in the transverse crease at the centre of the popliteal fossa [Song editors: On examination, the Weizhong point agrees with the *Jiayi jing* and the notes to the *Cinüe pian* and *Bi lun*; the *Gukong lun* says "in the bend of the leg behind the knee joint, taken with the back facing up"; and the notes to the *Rexue lun* and *Cire pian* say "at the bend behind the knee"], where the foot greater yang vessel enters; one may needle five body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Thus the transport points of the six fu are six per fu, thirty-six in all; counting both left and right vessels, seventy-two points.

[On "the heat transport points, fifty-nine points; the water transport points, fifty-seven points"] Both are given in the *Shuire* [*xue*] *lun*.
Song editors: The heat transport points also appear in the note to the *Cire pian*.

[On "On the head, five lines ... twenty-five points"] These too are part of the fifty-nine heat transport points.

[On "On both sides of the central spine, five each, ten points in all"] This means the back transport points of the five zang: the lung transport point on both sides below the third vertebra, the heart's below the fifth, the liver's below the ninth, the spleen's below the eleventh, the kidney's below the fourteenth. These five zang transport points are each one and a half body-*cun* from the spine, all meetings of the foot greater yang vessel. One may needle three body-*fen*; retain six breaths for the liver point and seven for the others; for moxibustion, three cones. Counting alongside the spine, ten points.

[On "On both sides above Dazhui, one each, two points in all"] They are not recorded in the present *Jiayi jing* or the *Jingmai liuzhu kongxue tujing*; which transport point is meant is unclear.
Song editors: There is no point beside the spine above Dazhui; the point beside it below Dazhui is called Dazhu, which comes later [as the back transport points]; hence Mr Wang says it is unclear.

[On "Tongzi[liao] of the eye and Fubai, two points"] Tongziliao is five body-*fen* outside the outer canthus, the meeting of the three vessels of the hand greater yang and hand and foot lesser yang; one may needle three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones. Fubai is one body-*cun* inside the hairline behind the ear, the meeting of the two vessels of the foot greater yang and lesser yang; one may needle three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones. Counting left and right, two each make four.

[On "In the divisions of both hip sockets, two points"] This means the Huantiao point, behind the hip joint, the meeting of the two vessels of the foot lesser yang and greater yang; one may needle one body-*cun* and retain twenty breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: Mr Wang says "behind the hip joint", but the *Jiayi jing* says "in the hip joint"; 後 should be 中. For three cones, the *Jiayi jing* writes five.

[On "Dubi, two points"] Below the kneecap, above the shin, in the great sinew on either side of the joint, where the vessel qi of the foot *yangming* issues; one may needle six body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "Duosuowen in the ear, two points"] This is the Tinggong point, at the bead of the ear, the size of a red adzuki bean, the meeting of the three vessels of the hand and foot lesser yang and hand greater yang; one may needle one body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* says one may needle three *fen*.

[On "The root of the eyebrow, two points"] This is the Cuanzhu point, in the depression at the head of the eyebrow, where the vessel qi of the foot greater yang issues; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain six breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "Wangu, two points"] Four body-*fen* inside the hairline behind the ear, the meeting of the foot greater yang and lesser yang; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* says one may needle two *fen* and apply seven cones.

[On "The centre of the vertex, one point"] This is the Fengfu point, one body-*cun* inside the hairline on the top [of the nape], in the hollow inside the great sinew, the meeting of the *du* and *yangwei* channels. When one speaks quickly the flesh rises at once; when one stops speaking it falls at once. One may needle four body-*fen* and retain three breaths; moxibustion may unfortunately cause loss of voice.

[On "The occipital bone, two points"] This is the Qiaoyin point, above Wangu and below the occipital bone, felt when moved, the meeting of the foot greater yang and lesser yin [lesser yang]; one may needle three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* says one may needle four *fen* and apply five cones.

[On "Shangguan, two points"] This is what the *Zhenjing* calls "needling it, one can open but not yawn". It is at the raised bone on the upper edge in front of the ear, where there is a space when the mouth is closed, the meeting of the hand lesser yang and foot *yangming*; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Deep needling makes one unable to hear.

[On "Daying, two points"] At the artery in the bone depression one body-*cun* three *fen* in front of the angle of the jaw, where the vessel qi of the foot *yangming* issues; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "Xiaguan, two points"] This is what the *Zhenjing* calls "needling it, one can yawn but not open". It is below Shangguan, at the lower edge of the artery in front of the ear, where there is a space when the mouth is closed and which closes when the mouth is opened, the meeting of the two vessels of the foot *yangming* and lesser yang; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. When there is dry [wax] in the ear and it is being picked, moxibustion must not be applied.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 擿抵 for 擿之.

[On "Tianzhu, two points"] In the depression on the outer edge of the great sinew at the hairline beside the back of the nape, where the vessel qi of the foot greater yang issues; one may needle two body-*fen* and retain six breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "The upper and lower Juxu, four points"] The upper one is the uniting point of the foot *yangming* with the large intestine, six body-*cun* below Dubi on the outer edge of the shin, where the vessel qi of the foot *yangming* issues; one may needle eight body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones. The lower one is the uniting point of the foot *yangming* with the small intestine, three body-*cun* below the upper one, where the vessel qi of the foot *yangming* issues; one may needle three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: According to the *Jiayi jing* and the notes to the *Cire pian* and *Shuire xue*, the upper one is three *cun* below Sanli. Saying six *cun* below Dubi here is presumably because Sanli is three *cun* below Dubi and the upper Juxu three *cun* below Sanli, hence six *cun*.

[On "Quya, two points"] This is the Jiache point, in the depression at the end of the curve of the jaw below the ear, where there is a space when the mouth is open, where the vessel qi of the foot *yangming* issues; one may needle three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "Tiantu, one point"] Already explained above.

[On "Tianfu, two points"] At the artery on the inner edge of the upper arm three body-*cun* below the armpit, where the vessel qi of the hand greater yin issues; moxibustion is prohibited; one may needle four body-*fen* and retain three breaths.

[On "Tianyou, two points"] Between the sinews of the neck, above the supraclavicular fossa, behind Tianrong, in front of Tianzhu, below Wangu, above the hairline, where the vessel qi of the hand lesser yang issues; one may needle one body-*cun* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "Futu, two points"] On the neck one body-*cun* below the angle of the jaw, behind Renying, where the vessel qi of the hand *yangming* issues; take it face up; one may needle four body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "Tianchuang, two points"] In the depression below the angle of the jaw behind Futu, where the pulse is felt, where the vessel qi of the hand greater yang issues; one may needle six body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "Jianjie, two points"] This means Jianjing, in the depression in the cleft on the shoulder, above the supraclavicular fossa and in front of the large bone, the meeting of the hand and foot lesser yang and the *yangwei*; one may needle five body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* gives five cones.

[On "Guanyuan, one point"] Song editors: On examination, this was already explained above; formerly it was annotated again in this chapter, which has now been removed.

[On "Weiyang, two points"] The lower auxiliary transport point of the triple burner, between the two sinews on the outer edge of the popliteal fossa; this is a separate network vessel of the foot greater yang; one may needle seven body-*fen* and retain five breaths; for moxibustion, three cones; take it with the body bent.

[On "Jianzhen, two points"] Below the curve of the shoulder blade, between the two bones, in the depression behind Jianyu, where the vessel qi of the hand greater yang issues; one may needle eight body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "Yinmen, one point"] In the hollow at the hairline of the nape, entering and tied to the root of the tongue, the meeting of the *du* and *yangwei* channels; take it with the head tilted back; one may needle four body-*fen*; moxibustion is not allowed, as it causes loss of voice.
Song editors: The note to the *Qifu* [*lun*] says "one *cun* from Fengfu".

[On "The navel, one point"] The centre of the navel. Needling is prohibited; needling it causes a malignant ulcer in the navel, and if stool comes out, one dies and cannot be treated. For moxibustion, three cones.

[On "The chest transport points, twelve points"] This means Shufu, Yuzhong, Shencang, Lingxu, Shenfeng and Bulang, twelve points left and right. Shufu is in the depression below the clavicle, beside the *ren* vessel, two body-*cun* transversely from it on each side; the five points below are successively one body-*cun* six *fen* apart, in depressions; all are where the vessel qi of the foot lesser yin issues; take them face up; one may needle four body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones.

[On "The back transport points, two points"] This is the Dazhu point, in the depressions one and a half body-*cun* to each side below the first vertebra, the meeting of the qi of three vessels: the branch of the *du* vessel and the hand and foot greater yang; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, seven cones.

[On "The breast transport points, twelve points"] This means Yunmen, Zhongfu, Zhouying, Xiongxiang, Tianxi and Shidou, twelve points left and right [Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes Zhourong and Xiongxiang]. Yunmen is below the clavicle, beside the *ren* vessel, six body-*cun* transversely from it on each side [Song editors: The note to the *Shuire xue* writes "on both sides of the central line of the chest"; though the wording differs, the location is the same], in the depression where the pulse is felt. Yunmen and Zhongfu are one body-*cun* apart; the other five points are successively one body-*cun* six *fen* apart, in depressions; all are where the vessel qi of the hand greater yin issues. Yunmen and Shidou are taken with the arm raised; the others face up. Yunmen may be needled seven body-*fen*; too deep, it makes the breath counterflow. Zhongfu may be needled three body-*fen*, retained five breaths. The others may be needled four body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones.
Song editors: On examination, Mr Wang assigns all twelve points to the hand greater yin. According to the *Jiayi jing*, Yunmen belongs to the hand greater yin, Zhongfu is the meeting of the hand and foot greater yin, and Zhourong and those below belong to the foot greater yin; the twelve points are not all hand greater yin.

[On "Fenrou, two points"] Three body-*fen* from the end of Juegu above the outer ankle, between the divisions of sinew and flesh, where the vessel qi of the *yangwei* issues; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* has no Fenrou point. Examining the location, it is suspected to be Yangfu, above the outer ankle, in front of the auxiliary bone, about three *fen* in front of the end of Juegu. Moreover, the note to *Ci yaotong* writes "two *fen* behind the end of Juegu, needle five *fen*, retain ten breaths", slightly different from this note.

[On "Transversely above the ankles, two points"] Above the inner ankle is the Jiaoxin point, two body-*cun* above the inner ankle, in front of the lesser yin and behind the greater yin, between sinew and bone, the cleft of the foot yin *qiao*; one may needle four body-*fen* and retain five breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Above the outer ankle is the Fuyang point, three body-*cun* above the outer ankle, in front of the greater yang and behind the lesser yin [lesser yang], between sinew and bone, the cleft of the yang *qiao*; one may needle six body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 付陽 for 附陽.

[On "The yin and yang *qiao*, four points"] The yin *qiao* point is below the inner ankle, called Zhaohai, where the yin *qiao* arises; one may needle four body-*fen* and retain six breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. The yang *qiao* point is called Shenmai, where the yang *qiao* arises, in the depression below the outer ankle [Song editors: The note to the *Ci yaotong pian* says "five *fen* below the outer ankle", and the note to the *Miuci lun* "half a *cun* below the outer ankle"], admitting a fingernail; one may needle two body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes six breaths for seven, and the note to the *Ci yaotong pian* ten breaths.

[On "The water transport points are in the divisions"] 分 means the spaces between the divisions and lines of the flesh; they are taken to treat water.

[On "the heat transport points are in the qi points"] They are taken to drain heat.

[On "the cold-heat transport points are in the hollows of both knee bones, two points"] 骸厭 means the hollow of the bones on either side of the knee on its outer side.

[On "The great prohibition, twenty-five, is five *cun* below Tianfu"] This means the Wuli point. It is called the great prohibition because needling it is prohibited. The *Zhenjing* says, "Meeting it at Wuli, it stops midway; after five arrivals it ends; after five pourings the qi of that zang is exhausted; thus five times five, twenty-five, exhausts its transport point" — this is presumably what is meant. It also says, "Wuli is the Wuli behind Chize," which agrees with this text.

[On "In all, three hundred sixty-five points, through which the needle works"] Song editors: On examination, from "the transport points of the zang, fifty" to here, including repetitions, there are three hundred sixty points; together with Tiantu, the tenth vertebra, and the upper and lower guides above, three hundred sixty-five points in all. Excluding repetitions, there are actually three hundred thirteen points.

## Notes

Lists the three hundred sixty-five qi points as transport points of the zang and fu, heat and water transport points, and various points of the head, trunk and limbs. Wang Bing's notes give each point's location, needling depth, breaths of retention and moxa cones in detail; Song editors' remarks inserted within the notes are given in square brackets. Qibo's speech, begun in unit 006, ends here.
- 𦛗 in 中𦛗 is presumably a variant of 膂 "spine".
- Wang Bing's notes use spellings such as Taling (太陵, i.e. Daling 大陵), Huantiao (環銚, i.e. 環跳) and Zhouying (周滎, i.e. Zhourong 周栄); the translation supplies the usual names in places.
- "Lesser yin" in Wang Bing's notes on Qiaoyin (meeting of the foot greater yang and lesser yin) and Fuyang (behind the lesser yin) is presumably an error for "lesser yang"; the translation supplies it in brackets.
- 曲頽, 余刂, 腿骨 and 六都 in Wang Bing's notes are presumably errors for 曲頰, 余刺, 跟骨 and 大都; the translation follows these readings.
- According to the Song editors, excluding repetitions there are actually three hundred thirteen points.
