# 素問 気穴論篇第五十八 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo bowed twice, rose and said: "Your servant asks to speak. When the back and heart pull on each other and ache, treat at Tiantu, the tenth vertebra and the upper guide. The upper guide is the stomach duct; the lower guide is Guanyuan. The back and chest are obliquely tied, yin and yang, left and right. In such a case the illness has pain and roughness front and back, pain in the chest and flanks preventing breathing and lying down, rising qi, shortness of breath and one-sided pain. The vessel fills and rises, emerges obliquely at the sacral vessel, connects with the chest and flanks, props the heart, passes through the diaphragm, rises to the shoulder to join Tiantu, descends obliquely from the shoulder and crosses below the tenth vertebra.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the back and heart pull on each other ... the upper guide"] Tiantu is four body-*cun* below the laryngeal prominence of the neck, in the hollow at the centre, the meeting of the *yinwei* and *ren* vessels; take it with the needle held low. One may needle one body-*cun* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. According to the present *Jiayi jing* and the *Jingmai liuzhu kongxue tujing*, there is no point below the tenth vertebra of the spine; it is probably the seventh vertebra. This is governed by the qi of the *du* vessel. The location of the upper guide is as explained next.
Song editors: According to the *Jiayi jing*, Tiantu is five *cun* below the laryngeal prominence.

[On "The upper guide is the stomach duct"] It means Zhongwan. Zhongwan is the alarm point of the stomach, one body-*cun* below Shangwan, midway between the xiphoid process and the navel, where the three vessels of the hand greater yang, lesser yang and foot *yangming* arise and where the qi of the *ren* vessel issues. One may needle one body-*cun* two *fen*; for moxibustion, seven cones.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* says "the meeting of the *ren* vessel".

[On "the lower guide is Guanyuan"] Guanyuan is the alarm point of the lesser yang [small intestine], three body-*cun* below the navel, the meeting of the three foot yin and the *ren* vessel. One may needle two body-*cun* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, seven cones.

[On "rising qi, shortness of breath and one-sided pain"] Song editors: Another edition writes 満 "full" for 偏.

[On "The vessel fills and rises ... crosses below the tenth vertebra"] Examining the course of this branch vessel and the form and signs of the illness, it is all the branch network vessel of the *du* vessel: it emerges from the coccyx, rises on each side, connects obliquely with the flanks, props the heart, passes through the diaphragm, rises to join Tiantu, goes obliquely to the shoulder and descends to cross at the seventh vertebra.
Song editors: On examination, from "When the back and heart pull on each other" to here is suspected to be text of the *Gukong lun* that has slipped out and been mistakenly placed here.

## Notes

The points (Tiantu, tenth vertebra, upper and lower guides) for illness in which the back and heart pull on each other with pain, and the course of the vessel concerned. Qibo's speech continues to unit 007.
- The Song editors suspect the text of this unit to be displaced from the *Gukong lun* (chapter 60).
- Wang Bing suggests that "tenth vertebra" should be "seventh vertebra".
- "Alarm point of the lesser yang" in Wang Bing's note is presumably an error for "small intestine" (Guanyuan being the alarm point of the small intestine), but the translation keeps the characters.
- 泳注 in Wang Bing's note is presumably an error for 流注.
