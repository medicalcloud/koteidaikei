# 素問 六微旨大論篇第六十八 §65 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The leaning and lurking of success and failure arise from movement. When movement does not cease, change arises."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The leaning and lurking of success and failure arise from movement ... change arises"] In the principle of movement and stillness, qi has its constant motion. Its slight form is the transformation of things; its severe form is the change of things. Transformation flows into things, so they obtain it and live; change acts on things, so they obtain it and die. Thus the leaning and lurking of success and failure arise from the slightness, severity, slowness and speed of movement. Is it only qi that is so? Humans are within qi, and the way of nurturing life and the use of advance and retreat should all be so.
Song editors: The *Zhizhen yao dalun* says "when the qi of yin and yang are clear and still, there is generation, transformation and order; when they move, severe illnesses arise"; this is what is meant.

## Notes

The leaning and lurking of success and failure arise from movement.
