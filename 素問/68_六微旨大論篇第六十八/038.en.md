# 素問 六微旨大論篇第六十八 §38 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "How clear the question! In the *jiazi* year, the first qi, by the heavenly number, begins at the first *ke* of the water clock and ends at eighty-seven and a half *ke*; the second qi begins at eighty-seven *ke* six *fen* and ends at seventy-five *ke*; the third qi begins at seventy-six *ke* and ends at sixty-two and a half *ke*; the fourth qi begins at sixty-two *ke* six *fen* and ends at fifty *ke*; the fifth qi begins at fifty-one *ke* and ends at thirty-seven and a half *ke*; the sixth qi begins at thirty-seven *ke* six *fen* and ends at twenty-five *ke*. This is the so-called first six, the number of heaven.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In the *jiazi* year, the first qi, by the heavenly number, begins at the first *ke* of the water clock"] It always rises at dawn, at the first *ke* of the beginning of *yin*, south of the middle of Gen.
Song editors: The years *wuchen*, *renshen*, *bingzi*, *gengchen*, *jiashen*, *wuzi*, *renchen*, *bingshen*, *gengzi*, *jiachen*, *wushen*, *renzi*, *bingchen* and *gengshen* are the same as this—the so-called *chen*, *shen* and *zi* years, whose qi meet and agree. In the method of yin and yang these are the three combinations.

[On "and ends at eighty-seven and a half *ke*"] The middle of *zi*, midnight. The remaining twelve and a half *ke* enter the beginning of the second qi; the other remaining *ke* likewise enter.

[On "the second qi begins at eighty-seven *ke* six *fen*"] Left of the middle of *zi*.

[On "and ends at seventy-five *ke*"] Four *ke* after *xu*. The remaining twenty-five *ke* enter the beginning of the next, third qi; this is the rule.

[On "the third qi begins at seventy-six *ke*"] The first *ke* of the beginning of *hai*.

[On "and ends at sixty-two and a half *ke*"] The middle of *you*. The remaining thirty-seven and a half *ke* are shifted into the next.

[On "the fourth qi begins at sixty-two *ke* six *fen*"] North of the middle of *you*.

[On "and ends at fifty *ke*"] Four *ke* after *wei*. The remaining fifty *ke* are shifted into the next.

[On "the fifth qi begins at fifty-one *ke*"] The first *ke* of the beginning of *shen*.

[On "and ends at thirty-seven and a half *ke*"] The middle of *wu*, midday. The remaining sixty-two and a half *ke* are shifted into the next.

[On "the sixth qi begins at thirty-seven *ke* six *fen*"] 酉 [west] of the middle of *wu*.

[On "and ends at twenty-five *ke*"] Four *ke* after the middle of *chen*. The remaining seventy-five *ke* are shifted into the next.

[On "This is the so-called first six, the number of heaven"] The numbers of heaven and earth come into great agreement only after twenty-four qi; hence this is called the heavenly number of the first six.

## Notes

The times of beginning and ending of the six qi in the *jiazi* year. Qibo's speech continues to unit 042.
- 午中之酉 in Wang Bing's note is read as 午中之西 ("west of the middle of *wu*"), since the parallel notes for the second and fourth qi read 午中之西 in the Siku quanshu edition.
