# 素問 六微旨大論篇第六十八 §69 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "When going out and coming in cease, the transformation of the spirit mechanism is extinguished; when rising and descending stop, the qi-established are isolated and endangered. So without going out and coming in, there can be no birth, growth, maturity, aging and ending; without rising and descending, there can be no generation, growth, transformation, gathering and storing. Thus there is no vessel that lacks rising, descending, going out and coming in. The vessel is the house of generation and transformation; when the vessel disperses, it is divided and generation and transformation cease. So there is nothing that does not go out and come in, nothing that does not rise and descend. Transformation is small or great; the time is near or far. These four exist, and what is valued is constant guarding; going against the constant brings disaster. Hence the saying 'Without form there is no affliction' means this."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When going out and coming in cease ... the qi-established are isolated and endangered"] Going out and coming in means breathing; rising and descending means transforming qi. The furred, feathered, naked, scaled and shelled, and the flying, running and crawling, all 去 [place] the root of qi within the body and take spirit as the master of movement and stillness; hence "spirit mechanism". Metals, jades, soils, stones, smelted and molded things, grasses and trees all have the root of living qi outside and rely on qi to be established and 特 [sustained]; hence "qi-established". The *Wuchang zheng dalun* says "what is rooted within is called spirit mechanism; when the spirit leaves, the mechanism stops; what is rooted without is called qi-established; when qi stops, transformation ends". This is what is meant. So without these four, both spirit-mechanism and qi-established beings lose life and death altogether.
Song editors: The *Changes* says "what is rooted in heaven draws near to what is above; what is rooted in earth draws near to what is below"; the *Rites of Zhou* has "heavenly products and earthly products" under the Grand Ancestral Officer, and the Grand Minister of Education speaks of "moving things and planted things". These are what is meant by spirit mechanism and qi-established.

[On "So without going out and coming in ... gathering and storing"] [Creatures that move] from east to west and south to north rely on the breath of going out and coming in as the master of transformation; things that are completed in substance through other things take the qi of yin and yang rising and descending as their source of life. Without this way, none of these ten could come about.

[On "Thus there is no vessel that lacks rising, descending, going out and coming in"] Whatever contains and stores living qi is called a vessel of generation and transformation; whatever one touches is so. Whatever has horizontal openings has qi going out and coming in; whatever has firm openings has the qi of yin and yang rising and descending, coming and going within. How is this shown? If one watches both sides of walls, windows and doors, the incoming qi strikes the person; this is the qi of going out and coming in. When yang rises, wells are cold; when yin rises, water is warm. When a thing is thrown into a well, or a leaf falls through the air fluttering slowly, it is hindered by rising qi. If an empty tube is filled and its end sealed with kneaded earth and hung up, the water stays and does not leak, because without rising qi it cannot descend. If one pours suddenly into an empty bottle with a small mouth, it does not go in, because the air cannot come out and so the water cannot go in. Seen thus, there is no rising without descending and no descending without rising; without going out there is no coming in, and without coming in no going out. Among all kinds, there has never been one that keeps going out, coming in, rising and descending constantly and yet is said not to transform; nor has there been one, aware or unaware, sentient or insentient, that abandons going out and coming in and stops rising and descending and yet is said to survive. Hence "there is no vessel that lacks rising, descending, going out and coming in".

[On "The vessel is the house of generation and transformation ... generation and transformation cease"] Vessel means heaven and earth and all bodies; house means a building. Since the body contains the bowels and depots and receives the spirit, as heaven and earth do, both are called vessels. Bodies are small houses of generation and transformation; the great void is the vast house of generation and transformation. Vessels of generation and transformation are naturally small or great, and none fails to disperse. Small and great vessels all have a limit to life, and their dispersal is near or far.

[On "So there is nothing that does not go out and come in, nothing that does not rise and descend"] Whatever truly lives and provisionally sets up a form-vessel has these two.

[On "Transformation is small or great; the time is near or far"] The near, not seeing the far, say the far has no limit; the far, being inconstant, see the near and lament its limit. Since near and far differ, the gathering and dispersal of their times differ, presence and absence contend, and differing views always diverge. When the time of dispersal comes, near and far alike return to one change.

[On "These four exist, and what is valued is constant guarding"] The four are going out, coming in, rising and descending. Having going out, coming in, rising and descending is constant guarding. Going out without coming in, coming in without going out, rising without descending, or descending without rising is not the qi of life. Unless one has perfected the way of embryonic breathing and lives while dwelling constantly, there has never been one who could suppress the breath of going out and coming in and extinguish the qi of rising and descending and still preserve generation and transformation. Hence constant guarding is valued.

[On "going against the constant brings disaster"] Going out, coming in, rising and descending are the source of generation and transformation and cannot be done without. In the way of going against the constant, the spirit leaves its chamber and the subtlety of life is cut off; what is this if not disaster?

[On "Hence the saying 'Without form there is no affliction' means this"] Delight in success, pleasure in beauty, dread of difficulty, fear of misfortune, aversion to wind, cold, heat and damp outside, and being troubled within by hunger, satiety, love and desire—all arise because one has form with nowhere to hide; hence one is always caught in afflictions among people. If longings spread like weeds and desires are insatiable, if outwardly one attaches to the powerful and inwardly abounds in falsehood, then every move entangles one in nets, and sitting still one invites burning; though one wishes to be freed from bonds, is it possible? Thus the body becomes the stair to affliction. Laozi says: "The reason I have great affliction is that I have a body. If I had no body, what affliction would I have?" This is what is meant. When the body disperses freely into the great void, one does not know whether the qi of generation and transformation gathers as being or is extinguished as nothing.

## Notes

Going out, coming in, rising and descending are the basis of generation and transformation; without them generation and transformation end. Wang Bing explains spirit mechanism and qi-established, vessel and house, and closes by citing the *Laozi*.
- 去 in Wang Bing's 去気根於身中 and 特 in 主特 may be errors; they are translated from the context, but this has not been checked. 嘆其其涯 presumably has a doubled 其. 故貴当守 is read in the sense of 常守 in the main text.
