# 素問 六微旨大論篇第六十八 §63 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The birth of things follows transformation; the extreme of things comes from change. The pressing together of change and transformation is the source of success and failure. So qi has going and returning, and its working has slowness and speed. Through these four, there is transformation and change, and this is the coming of wind."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The birth of things follows transformation ... the source of success and failure"] Qi has generation and transformation, but its form is unseen, its nature unknown, where it starts unfathomable and where it stops unknowable; yet the myriad things generate and transform themselves, completing without limit. This is called the harmony of heaven. When its image is visible and its movement manifest—shaking, violent, hard and fierce, gusting, sudden and abrupt, breaking the firm, smashing and ruining, snapping, splitting, drumming and shaking—this is called pathogenic qi. So when things are born, they are still and complete through transformation; when they are destroyed, they are agitated and altered through change. Thus birth follows transformation and the extreme comes from change. Since change and transformation never cease, the source of success and failure is always present. That life has its limit means that it has beginning and end.
Song editors: The *Tianyuan ji dalun* says "the birth of things is called transformation; the extreme of things is called change".

[On "So qi has going and returning ... this is the coming of wind"] Heaven and earth exchange positions, cold and heat shift direction, water and fire exchange places. At the time of movement and action, the slowness, speed, going and returning of qi are not constant. Though it cannot be fully known, in outline, the working of its slight and severe forms becomes transformation and change; this is where wind comes from. When human qi does not prevail, it is affected by it; hence illness arises. Wind does not seek to overcome people.

## Notes

Transformation and change, and the going, returning, slowness and speed of qi, give rise to wind (the movement of qi).
