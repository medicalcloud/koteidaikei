# 素問 六微旨大論篇第六十八 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "When it arrives at its time, there is harmony. When the time arrives but it does not, the arriving qi is deficient. When it arrives before its time, the arriving qi is in surplus."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When it arrives at its time, there is harmony ... the arriving qi is in surplus"] When the time arrives and the qi arrives, it is the response of harmony and balance; this is a balanced year. Suppose the qi of the *jiazi* year has a surplus over the *guihai* year: it arrives ahead of time, before the due date. If the qi of the *yichou* year falls short of the *jiazi* year, it arrives behind time at the due date. Hence "the arriving qi is deficient", "the arriving qi is in surplus". Such is the due arrival of the first qi. When the year qi is in surplus, all six qi arrive ahead of time; when deficient, all arrive behind time. Ahead of time or behind time, each responds with a difference of thirteen days.
Song editors: The *Jingui yaolüe* says: "There is arriving before the time, not arriving when the time comes, arriving and not departing, and arriving in excess. After the winter solstice, at midnight of the *jiazi* day, lesser yang arises. At the time of lesser yin, yang first arises and heaven becomes warm and mild. If before *jiazi* heaven is already warm, this is arriving before the time. If *jiazi* has come but heaven is not yet warm, this is not arriving when the time comes. If *jiazi* has come but heaven's cold does not abate, this is arriving and not departing. If *jiazi* has come and heaven is as warm as midsummer, this is arriving in excess." This too discusses one aspect of the response of qi.

## Notes

Harmony, deficiency and surplus according to how the six qi arrive.
