# 素問 六微旨大論篇第六十八 §64 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Emperor said: "Slowness, speed, going and returning are where wind arises from; transformation and change come from the changes of abundance and decline. What of success and failure leaning on and lurking in each other, roaming within?"

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "What of success and failure leaning on and lurking in each other, roaming within?"] Leaning and lurking are the sprouts of misfortune and fortune. Where there is misfortune, fortune leans on it; where there is fortune, misfortune lurks in it. Thus misfortune and fortune lean on and lurk in each other. When things are abundant they decline; when pleasure is extreme there is sorrow; this is the extreme of fortune, on which misfortune leans. Obstruction at its extreme goes to Peace, and the Not-yet-completed becomes completed; this is the extreme of misfortune, in which fortune lurks. Good and bad fortune, success and failure, are evident at a glance and cannot be brought to an end; this is the principle of nature, so there is no blame.

## Notes

A question on why success and failure lean on and lurk in each other. Wang Bing annotates with the *Laozi*'s doctrine of fortune and misfortune leaning and lurking.
