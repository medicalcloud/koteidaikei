# 素問 宝命全形論篇第二十五 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Wood meeting metal is felled; fire meeting water is extinguished; earth meeting wood is penetrated; metal meeting fire is diminished; water meeting earth is cut off. All things are thus, without end. Therefore in needling there are five things hung up and spread throughout the world, yet the black-haired people, all eating their fill, do not know them. The first is governing the spirit; the second, knowing how to nourish the body; the third, knowing that toxic drugs are true; the fourth, making *bian* stones of the right size; the fifth, knowing the diagnosis of the fu and zang, blood and qi. The five methods stand together, and each has its priority. Needling in this late age fills the depleted and drains the full; this is what all ordinary practitioners know. But as for modelling on heaven and following earth, moving in response, harmonizing like an echo and following like a shadow — the Way has no ghosts or spirits; it comes alone and goes alone."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Wood meeting metal is felled ... without end"] 達 means penetrate. It says that although the kinds of things cannot be exhausted, in summary they all have, like the qi of the five phases, the nature of overcoming and being overcome.

[On "Therefore in needling ... do not know them"] It says that in the way of needling there are five things as if hung high to show people and spread clearly through the world, yet the people, though they know them, eat their fill and all neglect and despise them, not attending to the root but esteeming the branches, unaware that the true essentials lie deep within. The five are as in the following sentences.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 飽食 "eating to satiety" for 余食, and his note says, "People are foolish, do not understand yin and yang and do not know the marvel of the needle; eating to satiety all day, they cannot know its marvellous benefit." The *Taisu* writes 飲食 "eating and drinking", and Yang Shangshan's note says, "The black-haired people all use this way but cannot grasp its meaning."

[On "The first is governing the spirit"] Concentrate the mind and do not move or become disordered rashly. Saying "the hand as if grasping a tiger, the spirit not occupied with the multitude of things" means regulating the spirit and concentrating the mind.
Song editors: Yang Shangshan says, "In the way of preserving life, if one knows these five and uses them for cultivation, one can attain long life. The *hun*, spirit, intention, *po* and will are the masters of the spirit, so all are called spirit. One who would needle must first govern the spirit. Thus if one has no sorrow and grief moving the interior, the *hun* is not harmed, the liver is free of illness and there is no trouble in autumn. If one has no apprehension and anxious thought, the spirit is not harmed, the heart is free of illness and there is no trouble in winter. If one has no unresolved anxiety and worry, the intention is not harmed, the spleen is free of illness and there is no trouble in spring. If one has no joy and pleasure without limit, the *po* is not harmed, the lung is free of illness and there is no trouble in summer. If one has no violent anger, the will is not harmed, the kidney is free of illness and there is no trouble in late summer. Thus when the five excesses do not arise in the heart, the spirit is clear and the nature bright; when the five spirits are each settled in their zang, life is prolonged to a great age."

[On "the second, knowing how to nourish the body"] Knowing the method of nourishing one's own body is like the way of nourishing others. The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* (chapter 5) says, "One who uses the needle knows the other through oneself, and in using it is not endangered" — this is what is meant.
Song editors: The *Taisu* writes 形 "form" for 身 "body". Yang Shangshan says, "Regulating food and drink and sexual relations within limits, and guarding against wind, cold, summer heat and damp according to the season, so as to avoid the harm of Shan Bao, who withered from outside — this is nourishing the form within. Truly loving others with kindness and forgiveness, blending with the dust of the world without leaving traces, so as to avoid the injury of Zhang Yi at the lofty gates — this is nourishing the form without. When inner and outer nourishment are complete, one lives long without seeking life and attains longevity without expecting it. This is the ultimate of needling and nourishing the form. The Xuanyuan Emperor said, 'The highest nourishes the spirit; the next nourishes the form.'" Examining Mr Wang's note, it confines governing the spirit and nourishing the body to the time of needling; his view is very narrow and not as good as Shangshan's. If these five must be explained as concerning the time of needling, then for "knowing that toxic drugs are true" below, Mr Wang too does not explain it solely in terms of needling.

[On "the third, knowing that toxic drugs are true"] Toxic drugs attack evil and are used according to what suits; the upright, true way lies here.

[On "the fourth, making *bian* stones of the right size"] In ancient times *bian* stones were used as needles; hence the nine needles are not mentioned, only *bian* stones. Making them of the right size means using them according to what suits the illness.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi says, "*Bian* stones are an ancient method of external treatment with three names: needle stones, *bian* stones and *chan* stones; in fact they are one. In ancient times iron could not yet be cast, so stones were used as needles, hence the name 'needle stones'. It means that the practitioner must grind and sharpen the point and make its size suit the illness. Huangdi created the nine needles to replace the *chan* stones. Those who treated in high antiquity each followed what suited their region; people of the east had many abscesses, swellings and knots; hence *bian* stones originated in the east."

[On "the fifth, knowing the diagnosis of the fu and zang, blood and qi"] All yang are fu, all yin are zang. Hence the *Xueqi xingzhi pian* (chapter 24) says, "The greater yang has much blood and little qi; the lesser yang little blood and much qi; the *yangming* much qi and much blood; the lesser yin little blood and much qi; the *jueyin* much blood and little qi; the greater yin much qi and little blood. Thus when needling the *yangming*, let out blood and qi; when needling the greater yang, let out blood and avoid qi; when needling the lesser yang, let out qi and avoid blood; when needling the greater yin, let out qi and avoid blood; when needling the lesser yin, let out qi and avoid blood; when needling the *jueyin*, let out blood and avoid qi." Knowing the amounts precisely, supplementing and draining are entirely safe.

[On "The five methods stand together, and each has its priority"] What suits the case and responds is used first.

[On "Needling in this late age ... it comes alone and goes alone"] "Moving in response" refers to its effect; "like a shadow, like an echo" refers to its immediacy. As a shadow follows the form and an echo answers the sound, how could ghosts or spirits summon or dispatch it? It is obtained naturally by moving in response.

## Notes

After stating the mutual control of the five phases, Qibo lists five things needed for needling (governing the spirit, nourishing the body, drugs, *bian* stones, diagnosis), and says that the needling of a master follows heaven and earth and works like shadow and echo.
- 𪫟惕 in Yang Shangshan's view quoted by the Song editors is a variant of 怵惕 (Kanripo writes 怵).
- 張𣪣 in the same quotation is presumably Zhang Yi (張毅). Shan Bao and Zhang Yi appear in the *Dasheng* chapter of the *Zhuangzi*: Shan Bao cultivated the inner but was eaten by a tiger from outside, while Zhang Yi frequented society but fell ill within and died. The phrases about Shan Bao and Zhang Yi allude to this.
- The Xuanyuan Emperor is an honorific title of Laozi.
