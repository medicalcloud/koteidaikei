# 素問 宝命全形論篇第二十五 §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "In needling depletion, wait for it to become replete; in needling repletion, wait for it to become depleted. Once the channel qi has arrived, guard it carefully and do not lose it. Depth lies in the will; far and near are as one. Be as if standing over a deep abyss, the hand as if grasping a tiger, the spirit not occupied with the multitude of things."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In needling depletion, wait for it to become replete; in needling repletion, wait for it to become depleted"] It says that the arrival of qi and its effect should be the criterion, not necessarily keeping to a number of breaths as a fixed rule.

[On "Once the channel qi has arrived, guard it carefully and do not lose it"] Do not change the method and lose the channel qi.

[On "Depth lies in the will ... not occupied with the multitude of things"] It means concentrating the mind single-mindedly. Although the channel vessels needled differ in depth, in supplementing and draining one should focus the mind as on a single transport point. Hence the hand as if grasping a tiger, the spirit not occupied with external things.
Song editors: The *Zhenjie lun* says, "'In needling repletion, wait for it to become depleted' means retaining the needle and removing it only when the yin qi arrives abundantly. 'In needling depletion, wait for it to become replete' means removing the needle only when the yang qi arrives abundantly and it becomes hot under the needle. 'Once the channel qi has arrived, guard it carefully and do not lose it' means do not change. 'Depth lies in the will' means knowing whether the illness is inside or outside. 'Far and near are as one' means the signs are equal whether deep or shallow. 'As if standing over a deep abyss' means not daring to slacken. 'The hand as if grasping a tiger' means wanting it to be strong. 'The spirit not occupied with the multitude of things' means calming the will to observe the patient without looking left or right."

## Notes

Supplementing and draining should wait for the arrival of qi, and the practitioner should concentrate the mind; this ends the chapter.
- The *Zhenjie lun* quoted by the Song editors is chapter 54.
- 旡 in the Song editors' note is a variant of 無.
