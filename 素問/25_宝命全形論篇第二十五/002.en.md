# 素問 宝命全形論篇第二十五 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "When salt tastes salty, its qi makes the vessel leak fluid; when a string is about to break, its sound becomes hoarse and broken; when a tree spreads, its leaves break out; when illness is deep, the voice is turbid. When a person has these three, this is called a ruined storehouse: toxic drugs cannot treat it and short needles cannot take it, for all of these cut the skin and harm the flesh, and the blood and qi contend and turn black."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When salt tastes salty, its qi makes the vessel leak fluid"] "Salty" refers to the flavour of salt, which is bitter and soaks and moistens things. Saltiness becomes bitter and generates saltiness; following water, it has water. It moistens and descends, and bitterness discharges; therefore it can make the water and fluids in a vessel moisten, seep and leak. Anything that is empty inside and receives things is called a vessel; outside the body it is the scrotum, and its counterpart within the body is the bladder. Assigning the illness to the five zang, this occurs when the heart qi lies hidden in the kidney and does not leave. Why? The kidney resembles water and its flavour is salty; the heart corresponds to fire and its flavour is bitter. Bitterness makes sweat flow, saltiness goes to the scrotum; fire is held by water. Hence outside the scrotum it is moist as if with sweat, seeping and leaking without end. The qi of saltiness, when the sky is overcast, becomes damp; in the soil it floats up; in a person the scrotum is damp and the skin peels.

[On "when a string is about to break, its sound becomes hoarse and broken"] When the scrotum leaks fluid and the pulse is stringlike and about to break off, on examination the voice should be hoarse and broken, altered from its former sound. Why? The liver qi is harmed. When the liver qi is harmed, the root of metal is deficient; when the root of metal is deficient, the lung qi is incomplete. The lung governs the voice; hence the voice is hoarse.

[On "when a tree spreads, its leaves break out"] 敷 means spread. It says that when the qi of wood disperses and flourishes outwardly in its region, the illness should break out within the lobes of the lung. Why? Because the qi of wood disperses. The *Pingren qixiang lun* (chapter 18) says, "The true qi of the zang disperses in the liver," and the liver corresponds to wood.

[On "when illness is deep, the voice is turbid"] 噦 means the voice is turbid and bad. The lung harbours bad blood; hence this.

[On "When a person has these three, this is called a ruined storehouse"] "Storehouse" means the chest, because the lung lies in the chest. "Ruined" means damaging the storehouse to remove the illness. The *Baopuzi* says, "Zhongjing opened the chest and inserted a red cake." From this, the chest can be opened to remove illness. "These three" means the stringlike pulse about to break off, the lung lobes breaking out, and the turbid voice.

[On "toxic drugs cannot treat it ... the blood and qi contend and turn black"] The illness has ulcerated inside the lung; therefore toxic drugs cannot treat it. Outside, it is not in the channels and network vessels; therefore short needles cannot take it. Thus only by cutting the skin and harming the flesh can it be attacked. Because the bad blood has long contended with the lung qi, the blood should appear and be black.
Song editors: Qibo's reply does not match what Huangdi asked. Checking the *Taisu*, it reads "... when a tree is old, its leaves fall ..." — three characters differ from this classic, and the meaning of the commentary differs greatly. Yang Shangshan's note says, "It means that to know subtle illness one must know its signs. When salt is in a vessel, fluid leaks outside; seeing the fluid one knows the salt is salty. When the sound is hoarse one knows the string of the zither is about to break. When the leaves fall one knows the old tree is exhausted. These three subtle signs of decay are cited to compare with a turbid voice, so as to recognize the signs of deep illness. When a person's voice is turbid like the three comparisons, it is a sign of a ruined storehouse. A ruined inner storehouse means the illness is deep; since the illness is deep, neither needles nor drugs can take it, because the skin, flesh, blood and qi no longer accord with one another." On further examination, only Shangshan's notes make the meaning coherent with Huangdi's questions before and after. Mr Wang's explanation of the saltiness of salt and the fluid of the vessel is profound and subtle, but his notes on the breaking string and hoarse sound and on the spreading tree and its leaves do not accord with the Emperor's question at all. On consideration, they do not gain as much as Yang's interpretation.

## Notes

Qibo cites three comparisons to show that when illness is deep, neither drugs nor needles are effective. The translation follows Wang Bing.
- Wang Bing takes the "vessel" as the scrotum and bladder and reads the three as bodily symptoms. The Song editors reject this and prefer the *Taisu* text ("when a tree is old, its leaves fall") with Yang Shangshan's note, reading salt, string and tree as comparisons for recognizing signs of illness. Modern commentaries also often read them as comparisons.
- The *Baopuzi* passage quoted by Wang Bing has not been checked.
- Wang Bing's note writes 脈弦絶 with 弦 for the text's 絃.
