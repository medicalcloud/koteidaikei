# 素問 宝命全形論篇第二十五 §8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The truth of needling is always first to govern the spirit; once the five zang [pulses] are determined and the nine indicators complete, only then keep one's mind on the needle. Not seeing the many pulses, not hearing the many ominous signs, with outside and inside in accord, not leading with [one's own] form, being able to master the comings and goings — only then apply it to the person. People have depletion and repletion. Do not approach the five depletions; do not keep away from the five repletions. When the moment to release comes, there is not even [the time of] a blink. Move the hand as if intent [on one task]; the needle shines and is even. Calm the mind and look to the principle; observe the changes of adjustment. This is called the obscure; no one knows its form. One sees it *wuwu*, one sees it *jiji*; following, one sees it fly, but does not know who it is. Lying low, it is like a crossbow laid flat; rising, it is like a released trigger."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The truth of needling is always first to govern the spirit"] Concentrate the spirit, calm and without disturbance; the true essential of needling lies here.

[On "once the five zang [pulses] are determined ... keep one's mind on the needle"] First determine the pulses of the five zang and go through the diagnosis of the nine indicators completely; where there is excess or insufficiency, only then keep one's mind on the method of using the needle.

[On "Not seeing the many pulses ... not leading with [one's own] form"] "The many pulses" means the pulses of the seven diagnoses; "the many ominous signs" means the five zang encroaching on one another. "Outside and inside in accord" means form and qi in accord. "Not leading with [one's own] form" means not gauging the patient's form and qi by the decline or abundance, cold or warmth of one's own form and making them the same as oneself. Hence the text below says:

[On "being able to master the comings and goings — only then apply it to the person"] 玩 means to handle, that is, to be thoroughly familiar. The *Biaoben bingchuan lun* says, "Be thoroughly familiar with yin and yang and do not consult the crowd" — this is of the same kind.
Song editors: This passage comes from the *Yinyang bielun* (chapter 7); saying *Biaoben bingchuan lun* here is an error.

[On "People have depletion and repletion ... not even [the time of] a blink"] A person's depletion and repletion do not depend on distance; they result from the momentary fullness and shrinking of blood and qi. Before it is released, it is like hanging clouds, which one can watch for a long time; when it is released, it is like lightning vanishing, which the finger cannot catch. Such is the difference between slow and fast.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 暄 for 𦟘, and Quan Yuanqi's edition and the *Taisu* write 眴.

[On "Move the hand as if intent [on one task]; the needle shines and is even"] When moving the hand to use the needle, the mind should be as if devoted to one task. The *Zhenjing* says, "Unify one's form and listen to its movement and stillness to know the evil and the upright" — this is what is meant. "The needle shines and is even" means the needle is bright and clean, and even from top to bottom.

[On "Calm the mind and look to the principle ... no one knows its form"] "The obscure" means the changes of blood and qi cannot be seen. Therefore calm the mind and watch the breath, weigh by principle, and observe the changes of the channel vessels being adjusted. Even if one applies one's mind under the needle and measures it finely, one still does not know whose image the changing form is.
Song editors: The *Bazheng shenming lun* (chapter 26) says, "Observing the obscure means that form and qi, nutritive and defensive, do not show outwardly, yet the practitioner alone knows them; adjusting them by combining the cold and warmth of the sun, the emptiness and fullness of the moon, and the floating and sinking of the qi of the four seasons. The practitioner always sees it first, yet it does not show outwardly; hence it is called observing the obscure."

[On "One sees it *wuwu*, one sees it *jiji* ... does not know who it is"] *Wuwu* exclaims at the arrival of the qi; *jiji* marvels at its response. It says that the gain and loss of needling are like seeing birds flying to and fro in the sky; how could one know the original master who sends them? One merely takes the fullness and emptiness of the channel vessels as evidence, without knowing who summons and dispatches them.

[On "Lying low, it is like a crossbow laid flat; rising, it is like a released trigger"] Before the blood and qi respond to the needle, they lie low, calm and still like a crossbow laid flat; when they respond to the needle, they rise swift and sudden like a released trigger.

## Notes

The essentials of needling: governing the spirit, pulse diagnosis, the practitioner's concentration, discerning depletion and repletion and the right moment, and observing the changes of qi under the needle.
- 𦟘: Daizhige writes 瞚 (blink); according to the Song editors the *Jiayi jing* writes 暄, and Quan Yuanqi's edition and the *Taisu* 眴. The translation takes it in the sense of "not even the time of a blink".
- For 冥冥, see the 【補】 notes.
- 烏烏 and 稷稷 are obscure and read in various ways; the translation follows Wang Bing and gives them in transliteration.
- 𩔖 in Wang Bing's note is a variant of 類.
