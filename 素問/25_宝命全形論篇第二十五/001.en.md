# 素問 宝命全形論篇第二十五 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Huangdi asked: "Heaven covers and earth supports, and the myriad things are all complete, but nothing is more precious than humans. Humans are born of the qi of heaven and earth and completed by the laws of the four seasons. Rulers and common people alike all wish to keep their bodies whole, yet the illnesses of the body — no one knows their condition; they linger and spread, deepening day by day, and lodge in the bones and marrow. I privately worry about this in my heart. I wish to remove such illnesses with the needle. How should it be done?"

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Humans are born of the qi of heaven and earth and completed by the laws of the four seasons"] Heaven flows with virtue, earth transforms with qi; when virtue and qi combine, life arises. The *Yijing* says, "Heaven and earth intermingle, and the myriad things are transformed and refined" — this is what is meant. Then, relying on warmth, coolness, cold and heat, on generation, growth, harvest and storage, as the four seasons revolve, one is completed.

[On "Rulers and common people alike all wish to keep their bodies whole"] Though noble and base differ, they are one in treasuring life. Hence loving life and hating death is the constant feeling of noble and base alike.

[On "they linger and spread ... I privately worry about this in my heart"] Song editors: The *Taisu* writes 患 "be troubled" for 慮 "worry".

[On "I wish to remove such illnesses with the needle. How should it be done?"] When depleting evil strikes a person, it is subtle and first appears in the complexion; one cannot tell in the body whether it has form or not; hence no one knows its condition. It lingers without leaving, spreads and deepens day by day, and the evil qi attacks the depletion; hence it lodges in the bones and marrow. The Emperor pities [the people] for not taking measure, and therefore asks to apply the needle.
Song editors: Another edition writes 不庶 for 不度.

## Notes

The opening question of the chapter: humans are the most precious of all things between heaven and earth, yet illness deepens unnoticed; how can it be removed with the needle?
- The *Yijing* passage quoted by Wang Bing is in the *Xici* commentary, part 2 (the received text writes 天地絪縕，万物化醇).
