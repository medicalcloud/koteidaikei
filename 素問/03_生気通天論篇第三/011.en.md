# 素問 生気通天論篇第三 §11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

If yin does not overcome its yang, the flow in the vessels becomes pressing and rapid, and when [yang] combines, there is mania. If yang does not overcome its yin, the qi of the five zang contend and the nine orifices do not open.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "If yin does not overcome its yang ... there is mania"] "Pressing and rapid" means extremely depleted and urgent and rapid. "Combine" means abundant and replete. "Mania" means running about madly or recklessly climbing up high. When yang combines in the four limbs, there is mania. The *Yangming maijie* (chapter 30) says: "The four limbs are the root of all yang. When yang is abundant, the four limbs are replete; when replete, one can climb high places and sing. Because heat is abundant in the body, one throws off one's clothes and wants to run." All of these are because yin does not overcome its yang.

[On "If yang does not overcome its yin ... the nine orifices do not open"] The nine orifices belong within to the zang and are set up without as their officials; therefore when the qi of the five zang contend, the nine orifices do not open. Speaking of the nine orifices means the front and rear yin not opening together with the seven upper orifices. Taken together: the eyes are the official of the liver, the nose of the lung, the mouth of the spleen, the ears of the kidney, and the tongue of the heart. The tongue is not an opening orifice. This is why the *Jingui zhenyan lun* (chapter 4) says: "The south, red in colour, enters and communicates with the heart and opens its orifice in the ears; the north, black in colour, enters and communicates with the kidney and opens its orifice in the two yin."

## Notes

Illnesses when either yin or yang fails to prevail: if yin does not overcome yang, the pulse becomes rapid and there is mania; if yang does not overcome yin, the qi of the five zang contend and the nine orifices do not open.
- "When [yang] combines, there is mania": Wang Bing glosses 并 as abundant and replete, and says that when yang combines in the four limbs there is mania.
- Wang Bing takes the nine orifices as the seven upper orifices plus the front and rear yin.
