# 素問 生気通天論篇第三 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the yang qi is subjected to great anger, the qi of the body is cut off and blood accumulates above, making a person suffer *bojue* (pressing reversal). If the sinews are harmed, they slacken, as if unable to hold [the body]. If sweat comes out and moistens one side, it makes a person suffer hemilateral withering. If sweat comes out and meets dampness, pimples and prickly heat arise. The change brought by rich food abundantly produces large boils; receiving [evil] is like holding an empty vessel. If one sweats from toil and meets the wind, cold presses in and forms comedones; when it stagnates, they become pimples.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the yang qi is subjected to great anger ... making a person suffer *bojue*"] This again warns that when joy and anger lack measure and are overused, illness arises. Anger harms the kidney, and in severe cases the qi is cut off. In great anger the qi rebels and the yang does not move downward; because the yang rebels, blood accumulates within the heart and chest. "Above" means the heart and chest. Yin and yang press against each other, qi and blood rush and combine, and reversal arises from the pressing; hence it is named *bojue*. The *Jutong lun* (chapter 39) says: "Anger makes the qi rebel; in severe cases one vomits blood." The *Lingshu jing* says: "Intense anger that does not stop harms the will." The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* (chapter 5) says: "Joy and anger harm the qi." By this, when anger is severe and the qi rebels, blood accumulates within the heart and chest. 菀 means accumulate.

[On "If the sinews are harmed, they slacken, as if unable to hold [the body]"] When one is angry and overuses [the qi], the qi may press upon the sinews, the sinews and network vessels are harmed within, the joints slacken, and the body becomes limp and useless, as if it could not be held together.

[On "If sweat comes out and moistens one side, it makes a person suffer hemilateral withering"] A person who constantly sweats and is moist on one side of the body will, after a long time, suffer hemilateral withering, with half the body not obeying.
Song editors: For 沮 the *Qianjin* writes 祖 and Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 恒.

[On "If sweat comes out and meets dampness, pimples and prickly heat arise"] When the yang qi is being discharged and cold water checks it, heat is pent up within and stagnates beneath the skin; in severe cases it becomes pimples and boils, in mild cases prickly-heat sores. 疿 is prickly heat.

[On "The change brought by rich food abundantly produces large boils; receiving [evil] is like holding an empty vessel"] 高 is 膏 (fat) and 梁 is 粱 (fine grain). If a person who cannot bear [the heat] sweats and washes in water, it forms pimples and prickly heat. People who eat rich food have much stagnant heat within, thick skin and dense flesh; therefore it changes within into boils. When dampness invades from outside and responds to the heat within, it is like holding an empty vessel to receive this evil toxin; hence "receiving is like holding an empty vessel". The reason boils arise on the feet is that the four limbs are the root of all yang; because there is great expenditure below, the evil toxin attacks the depletion.
Song editors: Boils do not always arise on the feet. It means that the change brought by rich food abundantly produces large boils, not that they occur especially on the feet.

[On "If one sweats from toil and meets the wind, cold presses in and forms comedones; when it stagnates, they become pimples"] When the season is cold and cool and one toils and sweats, a cold wind presses from outside and cold lodges in the skin and interstices; the oily fluids then congeal and accumulate in the dark mansions [sweat pores], seeping and drying in the openings. Comedones grow like thorns in the skin, shaped like grains of rice or like needles; old ones are black on top, a little over one *fen* long, white-yellow in colour, and thin within the dark mansions; they are commonly called "powder thorns". Releasing the exterior [cures them]. The dark mansions are the sweat pores. Pimples are red, swollen and inflamed, containing blood and pus within; they are small but can become as large as a sour jujube or a bean. All of these are caused by yang qi stagnating within. Wait until they soften and then attack them; if very large, cauterize them.

## Notes

Illnesses arising when the yang qi is disordered by anger, sweating, rich food and sweating from toil: *bojue* from great anger, harm to the sinews, hemilateral withering from one-sided sweating, pimples and prickly heat when sweat meets dampness, boils from rich food, and comedones and pimples when sweat from toil meets wind.
- "Abundantly produces large boils" (*zu sheng da ding*): Wang Bing reads 足 as "foot", explaining that the four limbs are the root of all yang; the Song editors object that boils are not limited to the feet and take 足 as "abundantly". The translation follows the Song editors.
- "Receiving is like holding an empty vessel": Wang Bing explains it as receiving evil toxin like an empty vessel.
- 𣡸 in the base text is a variant of 鬱; Kanripo, Wikisource (the plain text) and Daizhige write 鬱.
- "Moistens one side" (*pian ju*): according to the Song editors, the *Qianjin* writes 祖 and Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 恒 for 沮.
- In Wang Bing's note, the base text writes 巳 in 解表巳; it is translated as 已. The character 癤 in Wang Bing's note was supplied from a component description in the transcription (see the 【補】 note).
