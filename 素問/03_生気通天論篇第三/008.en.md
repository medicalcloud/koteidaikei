# 素問 生気通天論篇第三 §8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Therefore when an illness lasts long, it is transmitted and transformed; above and below no longer combine, and even a good physician can do nothing. Therefore when yang accumulates, one falls ill and dies. When the yang qi is about to be blocked, what is blocked should be drained; if it is not promptly and correctly treated, the crude [physician] will ruin it.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Therefore when an illness lasts long ... even a good physician can do nothing"] "Combine" means the qi intermingling and communicating. When an illness is deep and long-lasting, it changes and is transmitted; above and below do not communicate, and yin and yang are obstructed and separated. Even with a good physician and wonderful methods, how could anything be done? The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* (chapter 5) says: "One who uses the needle well draws yang from yin and yin from yang, treats the left by the right and the right by the left." If the qi resist each other in this way, even a good physician can do nothing.

[On "Therefore when yang accumulates ... the crude [physician] will ruin it"] It says that if the three yang accumulate, are pent up, knotted and do not communicate, and they are not urgently drained, one will also fall ill and die. Why? If the accumulation does not stop, above and below also do not combine. How is this verified? Blockage and obstruction with difficulty [in evacuation] is the sign. If one does not drain urgently and a crude practitioner treats it lightly, ruin is certain. The *Yinyang bielun* (chapter 7) says, "The binding of the three yang is called blockage," and also, "Hard with hard: the yang qi is broken and scattered, and the yin qi then wastes away; if it is sodden, hard and soft are not in harmony and the channel qi is then cut off."

## Notes

When an illness lasts long, above and below no longer communicate and even a good physician cannot cure it. When yang accumulates and is blocked, it must be promptly drained; otherwise a crude practitioner will harm the patient.
- "Above and below no longer combine": Wang Bing glosses 并 as the qi intermingling and communicating.
- "Blockage" (*ge*): Wang Bing cites the *Yinyang bielun*: "The binding of the three yang is called blockage."
- In Wang Bing's note the base text writes 巳 in 畜積不巳; it is translated as 已.
- Daizhige (*Huangdi neijing suwen*) writes 蓄 for 畜.
