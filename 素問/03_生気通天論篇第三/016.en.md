# 素問 生気通天論篇第三 §16 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

What yin arises from has its basis in the five flavors; what harms the five palaces of yin also lies in the five flavors.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On the whole sentence] What is called yin is the five spirit storehouses; "palaces" are the dwellings of the five spirits. It says that what gives rise to the five zang is originally the five flavors; the five flavors spread and transform, each gathering in its own palace. Although they arise from the five flavors, they are also damaged by the five flavors: precisely when one is fond of them and exceeds measure, harm appears. Hence the text below says:

## Notes

Yin (the five zang) arises from the five flavors but can also be harmed by them. The next unit sets out the harm from excess of each flavor.
- "Five palaces": Wang Bing takes them as the dwellings of the five spirits, that is, the five zang.
