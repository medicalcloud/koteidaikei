# 素問 生気通天論篇第三 §7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Therefore wind is the beginning of all illnesses. If one is clear and still, the flesh and interstices close and resist; even if there are great winds and severe toxins, they cannot harm. This is due to the order of the seasons.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "If one is clear and still ... This is due to the order of the seasons"] Cravings cannot weary the eyes, lewdness and evil cannot delude the heart, and one does not toil recklessly: this is being clear and still. Because one is clear and still, the flesh and interstices close, the skin is dense, the true and correct [qi] resists within, and depleting evils do not invade. Great winds and severe toxins do not necessarily seek people out all the time; it is surely because people expose themselves to them. Therefore if one is clear and pure, the flesh and interstices close, the yang qi resists, and great winds and severe toxins cannot harm. Being clear and still simply means following the order of the qi of the four seasons, following what is fitting for nourishing life and regulation, not toiling recklessly, and keeping measure in rising and resting; then the vital qi is not exhausted and one keeps healthy and at peace for ever.

## Notes

Wind is the beginning of all illnesses, but if one is clear and still, the flesh and interstices close and resist evil, and even great winds and severe toxins cannot harm.
- Wang Bing explains "clear and still" by phrases from the *Shanggu tianzhen lun* (chapter 1): "cravings cannot weary the eyes, lewdness and evil cannot delude the heart" and "not toiling recklessly".
- Wang Bing writes both 清静 and 清浄 (as in the base text).
