# 素問 生気通天論篇第三 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the yang qi is vexed and overworked, it becomes distended; the essence is cut off; and when this accumulates into summer, it makes a person suffer *jianjue* (boiling reversal). The eyes are blind and cannot see, the ears are closed and cannot hear; it is like a dyke giving way, a gushing flood that cannot be stopped.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the yang qi is vexed and overworked ... it makes a person suffer *jianjue*"] This again warns against sudden and abrupt rising and resting that vex and disturb the yang harmony. If one vexes and disturbs the yang harmony, overworks and tires the sinews and bones, harms the spirit qi by movement, and depletes and exhausts the heavenly truth, then the sinews and vessels swell and distend and the essential qi is exhausted and cut off. Having harmed the kidney qi, one also damages the bladder; therefore in the summer season it makes a person suffer *jianjue*. Because one is boiled and pressed and the qi rebels, it is named *jianjue*. *Jue* means rebellion of qi. The condition of *jianjue* is as described below.
Song editors: The *Maijie* (chapter 49) says: "What is called being short of breath and prone to anger is because the yang qi is not ordered. When the yang qi is not ordered, it cannot come out; the liver qi should be ordered but cannot be; therefore one is prone to anger. Being prone to anger is called *jianjue*."

[On "The eyes are blind ... a gushing flood that cannot be stopped"] Having harmed the kidney, one also exhausts the bladder. The kidney channel connects internally with the ear, and the bladder vessel arises at the corner of the eye; therefore the eyes are blind and see nothing, and the ears are closed and hear nothing. How great this is! This is the harm of the bedchamber. When the sight of the eyes is blinded and the hearing of the ears is closed, then will, intention, heart and spirit, sinews and bones, intestines and stomach are like a dyke giving way, gushing in vexation that cannot be stopped.

## Notes

If the yang qi is distended by vexation and overwork, the essence is cut off, and this accumulates into the illness called *jianjue* in summer; sight and hearing fail, and it cannot be stopped, like a dyke giving way.
- *Jianjue*: Wang Bing explains it as an illness of rebellious qi caused by being "boiled and pressed". The Song editors cite the *Maijie*: "being prone to anger is called *jianjue*".
- Wang Bing takes this as damage to the kidney and bladder from sexual excess.
- 都 is taken in the sense of a dyke or reservoir.
- Wikisource (the plain text) and Daizhige write 汨汨 for 汩汩.
