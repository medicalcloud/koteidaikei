# 素問 生気通天論篇第三 §13 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When wind lodges and disorders the qi, the essence is lost, and the evil harms the liver. If on top of this one eats to excess, the sinews and vessels slacken crosswise, and intestinal flux becomes piles. If on top of this one drinks heavily, the qi rebels. If on top of this one forces one's strength, the kidney qi is harmed and the high bone is ruined.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When wind lodges and disorders the qi, the essence is lost, and the evil harms the liver"] From here on, the four categories all refer to losing the way of the sage. The qi of wind corresponds to the liver; therefore when wind is excessive and the essence lost, the liver is harmed. The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* (chapter 5) says: "The qi of wind communicates with the liver." When wind presses, heat arises; when heat is abundant, water dries up; when water dries up, the kidney qi does not circulate; therefore the essence is lost. 亡 means to be without.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi says: "Disordered qi is the confused qi of yin and yang. When, because of this confusion, wind lodges in it, the essence is harmed; when the essence is harmed, the evil enters the liver."

[On "If on top of this one eats to excess ... intestinal flux becomes piles"] Eating to great excess fills the intestines and stomach crosswise; when they are full, the sinews and vessels slacken and no longer hold together; therefore there is intestinal flux, which becomes piles. The *Bilun* (chapter 43) says, "If food and drink are doubled, the intestines and stomach are harmed" — this is proof of the harm.

[On "If on top of this one drinks heavily, the qi rebels"] When one drinks much, the lung spreads out and its lobes rise; therefore the qi rebels and rushes upward.

[On "If on top of this one forces one's strength ... the high bone is ruined"] "Forcing one's strength" means forcing oneself to enter the bedchamber. The "high bone" is the high bone of the lower back. If one forces oneself to have sex, the essence is wasted; if the essence is wasted, the kidney is harmed; if the kidney is harmed, the marrow qi withers within; therefore the high bone is ruined and useless. The sages' intercourse is not like this; it is as the following sentence says.

## Notes

Four examples of losing the way of the sage: wind disordering the qi so that the essence is lost and the liver harmed; intestinal flux and piles from overeating; rebellious qi from heavy drinking; and damage to the kidney and the lumbar bone from forced sexual activity.
- "When wind lodges and disorders the qi": Wang Bing explains that excessive wind causes loss of essence. Quan Yuanqi, cited by the Song editors, takes "disordered qi" as the confused qi of yin and yang.
- "Forcing one's strength": Wang Bing takes it as forcing oneself to have sex.
- "High bone": Wang Bing takes it as the high bone of the lower back.
