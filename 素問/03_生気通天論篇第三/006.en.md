# 素問 生気通天論篇第三 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The yang qi, when refined, nourishes the spirit; when supple, it nourishes the sinews. If opening and closing go wrong and cold qi follows, there arises severe hunching. [When cold] sinks into the vessels it forms fistulas, lingering and connecting in the flesh and interstices. When the qi of the transport points transforms and presses [inward], it is transmitted as a tendency to fear and as fright and alarm. When the nutritive qi does not comply and runs counter in the flesh, abscesses and swellings arise. If, before the *po*-sweat has finished, the body is weak and the qi melted away and the acupoints close, it breaks out as wind malaria.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The yang qi, when refined, nourishes the spirit; when supple, it nourishes the sinews"] This again clarifies the circulation and nourishing of the yang qi. The yang qi transforms within into subtle essence to nourish the spirit qi, and without it becomes supple to strengthen the sinews. If movement and rest lose their fitness, all kinds of illness arise.

[On "If opening and closing go wrong and cold qi follows, there arises severe hunching"] "Opening" means the skin and interstices discharging; "closing" means the dark mansions [sweat pores] closing and sealing. If opening and closing lose their fitness and cold attacks, the sinews and network vessels within are deeply knotted and fixed; with depletion and cold the sinews and network vessels contract, and the body hunches and bends. The *Lingshu jing* says, "With cold, the sinews tighten" — this is of that kind.

[On "[When cold] sinks into the vessels it forms fistulas, lingering and connecting in the flesh and interstices"] "Sinking into the vessels" means cold qi sinking in and impairing the vessels. Accumulated cold lingers and lodges, the channel blood stagnates and congeals, and long-standing stasis attacks the flesh and knots in the flesh; therefore it breaks out as ulcers and fistulas connected through the flesh and interstices.

[On "When the qi of the transport points transforms and presses [inward] ... fright and alarm"] It says that if cold strikes the transport points on the back and its qi transforms, enters deeply and presses upon the zang and fu, one becomes prone to fear and breaks out in fright and alarm.

[On "When the nutritive qi does not comply ... abscesses and swellings arise"] When the nutritive qi runs counter the blood stagnates; when the blood stagnates, heat gathers and forms pus; therefore there are abscesses and swellings. The *Zhengli lun* says: "Where heat passes, abscesses and swellings form."

[On "If, before the *po*-sweat has finished ... it breaks out as wind malaria"] When sweat has come out and not yet stopped, the body is weak and the qi wasted; if wind and cold press upon it, the acupoints close accordingly, the heat is stored and cannot come out, and so it remains until autumn. In autumn the yang gathers in again and the two heats combine; therefore there is shivering, and cold and heat alternate. Since it arises from wind, it is named wind malaria. The *Jingui zhenyan lun* (chapter 4) says, "Those who do not sweat in the summer heat develop wind malaria in autumn"; this is surely about its arising from wind. Hence the text below says:

## Notes

The yang qi nourishes the spirit and the sinews; when opening and closing go wrong and cold enters, illnesses such as severe hunching, fistulas, fright, abscesses and wind malaria arise.
- *Po*-sweat (魄汗) means sweat; Wang Bing paraphrases it as "sweat has come out and not yet stopped".
- "The qi of the transport points transforms and presses": Wang Bing takes this as cold striking the back transport points, transforming, entering deeply and pressing on the zang and fu.
- Wang Bing's closing "Hence the text below says" leads into the next unit (wind is the beginning of all illnesses).
