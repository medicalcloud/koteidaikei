# 素問 生気通天論篇第三 §15 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When due to dew and wind, cold and heat arise. Therefore, if one is harmed by wind in spring and the evil qi lingers, there is outpouring diarrhoea; if harmed by summer heat in summer, there is intermittent fever in autumn; if harmed by dampness in autumn, [qi] rebels upward with coughing and breaks out as limpness and reversal; if harmed by cold in winter, there will surely be a warm disease in spring. The qi of the four seasons in turn harm the five zang.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When due to dew and wind, cold and heat arise"] This is due to exposing the body and being struck by wind evil. The wind qi invades from outside and the yang qi resists within; wind and yang press against each other; hence cold and heat arise.

[On "Therefore, if one is harmed by wind in spring ... there is outpouring diarrhoea"] The qi of wind communicates with the liver. In spring the liver-wood is dominant, and wood overcomes spleen-earth; therefore outpouring diarrhoea arises.
Song editors: The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* (chapter 5) says: "If harmed by wind in spring, there is undigested diarrhoea in summer."

[On "if harmed by summer heat in summer, there is intermittent fever in autumn"] When the summer heat has already been severe and in autumn the yang gathers in again, the yang and the heat attack each other, producing intermittent fever. 痎 means old, and is also said to mean emaciated.

[On "if harmed by dampness in autumn, [qi] rebels upward with coughing"] "Dampness" means the damp qi of the earth. When dampness has prevailed in autumn and water is dominant again in winter, the water comes and encroaches on the lung; therefore the illness of cough and rebellion arises.
Song editors: The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* (chapter 5) says: "If harmed by dampness in autumn, there is coughing in winter."

[On "and breaks out as limpness and reversal"] When damp qi attacks the zang and fu within, there is cough and rebellion; when it disperses into the sinews and vessels without, there is limpness. The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* (chapter 5) says: "If one is affected by the damp qi of the earth, it harms the skin, flesh, sinews and vessels." Therefore, aided by the damp qi, it breaks out as limpness and reversal. "Reversal" means rebellious qi.

[On "if harmed by cold in winter, there will surely be a warm disease in spring"] The winter cold has congealed; when the yang qi issues in spring, the cold does not release, the yang is pent up within, and the cold and the pent-up [yang] hold each other; therefore there is warm disease.
Song editors: This overlaps with the *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* (chapter 5); the note there is very detailed.

[On "The qi of the four seasons in turn harm the five zang"] Cold, heat, warmth and coolness in turn win and lose; therefore the qi of the four seasons in turn harm the harmony of the five zang.

## Notes

Being harmed by the evil of one season leads to illness in the next: wind in spring causes outpouring diarrhoea, summer heat causes intermittent fever in autumn, dampness in autumn causes cough and limpness, and cold in winter causes warm disease in spring.
- As the Song editors note, this largely overlaps with the *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* (chapter 5): "If harmed by wind in spring, there is undigested diarrhoea in summer ... if harmed by cold in winter, there will surely be a warm disease in spring."
- *Jienüe*: Wang Bing glosses 痎 as "old" or "emaciated".
- In Wang Bing's note, Kanripo repeats the sentence "Song editors: The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* says, 'If harmed by dampness in autumn, there is coughing in winter'" (the same as the Song editors' note in the base text).
