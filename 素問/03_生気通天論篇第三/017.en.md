# 素問 生気通天論篇第三 §17 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Therefore, if the flavours are excessively sour, the liver qi overflows with fluids and the spleen qi is cut off. If excessively salty, the qi of the large bones is overworked, the flesh shrinks, and the heart qi is suppressed. If excessively sweet, the heart qi pants and is full, the complexion turns black, and the kidney qi is not balanced. If excessively bitter, the spleen qi is not moistened and the stomach qi becomes thick. If excessively acrid, the sinews and vessels are damaged and slack, and the essence and spirit suffer calamity.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "if the flavours are excessively sour, the liver qi overflows with fluids and the spleen qi is cut off"] Eating much sour causes retention of urine; when urination is impeded, the liver has much fluid; when the fluid overflows within, the liver lobes rise; when the liver lobes rise, the qi of the spleen channel is cut off and does not move. Why? Because wood controls earth.

[On "If excessively salty ... the heart qi is suppressed"] Eating much salty makes the flesh and skin shrink and also makes the heart qi suppressed and stagnant so that it does not move. Why? Because salty goes to the blood. The qi of the large bones is overworked because salty returns to the kidney.

[On "If excessively sweet ... the kidney qi is not balanced"] Eating much sweet makes one's heart oppressed. The nature of sweet is stagnant and slow; therefore it makes the qi pant and be full and the kidney unbalanced. Why? Because earth suppresses water. 衡 means balanced.

[On "If excessively bitter, the spleen qi is not moistened and the stomach qi becomes thick"] The nature of bitter is firm and dry, and it also nourishes the spleen and stomach; therefore the spleen qi is not moistened and the stomach qi becomes strong and thick.

[On "If excessively acrid ... the essence and spirit suffer calamity"] 沮 means moist, 弛 means relaxed, 央 means long-lasting. The nature of acrid is moistening and lubricating, dispersing and nourishing the sinews; therefore it relaxes the sinews, moistens the vessels, and makes the essence and spirit long-lasting. Why? Because acrid supplements the liver. The *Zangqi fashi lun* (chapter 22) says: "The liver desires to disperse; quickly eat acrid to disperse it, and use acrid to supplement it."
Song editors: This passage discusses harm caused by excess of flavours, so it is hard to explain it as "the essence and spirit are long-lasting". 央 is 殃 "calamity"; in ancient script they are used interchangeably, like writing 高梁 for 膏梁 or 草茲 for 草滋. Ancient texts were terse, and many characters were used as loan characters.

## Notes

The harm from excess of each of the five flavours: sour harms the liver and spleen; salty the bones, flesh and heart; sweet the heart and kidney; bitter the spleen and stomach; acrid the sinews, vessels and spirit.
- "The essence and spirit suffer calamity" (*jingshen nai yang*): Wang Bing glosses 央 as "long-lasting" and 沮 as "moist", saying that acrid supplements the liver and makes the spirit long-lasting. The Song editors object that the passage is about harm from excess and read 央 as 殃 "calamity". The translation follows the Song editors, and renders 沮弛 as "damaged and slack" accordingly.
- Wang Bing's explanations rest on the controlling cycle of the five phases (wood controls earth, earth suppresses water).
- In Wang Bing's note, the base text has a blank between 蔵気 and 法時論.
- Wikisource (the plain text) writes 咸 for 鹹.
