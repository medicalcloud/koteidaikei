# 素問 刺腰痛篇第四十一 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In lumbar pain with pain alongside the spine reaching the head, stiffness, blurred vision and a tendency to fall rigid, needle the cleft centre of the foot greater yang and let out blood. In lumbar pain with cold above, needle the foot greater yang and *yangming*; with heat above, needle the foot *jueyin*; when one cannot bend forward or back, needle the foot lesser yang; with heat in the centre and panting, needle the foot lesser yin and needle the cleft centre to let out blood. In lumbar pain with cold above and inability to turn the head, needle the foot *yangming*; with heat above, needle the foot greater yin; with heat in the centre and panting, needle the foot lesser yin. With difficult defecation, needle the foot lesser yin. With fullness of the lower abdomen, needle the foot *jueyin*. When it is as if broken, one cannot bend forward or back and cannot lift, needle the foot greater yang. When it draws to the inner edge of the spine, needle the foot lesser yin. In lumbar pain drawing to the lower abdomen and pulling the soft flank, so that one cannot bend back, needle where the lumbus and buttocks meet, on the muscles of both iliac crests, taking the waxing and waning of the moon as the number of needlings; on releasing the needle it ends at once. For the left take the right; for the right take the left.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In lumbar pain with pain alongside the spine ... needle the cleft centre of the foot greater yang and let out blood"] The cleft centre is Weizhong.
Song editors: The *Taisu* writes 頭沈沈然 "the head heavy" [for 几几然].

[On "with heat in the centre and panting, needle the foot lesser yin and needle the cleft centre to let out blood"] This method is profound and differs from the *Zhonggao*; it cannot be fathomed. One should use someone who knows its responses; otherwise one should always first remove the blood network vessels and then regulate.

[On "In lumbar pain with cold above and inability to turn the head, needle the foot *yangming*"] For cold above, Yinshi governs it. Yinshi is three body-*cun* above the knee, in the depression below Futu, where the vessel qi of the foot *yangming* issues. One may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. For inability to turn the head, Sanli governs it. Sanli is three body-*cun* below the knee, between the two sinews on the outer edge of the shin, where the foot *yangming* vessel enters. One may needle one body-*cun* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "with heat above, needle the foot greater yin"] Diji governs it. Diji is five body-*cun* below the knee, the cleft of the foot greater yin. One may needle three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes five cones.

[On "with heat in the centre and panting, needle the foot lesser yin"] Yongquan and Taizhong both govern it. Yongquan is in the depression in the sole, in the hollow formed when the foot is bent and the toes curled, where the foot lesser yin vessel emerges. One may needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Taizhong is at the artery in the passage behind the heel, the network point of the foot lesser yin. One may needle two body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The note to *Cinüe* puts Taizhong in the passage behind the inner ankle; the note to the *Shuixue lun* puts it behind the inner ankle; this note puts it at the artery in the passage behind the heel. The three notes differ. The *Jiayi jing* also says "in the thoroughfare behind the heel". The *Jiayi jing* should be followed as correct.

[On "With difficult defecation, needle the foot lesser yin"] Yongquan governs it.

[On "With fullness of the lower abdomen, needle the foot *jueyin*"] Taichong governs it, on the inner side behind the base joint of the big toe, in the depression two body-*cun* back where the pulse is felt; it is where the foot *jueyin* vessel pours. One may needle three body-*fen* and retain ten breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "When it is as if broken ... needle the foot greater yang"] For "as if broken", Shugu governs it; for inability to bend forward or back, Jinggu and Kunlun govern it; for inability to lift, Shenmai and Pucan govern it. Shugu is on the outer side of the little toe, in the depression at the border of red and white flesh behind the base joint, where the foot greater yang vessel pours. One may needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Jinggu is on the outer side of the foot below the large bone, in the depression at the border of red and white flesh, found by pressing; it is where the foot greater yang vessel passes. One may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Kunlun is behind the outer ankle, in the depression above the heel bone, where a fine pulse is felt; it is where the foot greater yang vessel runs. One may needle five body-*fen* and retain ten breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Shenmai is five body-*fen* below the outer ankle, in a space admitting a fingernail, where the yang *qiao* arises. One may needle six body-*fen* and retain ten breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Pucan is in the depression below the heel bone, the meeting of the foot greater yang and yang *qiao* vessels. One may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: In the *Jiayi jing*, Shenmai is in the depression below the outer ankle, without "five *fen*"; needling six *fen* is three *fen*, and retaining ten breaths is six breaths; the note to the *Qixue lun* writes seven breaths. Pucan is retained seven breaths; the *Jiayi jing* writes six.

[On "When it draws to the inner edge of the spine, needle the foot lesser yin"] Fuliu governs it; take it as in the note on Feiyang. From "lumbar pain with cold above and inability to turn the head" to here, these words of the classic, excluding the notes, are all to be written in red.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition, the *Jiayi jing* and the *Taisu* all lack everything from "lumbar pain with cold above" to here; Mr Wang added it. The nineteen characters in the present note, "from lumbar pain with cold above ... all to be written in red", are not Wang Bing's words; presumably they were added by someone later.

[On "In lumbar pain drawing to the lower abdomen and pulling the soft flank, so that one cannot bend back"] Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes "cannot bend forward or back".

[On "needle where the lumbus and buttocks meet ... it ends at once"] Here the evil lodges in the network vessel of the foot greater yin. 控 means draw through; 䏚 means the empty, soft place below the lower ribs. "Where the lumbus and buttocks meet" means the four bone holes on both sides of the sacrum below the iliac crest, eight points left and right; this bone is commonly called the eight-*liao* bone. This lumbar pain is treated at the fourth *liao* below the lumbar crest, namely the Xialiao point. The three vessels of the foot greater yin, *jueyin* and lesser yang cross and knot left and right within it; hence "where the lumbus and buttocks meet". "The muscles of both iliac crests" means the hard raised flesh below both iliac bones. 胂上 does not mean the top of the muscle; it means needling the muscle itself; needling straight into the muscle is "on the muscle". Why? On top of the muscle there are separately the Zhonglüshu and Baihuanshu points, which also govern lumbar pain, but considering their forms and signs they do not correspond to the classic. The iliac bones are the raised bones on both sides of the lumbar spine. Alongside the spine below the iliac crests, on each side, the muscle bulges and slants toward behind the iliac bone, receiving the crest inwardly; hence "the muscles of both iliac crests". Below, receiving the iliac muscle, each of the left and right muscles has four bone holes, called Shangliao, Ciliao, Zhongliao and Xialiao. Shangliao is in the depression below the iliac bone; the other three slant slightly downward and are found as depressions on pressing. All four holes govern lumbar pain, but only what Xialiao governs agrees with the classic; it is where the greater yin, *jueyin* and lesser yang knot. One may needle two body-*cun* and retain ten breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. "Taking the waxing and waning of the moon as the number of needlings": the beginning of the month, moving toward full, is the waxing; the middle of the month, moving toward empty, is the waning. In the waning month needle fewer times; in the waxing month needle more. The *Miuci lun* says, "When the moon is born, on the first day one needling, on the second day two, increasing gradually; on the fifteenth day fifteen needlings, on the sixteenth day fourteen, decreasing gradually." This is how the number of needlings is known.

[On "For the left take the right; for the right take the left"] When the pain is on the left, needle the right; when on the right, needle the left. This is because the vessels cross and knot left and right within the sacrum.
Song editors: On examination, this passage on "lumbar pain drawing to the lower abdomen" repeats the *Miuci lun*.

## Notes

Needling according to the accompanying symptoms of lumbar pain, and the method of needling where the lumbus and buttocks meet; this ends the chapter.
- According to Wang Bing's note, from "lumbar pain with cold above and inability to turn the head" to "needle the foot lesser yin" (drawing to the inner edge of the spine) is written in red (text supplied by Wang Bing); according to the Song editors, everything from "lumbar pain with cold above" to here is absent from Quan Yuanqi's edition, the *Jiayi jing* and the *Taisu*, and was added by Wang Bing.
- The Song editors note that the passage from "lumbar pain drawing to the lower abdomen" repeats the *Miuci lun* (chapter 63).
- Daizhige (重广补注黄帝内经素问) has 巻十二 at the end of the chapter; this marks the juan division in that source.
