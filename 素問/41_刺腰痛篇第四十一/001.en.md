# 素問 刺腰痛篇第四十一 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The foot greater yang vessel makes one's lumbus ache, drawing to the nape, spine, buttocks and back as if heavy. Needle the main channel of the greater yang at the cleft centre and let out blood; in spring do not let blood appear. The lesser yang makes one's lumbus ache as if needles were pricking the skin, so that one cannot bend forward or back or turn the head. Needle the end of the lesser yang's *chenggu* and let out blood; the *chenggu* is the bone standing out alone on the outer edge of the knee. In summer do not let blood appear. The *yangming* makes one's lumbus ache so that one cannot turn the head, and on turning one seems to see things, and is prone to sorrow. Needle the *yangming* in front of the shin three times, harmonizing above and below, and let out blood; in autumn do not let blood appear. The foot lesser yin makes one's lumbus ache, the pain drawing to the inner edge of the spine. Needle the lesser yin above the inner ankle twice; in spring do not let blood appear; if too much blood is let, one cannot recover. The *jueyin* vessel makes one's lumbus ache, the inside of the lumbus taut like a drawn bowstring. Needle the *jueyin* vessel, outside the fish belly of the calf and heel; follow it, and where it is knotted like a string of beads, needle it. This illness makes one prone to talk, or silent and unclear; needle it three times. The divergent vessel makes one's lumbus ache, the pain drawing to the shoulder, the eyes blurred, with occasional incontinence of urine. Needle the divergent vessel, at the transverse vessel on the outer edge of the cleft between the sinews and flesh of the knee, and let out blood; stop when the blood changes. The divergent vessel makes one's lumbus ache as if pulled by a belt, always as if the lumbus were broken, and prone to fear. Needle the divergent vessel at the knotted network vessel like a millet grain in the cleft centre; when needled, the blood spurts out black; stop when red blood appears. The same-yin vessel makes one's lumbus ache, the pain as if a small hammer were inside, with angry swelling. Needle the same-yin vessel, above the outer ankle at the end of Juegu, three times. The *yangwei* vessel makes one's lumbus ache, with angry swelling above the pain. Needle the *yangwei* vessel; the vessel joins the greater yang in the space below the calf, about one *chi* from the ground. The transverse network vessel makes one's lumbus ache so that one cannot bend forward or back, and bending back one fears falling. It is contracted by injuring the lumbus lifting heavy things: the transverse network vessel is severed and bad blood collects there. Needle it between the *xiyang* and the sinew, several *cun* above the cleft, at the two transverse points, and let out blood. The meeting-yin vessel makes one's lumbus ache, with dripping sweat above the pain; when the sweat dries one wants to drink, and after drinking one wants to run. Needle three times above the straight-yang vessel, transversely five *cun* above the *qiao* and below the cleft; look for where it is abundant and let out blood. The flying-yang vessel makes one's lumbus ache, with agitation above the pain, and when severe, sorrow and fear. Needle the flying-yang vessel, five *cun* above the inner ankle, in front of the lesser yin, where it meets the *yinwei*. The flourishing-yang vessel makes one's lumbus ache, the pain drawing to the breast, the eyes blurred; when severe, one arches backward and the tongue curls so that one cannot speak. Needle the inner sinew twice, above the inner ankle in front of the great sinew and behind the greater yin, about two *cun* above the ankle. The scattered vessel makes one's lumbus ache and hot; when the heat is severe there is vexation, below the lumbus it is as if a crossbeam lay within, and when severe there is incontinence of urine. Needle the scattered vessel, in front of the knee in the division between bone and flesh, at the binding vessel on the outer edge of the network vessel, three times. The inner-flesh vessel makes one's lumbus ache so that one cannot cough, and coughing makes the sinews contract tightly. Needle the inner-flesh vessel twice, outside the greater yang, behind the lesser yang's Juegu.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The foot greater yang vessel makes one's lumbus ache ... as if heavy"] The foot greater yang vessel branches down the nape, follows the inside of the shoulder blade, runs alongside the spine to the lumbus, and branches down through the buttocks; therefore it makes the lumbus ache, drawing to the nape, spine, buttocks and back as if heavy.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 貫胂 for 貫臋; the note to *Cinüe* also writes 貫胂; the note to *Sanbu jiuhou* writes 貫臋.

[On "Needle the main channel of the greater yang at the cleft centre ... in spring do not let blood appear"] The cleft centre is Weizhong, at the artery in the transverse crease at the centre of the popliteal fossa at the bend behind the knee, where the foot greater yang vessel enters. One may needle five body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. The greater yang is paired with the kidney; the kidney dominates in winter, and water declines in spring; therefore in spring do not let blood appear.

[On "The lesser yang makes one's lumbus ache ... or turn the head"] The foot lesser yang vessel winds around the pubic hairline and enters transversely into the hip socket; therefore it makes the lumbus ache as if needles were pricking the skin, so that one cannot bend forward or back. The lesser yang vessel arises at the outer canthus, rises to the corner of the head, descends behind the ear, follows the neck in front of the hand *yangming*, reaches the top of the shoulder and crosses behind the hand lesser yang. A branch goes from the outer canthus down to Daying, joins the hand lesser yang below the cheekbone, passes Jiache, descends the neck and joins at the supraclavicular fossa; therefore one cannot turn the head.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes "in front of the hand lesser yang" for "in front of the hand *yangming*".

[On "Needle the end of the lesser yang's *chenggu* ... In summer do not let blood appear"] *Chenggu* means the finger-sized depression between the two raised bones at the upper end of the shin, on the outer side of the knee toward the bottom. The shin bone forms the pillar of the knee and thigh bone; hence it is called *chenggu* "forming bone". The lesser yang is paired with the liver; the liver dominates in spring, and wood declines in summer; therefore [in summer] do not let blood appear.

[On "The *yangming* makes one's lumbus ache ... prone to sorrow"] The foot *yangming* vessel arises at the nose, crosses at the bridge, descends along the outside of the nose, enters the upper teeth, comes out alongside the mouth and around the lips, crosses below at Chengjiang, turns back along the lower edge behind the chin and emerges at Daying; a branch descends from in front of Daying to Renying, follows the throat and enters the supraclavicular fossa; another branch arises at the lower opening of the stomach, follows the inside of the abdomen to Qijie where it joins, and descends to the thigh. Therefore it makes the lumbus ache so that one cannot turn the head, and on turning one seems to see things. Yang is depleted; therefore there is sorrow.

[On "Needle the *yangming* in front of the shin three times ... in autumn do not let blood appear"] According to the *Neijing zhonggao liuzhu tujing*, among the points governed by the *yangming* vessel, for this lumbar pain one needles in front of the shin three times, which is precisely the Sanli point. Sanli is three body-*cun* below the knee, between the two sinews on the outer edge of the shin. One may needle one body-*cun* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. The *yangming* is paired with the spleen; the spleen dominates in late summer, and earth declines in autumn; therefore in autumn do not let blood appear.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 骭 for 䯒.

[On "The foot lesser yin makes one's lumbus ache ... the inner edge of the spine"] The foot lesser yin vessel rises along the posterior edge of the inner thigh, passes through the spine and belongs to the kidney; therefore it makes the lumbus ache, the pain drawing to the inner edge of the spine.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 脊内痛 for 脊内廉; the *Taisu* is the same. Before this, the signs of foot greater yin lumbar pain and the method of needling the foot greater yin are missing; presumably a loss of slips in the ancient text.

[On "Needle the lesser yin above the inner ankle twice ... one cannot recover"] According to the *Neijing zhonggao liuzhu tujing*, among the points governed by the lesser yin vessel, for this lumbar pain one should needle above the inner ankle, which is precisely the Fuliu point. Fuliu is in the depression at the artery two body-*cun* above and behind the inner ankle. One may needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, five cones.

[On "The *jueyin* vessel makes one's lumbus ache ... like a drawn bowstring"] The foot *jueyin* vessel goes from the inner thigh around the genitals to the lower abdomen; a branch knots with the greater yin and lesser yang in the third and fourth bone holes alongside the spine below the lumbar crest, namely the Zhongliao and Xialiao points. Therefore when the lumbus aches, the inside is like a drawn bowstring. "Like a drawn string" expresses extreme stiffness and tension.

[On "Needle the *jueyin* vessel, outside the fish belly ... needle it"] 腨踵 means the vessel is on the lower outer side of the calf toward the heel. The calf is shaped like the belly of a lying fish; hence "outside the fish belly". Follow its divisions of flesh, and where blood network vessels are knotted like beads, needle and let them out. This corresponds precisely to the region of the Ligou point, the network point of the foot *jueyin*, five *cun* above the inner ankle, branching to the lesser yang. One may needle two body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. One classic writes 居陰 for 厥陰; this is a copying error of the cursive 厥 as 居.
Song editors: The classic says "the *jueyin* vessel makes one's lumbus ache" and next "needle the *jueyin* vessel", while the note says "needle the network vessel of the *jueyin*". Text and note disagree; 脈 in the classic is suspected to be an error for 絡.

[On "This illness makes one prone to talk, or silent and unclear; needle it three times"] The *jueyin* vessel follows the back of the throat, rises into the nasopharynx and connects with the root of the tongue; therefore in illness one is prone to talk. When wind is abundant, one is dazed; therefore one is not clear. Needle the place three times and the lumbar pain is removed.
Song editors: The classic says 善言，黙黙然不慧, but on examination, being prone to talk and being silent are hard to combine as two illnesses. Quan Yuanqi's edition lacks 善, which makes sense. Moreover, according to the *Jiayi jing*, the *jueyin* vessel does not connect with the root of the tongue. Mr Wang cites and annotates this in five places in the *Suwen*: in the notes to the *Jue lun*, *Cire* and this chapter he says it connects with the root of the tongue; in the notes to the *Feng lun* and *Bi lun* he does not. Presumably Mr Wang too was in doubt and stated both.

[On "The divergent vessel makes one's lumbus ache ... occasional incontinence of urine"] The divergent vessel is a vessel that runs scattered, meaning it does not join but runs separately. This is the foot greater yang channel, which arises at the inner canthus, rises over the forehead, crosses at the vertex, follows the shoulder blade, runs alongside the spine to the lumbus, enters along the spinal muscles, connects with the kidney, belongs to the bladder and descends into the popliteal fossa; hence these signs. A branch goes from inside the shoulder blade down through the paraspinal muscles, along the posterior outer edge of the shoulder blade, and joins in the popliteal fossa. The two vessels are like the strands of an untwisted rope; hence the name "divergent vessel".

[On "Needle the divergent vessel, at the transverse vessel ... stop when the blood changes"] Behind the knee, the great sinews on both sides rise in pairs to the back of the thigh; between the two sinews, at the transverse crease, where the flesh bulges, is the region of the cleft centre. The old *Zhonggao* takes the popliteal centre as the cleft of the greater yang. One should take the blood network vessel that appears transversely on the outer edge of the cleft, distinctly purple-black and full, and needle it. Black blood should appear; wait until the blood colour changes to red and then stop. If the blood does not turn red and one drains to the extreme, it will surely make the blood flow; stop when the colour turns red. This is lumbar pain of the central channel of the greater yang.

[On "The divergent vessel makes one's lumbus ache as if pulled by a belt ... prone to fear"] The divergent branch of the foot greater yang separates at the shoulder, runs down the back and spine to the lumbus, enters transversely at the posterior outer edge of the thigh and descends to join in the popliteal fossa; therefore it is as if pulled by a belt, as if the lumbus were broken.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 如裂 "as if split" for 如引帯 and 善怒 "prone to anger" for 善恐.

[On "Needle the divergent vessel at the knotted network vessel ... stop when red blood appears"] The cleft centre is the Weizhong point, the uniting point of the foot greater yang, at the artery in the transverse crease at the centre of the popliteal fossa at the bend behind the knee. One may needle five body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. This is the method of channel needling. Here one takes the knotted network vessel the size of a millet grain; black blood should spurt out like an arrow, and one may stop when the blood turns red.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi says, "There are two divergent vessels with different origins of illness; I fear an error, but it is unclear."

[On "The same-yin vessel makes one's lumbus ache ... with angry swelling"] It is the separate network vessel of the foot lesser yang. It rises alongside the lesser yang channel, five body-*cun* above the outer ankle branches off to the *jueyin*, and runs down alongside the channel to connect with the instep; hence it is called the same-yin vessel. 怫 means anger: the swelling is like anger.
Song editors: The *Taisu* writes 小鍼 "small needle" for 小錘 "small hammer".

[On "Needle the same-yin vessel, above the outer ankle at the end of Juegu, three times"] At the end of Juegu, three body-*fen* forward, is the Yangfu point, where the foot lesser yang vessel runs. One may needle five body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "The *yangwei* vessel makes one's lumbus ache, with angry swelling above the pain"] The *yangwei* arises from yang, that is, where the greater yang arises; it is one of the eight extraordinary vessels.

[On "Needle the *yangwei* vessel ... about one *chi* from the ground"] It is governed by the greater yang; it runs up alongside the main channel, and below the calf joins the greater yang again and rises. Below the calf is exactly one body-*chi* from the ground; this is the Chengguang point, in the depression between the divisions of flesh below the pointed belly of the calf. One may needle seven body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones. Because it is taken between the divisions of flesh below the calf, it says "joins in the space below the calf".
Song editors: The location of the point is Chengshan, not Chengguang; 山 has been miscopied as 光.

[On "The transverse network vessel makes one's lumbus ache ... bad blood collects there"] 衡 means transverse; it refers to outside the greater yang: the network vessel that enters transversely from the lumbus at the posterior outer edge of the thigh and descends to join the central channel in the popliteal fossa. Now when the lumbus is injured lifting heavy things, the transverse network vessel is severed and the central channel alone is abundant; therefore the lumbus aches and one cannot bend forward or back. One classic writes 衡絶之脈, a copying error of the "fish-and-lu" kind. If it were the 衡 vessel, the *Zhonggao* would not say to take the points Weiyang and Yinmen of the greater yang vessel.

[On "Needle it between the *xiyang* and the sinew ... and let out blood"] The two transverse points are Weiyang and Yinmen, which correspond horizontally when viewed level. *Xiyang* means the Weiyang point on the upper side of the Fuxi point. "Between the sinews" means the Yinmen point between the two sinews above the popliteal fossa behind the knee. The two points are each six body-*cun* from the transverse crease below the buttocks; hence "several *cun* above the cleft". Weiyang may be needled seven body-*fen*, retained five breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Yinmen may be needled five body-*fen*, retained seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Hence "at the two transverse points".
Song editors: On examination, Mr Wang says "the Weiyang point on the upper side of the Fuxi point", but according to the *Jiayi jing* Weiyang is one *cun* below Fuxi and cannot be called the upper side.

[On "The meeting-yin vessel makes one's lumbus ache ... one wants to run"] It is the central channel of the foot greater yang. Its vessel runs down the lumbus and meets at the posterior orifice; hence it is called the meeting-yin vessel. The channel runs down from the lumbus to the foot. Now yang qi is greatly abundant; therefore sweat drips above the pain. Once the sweat has come out, the kidney is dry and yin depleted; therefore when the sweat dries one wants to drink water to rescue the kidney. Once water has entered the abdomen, the kidney qi revives, yin qi flows and the greater yang is abundant again; therefore after drinking one wants to run.

[On "Needle three times above the straight-yang vessel ... let out blood"] The straight-yang vessel is the greater yang vessel that runs down alongside the spine, through the buttocks, down to the popliteal fossa, down along the calf and behind the outer ankle, running straight; hence it is called the straight-yang vessel. 蹻 is where the yang *qiao* arises, the Shenmai point below the outer ankle. "Below the cleft" means below the popliteal fossa. The place needled is five body-*cun* below the popliteal fossa, receiving the cleft-centre point above and corresponding to the position of Shenmai below; this is the Chengjin point, in the depression slightly outward at the centre of the calf. It is where the qi of the greater yang vessel issues; needling is prohibited, and moxibustion of three cones is permitted. Here "needle" means needling the blood network vessels that are abundantly full. In both calves the channel qi of the greater yang descends; look at the centre of both calves for blood network vessels that are abundantly full and needle them out; hence "look for where it is abundant and let out blood".
Song editors: On examination, above it says "the meeting-yin vessel makes one's lumbus ache" and here "needle the straight-yang vessel"; this straight-yang vessel is the meeting-yin vessel; the wording changes but the matter does not differ. Moreover, the note on the Chengjin point says "slightly outward at the centre of the calf", but the *Jiayi jing* and the note to the *Gukong lun* lack the two characters 如外.

[On "The flying-yang vessel makes one's lumbus ache ... sorrow and fear"] This is the *yinwei* vessel. Five body-*cun* above the inner ankle, in the division of the calf, it rises alongside the lesser yin channel; in front of the lesser yin vessel is where the *yinwei* vessel runs. The foot lesser yin vessel rises from the kidney through the liver and diaphragm, enters the lung, follows the throat and runs alongside the root of the tongue; a branch comes out from the lung, connects with the heart and pours into the chest; therefore when severe there are sorrow and fear. Fear arises in the kidney, sorrow in the heart.

[On "Needle the flying-yang vessel, five *cun* above the inner ankle"] Your servant [Lin] Yi and others note: the *Jiayi jing* writes two *cun*.

[On "in front of the lesser yin, where it meets the *yinwei*"] Five body-*cun* above and behind the inner ankle is the Fuliu point, where the lesser yin vessel runs; one may needle three body-*fen*. Behind the inner ankle is the Zhubin point, the cleft of the *yinwei*; one may needle three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones. "In front of the lesser yin, where it meets the *yinwei*" is because three vessels meet in the region of this point. One may needle three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, five cones. The text of the present *Zhonggao* agrees exactly with this method. Your servant Yi and others note: the *Jiayi jing* says, "The network vessel of the foot greater yang that branches to the lesser yin is called Feiyang, seven *cun* above the outer ankle," and also, "Zhubin, the cleft of the *yinwei*, is in the division of the calf above the inner ankle; the Fuliu point is two *cun* above the inner ankle." That this classic and note do not agree with the *Jiayi jing* suggests that "five *cun*" in the classic and note should be "two *cun*"; then the *Suwen* and *Jiayi jing* would correspond.

[On "The flourishing-yang vessel makes one's lumbus ache ... cannot speak"] This is the *yinqiao* vessel. The *yinqiao* is a branch of the foot lesser yin; it arises behind the Rangu bone, rises above the inner ankle, goes straight up along the inner thigh, enters the genitals, rises along the abdomen, enters the chest, enters the supraclavicular fossa, emerges in front of Renying, enters the inner edge of the cheekbone, belongs to the inner canthus, and joins the greater yang and yang *qiao* to rise; hence the lumbar pain takes this form.

[On "Needle the inner sinew twice ... about two *cun* above the ankle"] The inner sinew means the divisions of flesh in front of the great sinew. Behind the greater yin and in front of the great sinew is the Jiaoxin point, the cleft of the *yinqiao*, two body-*cun* above the inner ankle, in front of the lesser yin and behind the greater yin, in the depression between sinew and bone. One may needle four body-*fen* and retain five breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. The text of the present *Zhonggao* governs exactly this.

[On "The scattered vessel makes one's lumbus ache and hot ... incontinence of urine"] The scattered vessel is a branch of the foot greater yin; it rises scattered, hence the name. Its vessel follows the inner thigh, enters the abdomen, and knots with the lesser yin and lesser yang in the bone hole below the lumbar crest; therefore in illness it is as if a crossbeam lay within below the lumbus, and when severe there is incontinence of urine.

[On "Needle the scattered vessel ... three times"] It means the inner side in front of the knee. "The division between bone and flesh" means the space between the lower edge below the inner auxiliary bone of the knee and the calf muscle. "The outer edge of the network vessel" is the greater yin network vessel that appears blue. Below and behind the auxiliary bone is a great sinew that grips and binds the bones of the knee and shin so that they connect. Taking the vessel where this sinew binds the bone removes the illness; this is called Diji. With three needlings it ends; hence "at the binding vessel, three times".

[On "The inner-flesh vessel makes one's lumbus ache ... contract tightly"] The inner-flesh vessel arises from the lesser yang; it is where the vessel qi of the *yangwei* issues. 里 means inside.

[On "Needle the inner-flesh vessel twice ... behind the lesser yang's Juegu"] Fenrou governs it. One classic says "in front of the lesser yang's Juegu", a copying error. In front of Juegu runs the foot lesser yang vessel; behind Juegu passes the *yangwei* vessel; hence it specifies "outside the greater yang, behind the lesser yang's Juegu". The Fenrou point is directly above the outer ankle, two body-*fen* behind the end of Juegu, between the divisions of sinew and flesh, where the vessel qi of the *yangwei* issues. One may needle five body-*fen* and retain ten breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The Fenrou point does not appear in the *Jiayi jing*. It appears twice, here and in the note to the *Qixue lun*, with differing measurements: the note to the *Qixue lun* writes three *fen* for two *fen*, three *fen* for five *fen*, and seven breaths for ten breaths.

## Notes

This chapter classifies lumbar pain by vessel and describes its symptoms and needling; it contains no dialogue. This unit covers lumbar pain of the three yang and three yin of the foot and of the divergent, same-yin, *yangwei*, transverse network, meeting-yin (straight-yang), flying-yang, flourishing-yang, scattered and inner-flesh vessels.
- "In spring do not let blood appear" and the like prohibit bloodletting in the season when the channel declines; Wang Bing explains this by the five-phase rise and decline of the channels and zang.
- According to the Song editors, the lumbar pain of the foot greater yin and its needling are missing.
- The notes on "Needle the flying-yang vessel, five *cun* above the inner ankle" and "in front of the lesser yin, where it meets the *yinwei*" contain passages beginning "Your servant Yi and others note"; these are the words of the Song editors (Lin Yi and others), but in the base material they appear within Wang Bing's note. The translation keeps them as they are.
- 頑顙 in Wang Bing's note is presumably an error for 頏顙 "nasopharynx".
- For the "Chengguang point" in Wang Bing's note, the Song editors take it as an error for Chengshan.
- 仏 in Wang Bing's 仏怒也 refers to 怫 in the text.
