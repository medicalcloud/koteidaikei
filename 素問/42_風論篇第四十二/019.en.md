# 素問 風論篇第四十二 §19 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Spleen wind: much sweating, aversion to wind, lassitude of the body, unwillingness to move the four limbs, a faint yellow complexion, no appetite; the diagnostic sign is on the nose, where the colour is yellow.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Spleen wind ... where the colour is yellow"] The spleen vessel arises at the foot, rises along the shin bone, then up the anterior inner edge of the knee and thigh, enters the abdomen, belongs to the spleen and connects with the stomach, rises through the diaphragm alongside the throat, joins the root of the tongue and spreads below the tongue; a branch goes again from the stomach up through the diaphragm and pours into the heart. The heart vessel emerges at the hair and follows the arm. Therefore there are lassitude of the body, unwillingness to move the limbs, and no appetite. Spleen qi corresponds to earth and governs the centre; the nose too is in the centre of the face; hence the diagnosis is there. Yellow is the spleen's colour.
Song editors: Mr Wang's note on spleen wind should not cite the seven characters "the heart vessel emerges at the hand and follows the arm"; they serve no purpose. The spleen governs the four limbs; with spleen wind the limbs do not want to move.

## Notes

Spleen wind.
- 毛 "hair" in Wang Bing's 心脈出於毛 appears as 手 "hand" in the Song editors' quotation and is presumably an error for 手.
