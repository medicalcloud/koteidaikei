# 素問 風論篇第四十二 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Wind qi enters together with the greater yang, travels through the transport points of the vessels, disperses between the divisions of the flesh, and interferes with the defensive qi; its path is not free; therefore it makes the flesh swell and develop sores. Where the defensive qi congeals and does not move, the flesh becomes numb. Pestilential [wind] is when the nutritive qi is hot and putrefies and its qi is not clear; therefore it destroys the bridge of the nose, ruins the complexion, and the skin develops sores and ulcerates. Wind and cold lodge in the vessels and do not leave; it is called pestilential wind, or cold and heat.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Wind qi enters together with the greater yang ... the flesh becomes numb"] The divisions of the flesh are where the defensive qi travels. Wind presses on the defensive qi and travels with it between the divisions of the flesh; therefore the path of qi is rough and not free. When the path of qi is not free, the wind qi attacks within and the defensive qi resists it; therefore the flesh swells and sores appear. 瘍 means sores. If the defensive qi, blown by the wind, cannot circulate and gathers one-sidedly somewhere, congealing and not moving, the flesh has numb places. 不仁 means 𤸷 (numbness), not knowing cold, heat, pain or itch.

[On "Pestilential [wind] is ... the skin develops sores and ulcerates"] When blown, the wind enters the channel vessels. The nutritive qi travels within the vessels; therefore when wind enters the vessels it attacks the blood within and combines with the nutritive qi; combined, it becomes hot and the blood putrefies. "Its qi is not clear" means it is ulcerated and disordered. When the blood vessels are ulcerated and disordered and the nutritive qi carries wind, since the yang vessels all rise to the head and the nose is where one breathes, the bridge of the nose is destroyed, the colour turns bad, and the skin breaks and ulcerates. The *Maiyao jingwei lun* says, "When wind in the vessels is abundant, it becomes pestilence."

[On "Wind and cold lodge in the vessels ... or cold and heat"] At first it is cold and heat; when the heat is complete, it is called pestilential wind.
Song editors: Another edition writes 盛 for 成.

## Notes

Explains the sores and numbness arising when wind enters with the greater yang, and pestilential wind (a severe skin disease).
- Pestilential wind is often understood to correspond to the later "great wind" or leprosy.
