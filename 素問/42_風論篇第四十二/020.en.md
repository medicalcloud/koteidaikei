# 素問 風論篇第四十二 §20 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Kidney wind: much sweating, aversion to wind, puffy swelling of the face, pain in the spine so that one cannot stand upright, a sooty complexion, and impairment of the hidden parts; the diagnostic sign is on the skin, where the colour is black.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Kidney wind ... where the colour is black"] 痝然 means swollen. 炲 is black. The kidney is yin, and below the eyes is also yin; therefore when the kidney zang receives wind, the face is puffy and swollen. The kidney vessel arises below the foot, rises along the inside of the calf, emerges at the inner edge of the knee crease, rises along the posterior inner thigh and passes through the spine; therefore the spine aches and one cannot stand upright. 隠曲 means the hidden, covered and intimate places. The kidney stores essence and outwardly corresponds to sexual intercourse; now the zang is pressed by wind and the essential qi is faint within; therefore the hidden, intimate affairs do not proceed freely. The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* says, "Qi returns to essence; essence feeds on qi." Now when essence is insufficient, qi returns inward and the essential qi does not reach the skin; therefore the skin is black. Black is the kidney's colour.

## Notes

Kidney wind.
- 隠曲不利: according to Wang Bing it refers to impaired sexual function; others take it as impaired urination and defecation.
