# 素問 風論篇第四十二 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "Wind qi is stored between the skin; it cannot pass inward and cannot leak outward. Wind moves readily and changes frequently. When the interstices open, one shivers with cold; when they close, there is heat and oppression. When it is cold, eating and drinking decline; when it is hot, the flesh wastes. Therefore it makes one shudder and unable to eat. It is called cold and heat.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Wind qi is stored between the skin; it cannot pass inward and cannot leak outward"] When the interstices are open and loose, evil wind enters; once the wind qi has entered, the sweat pores close and seal; therefore it cannot pass inward or leak outward.

[On "Wind moves readily ... heat and oppression"] 洒然 describes cold; 悶 means not refreshed. When the interstices open, the wind blows about; therefore there is cold. When the interstices close, the wind is confused; therefore there is oppression.

[On "When it is cold, eating and drinking decline ... It is called cold and heat"] Cold wind enters the stomach; therefore eating and drinking decline. Hot qi is stored within; therefore the flesh wastes. Cold and heat combine; therefore one shudders and cannot eat; it is called cold and heat. 怢慄 describes sudden shivering with cold.
Song editors: On examination, Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 失味 "loss of taste" for 怢慄, and the *Jiayi jing* writes 解㑊 "languor".
