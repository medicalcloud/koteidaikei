# 素問 挙痛論篇第三十九 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Huangdi asked: "I have heard that one who speaks well of heaven must have verification in humans; one who speaks well of antiquity must have agreement with the present; one who speaks well of humans must have accord with oneself. If so, one is not confused about the Way and reaches the utmost of the essential numbers; this is what is called clarity. Now I ask you, Master, to make it knowable by speaking, visible by looking, and graspable by touching, verifying it in myself, so as to open ignorance and dispel confusion. May I hear it?" Qibo bowed twice, touched his head to the ground and replied: "About what way do you ask?"

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On the chapter title *Jutong lun*] It concerns sudden pain; 挙 is suspected to be an error for the character 卒 "sudden".

[On "I have heard that one who speaks well of heaven ... what is called clarity"] "One who speaks well of heaven" speaks of the qi of heaven's four seasons — warmth, coolness, cold and heat, generation, growth, harvest and storage — with which the form, qi and five zang of humans correspond, so that good and bad can be verified and pointed out; hence "must have verification in humans". "One who speaks well of antiquity" speaks of the traces of gain and loss in the life-nurturing of the sages of high antiquity, which can be matched with the principles of gain and loss in present life-nurturing to discuss success and failure; hence "must have agreement with the present". "One who speaks well of humans" speaks of how the frame and joints prop one another, the sinews and vessels bind and connect, the skin and flesh wrap them, and the five zang and six fu lie in order within, using the seven spirits and five zang to operate them, so that when the qi is cut off and the spirit departs, one goes to death. Thus one knows that that floating form cannot be firm and lasting, and quietly reflecting on oneself, finds oneself the same; hence "must have accord with oneself". Only one who knows the utmost of the essential numbers of the Way, without any doubt or confusion, and deeply understands the ultimate principles, can be so.

[On "Now I ask you ... May I hear it?"] It means opening the ears of the ignorant young and dispelling the confusion in the minds of the doubtful, setting things out one by one so that they can be verified by looking with the eyes and following with the hands. 捫 means follow along.

[On "Qibo bowed twice ... About what way do you ask?"] He asks to be shown the starting point of the question.

## Notes

The opening of the chapter: Huangdi asks about the way that can be known through speech, inspection and palpation.
- Wang Bing suggests that 挙痛 in the chapter title is an error for 卒痛 "sudden pain"; the first half of the chapter discusses sudden pain.
- 厭 in 必有厭於己 is understood as "accord, satisfy".
