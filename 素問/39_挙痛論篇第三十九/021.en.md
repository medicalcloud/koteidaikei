# 素問 挙痛論篇第三十九 §21 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Emperor said: "Good. I know that the hundred illnesses arise from qi: with anger the qi rises, with joy the qi slackens, with sorrow the qi dissipates, with fear the qi descends, with cold the qi contracts, with heat the qi leaks, with fright the qi is disordered, with toil the qi is consumed, with pensiveness the qi knots. The nine qi differ; what illnesses arise?"

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Good. I know that the hundred illnesses arise from qi"] The functioning of qi — depletion and repletion, counterflow and compliance, slackness and tension — can all cause illness; therefore he raises this question.

[On "with cold the qi contracts, with heat the qi leaks, with fright the qi is disordered"] Song editors: The *Taisu* writes 憂 "worry" for 驚 "fright".

## Notes

Stating that the hundred illnesses arise from qi, Huangdi lists nine changes of qi (the nine qi) due to emotions, cold and heat, toil and pensiveness, and asks about the illness of each. A frequently quoted passage.
