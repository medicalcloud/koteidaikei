# 素問 熱論篇第三十一 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "The greater yang is that to which all the yang belong; its vessel connects with Fengfu; therefore it governs the qi for all the yang. When a person is harmed by cold, there is febrile illness; though the fever is severe, one does not die. When both [exterior and interior] are affected by cold and fall ill, one cannot escape death."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The greater yang is that to which all the yang belong"] 巨 means great. The qi of the greater yang, with its channels, network vessels, blood and qi, nourishes and defends the body; therefore the qi of all the yang take it as their head and belong to it.

[On "its vessel connects with Fengfu"] Fengfu is the name of a point, in the hollow on the nape, one body-*cun* inside the hairline.

[On "therefore it governs the qi for all the yang"] The floating qi of the foot greater yin vessel in the head runs in five lines in all; therefore it governs the qi of all the yang.

[On "When a person is harmed by cold ... one does not die"] The cold toxin presses on the skin, and the yang qi cannot disperse and is knotted within; therefore one harmed by cold instead has febrile illness.

[On "When both are affected by cold ... one cannot escape death"] When zang and fu correspond and both receive cold, it is called dual affection.

## Notes

The greater yang governs the qi of all the yang; harm from cold produces febrile illness; dual affection, in which exterior and interior both receive cold, is fatal.
- 足太陰脈浮気 in Wang Bing's note is presumably an error for 足太陽 from the context, but the translation keeps the characters as they are.
