# 素問 熱論篇第三十一 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "On the first day of cold damage, the greater yang receives it; therefore the head and nape ache and the lumbus and spine are stiff. On the second day, the *yangming* receives it; the *yangming* governs the flesh, and its vessel runs alongside the nose and connects with the eyes; therefore the body is hot, the eyes ache, the nose is dry, and one cannot lie down. On the third day, the lesser yang receives it; the lesser yang governs the gallbladder, and its vessel follows the flanks and connects with the ears; therefore the chest and flanks ache and one is deaf. When the channels and network vessels of the three yang have all received the illness but it has not yet entered the zang, sweating will end it. On the fourth day, the greater yin receives it; the greater yin vessel spreads in the stomach and connects with the throat; therefore the abdomen is full and the throat dry. On the fifth day, the lesser yin receives it; the lesser yin vessel passes through the kidney, connects with the lung and attaches to the root of the tongue; therefore the mouth is parched, the tongue dry, and there is thirst. On the sixth day, the *jueyin* receives it; the *jueyin* vessel follows the genitals and connects with the liver; therefore there is vexation and fullness and the scrotum retracts. When the three yin and three yang, the five zang and six fu all receive the illness, the nutritive and defensive do not move and the five zang are blocked, one dies.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "On the first day of cold damage, the greater yang receives it"] Among the qi of the three yang, the greater yang vessel floats; what floats is outside, in the skin and hair; therefore on the first day of cold damage the greater yang receives it first.

[On "therefore the head and nape ache and the lumbus and spine are stiff"] The text above saying "its vessel connects with Fengfu" was a brief statement. In detail, the foot greater yang vessel enters from the vertex to connect with the brain, comes out again, branches down the nape, follows the inside of the shoulder blade, runs alongside the spine and reaches the lumbus; therefore the head and nape ache and the lumbus and spine are stiff.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* and *Taisu* read "the head, nape, lumbus and spine all ache".

[On "On the second day, the *yangming* receives it"] Yang is stirred by heat, and like qi seek each other; therefore it enters the *yangming* from the greater yang.

[On "the *yangming* governs the flesh ... one cannot lie down"] The body is hot because the flesh receives the evil. The stomach is hot and vexed; therefore one cannot lie down. The rest follows what the vessels produce.

[On "On the third day, the lesser yang receives it; the lesser yang governs the gallbladder"] Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 骨 "bone" for 胆 "gallbladder". Yuanqi's note says, "The lesser yang is the exterior of the liver; the liver governs the sinews, and the sinews meet at the bones; this is where the lesser yang qi nourishes; hence it says it governs the bones." The *Jiayi jing*, *Taisu* and others all write 骨.

[On "When the channels and network vessels of the three yang ... sweating will end it"] The illness is in the exterior; therefore one can sweat it.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 府 for 蔵. Yuanqi's note says, "The illness of cold damage first enters the interstices of the skin and gradually overcomes the yang, but has not yet entered the fu; therefore sweating must be used to bring out the cold and heat and disperse them." The *Taisu* also writes 府.

[On "On the fourth day, the greater yin receives it"] Yang reaches its extreme and yin receives it.

[On "When the three yin and three yang ... one dies"] 死 is like 嘶 "exhaustion": the essential qi is all exhausted. This is why those who die all do so within six or seven days of illness.

## Notes

Cold damage is transmitted day by day through the greater yang, *yangming*, lesser yang, greater yin, lesser yin and *jueyin*, with symptoms corresponding to the course of each channel (transmission through the six channels). Qibo's speech continues in the next unit.
- "The lesser yang governs the gallbladder": according to the Song editors, Quan Yuanqi's edition, the *Jiayi jing* and the *Taisu* read "governs the bones".
- "Has not yet entered the zang": according to the Song editors, Quan Yuanqi's edition and the *Taisu* write 府 for 蔵.
- 嘶 in Wang Bing's note is presumably used in the sense of 澌 "exhaust".
