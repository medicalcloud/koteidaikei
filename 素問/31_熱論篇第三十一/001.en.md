# 素問 熱論篇第三十一 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Huangdi asked: "Now, febrile illnesses are all of the category of cold damage. Some recover and some die; those who die all do so within six or seven days, and those who recover all take ten days or more. Why is this? I do not know the explanation and wish to hear the reason."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Now, febrile illnesses ... wish to hear the reason"] Cold is the qi of winter. In winter it is severely cold and all things are deeply stored; the gentleman keeps himself firm and closed and is not harmed by cold. One who is exposed to it is said to have cold damage. Harm from the qi of any of the four seasons can cause illness, but cold damage is the most toxic because it rides on the qi of killing and harshness. When one falls ill immediately on being struck, it is called cold damage. When one does not fall ill immediately, the cold toxin is stored in the skin; before the summer solstice it changes into warm illness, and after the summer solstice into febrile illness. Yet their onset is all caused by cold damage; hence "febrile illnesses are all of the category of cold damage".
Song editors: The *Shanghan lun* says, "In spring it changes into warm illness; in summer it changes into summer-heat illness," which differs from Wang's note. Wang's note is based on the *Suwen*; the *Shanghan lun* is based on the *Yinyang dalun*; hence the difference.

## Notes

The opening question of the chapter: febrile illnesses are all of the category of cold damage; why do the days differ between those who die and those who recover?
- The *Shanghan lun* quoted by the Song editors corresponds to 不即病者，寒毒藏於肌膚，至春變為溫病，至夏變為暑病 in the "Shanghan li".
