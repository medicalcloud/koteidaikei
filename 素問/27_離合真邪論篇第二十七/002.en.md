# 素問 離合真邪論篇第二十七 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "When the sages established measures and numbers, they always corresponded to heaven and earth. Therefore heaven has its lunar mansions and degrees, earth has its channel rivers, and humans have their channel vessels. When heaven and earth are warm and mild, the channel rivers are calm and quiet; when heaven is cold and the earth frozen, the channel rivers congeal; when heaven is hot and the earth scorching, the channel rivers boil and overflow; when a sudden violent wind arises, the channel rivers surge in waves and swell up. When evil enters the vessels, with cold the blood congeals, with heat the qi becomes moist and flowing. When depleting evil enters and lodges accordingly, it is also like the channel rivers meeting wind: the pulsating vessels of the channels, when it arrives, also at times swell up, and their movement in the vessels is orderly; when it arrives at the *cunkou* and strikes the fingers, it is sometimes large and sometimes small. When large, the evil has arrived; when small, it is calm. Its movement has no fixed place; it is in yin or yang and cannot be measured. Follow and examine it, and when suddenly encountering it in the three regions and nine indicators, block its path early. Insert the needle on the in-breath, without letting the qi go against it; keep it still and retain it long, without letting the evil spread. Turn the needle on the in-breath, taking the obtaining of qi as the criterion. Wait for the out-breath to withdraw the needle, and remove it only when the out-breath is complete; the great qi all comes out. Therefore it is called draining."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Therefore heaven has its lunar mansions and degrees, earth has its channel rivers, and humans have their channel vessels"] "Mansions" means the twenty-eight lunar mansions; "degrees" means the 365 degrees of heaven. The channel rivers are the Hai, Du, Wei, Hu, Mian, Ru, Jiang, Huai, Luo, He, Zhang and Ji rivers; because they correspond inwardly to the channel vessels, they are called channel rivers. The channel vessels are the vessels of the three yin and three yang of hand and foot. They are mentioned because inner and outer correspond and the person's qi responds and communicates.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* says, "The foot *yangming* corresponds outwardly to the Hai and belongs inwardly to the stomach; the foot greater yang corresponds outwardly to the Du and belongs inwardly to the bladder; the foot lesser yang corresponds outwardly to the Wei and belongs inwardly to the gallbladder; the foot greater yin corresponds outwardly to the Hu and belongs inwardly to the spleen; the foot *jueyin* corresponds outwardly to the Mian and belongs inwardly to the liver; the foot lesser yin corresponds outwardly to the Ru and belongs inwardly to the kidney; the hand *yangming* corresponds outwardly to the Jiang and belongs inwardly to the large intestine; the hand greater yang corresponds outwardly to the Huai and belongs inwardly to the small intestine; the hand lesser yang corresponds outwardly to the Luo and belongs inwardly to the triple burner; the hand greater yin corresponds outwardly to the He and belongs inwardly to the lung; the hand heart master corresponds outwardly to the Zhang and belongs inwardly to the pericardium; the hand lesser yin corresponds outwardly to the Ji and belongs inwardly to the heart."

[On "when a sudden violent wind arises, the channel rivers surge in waves and swell up"] The human channel vessels also respond to this.

[On "the pulsating vessels of the channels ... their movement in the vessels is orderly"] 循循然 describes compliant movement. It says that they follow the movement of the channels and the breath, and accord with the coming and going of respiration, only their form sometimes differs. 循循 is written 輴輴 in one version.

[On "when it arrives at the *cunkou* ... Its movement has no fixed place"] "Large" means larger than the usual calm form in diagnosis. "Small" does not mean fine and small; it is called small in comparison with the large; if there were no large to compare, it would simply be the ordinary channel qi. Evil qi, following yin qi, enters the yin channels, and following yang qi, enters the yang vessels; therefore its movement has no fixed place.

[On "it is in yin or yang and cannot be measured"] Because it follows the flow of the channel vessels.

[On "Follow and examine it ... block its path early"] 逢 means encounter; 遏 means block off. When suddenly encountering it at the positions of the nine indicators within the three regions, press to stop it and drain it there. Once its path is cut off, the qi of the great evil can do nothing. What is meant by draining is as stated in the text below.

[On "Insert the needle on the in-breath ... Therefore it is called draining"] Considering the purport of the classic, one first supplements the true qi and then drains the evil. Why? In the method of supplementing below, "insert the needle at the end of the out-breath, keep it still and retain it long"; in the method of draining in this section, "insert the needle on the in-breath" and also "keep it still and retain it long". The end of the out-breath is followed by the in-breath, and when the in-breath comes there is no out-breath with it; thus the timing of insertion is the same and the principle of long retention is also one. The meaning of supplementing first is therefore clear. The *Zhenjing* says, "Draining is called meeting. The idea of meeting: hold firmly and insert, release and withdraw, push aside the yang and withdraw the needle, so that the sick qi can drain. Supplementing is called following. The idea of following: as if forgetting it, as if going, as if regretting, like a mosquito or gadfly alighting, as if staying, as if returning." So supplementing surely involves long retention. One supplements first because if the true qi is insufficient and one drains with the needle, the channel vessels are not full and the evil qi has no means of being expelled; so one first supplements the true qi to make it sufficient and then drains out the evil. 引 means draw out; 去 means leave the point. Waiting for the out-breath, one draws it to the gate, and leaves the door of the point only at the end of the out-breath; then the channel qi is carefully settled and balanced, and the evil qi has nothing to hold on to; therefore the qi of the great evil comes out with the needle. 呼 means qi going out; 吸 means qi coming in; 転 means turning. "Great qi" means the qi of the great evil that disorders yin and yang.

## Notes

Beginning from the correspondence of heaven, earth and the human body (mansions and degrees, channel rivers, channel vessels), Qibo explains that when evil enters the channels the pulse at the *cunkou* at times becomes large, that on encountering it in the three regions and nine indicators one should block it early, and the technique of draining.
- Wang Bing holds that even the method of draining includes first supplementing the true qi.
- The *Zhenjing* passage quoted by Wang Bing is close to the *Jiuzhen shi'er yuan* chapter of the *Lingshu*, with some differences in wording.
- 叚 in Wang Bing's note is a variant of 段.
