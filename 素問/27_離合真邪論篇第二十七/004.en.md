# 素問 離合真邪論篇第二十七 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "One must first feel and follow along it, press to disperse it, push and press it, flick to make it swell, scratch to bring it down, open and take it; draw on its gate outside so as to close in its spirit. Insert the needle at the end of the out-breath, keep it still and retain it long, taking the arrival of qi as the criterion, as if waiting for an honoured guest, not noticing the sun setting. When the qi arrives, adjust it and guard it oneself. Wait for the in-breath to withdraw the needle; the qi cannot come out, and each stays in its place. Push and close its gate, so that the spirit qi is preserved and the great qi retained. Therefore it is called supplementing."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "One must first feel and follow along it ... so as to close in its spirit"] 捫循 means feeling with the hand; 切 means pressing with the fingers. "Feel and follow along it" aims to relax the qi. "Press to disperse it" makes the channel vessels spread out. "Push and press it" pushes and gathers the skin. "Flick to make it swell" makes the qi of the vessel swell and fill. "Scratch to bring it down" marks where to place the needle. "Open and take it" follows the usual method. "Draw on its gate outside so as to close in its spirit" is the same as pushing and pressing: gather and press the skin outside the point so that it covers the spot where the needle goes in; once the needle has been released and removed, the unbroken skin covers the gate that was pierced. When the gate does not open, the spirit qi is guarded within; hence "close in its spirit". The *Jingtiao lun* says, "Draw the skin outside so that it covers the gate," and also, "Push and close its gate, so that the spirit qi is preserved" — this is what is meant.
Song editors: The passage Mr Wang cites from the *Tiaojing lun*, on close examination, is not from that discourse; it appears in the *Zhendao* chapter of the *Jiayi jing*. What follows "also" is from the present chapter.

[On "Insert the needle at the end of the out-breath ... taking the arrival of qi as the criterion"] Inserting the needle at the end of the out-breath is the same as on the in-breath. It says that the arrival of qi must be the criterion for removing the needle, and one does not remove the needle merely after a large number of breaths. The *Zhenjing* says, "If one needles and the qi does not arrive, do not count the number; if one needles and the qi arrives, remove it and do not needle again" — this is what is meant. The number of breaths is not used as the rule for slow or fast; essentially, the needle should be removed when the qi arrives, not withdrawn before the qi arrives under the needle and then redone.

[On "as if waiting for an honoured guest, not noticing the sun setting"] It compares the observation of qi to human affairs. 暮 means evening.

[On "When the qi arrives, adjust it and guard it oneself"] 適 means adjust. 護 means guard carefully. It says that once the qi is balanced and adjusted, one should guard it carefully and not change it, lest the illness arise again. The *Zhenjing* says, "Once the channel qi has arrived, guard it carefully and do not lose it" — this is the meaning. Guarding carefully should be as described below.
Song editors: The words Mr Wang cites from the *Zhenjing* are from the *Baoming quanxing lun* of the *Suwen*, and also appear in the *Zhenjie lun*.

[On "Wait for the in-breath to withdraw the needle ... Therefore it is called supplementing"] This states it properly. When the outer gate is closed, the spirit qi is preserved; waiting for the in-breath to withdraw the needle, the great qi does not leak; the meaning of supplementing can be known for certain. This great qi means the qi of the great channels that circulates the nutritive and defensive.

## Notes

The technique of supplementing: manipulations before insertion, insertion on the out-breath, waiting for the qi to arrive, withdrawal on the in-breath, and closing the needle hole.
- For "great qi", Wang Bing takes it as the qi of the great evil in the draining method (unit 002), but here as the qi of the great channels.
- 䕶 in Wang Bing's note is a variant of 護 (other sources write 護). 䐜 is presumably close in sense to 脹 "swell".
- According to the Song editors, the passage Wang Bing cites as from the "Jingtiao lun" is not from the *Tiaojing lun* but appears in the *Zhendao* chapter of the *Jiayi jing*.
