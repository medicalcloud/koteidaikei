# 素問 離合真邪論篇第二十七 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "When pathogen leaves the collaterals and enters the meridians, it lodges in the blood vessels. Its cold and warmth have not yet come into accord [with the genuine qi]; it is like surging waves rising, sometimes coming and sometimes going; therefore it is not always present. Hence it is said: just when it comes, one must press to stop it, and having stopped it, take it; do not meet its surge and drain it. The genuine qi is the meridian qi; [draining then] greatly depletes the meridian qi. Therefore 'its coming must not be met' means this. Hence it is said: if one observes the pathogen without care and drains after the great qi has already passed, the genuine qi escapes; once it escapes it does not recover, the pathogenic qi arrives again and the illness accumulates further. Therefore 'its going must not be pursued' means this. 'Not [even] a hair can be hung' means waiting for the moment the pathogen arrives and releasing the needle to drain; if too early or too late, the blood and qi are exhausted and the illness cannot be brought down. Therefore it is said, 'Knowing when it can be taken is like releasing a trigger; not knowing how to take it is like striking with a mallet.' Therefore 'one who knows the way of the trigger cannot hang [even] a hair; one who does not know the trigger strikes it and it does not release' means this."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When pathogen leaves the collaterals and enters the meridians, it lodges in the blood vessels"] The *Miuci lun* (chapter 63) says, "When pathogen lodges in the body, it first lodges in the skin and hair; if it lingers without leaving, it enters and lodges in the minute vessels; if it lingers without leaving, it enters and lodges in the collaterals; if it lingers without leaving, it enters and lodges in the meridian vessels." Hence "leaves the collaterals and enters the meridians".

[On "Its cold and warmth ... therefore it is not always present"] It roams around the 16 *zhang* 2 *chi* of the meridian vessels; therefore it is not always at the place observed.

[On "Hence it is said: just when it comes ... do not meet its surge and drain it"] 衝 means the normal qi that corresponds to the marks of the water clock. The *Lingshu jing* says, "When the water falls one mark, the person's qi is in the greater yang; two marks, in the lesser yang; three marks, in the *Yangming*; four marks, in the yin division." When the qi is in the greater yang, the greater yang alone is abundant; when the qi is in the lesser yang, the lesser yang alone is abundant. If, seeing it alone abundant, one takes it as the arrival of pathogen and drains it with the needle, one instead harms the genuine qi. Hence the text below says:

[On "The genuine qi is the meridian qi ... means this"] Taking the meridian qi that corresponds to the marks as pathogen and draining it is a grave error on the practitioner's part. Hence "its coming must not be met".

[On "Hence it is said: if one observes the pathogen without care ... the illness accumulates further"] Not recognizing the pathogen and instead punishing the innocent, the genuine qi leaks and escapes, the pathogenic qi invades again, and the meridian qi is greatly deficient; hence the illness accumulates all the more.

[On "Therefore 'its going must not be pursued' means this"] It has already gone with the flow of the meridian vessels and cannot be pursued and called back.

[On "'Not [even] a hair can be hung' means waiting for the moment the pathogen arrives and releasing the needle to drain"] It says that even what is light and subtle is still known; how much more something like surging waves — how could one not know its arrival?

[On "if too early or too late ... the illness cannot be brought down"] It means taking when it should not be taken and missing the time.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition reads "the blood and qi are already deficient"; 尽 should be 虚, a scribal error.

[On "Therefore it is said, 'Knowing when it can be taken ...' means this"] The trigger is the subtlety of movement; it says that knowing the subtle is what is valued.

## Notes

Pathogen comes and goes in the meridians like waves, so one must judge the moment of its arrival to take it; mistiming harms the genuine qi.
- "Do not meet its surge and drain it": Wang Bing takes 衝 as the normal meridian qi that becomes abundant according to the hour, and says it must not be mistaken for pathogen and drained.
- The phrases introduced by 故曰 and set in quotation marks in the translation are presumably quotations from an older needling classic (similar passages occur in the *Jiuzhen shi'er yuan* and *Xiaozhen jie* chapters of the *Lingshu*).
- 椎 in 扣椎 means a mallet; in contrast with the trigger of a crossbow, it is understood as a figure for sluggish response.
