# 素問 六元正紀大論篇第七十一 §124 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Where *jueyin* arrives there is contraction and twisting; where lesser yin arrives, sorrow, wild speech, nosebleed and foul blood; where greater yin arrives, central fullness, cholera with vomiting and diarrhea; where lesser yang arrives, throat impediment, ringing in the ears and gushing vomiting; where *yangming* arrives, chapping and peeling; where greater yang arrives, sleep sweating and convulsions. This is the constancy of illness.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "where lesser yin arrives, sorrow, wild speech, nosebleed and foul blood"] 衊 is foul blood; it also means grease.

[On "where lesser yang arrives, throat impediment, ringing in the ears and gushing vomiting"] 涌 means overflowing, food not going down.

[On "where *yangming* arrives, chapping and peeling"] The skin of the body looks like 䴸.

[On "where greater yang arrives, sleep sweating and convulsions"] Sleep sweating means sweat breaking out on the chest during sleep and overflowing to the neck and armpits. It is commonly and wrongly called "thief sweating".

## Notes

The eleventh of the twelve changes: the constancy of illness.
- Unlike the other phrases, 陽明所至皴掲 in the main text lacks 為.
- 䴸 in Wang Bing's note seems to be a variant of 麩 ("bran"), describing skin peeling like bran, but this has not been verified.
