# 素問 六元正紀大論篇第七十一 §117 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Where *jueyin* arrives, wind arises, and in the end there is stillness; where lesser yin arrives, heat arises, and in the middle there is cold; where greater yin arrives, damp arises, and in the end there is pouring rain; where lesser yang arrives, fire arises, and in the end there is steaming humidity; where *yangming* arrives, dryness arises, and in the end there is coolness; where greater yang arrives, cold arises, and in the middle there is warmth. This is the constancy of the transformations of virtue.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Where *jueyin* arrives, wind arises, and in the end there is stillness"] When [things] arise through the transformation of wind, wind arises. 粛 means still.
Song editors: According to the *Liuwei zhi dalun*, "below the position of wind, metal qi receives it". Hence with *jueyin* wind arises and in the end there is stillness.

[On "where lesser yin arrives, heat arises, and in the middle there is cold"] When [things] arise through the transformation of heat, heat arises. Yin essence receives what is above, so in the middle there is cold.
Song editors: According to the *Liuwei zhi dalun*, "above lesser yin, heat qi governs it, and in the middle greater yang appears". Hence heat arises and in the middle there is cold. It also says "below the sovereign's position, yin essence receives it", which also explains the cold.

[On "where greater yin arrives, damp arises, and in the end there is pouring rain"] When [things] arise through the transformation of damp, damp arises. Greater yin is above, so in the end there is pouring rain.
Song editors: According to the *Liuwei zhi dalun*, "below the position of earth, wind qi receives it", and Wang Bing's note says "after a gale, rain falls". Damp is blown by wind and transforms into rain. Hence with greater yin damp arises and in the end there is pouring rain.

[On "where lesser yang arrives, fire arises, and in the end there is steaming humidity"] When [things] arise through the transformation of fire, fire arises. Yang is above, so in the end there is steaming humidity.
Song editors: According to the *Liuwei zhi dalun*, "below ministerial fire, water qi receives it". Hence with lesser yang fire arises and in the end there is steaming humidity.

[On "where *yangming* arrives, dryness arises, and in the end there is coolness"] When [things] arise through the transformation of dryness, dryness arises. Yin is above, so in the end there is coolness.
Song editors: On examination, for each of these six qi the native transformation is stated first and then the opposing qi; only for *yangming* does it say "dryness arises, and in the end there is coolness", where no opposing qi is seen. Examining the sense of the passages above and below, it should say "where *yangming* arrives, coolness arises, and in the end there is dryness"; only then is it consistent with the other qi. Presumably, since below the position of metal fire qi receives it, with *yangming* clarity arises and in the end there is dryness.

[On "where greater yang arrives, cold arises, and in the middle there is warmth"] When [things] arise through the transformation of cold, cold arises. Yang is within, so in the middle there is warmth.
Song editors: According to the *Wuyun xing dalun*, "above greater yang, cold qi governs it, and in the middle lesser yin appears". Hence cold arises and in the middle there is warmth.

[On "This is the constancy of the transformations of virtue"] Wind generates hairy forms, heat generates winged forms, damp generates naked forms, fire generates feathered forms, dryness generates shelled forms, cold generates scaled forms. The six transformations, wherever they are the year's governing qi or intervening qi, each transform and generate constantly without fail. Without the transformation of virtue nothing could transform and generate.

## Notes

The fourth of the twelve changes: the constancy of the transformations of virtue.
- The passage the Song editors quote in the sixth note as from the *Wuyun xing dalun* (太陽之上，寒氣治之，中見少陰) is not in chapter 67 (*Wuyun xing dalun*) but in chapter 68 (*Liuwei zhi dalun*); the chapter name given by the Song editors is presumably mistaken.
