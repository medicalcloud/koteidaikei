# 素問 六元正紀大論篇第七十一 §93 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In the outbreak of fire constraint, the great void is swollen and clouded, the great brightness is not manifest, blazing fire prevails, great heat arrives, mountains and marshes burn, timber oozes sap, smoke rises from great halls, frost-like saline crust floats on the soil, standing water diminishes, creeping plants are scorched yellow, wind prevails with confusing talk, and the transformation of damp comes late. Hence people suffer shortage of qi, sores, abscesses and swellings, distension and bloating of the rib-sides, abdomen, chest, back, face, head and four limbs, sores and prickly heat, retching counterflow, tugging and slackening, bone pain, movement in the joints, pouring diarrhea, warm malaria, sudden pain in the abdomen, overflowing and streaming of blood, scantiness of essential fluids, red eyes and heat in the heart, and in severe cases visual dimness and oppression, anguish, and frequent sudden death. Great warmth at the end of the [night's] clepsydra marks, with sweat soaking the dark mansions: this is its outbreak, which comes in the fourth qi. When movement returns it becomes still; when yang reaches its extreme it reverts to yin, and the decree of damp then transforms and completes. Water freezing when flowers bloom, ice and snow on mountains and rivers, yang blazing over the marshes at noon: these are the forewarning signs of the pent-up [qi]. There is first a response of the pent-up [qi] and afterwards the requital; in every case, observe its extreme, and then it breaks out. The outbreak of wood has no fixed time; [that of] water follows fire. Carefully watch for its time and the illness can be anticipated; if the time is missed and the year contravened, the five qi do not proceed, and the governance of generation, transformation, gathering and storing has no constancy."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In the outbreak of fire constraint, the great void is swollen and clouded, the great brightness is not manifest"] "Swollen and clouded" means red qi. The great brightness is the sun.
Song editors: On examination, 腫 ("swollen") in the classic and the note is perhaps an error.

[On "creeping plants are scorched yellow, wind prevails with confusing talk, and the transformation of damp comes late"] Greater yin and greater yang are above, cold and damp flow in the great void, and heart fire, corresponding to heaven, is repressed and cannot become manifest. When the abundance of cold and damp has ended, fire then moves along with it, and yang qi becomes the light of fire; hence "marshes burn". Well water diminishes and false rumors are invented. The rain has already missed its time. "The transformation of damp comes late" means that yang is excessive and governs the season, and [damp] qi does not contend for the growing season, so there is first drought and then rain.

[On "red eyes and heat in the heart, and in severe cases visual dimness and oppression, anguish, and frequent sudden death"] Fire is constrained and angry because it holds out against earth and water, and this is so for both guest and host. If none of them invades deeply, there is no harm. But when heat has already overcome cold, it crushes the enemy and heat rises from the heart. Then the spirit qi is isolated and endangered; if not rescued quickly, the heavenly true [qi] is about to be exhausted, hence death. Since fire acts quickly, there is frequent sudden death.

[On "Great warmth at the end of the clepsydra marks ... which comes in the fourth qi"] The end of the marks means the time when the water marks of the day-and-night clepsydra come to an end. Great warmth is next to heat. The dark mansions are the sweat pores. "Sweat soaking the dark mansions" means that when going out early in the morning the body is steamed and hot. At the time when the marks end, yin is abundant, yet on the contrary there is no cool qi; this is yin not overcoming yang, and since heat has already 巳 [已] sprouted, it must break out in anger.
Song editors: On examination, why do the two fires both break out in the fourth qi? Presumably fire has two positions, which are where water breaks out; also great heat breaks out in *shen* and *wei*. Hence the outbreak of fire constraint is in the fourth qi.

[On "When movement returns it becomes still ... the decree of damp then transforms and completes"] Fire in anger melts metal; when yang reaches its extreme and becomes excessive, [metal], fearing fire, seeks rescue in earth, and earth rescues it from heat. Metal breaks out as whirlwinds and squalls, followed by timely rain, and the qi becomes harmonious and balanced. Thus the myriad things are born, grow, transform and complete. When vigor reaches its extreme it reverts; however abundant, how could it last long?

[On "Water freezing when flowers bloom ... these are the forewarning signs of the pent-up [qi]"] This means that cold sometimes arrives when sovereign fire is flourishing. Hence the outbreak of the year's sovereign fire also waits for its time.

[On "There is first a response ... [that of] water follows fire"] The response is the forewarning sign; the outbreak always comes later. Hence there is first a response and then the outbreak. Things cannot remain vigorous to the end; observe when their vigor reaches its extreme, and the pent-up qi arises. Where there is constraint there is outbreak; this is the constancy of qi.

[On "Carefully watch for its time ... has no constancy"] If people miss the time, the signs have no standard for anticipation.

## Notes

The outbreak of fire constraint, closing the account of the five outbreaks. Qibo's speech ends here.
- The Song editors' point seems to be that the two fire positions [the second and third qi] are the times of water's outbreak, while fire constraint breaks out in *shen* and *wei* when great heat breaks out, i.e., in the fourth qi; but this has not been verified.
