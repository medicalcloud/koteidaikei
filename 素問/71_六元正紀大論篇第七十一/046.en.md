# 素問 六元正紀大論篇第七十一 §46 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "When heaven's qi goes against the season, one may follow the 則 [season]; when it comes to overcome its governor, one may offend. Aim at balance and do not go beyond. This is called pathogenic qi prevailing contrarily.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When heaven's qi goes against the season, one may follow the season"] When the contrariness is severe and causes illness, one may follow the season.

[On "when it comes to overcome its governor, one may offend"] When summer heat is severe, one may offend heat with heat; when cold qi is not severe, one must not offend it.

[On "Aim at balance and do not go beyond"] When the qi is balanced, stop; going beyond produces illness, and illness produced by going beyond is the same as offending.

[On "This is called pathogenic qi prevailing contrarily"] When qi moves, something prevails; this is called the pathogen as guest overcoming the host, which must be resisted. Within the six positions, when the qi of the six steps is hot where it should be cold, cold where it should be hot, cool where it should be warm, warm where it should be cool, this is called pathogenic prevalence of the six steps. When winter is contrarily warm, summer contrarily cold, autumn contrarily hot, spring contrarily cool, this is called pathogenic prevalence of the four seasons. When it prevails, balance it by going against its qi.

## Notes

When heaven's qi goes against the season or overcomes the host qi, one may offend, but only until balance is reached. Qibo's speech continues into unit 047.
- For 則 in 可依則, other sources have 時 (see the variants), and Wang Bing also says 可依時; the translation reads 時.
- Wang Bing's "when summer heat is severe, one may offend heat with heat" might be expected to concern cold from the context, but this has not been checked; the translation follows the characters.
