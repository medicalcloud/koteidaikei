# 素問 六元正紀大論篇第七十一 §89 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "In the outbreak of earth constraint, cliffs and valleys quake in alarm, thunder rumbles where the qi meet, dust grows dark yellow-black and transforms into white qi, whirlwinds and squalls reach high and deep, striking stones and sending them flying into the air; floods follow, rivers overflow and spread, and in the fields lie earthen colts as if at pasture. Then the transforming qi spreads out, timely rain falls well, and [things] first grow, first mature, first transform and first complete. Hence people suffer distension of the heart and abdomen, rumbling of the intestines with frequent stools, and in severe cases heart pain and rib-side distension, vomiting and cholera, outbreak of fluid with pouring diarrhea, puffy swelling and heaviness of the body. Clouds rushing to the rain-store, rosy clouds embracing the morning sun, mountains and marshes darkened with dust: this is its outbreak, which comes in the fourth qi. Clouds lying across heaven and the mountains, drifting, appearing and vanishing: these are the forewarning signs of the pent-up [qi].

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In the outbreak of earth constraint ... whirlwinds and squalls reach high and deep"] Constraint means an extreme repression of heaven's qi. Hence even heaven's qi has limits: when its portion ends it declines, and so the constrained breaks out in anger. When earth's transformation does not proceed, blazing heat is excessive with no rain, and wood flourishes beyond its extreme, so [earth], constrained and angry, breaks out. Earth's nature is still and settled, but when it comes to move, thunder and rain arise greatly and the qi by which wood and earth held each other is then released. The *Changes* says "thunder and rain arise: release"; this is what it means. Though earth alone is angry, wood still restrains it, so it only quakes in alarm within the meeting of qi and its sound cannot yet reach high and far. Hence "thunder rumbles where the qi meet". The meeting of qi means above the earth, up to the height of the mountains. The *Odes* says "Rumbling is the thunder"; this is the so-called thunder and rain arising in the mountains. When earth is repressed and heaven's wood restrains it, on flat river land the soil is thin and the qi always dry, so it cannot break out first; on mountains and plateaus the soil is thick and the transformation of damp abundant and deep; because the soil is thick and the qi deep, it breaks out in anger first.

[On "striking stones and sending them flying ... earthen colts as if at pasture"] With swift qi and squalls, banks collapse and mountains transform, great waters flow across, stones burst out with urgent force. In high mountains and empty valleys, stones are struck and fly first, and the flood follows. 洪 means great. Great rivers overflow and spread over the flat land, sweeping away and burying the grain. When the great water has gone, stones and earth stand precariously like a herd of colts scattered at pasture in the fields. Wherever "earth" is said, sand and stones are included.

[On "Then the transforming qi spreads out ... first complete"] Transformation is earth's transformation. When earth is restrained, the transforming qi does not spread. When obstruction is extreme there is peace; when bending is extreme there is stretching. At the time of being pent up, the transforming qi is thereby able to spread to all kinds of things, raining in season and moistening the plants to completion. 善 ("well") means harmonious and in accord with the season. Since the transforming qi is already scant and the qi of growth already past, the myriad things first grow, first mature, first transform and first complete; these four "first"s make clear that the transformation and completion of things is late.

[On "vomiting and cholera, outbreak of fluid with pouring diarrhea, puffy swelling and heaviness of the body"] This is the arising of spleen heat.

[On "Clouds rushing to the rain-store ... which comes in the fourth qi"] The rain-store is where greater yin resides. Dust is white qi, like cloud but thinner. Dust has slight and severe forms: the slight is like rising gauze, the severe like thin cloud and mist. The severe breaks out nearby, the slight far away. The fourth qi is the period beginning thirty-one days after the summer solstice and ending on the autumn equinox.

[On "Clouds lying across heaven and the mountains, drifting, appearing and vanishing: these are the forewarning signs of the pent-up [qi]"] Clouds lie across the edge of the sky, the mountains seem to wear caps and sashes, and in the cliffs, valleys and thickets [clouds] suddenly vanish and appear. Where earth shows this, the sign of pent-up qi is already evident. All are divined at dawn; the drifting is watched for before noon.

## Notes

The outbreak of earth constraint, the first of the five. The description gives the natural phenomena, people's illnesses, the time of outbreak and the forewarning signs; metal, water, wood and fire follow the same pattern. Qibo's speech continues through unit 93.
- 天気 ("heaven's qi") in Wang Bing's note seems from the context to refer to earth qi, but this has not been verified.
