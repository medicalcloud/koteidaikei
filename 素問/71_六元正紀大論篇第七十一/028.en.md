# 素問 六元正紀大論篇第七十一 §28 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "How thorough the question! This is a clear way. The count begins above and ends below. Before the middle of the year, heaven's qi governs; after the middle of the year, earth's qi governs; where above and below interchange, the intersection of qi governs; and the record of the year is complete. Hence it is said: when the positions are clear, can the qi and months not be known? This is what is called qi."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Before the middle of the year, heaven's qi governs; after the middle of the year, earth's qi governs"] The middle of the year means the day of the Beginning of Autumn.
Song editors: On examination, the exchange of the first qi takes place on the day of Great Cold of the previous year; the middle of the year should be one qi, fifteen days, before the Beginning of Autumn; it cannot be said to be the day of the Beginning of Autumn.

[On "where above and below interchange, the intersection of qi governs; and the record of the year is complete"] 交互 means interlocking bodies: between the upper body and the lower body there are two interlocking bodies.

[On "Hence it is said: when the positions are clear, can the qi and months not be known? This is what is called qi"] Generally one qi governs sixty-odd days. Establishing the positions and counting them, when a position coincides with a qi, the nodal and central solar periods of the month can be known. Hence speaking of the qi of heaven and earth, one speaks by the upper and lower bodies; speaking of prevalence and retaliation, by the intersection of qi; speaking of horizontal motion, by the upper and lower interlocking bodies; all are measured by the solar periods; observing them, disasters, changes and retaliations can be anticipated.

## Notes

The first half of the year is governed by the controller of heaven, the second half by the spring in earth, and where they interchange, by the intersection of qi.
- 故曰位明気月可知乎所謂気也 is hard to punctuate; the translation draws on Wang Bing's note.
