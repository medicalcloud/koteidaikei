# 素問 六元正紀大論篇第七十一 §123 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Where *jueyin* arrives there is propping pain; where lesser yin arrives, alarm and confusion, aversion to cold and shivering, and delirious speech; where greater yin arrives, accumulation and fullness; where lesser yang arrives, alarm and agitation, visual dimness, and sudden illness; where *yangming* arrives, stuffy nose, and illness of the buttocks, genitals, thighs, knees, upper legs, calves, shins and feet; where greater yang arrives, lumbar pain. This is the constancy of illness.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Where *jueyin* arrives there is propping pain"] 支 means bracing and obstructing.

[On "where lesser yin arrives, alarm and confusion, aversion to cold and shivering, and delirious speech"] 譫 means disordered speech. Now on examination, the character 慓 should be written 慓.

## Notes

The tenth of the twelve changes: the constancy of illness.
- 今詳慓字当作慓字 in Wang Bing's note repeats the same character and makes no sense. The main text has 慄. One of the characters is presumably erroneous, but this has not been verified. Also 今詳 resembles the Song editors' phrasing, but in the base text's transcription it stands within Wang Bing's note.
