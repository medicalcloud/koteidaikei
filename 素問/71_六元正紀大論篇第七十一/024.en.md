# 素問 六元正紀大論篇第七十一 §24 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "How illuminating the question! The six qi move in order and stop in their positions. So always observe them at dawn on the first day of the first month; seeing their positions, one knows where they are. When the movement is in surplus, its arrival is early; when the movement is deficient, its arrival is late. This is the way of heaven and the constancy of qi. When the movement is neither in surplus nor deficient, it is called a proper year, and its arrival coincides with its time."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "So always observe them at dawn ... one knows where they are"] Where yin is, heaven responds with clouds; where yang is, heaven responds with clarity and purity. They are naturally distributed, and the images appear without error.

[On "When the movement is in surplus, its arrival is early; when the movement is deficient, its arrival is late"] Early and late are both relative to the hour of *yin*: early is after *chou*, late is the beginning of *mao*.

[On "This is the way of heaven and the constancy of qi"] The way of heaven is plain; at the due time it surely responds visibly without error; this is the constancy of qi.

[On "it is called a proper year, and its arrival coincides with its time"] Coinciding with its time means coinciding with the middle of *yin*.

## Notes

Observing the heavens at dawn on New Year's Day to know where the six qi are, and the earliness or lateness of their arrival according to the excess or deficiency of the movement.
