# 素問 六元正紀大論篇第七十一 §120 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Where *jueyin* arrives there are whirling, raging winds, and great coolness; where lesser yin arrives, great warmth, and cold; where greater yin arrives, thunder and downpours, and fierce wind; where lesser yang arrives, whirlwinds and burning, and congealed frost; where *yangming* arrives, scattering and falling, and warmth; where greater yang arrives, cold snow, ice and hail, and white dust. This is the constancy of the changes of qi.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Where *jueyin* arrives there are whirling, raging winds, and great coolness"] Whirling, raging winds are wood; great coolness is the metal qi that receives it from below.

[On "where lesser yin arrives, great warmth, and cold"] Great warmth is sovereign fire; cold is the yin essence that receives it from below.

[On "where greater yin arrives, thunder and downpours, and fierce wind"] Thunder and downpours are earth; fierce wind is the water [wood] qi that receives it from below.

[On "where lesser yang arrives, whirlwinds and burning, and congealed frost"] 飄風 is a whirling wind; congealed frost is the water qi that receives it from below.

[On "where *yangming* arrives, scattering and falling, and warmth"] Scattering and falling are metal; warmth is the fire qi that receives it from below.

[On "where greater yang arrives, cold snow, ice and hail, and white dust"] Frost, snow, ice and hail are water; white dust is the earth qi that receives it from below.

[On "This is the constancy of the changes of qi"] Change means altering the constant, balanced qi into excessive function. When the function is excessive and does not stop, the receiving qi below acts as well; hence none of these is the native qi.

## Notes

The seventh of the twelve changes: the constancy of the changes of qi. These are concrete examples of "the severe includes what is below it" in unit 95.
- In the note on greater yin, 水 ("water") in "fierce wind is the water qi that receives it from below" is presumably an error for 木 ("wood"), judging from Wang Bing's note in unit 95 (below earth, wood qi receives it); but the Siku quanshu edition (Daizhige *Huangdi neijing suwen*) also has 水, and this has not been verified.
