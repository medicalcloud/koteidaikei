# 素問 六元正紀大論篇第七十一 §92 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In the outbreak of wood constraint, the great void is darkened with dust, clouds and things are thrown into turmoil, great wind arrives, roofs are torn open and trees broken, and trees undergo changes. Hence people suffer pain in the stomach duct right at the heart, propping up both rib-sides, blockage of the diaphragm and throat so that food and drink do not go down, and in severe cases ringing in the ears and spinning dizziness, eyes that do not recognize people, and frequent sudden rigid collapse. The great void with blue-green dust, heaven and mountains one color, or the qi turbid and of a gloomy yellow-black, lateral clouds that do not bring rain: then it breaks out; its qi has no fixed time. Grasses lying flat along long rivers, tender leaves showing their undersides, pines humming on high mountains, tigers roaring on the crags: these are the forewarning signs of the pent-up [qi].

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "great wind arrives, roofs are torn open and trees broken, and trees undergo changes"] "Roofs torn open" means the owl-tail ridge ornaments are blown off. "Trees broken" means great trees are smashed and uprooted, snapped and fallen, 懸辛中拉 [meaning unclear]. "Changes" means the earth produces strange trees and odd shapes.

[On "in severe cases ringing in the ears and spinning dizziness, eyes that do not recognize people, and frequent sudden rigid collapse"] The sinews and bones are rigid and useless; one falls suddenly and knows nothing.

[On "lateral clouds that do not bring rain: then it breaks out; its qi has no fixed time"] Qi like dust or cloud, or a gloomy yellow-black still in the great void, distinctly different from the usual: this is its sign.

[On "Grasses lying flat along long rivers ... these are the forewarning signs of the pent-up [qi]"] "Grasses lying flat" means they bend low of themselves without wind. "Tender leaves" means the leaves of the white poplar. Without wind the leaves all show their backs; this is called "showing their undersides". All these apply to slight and severe cases: the severe breaks out quickly, the slight slowly. When travelling in the mountains, one expects it by the pines and tigers; when travelling on the plains, one also takes ephedra as the sign; in autumn and winter one watches the leaves of the paulownia and cicadas.

## Notes

The outbreak of wood constraint.
- 懸辛中拉 in Wang Bing's note does not make sense; there may be a textual error, but this has not been verified.
