# 素問 六元正紀大論篇第七十一 §90 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In the outbreak of metal constraint, heaven is pure and earth bright, the wind is clear and the qi sharp, great coolness takes charge, smoke drifts over grasses and trees, dry qi prevails, haze and mist arise frequently, killing qi arrives, plants turn pale and dry, and metal then has a sound. Hence people suffer coughing counterflow, fullness of the heart and rib-sides pulling on the lower abdomen, frequent sudden pain so that they cannot turn over, dryness of the throat, and a dusty face of bad color. Mountains and marshes scorched and withered, the soil congealed with frost-like saline crust: this is the outbreak of the pent-up [qi], which comes in the fifth qi. White dew falling at night and a mournful sound in the forests and thickets: these are the signs of the pent-up [qi].

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "killing qi arrives, plants turn pale and dry, and metal then has a sound"] Great coolness is next to cold. 挙 means taking charge. Drifting smoke is dry qi. Killing qi is the vapor of frost. True killing qi arrives at the *chou* hour; when long, it comes also at the *mao* and *chen* hours. When this qi comes, it arrives in mixed colors of yellow, red and black. Things cannot withstand killing, so plants turn pale and dry. 蒼 is a pale blue-green color.

[On "frequent sudden pain so that they cannot turn over, dryness of the throat, and a dusty face of bad color"] Metal prevails and wood falls ill.

[On "Mountains and marshes scorched and withered ... which comes in the fifth qi"] Summer fire blazes excessively and timely rain has failed, so mountains and marshes are scorched and withered, and white saline crust congeals on the soil, looking like frost. The fifth qi is the period from after the autumn equinox to within fifteen days after the beginning of winter.

[On "White dew falling at night and a mournful sound in the forests and thickets: these are the signs of the pent-up [qi]"] White dew moistens at night and a mournful wind is heard at dawn; when these occur, they are the signs of metal's outbreak.
