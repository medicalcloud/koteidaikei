# 素問 六元正紀大論篇第七十一 §91 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In the outbreak of water constraint, yang qi withdraws, yin qi rises violently, great cold arrives, rivers and marshes freeze hard, cold vapor condenses into frost and snow, and in severe cases a dark, murky yellow-black [qi] moves where the qi meet, frost kills, and water shows omens. Hence people suffer cold lodging with heart pain, pain of the lower back and buttocks, stiffness of the large joints with difficulty bending and stretching, frequent reversal counterflow, glomus with hardness and abdominal fullness. When the sunlight does not govern, deep yin accumulates in the sky and white dust grows dark and gloomy, then it breaks out; it comes before and after the two fires. The great void deep and dark, qi like scattered hemp, faintly seen and then hidden, black in color and faintly yellow: these are the forewarning signs of the pent-up [qi].

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "great cold arrives, rivers and marshes freeze hard, cold vapor condenses into frost and snow"] 雰 is pronounced *fen*. Cold vapor is white qi. It looks like mist but does not move; falling to the ground it is like frost and snow, and dries when it gets the sun.

[On "moves where the qi meet, frost kills, and water shows omens"] The yellow-black is also turbid, foul qi, the qi of water. 祥 means ominous signs; it also refers to springs emerging on flat ground.

[On "stiffness of the large joints ... glomus with hardness and abdominal fullness"] Because yin overcomes yang.

[On "white dust grows dark and gloomy, then it breaks out; it comes before and after the two fires"] Yin essence and water both rise to receive fire, so their outbreak is before and after the two fires, sovereign and ministerial, just as the Chen star [Mercury] goes to meet and follows the sun.

[On "faintly seen and then hidden, black in color and faintly yellow: these are the forewarning signs of the pent-up [qi]"] Deep and dark means high, far and dusky black. The qi is like scattered hemp, thin and faintly visible. It is watched for at the *mao* hour after *yin*; in the summer months it may also be watched for in the time before *chen*.

## Notes

The outbreak of water constraint.
