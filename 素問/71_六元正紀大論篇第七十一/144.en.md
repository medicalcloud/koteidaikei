# 素問 六元正紀大論篇第七十一 §144 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "If one does not keep away from heat, heat arrives; if one does not keep away from cold, cold arrives. When cold arrives, illnesses of hard glomus, abdominal fullness, pain and tension, and diarrhea arise. When heat arrives, illnesses of body heat, vomiting and diarrhea with cholera, abscesses, ulcers and sores, visual dimness and oppression with pouring diarrhea, twitching and spasms, swelling and distension, retching, stuffy nose and nosebleed, headache, changes in the bones and joints, pain of the flesh, overflowing and discharge of blood, and dribbling and blocked urination arise."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When cold arrives, illnesses of hard glomus ... and diarrhea arise"] Not being hungry after eating, vomiting and diarrhea that are rank and foul, are also diseases of cold.

[On "pain of the flesh, overflowing and discharge of blood, and dribbling and blocked urination arise"] Sudden loss of voice, clouding, eyes that do not recognize people, agitation and mania, seeing and hearing things falsely, cursing, and fright epilepsy are also illnesses of heat.

## Notes

The illnesses produced by offending with cold and heat.
