# 素問 骨空論篇第六十 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

For hobbled knees that stretch but do not bend, treat the *jian*. For knee pain when sitting, treat the *ji*. For heat in the joint when standing, treat the *haiguan*. For knee pain reaching the big toe, treat the popliteal fossa. For knee pain when sitting, as though something were hidden there, treat the *guan*. For knee pain preventing bending and stretching, treat the inside of the back. When it connects to the shin as if broken, treat the *shu-liao* in the *yangming*. If it is as though separated, treat the *ying* points of the greater yang and lesser yin. For aching weakness with sore shins and inability to stand long, treat the *wei* of the lesser yang, five [*cun*] above the outer [ankle].

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "For hobbled knees that stretch but do not bend, treat the *jian*"] Hobbled knees means knee pain with difficult bending and stretching. *Jian* means the middle of the outer thigh, above the auxiliary bone of the thigh and below the transverse bone. Have [the patient] stand sideways and shake it; the sinew moves under the hand.

[On "For knee pain when sitting, treat the *ji*"] Where the two sides of the hip bone meet.

[On "For heat in the joint when standing, treat the *haiguan*"] 暑 means heat. If the knee hurts and on standing there is heat in the knee joint, treat the *haiguan*; *haiguan* means the knee joint. One classic has 起而引解, meaning that with knee pain, on rising, the pain draws into the knee joint. The two characters 暑 and 引 differ in meaning; the two characters 起 and 立 are much the same.

[On "For knee pain reaching the big toe, treat the popliteal fossa"] The popliteal fossa means behind the knee joint, in the fold of the bent leg, the Weizhong point. Take it with the patient facing away; the pulse is felt under the hand. It is where the foot greater yang vessel enters; one may needle five body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "For knee pain when sitting, as though something were hidden there, treat the *guan*"] The *guan* is above the popliteal fossa, behind the *jian*. Have [the patient] stand with the back turned, press it and shake; the sinew responds under the hand.

[On "For knee pain preventing bending and stretching, treat the inside of the back"] This means the Dazhu point; location, needling, moxibustion, *fen* and cones follow the *Qixue lun*.

[On "When it connects to the shin as if broken, treat the *shu-liao* in the *yangming*"] If the knee hurts and cannot bend or stretch, and the pain connects to the shin as if broken, needle the *shu-liao* in the *yangming* vessel; this is precisely taking the Sanli point.

[On "If it is as though separated, treat the *ying* points of the greater yang and lesser yin"] If it hurts and the knee feels as though separated, treat the *ying* points of the foot greater yang and lesser yin. The *ying* point of the foot greater yang is Tonggu, in the depression in front of the base joint on the outer side of the little toe; one may needle two body-*fen* and retain five breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. The *ying* point of the foot lesser yin is Rangu, in the depression below the large raised bone in front of the inner ankle; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "For aching weakness with sore shins and inability to stand long, treat the *wei* of the lesser yang"] Song editors: According to the *Jiayi jing*, five [*cun*] above the outer ankle is the network point of the foot lesser yang; *wei* here is a scribal error.

[On "five [*cun*] above the outer [ankle]"] 淫濼 means something like aching pain without strength. Three *cun*; one version says four *cun*. In the *Zhonggao tujing* there is no point four *cun* above the outer ankle; five *cun* is the Guangming point, the network point of the foot lesser yang; one may needle seven body-*fen* and retain ten breaths; for moxibustion, five cones.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* says "needle six *fen*, retain seven breaths".

## Notes

Points for each kind of knee pain. The place names *jian*, *ji*, *haiguan*, *guan* and so on are defined in the next unit.
- Wang Bing's "three *cun*; one version says four *cun*" does not match the main text's "five *cun*"; the text Wang saw may have read "three *cun*", but this has not been checked.
- 在外上五寸 means above the outer ankle; Wikisource has the character 踝 (see the variants).
