# 素問 骨空論篇第六十 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In great wind with pain in the neck and nape, needle Fengfu; Fengfu is at the upper vertebra. In great wind with sweating, apply moxibustion to Yixi; Yixi is on the back below, alongside the spine about three *cun* to the side; press it and have the patient call out "yixi", and Yixi responds under the hand. For aversion to wind when exposed to wind, needle the head of the eyebrow. Stiff neck: it is between the transverse bones above the shoulder. For the bending, have [the patient] shake the arm, line up the elbows properly, and apply moxibustion in the middle of the spine. When the network vessels of the soft flank draw on the lower ribs and the lower abdomen with pain and distension, needle Yixi. When lumbar pain prevents turning and swaying and draws sharply to the testicles, needle the eight *liao* and above the painful place; the eight *liao* are in the divisions of the lumbus and buttocks.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In great wind with pain in the neck and nape ... at the upper vertebra"] The upper vertebra means above Dazhui, one body-*cun* inside the hairline.

[On "In great wind with sweating ... responds under the hand"] This is the Yixi point, on the inner edge of the shoulder blade, three body-*cun* to each side below the sixth vertebra. When one presses it with the hand and has the patient utter the sound "yixi", it moves under the finger. It is where the vessel qi of the foot greater yang issues; one may needle six body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, five cones. "Yixi" is named from this.

[On "For aversion to wind when exposed to wind, needle the head of the eyebrow"] This means the Cuanzhu point, in the depression at the head of the eyebrow where the pulse is felt, where the vessel qi of the foot greater yang issues; one may needle three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "Stiff neck: it is between the transverse bones above the shoulder"] This means the Quepen point, in the depression of the transverse bone above the shoulder, where the vessel qi of the hand *yangming* issues; one may needle two body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones; deep needling makes the breath counterflow.
Song editors: The note to the *Qifu lun* writes "foot *yangming*", here "hand *yangming*"; on examination, both channels issue here, so Wang's notes mention both.

[On "For the bending, have [the patient] shake the arm ... in the middle of the spine"] 楡 is read as 揺 "shake". For stiff neck one should not only take between the transverse bones above the shoulder but also straighten the body and apply moxibustion in the middle of the spine. To find it, have [the patient] shake the arm and bend the elbows; level with the tips of the elbows below the nape, the point in the middle is the place. It is called Yangguan, in the space below the sixteenth vertebra, where the qi of the *du* vessel issues; one may needle five body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: On examination, the Yangguan point is not in the *Jiayi jing*.

[On "When the network vessels of the soft flank ... needle Yixi"] 䏚 means the empty, soft places on both sides of the spine. The lower abdomen means below the navel.

[On "When lumbar pain prevents turning ... the divisions of the lumbus and buttocks"] 八 is sometimes written 九. Checking the actual bones and the *Zhonggao kongxue jing*, there are indeed eight *liao* and no nine *liao*. 分 means the sunken spaces between the divisions of muscle of the lumbus and buttocks.

## Notes

The points for great wind, stiff neck, lumbar pain and so on.
- 折，使楡臂，斉肘正，灸脊中 is obscure; Wang Bing takes it as continuing the treatment of stiff neck and identifies the point as Yangguan. Others read 折 as a separate illness (pain as if the lumbus were broken). The translation stays close to the characters.
- 問 in Wang Bing's 横骨問 is presumably an error for 間.
