# 素問 刺要論篇第五十 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Therefore it is said: illness may be in the fine hair and interstices, in the skin, in the flesh, in the vessels, in the sinews, in the bones, or in the marrow. Thus in needling the fine hair and interstices, do not injure the skin; if the skin is injured, the lung is stirred within, and when the lung is stirred, in autumn there is warm malaria with shivering and chills. In needling the skin, do not injure the flesh; if the flesh is injured, the spleen is stirred within, and when the spleen is stirred, in the seventy-two days of the four seasonal months there are abdominal distension, vexation and loss of appetite. In needling the flesh, do not injure the vessels; if the vessels are injured, the heart is stirred within, and when the heart is stirred, in summer there is heart pain. In needling the vessels, do not injure the sinews; if the sinews are injured, the liver is stirred within, and when the liver is stirred, in spring there are heat and slack sinews. In needling the sinews, do not injure the bones; if the bones are injured, the kidney is stirred within, and when the kidney is stirred, in winter there are distension and lumbar pain. In needling the bones, do not injure the marrow; if the marrow is injured, it wastes and melts, the shins ache, and the body is languid without relief."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Therefore it is said: illness may be in the fine hair ... or in the marrow"] Long hairs are called 毫; the lines of the skin are called 腠理. Both are visible parts of the skin.

[On "Thus in needling the fine hair and interstices ... shivering and chills"] The *Zhenjing* says, "There are five kinds of needling corresponding to the five zang. The first is half-needling: insert shallowly and withdraw quickly, making many needle marks like pulling out hairs, to take the qi of the skin; this corresponds to the lung." This is shallow and corresponds to the lung; the interstices and fine hair correspond still more shallowly, and one should take half the depth of the hair roots. The lung corresponds to the skin and dominates in the autumn qi; therefore when the lung is stirred, in autumn there is warm malaria with shivering and chills.

[On "In needling the skin, do not injure the flesh ... loss of appetite"] The spleen corresponds to the flesh and dominates lodged in the four seasons. Also, its vessel enters the abdomen from the anterior edge of the inner thigh, belongs to the spleen and connects with the stomach, rises through the diaphragm alongside the throat, joins the root of the tongue and spreads below it; a branch goes again from the stomach up through the diaphragm and pours into the heart. Therefore injuring the flesh stirs the spleen, and when the spleen is stirred, in the four seasonal months there are abdominal distension, vexation and loss of appetite. "Seventy-two days of the four seasonal months" means the eighteen days in which earth dominates by lodging, after the twelfth day of each of the third, sixth, ninth and twelfth months.

[On "In needling the flesh, do not injure the vessels ... heart pain"] The heart corresponds to the vessels and dominates in the summer qi. The lesser yin vessel of the true heart arises in the heart and emerges to belong to the heart connector; the heart-master vessel of the pericardium arises in the chest and emerges to belong to the pericardium. The *Pingren qixiang lun* says, "The true qi of the zang communicates with the heart." Therefore injuring the vessels stirs the heart, and when the heart is stirred, in summer there is heart pain.

[On "In needling the vessels, do not injure the sinews ... slack sinews"] The liver corresponds to the sinews and dominates in the spring qi. The *Zhenjing* says, "With heat the sinews are slack." Therefore injuring the sinews stirs the liver, and when the liver is stirred, in spring there are heat and slack sinews. 弛 means slack.

[On "In needling the sinews, do not injure the bones ... lumbar pain"] The kidney corresponds to the bones and dominates in the winter qi. The lumbus is the house of the kidney; therefore injuring the bones stirs the kidney, and when the kidney is stirred, in winter there is lumbar pain. The straight course of the kidney vessel rises from the kidney through the liver and diaphragm; therefore there is distension.

[On "In needling the bones, do not injure the marrow ... without relief"] The marrow fills the bones. The *Zhenjing* says, "When the sea of marrow is insufficient, the brain spins, the ears ring, the shins ache, and there is dizziness." Therefore when the marrow is injured, the brain and marrow waste and melt, the shins ache, and the body is languid without relief. 銷鑠 means the marrow and brain waste and melt. 解㑊 means strong yet not strong, weak yet not weak, hot yet not hot, cold yet not cold — a languid state that cannot be named. The wasting of brain and marrow is caused by the emptiness of the bones.

## Notes

Needling should match the depth of the illness (fine hair, skin, flesh, vessels, sinews, bones, marrow) without injuring deeper layers; injury stirs the corresponding zang and causes illness in its season. This ends the chapter. Qibo's speech, begun in unit 002, ends here.
- 胸巾 in Wang Bing's note is an error for 胸中 "chest", and 㢘 is presumably a variant of 廉.
- The *Zhenjing* passages quoted by Wang Bing presumably correspond to the *Guanzhen* and *Hai lun* chapters of the *Lingshu*.
