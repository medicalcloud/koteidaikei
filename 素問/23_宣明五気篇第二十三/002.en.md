# 素問 宣明五気篇第二十三 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Where the five qi fall ill: the heart produces belching, the lung coughing, the liver talking, the spleen swallowing, the kidney yawning and sneezing, the stomach counterflow of qi, hiccup and fear; the large and small intestines produce diarrhoea; the lower burner overflows and produces water [swelling]; the bladder, when not free, produces retention of urine, and when not restrained, incontinence; the gallbladder produces anger. These are called the five illnesses.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "the heart produces belching"] It resembles fire blazing upward, smoke coming out with the flame. The heart does not accept filth; therefore it is expelled by belching.

[On "the lung coughing"] It resembles metal, hard and strong, which sounds when struck. Evil strikes the lung; hence coughing.

[On "the liver talking"] It resembles the branches of wood, whose form divides; talking expresses twists and turns; therefore it comes from the liver.

[On "the spleen swallowing"] It resembles earth, which contains things and takes them inward, gathering and receiving all; hence swallowing.

[On "the kidney yawning and sneezing"] It resembles water flowing down and rising as clouds and mist. Qi stagnates in the stomach, so yawning arises; when the greater yang qi is harmonious and free, fills the heart and comes out through the nose, sneezing arises.

[On "the stomach counterflow of qi, hiccup and fear"] [The stomach] is the sea of water and grain, and the kidney is its gate; when the gate is closed and not free, the qi counterflows upward. It contains water and grain and by nature likes to receive cold; cold and grain press on each other; hence hiccup. When cold is abundant, hiccup arises; when heat is abundant, fear arises. Why? When the stomach is hot, the kidney qi becomes weak; hence fear. The text below says, "When essential qi combines in the kidney, there is fear."

[On "the large and small intestines produce diarrhoea; the lower burner overflows and produces water"] The large intestine is the fu of conveyance and the small intestine the fu of reception. When the qi of reception is depleted, the office of conveyance cannot hold; hence diarrhoea. The lower burner is where [fluids] are divided and poured; when its qi is blocked and does not drain, it overflows and becomes water.

[On "the bladder, when not free, produces retention of urine, and when not restrained, incontinence"] The bladder is the fu of fluids, through which water pours. When the foot triple burner vessel is replete and restrains the lower burner so that it does not open, one cannot urinate; when the foot triple burner vessel is depleted and does not restrain the lower burner, there is incontinence. The *Lingshu jing* says, "The foot triple burner is a divergence of the greater yang; it runs alongside the main course of the greater yang, enters and connects with the bladder and restrains the lower burner. When replete, there is retention; when depleted, incontinence."

[On "the gallbladder produces anger"] It is central and upright and makes decisions, without partiality or bias; its nature is firm and decisive; hence anger. The *Liujie zangxiang lun* (chapter 9) says, "All eleven zang take their decisions from the gallbladder."

## Notes

The symptoms that appear when the qi of the five zang and six fu fall ill.
- 㱕 in Wang Bing's note is a variant of 帰, and 熖 of 焔.
- "The liver talking": Wang Bing likens talk that expresses twists and turns to the branching of wood.
