# 素問 宣明五気篇第二十三 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

What the five flavours prohibit: acrid goes to the qi, so in illness of the qi do not eat much acrid; salty goes to the blood, so in illness of the blood do not eat much salty; bitter goes to the bones, so in illness of the bones do not eat much bitter; sweet goes to the flesh, so in illness of the flesh do not eat much sweet; sour goes to the sinews, so in illness of the sinews do not eat much sour. These are called the five prohibitions; do not let [the patient] eat much.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "acrid goes to the qi, so in illness of the qi do not eat much acrid"] "Illness" means having little strength and being unable to overcome by oneself.

[On "bitter goes to the bones, so in illness of the bones do not eat much bitter"] Song editors: Huangfu Shi'an says, "Salty first goes to the kidney." That here it is said to go to the blood is because the kidney is paired with the triple burner, and the blood vessels, though belonging to the liver and heart, form the pathway of the middle burner; hence salty enters and goes to the blood. [He also says] bitter goes to the heart; that here it is said to go to the bones is because water and fire assist each other and the qi of the bones communicates with the heart.

[On "sour goes to the sinews, so in illness of the sinews do not eat much sour"] All these move their qi quickly; hence one should not eat much of them. Eating much aggravates the illness; therefore the sick should not eat much.

[On "These are called the five prohibitions; do not let [the patient] eat much"] Song editors: The five prohibitions in the *Taisu* say, "In liver illness prohibit acrid, in heart illness prohibit salty, in spleen illness prohibit sour, in lung illness prohibit bitter, in kidney illness prohibit sweet," and call these the five restraints. Yang Shangshan says, "Even if the mouth craves and wants to eat it, one must not take much; one must restrain oneself; hence they are called the five restraints."

## Notes

The regions to which each of the five flavours goes, and the rule not to eat much of the corresponding flavour when that region is ill.
- The view of Huangfu Shi'an (Huangfu Mi) quoted by the Song editors corresponds to 鹹先走腎 in fascicle 6 of the *Jiayi jing* (where the views of the *Suwen* and the *Jiujuan* are compared).
