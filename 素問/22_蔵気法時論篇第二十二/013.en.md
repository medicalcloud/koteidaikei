# 素問 蔵気法時論篇第二十二 §13 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In spleen illness, the body is heavy, the flesh is prone to wasting, the feet cannot be drawn in, walking is prone to spasm, and there is pain under the feet. When depleted, there are abdominal fullness, borborygmus, diarrhoea with undigested food, and food is not transformed. Take its channels, the greater yin and *yangming*, and [the vessels of] the lesser yin with blood.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In spleen illness, the body is heavy ... pain under the feet"] The spleen resembles earth and governs the flesh; hence the body is heavy and the flesh wastes. 痿 means withered and without strength. The spleen greater yin vessel arises at the tip of the big toe, follows the inner side of the toe, rises along the anterior edge of the inner ankle and up the inside of the calf. The kidney lesser yin vessel arises below the little toe, runs obliquely to the sole, rises up the inside of the calf and emerges at the inner edge of the knee crease. Therefore in illness the feet cannot be drawn in, walking is prone to spasm and there is pain under the feet; hence the lesser yin is taken below.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* reads "prone to hunger, the flesh wastes". The *Qianjin fang* says "prone to hunger, the feet waste and cannot be drawn in". The *Qijiao bian dalun* (chapter 69) says "the flesh withers, the feet waste and cannot be drawn in, walking is prone to spasm".

[On "When depleted, there are abdominal fullness ... food is not transformed"] The spleen greater yin vessel enters the abdomen from the anterior edge of the inner thigh, belongs to the spleen and connects with the stomach. Hence such illness. The *Lingshu jing* says, "When the middle qi is insufficient, the abdomen is prone to fullness and the intestines prone to rumbling."

[On "Take its channels, the greater yin and *yangming*, and [the vessels of] the lesser yin with blood"] The lesser yin is the kidney vessel. Because of the earlier illness of spasm in walking and pain under the feet, it is taken and bled; where the blood is full, let it out.

## Notes

Symptoms of repletion and depletion in spleen illness, and the needling.
- 善肌肉痿: according to the Song editors the *Jiayi jing* reads 善飢，肌肉痿 "prone to hunger, the flesh wastes"; Daizhige (plain text) also reads 善飢肉痿. The translation follows the base text.
