# 素問 蔵気法時論篇第二十二 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When illness is in the liver, it heals in summer; if it does not heal in summer, it worsens in autumn; if one does not die in autumn, it holds in winter and one rises in spring. Avoid exposure to wind. Liver illness heals on *bing* and *ding* [days]; if it does not heal on *bing* and *ding*, it intensifies on *geng* and *xin*; if one does not die on *geng* and *xin*, it holds on *ren* and *gui*, and one rises on *jia* and *yi*. Liver illness is clear at dawn, worse in the late afternoon and calm at midnight. The liver desires dispersal; quickly eat acrid things to disperse it. Use acridity to supplement it and sourness to drain it.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When illness is in the liver, it heals in summer"] The child controls its ghost; "heals" for the other [zang] is the same.

[On "if it does not heal in summer, it worsens in autumn"] The child rests and the ghost dominates again; "worsens" for the others is the same.

[On "if one does not die in autumn, it holds in winter"] The ghost rests and the mother nourishes; therefore the qi is held in the home of its parent; "holds" for the others is the same.

[On "and one rises in spring"] It obtains its own position, so it rises again; "rises" for the others is the same.

[On "Avoid exposure to wind"] Wind qi communicates with the liver; therefore it is prohibited and not to be incurred.

[On "Liver illness heals on *bing* and *ding*"] *Bing* and *ding* correspond to summer.

[On "if it does not heal on *bing* and *ding*, it intensifies on *geng* and *xin*"] *Geng* and *xin* correspond to autumn.

[On "if one does not die on *geng* and *xin*, it holds on *ren* and *gui*"] *Ren* and *gui* correspond to winter.

[On "and one rises on *jia* and *yi*"] They correspond to spring-wood.

[On "Liver illness is clear at dawn, worse in the late afternoon and calm at midnight"] It is the time when wood dominates, hence clear; the time when metal dominates, hence worse; the time when water dominates, hence calm and receding. "Clear" and "worse" for the others are the same, but their "calm" differs slightly.

[On "The liver desires dispersal; quickly eat acrid things to disperse it"] Since the qi of this zang constantly disperses, acridity is used to disperse it. The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* (chapter 5) says, "Acrid and sweet disperse and are yang," and the *Pingren qixiang lun* (chapter 18) says, "The true qi of the zang disperses in the liver," meaning that it constantly disperses.

[On "Use acridity to supplement it and sourness to drain it"] Acrid flavour disperses, so it supplements; sour flavour contracts, so it drains.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition reads "use sourness to supplement it and acridity to drain it", which is a reading in its own right.

## Notes

Liver illness heals or worsens by season, day and hour according to the generation and control of the five phases; the flavours that supplement and drain the liver.
- "Child", "ghost" and "mother" in Wang Bing's note: in the five phases, from the liver (wood), fire is the child, metal the ghost (that which controls it), and water the mother.
