# 素問 蔵気法時論篇第二十二 §7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When illness is in the spleen, it heals in autumn; if it does not heal in autumn, it worsens in spring; if one does not die in spring, it holds in summer and one rises in late summer. Avoid warm food, overeating, damp ground and wet clothing. Spleen illness heals on *geng* and *xin*; if it does not heal on *geng* and *xin*, it intensifies on *jia* and *yi*; if one does not die on *jia* and *yi*, it holds on *bing* and *ding*, and one rises on *wu* and *ji*. Spleen illness is clear in the early afternoon, worse at sunrise and calm in the late afternoon. The spleen desires relaxation; quickly eat sweet things to relax it. Use bitterness to drain it and sweetness to supplement it.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Avoid warm food, overeating, damp ground and wet clothing"] Warmth, damp and fullness all harm the spleen qi; therefore they are prohibited.

[On "Spleen illness heals on *geng* and *xin*"] They correspond to the qi of autumn.

[On "if it does not heal on *geng* and *xin*, it intensifies on *jia* and *yi*"] They correspond to the qi of spring.

[On "if one does not die on *jia* and *yi*, it holds on *bing* and *ding*"] They correspond to the qi of summer.

[On "and one rises on *wu* and *ji*"] They correspond to late summer.

[On "Spleen illness is clear in the early afternoon, worse at sunrise"] Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes "dawn" for "sunrise". Although sunrise and dawn are the same time, the preceding text always says "dawn", not "sunrise", for the time when wood dominates; moreover, sunrise is earlier or later in winter and summer, which is not as apt as "dawn".

[On "Spleen illness is clear in the early afternoon, worse at sunrise and calm in the late afternoon"] When earth dominates it is clear; when wood controls it, it worsens; when metal supports it, it calms and recedes. This too is the principle of resting and dominating. One version says "it holds at midday", which is wrong. Indeed, the illnesses of the five zang all intensify through what overcomes them, heal on reaching what they generate, worsen on reaching what they cannot overcome, hold on reaching what generates them, and rise on obtaining their own position. Therefore all have times of easing and worsening and dates of death and life.

[On "The spleen desires relaxation; quickly eat sweet things to relax it"] Sweetness is by nature mild and relaxing; it complies with its relaxation.

[On "Use bitterness to drain it and sweetness to supplement it"] Bitterness drains by its hardening and drying; sweetness supplements by its calming and relaxing.

## Notes

The course of spleen illness and the flavours that supplement and drain it.
- The part of Wang Bing's note beginning "One version says ..." summarizes much the same content as the text of unit 010.
- The long variant readings from Daizhige (黄帝内经素问) appear to result from a misalignment of text and notes in that source, not from real differences in content.
- The base text's 戊巳 means 戊己 (see the note to unit 006).
