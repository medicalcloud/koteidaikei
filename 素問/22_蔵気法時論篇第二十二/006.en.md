# 素問 蔵気法時論篇第二十二 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When illness is in the heart, it heals in late summer; if it does not heal in late summer, it worsens in winter; if one does not die in winter, it holds in spring and one rises in summer. Avoid warm food and hot clothing. Heart illness heals on *wu* and *ji*; if it does not heal on *wu* and *ji*, it intensifies on *ren* and *gui*; if one does not die on *ren* and *gui*, it holds on *jia* and *yi*, and one rises on *bing* and *ding*. Heart illness is clear at midday, worse at midnight and calm at dawn. The heart desires softness; quickly eat salty things to soften it. Use saltiness to supplement it and sweetness to drain it.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "if one does not die in winter, it holds in spring and one rises in summer"] As in the case of the liver.

[On "Avoid warm food and hot clothing"] Heat makes the heart agitated; therefore they are prohibited.

[On "Heart illness heals on *wu* and *ji*"] *Wu* and *ji* correspond to late summer.

[On "if it does not heal on *wu* and *ji*, it intensifies on *ren* and *gui*"] *Ren* and *gui* correspond to winter.

[On "if one does not die on *ren* and *gui*, it holds on *jia* and *yi*"] *Jia* and *yi* correspond to spring.

[On "and one rises on *bing* and *ding*"] They correspond to summer-fire.

[On "Heart illness is clear at midday, worse at midnight and calm at dawn"] This too is the principle of resting and dominating.

[On "The heart desires softness; quickly eat salty things to soften it"] Since the qi of this zang likes softness, saltiness is used to soften it. The *Pingren qixiang lun* (chapter 18) says, "The true qi of the zang communicates with the heart," meaning that it constantly desires softness.

[On "Use saltiness to supplement it and sweetness to drain it"] Saltiness supplements by its softening; sweetness drains by its relaxing.

## Notes

The course of heart illness and the flavours that supplement and drain it.
- In the base, 2 instance(s) of 巳 standing for the stem 己 to 己 are emended (graphic confusion; the base often does not distinguish 巳, 已 and 己, while Wikisource's plain text and other sources mostly have the correct character).
