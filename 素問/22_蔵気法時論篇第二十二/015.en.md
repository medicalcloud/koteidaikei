# 素問 蔵気法時論篇第二十二 §15 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In kidney illness, the abdomen is enlarged and the shins swollen, there are panting, coughing and heaviness of the body, night sweating and aversion to wind. When depleted, there are pain in the chest, pain in the greater and lesser abdomen, cold reversal, and the mind is unhappy. Take its channels, [the vessels of] the lesser yin and greater yang with blood.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In kidney illness, the abdomen is enlarged and the shins swollen"] Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* says "the shins are swollen and painful".

[On "In kidney illness ... night sweating and aversion to wind"] The kidney lesser yin vessel arises in the foot and rises along the calf, and from the pubic bone runs alongside the navel, up through the abdomen and into the lung; hence the abdomen is enlarged, the shins swollen, and there are panting and coughing. When the kidney is ill the bones cannot function; hence the body is heavy. Kidney evil attacks the lung and the heart qi becomes faint within; the fluid of the heart is sweat; hence night sweating. The shins are already swollen, and sweat leaks as fluid; yin congeals in the sweat pores and yang scorches the upper burner, hot within and cold without; hence aversion to wind. "Aversion to wind" means intense dislike of it.

[On "When depleted, there are pain in the chest ... the mind is unhappy"] The kidney lesser yin vessel emerges from the lung, connects with the heart and pours into the chest. When the kidney qi is depleted, the heart is uncontrolled and the heart qi fumigates the lung; hence pain gathers in the chest. The foot greater yang vessel descends from the nape to the foot; when the kidney is depleted, the greater yang qi cannot move abundantly to the foot; hence the feet are cold and the qi counterflows. 清 means the qi is cold; 厥 means the qi counterflows. Because of the cold and counterflow, the greater and lesser abdomen ache. When the will is insufficient, the spirit is agitated; hence unhappiness.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes "large intestine, small intestine" for "greater abdomen, lesser abdomen".

[On "Take its channels, [the vessels of] the lesser yin and greater yang with blood"] The way of needling is: when depleted, supplement; when replete, drain; when neither abundant nor depleted, take it by the channel — this is called attaining the way. When there is blood in the channels and network vessels, needle and remove it — this is called keeping the method. One should still gauge the form and settle the qi, first remove the blood vessels and only then balance surplus and insufficiency. The *Sanbu jiuhou lun* (chapter 20) says, "One must first measure whether the form is fat or thin to regulate whether the qi is depleted or replete; when replete, drain it; when depleted, supplement it; one must first remove the blood vessels and then regulate it" — this is what is meant.

## Notes

Symptoms of repletion and depletion in kidney illness, and the needling. Wang Bing's note summarizes the general principles of needling.
- "Cold reversal": Wang Bing explains it as cold, counterflowing qi (cold feet).
