# 素問 蔵気法時論篇第二十二 §16 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The liver's colour is green; it should eat sweet things: non-glutinous rice, beef, jujube and mallow are all sweet. The heart's colour is red; it should eat sour things: adzuki beans, dog meat, plum and Chinese chives are all sour. The lung's colour is white; it should eat bitter things: wheat, mutton, apricot and shallot are all bitter. The spleen's colour is yellow; it should eat salty things: soybeans, pork, chestnut and bean leaves are all salty. The kidney's colour is black; it should eat acrid things: yellow millet, chicken, peach and scallion are all acrid. Acrid disperses, sour contracts, sweet relaxes, bitter firms, salty softens. Toxic drugs attack evil; the five grains provide nourishment, the five fruits help, the five domestic animals benefit, and the five vegetables fill out. Taking them with qi and flavour combined supplements the essence and benefits the qi. These five have acrid, sour, sweet, bitter and salty, each with its benefit: some disperse, some contract, some relax, some tense, some firm, some soften. The illnesses of the four seasons and five zang [are treated] according to what suits them among the five flavours."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The liver's colour is green ... are all sweet"] The liver by nature likes tension; therefore one eats sweet things for their relaxing effect.
Song editors: From "The liver's colour is green" to the end of the chapter is in juan 6 in Quan Yuanqi's edition; Mr Wang moved it here.

[On "The heart's colour is red; it should eat sour things: adzuki beans"] Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* and *Taisu* write "hemp seed" for "adzuki beans".

[On "The heart's colour is red ... are all sour"] The heart by nature likes slackness; therefore one eats sour things for their astringent effect.

[On "The lung's colour is white ... are all bitter"] The lung likes counterflow of qi; therefore one eats bitter things for their dispersing and discharging effect.

[On "The spleen's colour is yellow ... are all salty"] Examining what should be eaten here, it is a matter of regulating and freeing the gate. The kidney is the gate of the stomach, and the spleen and stomach are paired; therefore the softening of saltiness is borrowed to free the gate. When the gate is free, the stomach qi moves; when the stomach moves, the spleen qi can transform. Hence the flavour suitable for the spleen differs from the others.
Song editors: The text above says, "The liver suffers from tension; quickly eat sweet things to relax it. The heart suffers from slackness; quickly eat sour things to contract it. The spleen suffers from damp; quickly eat bitter things to dry it. The lung suffers from upward counterflow of qi; quickly eat bitter things to discharge it. The kidney suffers from dryness; quickly eat acrid things to moisten it." Here the suitable foods for the liver, heart, lung and kidney all agree with the earlier text; only the spleen is given salty instead of bitter. Hence Mr Wang specially annotated its meaning.

[On "The kidney's colour is black ... are all acrid"] The kidney by nature likes dryness; therefore one eats acrid things for their moistening effect.

[On "Acrid disperses, sour contracts, sweet relaxes, bitter firms, salty softens"] These are all natural qi. Yet acrid and bitter flavours do not merely firm and disperse: acridity can also moisten and disperse, and bitterness can also dry and discharge. Thus the text above saying "The spleen suffers from damp; quickly eat bitter things to dry it. The lung suffers from upward counterflow of qi; quickly eat bitter things to discharge it" refers to the drying and discharging of bitterness; and "The kidney suffers from dryness; quickly eat acrid things to moisten it" refers to the moistening of acridity.

[On "Toxic drugs attack evil"] Drugs means metals, jade, earths, stones, herbs, trees, vegetables, fruits, insects, fish, birds, beasts and the like, all of which can expel evil and nourish the upright. Yet only toxins can ward off evil and settle the upright; because they can do so, they are generally called toxic drugs.
Song editors: The *Bencao* says, "The lower drugs serve as assistants and envoys, govern the treatment of illness, and correspond to earth. They are often toxic and may not be taken long. Those who wish to remove the evil qi of cold and heat, break up accumulations and heal disease base themselves on the lower classic." Hence it says "Toxic drugs attack evil."

[On "the five grains provide nourishment"] Namely non-glutinous rice, adzuki beans, wheat, soybeans and yellow millet.

[On "the five fruits help"] Namely peach, plum, apricot, chestnut and jujube.

[On "the five domestic animals benefit"] Namely ox, sheep, pig, dog and chicken.

[On "the five vegetables fill out"] Namely mallow, bean leaves, shallot, scallion and Chinese chives.
Song editors: The *Wuchangzheng dalun* (chapter 70) says, "In treating illness with great toxins, remove six parts in ten; with ordinary toxins, seven in ten; with slight toxins, eight in ten; with non-toxic drugs, nine in ten. Nourish with grains, meat, fruits and vegetables to finish it, without going too far and harming the upright."

[On "Taking them with qi and flavour combined supplements the essence and benefits the qi"] Qi transforms as yang, flavour bestows as yin; when qi and flavour are combined and harmonized, they supplement and benefit the essential qi. The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* (chapter 5) says, "Yang is qi, yin is flavour. Flavour returns to form, form returns to qi, qi returns to essence, essence returns to transformation. Essence feeds on qi, form feeds on flavour," and also, "When the form is insufficient, warm it with qi; when the essence is insufficient, supplement it with flavour." From this the meaning of supplementing the essence and benefiting the qi can be understood.
Song editors: Sun Simiao says, "Essence feeds on qi, and qi nourishes essence to make the complexion flourish; form feeds on flavour, and flavour nourishes form to produce strength. Essence follows the five qi to become spiritual; if the qi of food is mutually averse, essence is harmed. Form is completed by receiving flavour; if the flavours of food are not regulated, form is damaged. Therefore the sages first used dietary prohibitions to preserve nature, and then prepared drugs to protect life, warming and supplementing with qi and flavour to preserve essence and form." This is what "taking them with qi and flavour combined supplements the essence and benefits the qi" means.

[On "These five have ... according to what suits them among the five flavours"] The five flavours are used to regulate the five zang, pairing sweet with the liver, sour with the heart, salty with the spleen, bitter with the lung, and acrid with the kidney, each according to what suits it — whether it wants relaxing, contracting, softening, discharging, dispersing or firming; the idea is not that of mutual generation and nourishment.

## Notes

The flavours and foods (five grains, five domestic animals, five fruits, five vegetables) suited to each zang, the actions of the five flavours, and the roles of toxic drugs and foods; this ends the chapter. Qibo's speech, begun in unit 004, ends here.
- According to the Song editors, the passage from "The liver's colour is green" to the end of the chapter is in juan 6 in Quan Yuanqi's edition and was moved here by Wang Bing.
- Pairing the spleen with salty disagrees with the earlier text (bitter for the spleen); Wang Bing explains it as freeing the gate (the kidney).
- The *Bencao* quotation in the Song editors' note seems to correspond to the preface to the lower class of the *Shennong bencao jing*, but the wording has not been checked.
- 旡 in the Song editors' quotation from the *Wuchangzheng dalun* is a variant of 無.
