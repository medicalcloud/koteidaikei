# 素問 蔵気法時論篇第二十二 §14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In lung illness, there are panting, coughing and counterflow of qi, pain in the shoulders and back, sweating, and pain in the buttocks, genitals, thighs, knees, hips, calves, shins and feet. When depleted, the qi is scant and one cannot sustain the breath, the ears are deaf and the throat dry. Take its channels, the greater yin, and [the vessels] outside the foot greater yang and inside the *jueyin* with blood.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In lung illness, there are panting, coughing and counterflow of qi, pain in the shoulders and back"] Song editors: The *Qianjin fang* reads "shoulder breathing, back pain".

[On "... sweating, and pain in the buttocks, genitals, thighs, knees"] Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* and *Maijing* read "knee spasm".

[On "sweating, and pain in the buttocks ... shins and feet"] The lung stores qi and governs breathing; among its changes is coughing. Therefore in illness there are panting, coughing and counterflow of qi. The back is the house of the chest, and the shoulders adjoin it; hence pain in the shoulders and back. The lung nourishes the skin and hair; when evil is abundant the fluid of the heart leaks outward; hence sweating. The kidney lesser yin vessel rises from the sole along the inside of the calf, emerges at the inner edge of the knee crease, rises along the posterior edge of the inner thigh, passes through the spine, belongs to the kidney and connects with the bladder. Now when the lung is ill, the kidney vessel receives the evil; hence pain in the buttocks, genitals, thighs, knees, hips, calves, shins and feet. Therefore the lesser yin is taken below.

[On "When depleted, the qi is scant ... the throat dry"] The qi is depleted and scant, so it is insufficient to answer the incoming breath. The network vessel of the lung greater yin meets in the ear; hence deafness. The kidney lesser yin vessel rises from the kidney through the liver and diaphragm, enters the lung, follows the throat and runs alongside the root of the tongue. Now when the lung is depleted, the kidney qi is insufficient to rise and moisten the throat; hence the throat is dry. This is why the text below also takes the lesser yin.

[On "Take its channels ... with blood"] "Outside the foot greater yang and inside the *jueyin*" refers precisely to the inside of the calf, directly above behind the inner ankle, which is the lesser yin vessel. Look at the lesser yin region of the vessels of the left and right feet; where blood is full and unlike the usual, take it there.

## Notes

Symptoms of repletion and depletion in lung illness, and the needling. According to Wang Bing, "outside the foot greater yang and inside the *jueyin*" refers to the region of the foot lesser yin vessel.
- In Wang Bing's restatement of the text, 髀 appears as 脾 (as in the base text).
- "Cannot sustain the breath": Wang Bing explains it as being unable to answer the incoming breath.
