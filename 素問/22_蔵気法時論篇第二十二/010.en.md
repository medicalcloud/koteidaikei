# 素問 蔵気法時論篇第二十二 §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When evil qi lodges in the body, [the illness] intensifies through what overcomes it, heals on reaching what it generates, worsens on reaching what it cannot overcome, holds on reaching what generates it, and rises on obtaining its own position. Only after first determining the pulses of the five zang can one speak of the times of easing and worsening and the dates of death and life.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When evil qi lodges in the body, it intensifies through what overcomes it"] Evil is the name for what is not upright. Wind, cold, summer heat, damp, hunger, satiety, toil and idleness are all evils, not only ghostly poisons and epidemics.

[On "heals on reaching what it generates"] It means reaching what one generates oneself.

[On "worsens on reaching what it cannot overcome"] It means reaching the qi that controls oneself.

[On "holds on reaching what generates it"] It means reaching the qi that generates oneself.

[On "rises on obtaining its own position"] Being in the place where it dominates is called obtaining its own position.

[On "Only after first determining the pulses ... the dates of death and life"] The pulses of the five zang are: the liver wiry, the heart hooked, the lung floating, the kidney encamped, the spleen intermittent. Knowing this, one can speak of death and life, easing and worsening. The *Sanbu jiuhou lun* (chapter 20) says, "One must first know the channel pulses, and then know the diseased pulses" — this is what is meant.

## Notes

The general principle of the course of illnesses of the five zang.
- 所生 in "holds on reaching 所生" is the same expression as the earlier 所生 ("what it generates"), but Wang Bing takes it as "what generates it"; the translation follows Wang Bing.
