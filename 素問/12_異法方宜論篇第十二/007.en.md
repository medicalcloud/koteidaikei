# 素問 異法方宜論篇第十二 §7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The centre — its land is flat and damp, and here heaven and earth produce the myriad things in abundance. Its people eat a variety of foods and do not toil; therefore their illnesses are mostly limpness and reversal, cold and heat. Their treatment is best with *daoyin* and massage. Thus *daoyin* and massage too came from the centre.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The centre — its land is flat and damp ... in abundance"] It follows the function of the virtue of earth; therefore it produces things in abundance. The east is sea, the south low, the west and north high; the land of the centre is flat and damp. When the landforms differ, the illnesses that arise differ.

[On "Its people eat a variety of foods and do not toil"] All things converge from the four directions and meet there; therefore people's food is varied and they do not toil.

[On "therefore their illnesses are mostly limpness and reversal, cold and heat"] Because damp qi is below, they often suffer limpness and weakness, counterflow of qi, and cold and heat. The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* (chapter 5) says: "If one is affected by the damp qi of earth, it harms the skin, flesh, sinews and vessels." It is because they live close to dampness.

[On "Their treatment is best with *daoyin* and massage"] *Daoyin* means swinging the sinews and bones and moving the limbs and joints; 按 means pressing the skin and flesh; 蹻 means nimbly raising the hands and feet.

[On "Thus *daoyin* and massage too came from the centre"] People of the centre use them as the correct way of nourishing the spirit and regulating the qi.

## Notes

The centre is flat and damp and produces things in abundance; its people eat varied food and do not toil, so they suffer limpness, reversal, cold and heat. *Daoyin* and massage, suited to treating them, came from the centre.
- "*Daoyin* and massage": Wang Bing explains *daoyin* as swinging the sinews and bones and moving the joints, 按 as pressing the skin and flesh, and 蹻 as nimbly raising the hands and feet.
- Wikisource (the plain text) writes 跷 (simplified form) for 蹻.
