# 素問 刺瘧篇第三十六 §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The twelve kinds of malaria each break out at different times; examine the form of the illness to know which vessel is ill. Needle about the time of a meal before the attack; with one needling it declines, with two there is effect, with three it ends. If it does not end, needle the two vessels under the tongue and let out blood. If it does not end, needle the abundant channel at the cleft centre and let out blood, and also needle along the spine below the nape, and it will surely end. The two vessels under the tongue are Lianquan.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The twelve kinds of malaria ... which vessel is ill"] By following its form and signs, the diseased vessel can be known.

[On "Needle about the time of a meal before the attack ... let out blood"] The explanation is given in the text below.

[On "If it does not end, needle the abundant channel at the cleft centre ... it will surely end"] Both are the vessel qi of the foot greater yang. The cleft centre is Weizhong. Along the spine means the Dazhu and Fengmen Refu points. Dazhu is in the depressions one and a half body-*cun* to each side below the first vertebra of the nape; one may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, five cones. Fengmen Refu is one and a half body-*cun* to each side below the second vertebra; one may needle five body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, five cones.
Song editors: On examination, Dazhu is given five moxa cones, but the *Jiayi jing* gives seven, as does the note to the *Qixue lun*; the notes to the *Cire lun* and the heat points both give five.

[On "The two vessels under the tongue are Lianquan"] Lianquan is the name of a point, below the chin, above the laryngeal prominence and below the root of the tongue, the meeting of the *yinwei* and *ren* vessels. One may needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

## Notes

The principle of needling the twelve kinds of malaria (six channels, five zang, stomach) and additional needling when it does not respond.
- The base text writes 己 for the 已 of 三刺則已; the translation takes it as 已.
