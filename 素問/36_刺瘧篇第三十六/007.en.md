# 素問 刺瘧篇第三十六 §7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Lung malaria makes one's heart cold; when the cold is severe there is heat; during the heat one is easily startled, as if seeing something. Needle the hand greater yin and *yangming*. Heart malaria makes one's heart greatly vexed, wanting clear water, yet with much cold and not much heat. Needle the hand lesser yin. Liver malaria makes one's complexion pale green-blue, with sighing, looking as if dead. Needle the foot *jueyin* until blood appears. Spleen malaria makes one cold with abdominal pain; when hot, the intestines rumble; when the rumbling ends, sweat comes out. Needle the foot greater yin. Kidney malaria makes one shiver, with pain in the lumbus and spine so that one writhes, difficult defecation, dazzled eyes, and cold hands and feet. Needle the foot greater yang and lesser yin. Stomach malaria, when it makes one ill, causes frequent hunger but inability to eat, and on eating, propping fullness and an enlarged abdomen. Needle the transverse vessels of the foot *yangming* and greater yin and let out blood.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Lung malaria ... Needle the hand greater yin and *yangming*"] Lieque governs it. Lieque is one and a half body-*cun* behind the wrist, the network point of the hand greater yin. One may needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, five cones. For the *yangming*, Hegu governs it. Hegu is in the cleft between the thumb and index finger, where the hand *yangming* vessel passes. One may needle three body-*fen* and retain six breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "Heart malaria ... Needle the hand lesser yin"] Shenmen governs it. Shenmen is in the depression at the end of the pisiform bone behind the palm, the stream point of the hand lesser yin. One may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The *Taisu* says, "wanting clear water, and much cold; when the cold is not severe, the heat is severe".

[On "Liver malaria ... Needle the foot *jueyin* until blood appears"] Zhongfeng governs it. Zhongfeng is in the depression one and a half body-*cun* in front of the inner ankle; take it with the foot turned up, found when the foot is stretched; it is the river point of the foot *jueyin*. Needle, let out blood and stop. In ordinary needling one may needle four body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "Spleen malaria ... Needle the foot greater yin"] Shangqiu governs it. Shangqiu is in the depression slightly in front of and below the inner ankle, the river point of the foot greater yin. One may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "Kidney malaria ... Needle the foot greater yang and lesser yin"] Taizhong governs it; take it as in the method for malaria of the foot lesser yin above.

[On "Stomach malaria, when it makes one ill ... an enlarged abdomen"] The stomach is hot and the spleen depleted; therefore one is often hungry but cannot eat, and on eating there is propping fullness and an enlarged abdomen. That is why the text below also needles the greater yin.
Song editors: The *Taisu* writes 疽病 for 且病.

[On "Needle the transverse vessels of the foot *yangming* and greater yin and let out blood"] Lidui, Jiexi and Sanli govern it. Lidui is at the tip of the second toe, a chive-leaf's breadth from the nail, the well point of the *yangming*. One may needle one body-*fen* and retain one breath; for moxibustion, one cone. Jiexi is three and a half body-*cun* behind Chongyang, in the depression above the ankle, the river point of the *yangming*. One may needle five body-*fen* and retain five breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Sanli is three body-*cun* below the knee, between the two sinews on the outer edge of the shin, the uniting point of the *yangming*. One may needle one body-*cun* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Thus for the foot *yangming* take these three points, and for the foot greater yin needle its transverse vessel and let out blood. The transverse vessel means the large vessel crossing obliquely in front of the inner ankle, which is the channel vessel of the greater yin.
Song editors: On examination, Jiexi is given as three and a half *cun* behind Chongyang, but the *Jiayi jing* gives one and a half *cun* and the note to the *Qixue lun* two and a half *cun*.

## Notes

The symptoms and needling of malaria of the five zang and the stomach. Wang Bing's notes give the location and needling of each point.
- 列欠 and 太鍾 in Wang Bing's notes are the Lieque (列缺) and Dazhong (大鍾) points.
- 令人且病也 is obscure; according to the Song editors the *Taisu* writes 疽病. The translation keeps the characters as they are.
