# 素問 刺瘧篇第三十六 §11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In needling malaria, one must first ask where the illness first breaks out and needle there first. Where there is first headache and heaviness, first needle the top of the head, both sides of the forehead and between the eyebrows, and let out blood. Where the nape and back ache first, needle there first. Where the lumbus and spine ache first, first needle the cleft centre and let out blood. Where the hands and arms ache first, first needle the hand lesser yin and *yangming* and between the ten fingers. Where the feet and shins are sore first, first needle the foot *yangming* and between the ten toes and let out blood. In wind malaria, when the attack comes there are sweating and aversion to wind; needle the back transport points of the three yang channels where there is blood. When the shins are very sore and painful and cannot be pressed, it is called *fusui* illness; needle Juegu with the arrowhead needle and let out blood, and it ends at once. When the body aches slightly, needle Zhiyin. Do not let blood at the well points of all the yin; needle once every other day. When malaria without thirst breaks out every other day, needle the foot greater yang. When with thirst it breaks out every other day, needle the foot lesser yang. In warm malaria without sweating, perform the fifty-nine needlings.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Where there is first headache and heaviness ... let out blood"] The top of the head means Shangxing and Baihui; both sides of the forehead means Xuanlu; between the eyebrows means Cuanzhu and other points.

[On "Where the nape and back ache first, needle there first"] For the nape, Fengchi and Fengfu govern it; for the back, Dazhu and Shendao.

[On "Where the hands and arms ache first ... between the ten fingers"] Song editors: Another edition writes 手陰陽 "the yin and yang of the hand"; Quan's edition also writes 手陰陽.

[On "Where the feet and shins are sore first ... let out blood"] In each case remove and drain according to where the evil resides.

[On "In wind malaria ... where there is blood"] The three yang means the greater yang.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* says "the three yang of the foot".

[On "When the shins are very sore ... it ends at once"] This is the Yangfu point; take it as in the method for the fu transport points in the *Qixue lun*.

[On "When the body aches slightly, needle Zhiyin"] Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* lacks the two characters 至陰.

[On "Do not let blood at the well points of all the yin; needle once every other day"] The well points are all at the tips of the digits; that of the foot lesser yin is in the hollow at the centre of the sole.

[On "When malaria without thirst ... needle the foot greater yang"] Song editors: The *Jiujuan* says "foot *yangming*"; the *Taisu* is the same.

[On "When with thirst ... needle the foot lesser yang"] Song editors: The *Jiujuan* says "hand lesser yang"; the *Taisu* is the same.

[On "In warm malaria without sweating, perform the fifty-nine needlings"] From stomach malaria down to here, examining what the *Huangdi zhonggao tujing* gives, some points do not agree with this text; it is presumably a different ancient method.

## Notes

The general principle of needling malaria (first needle where it first breaks out) and the needling for wind malaria, *fusui* illness, warm malaria and others; this ends the chapter.
- 并 in Wang Bing's 足少陰并 is presumably an error for 井 "well point".
- The *Jiujuan* cited by the Song editors presumably refers to the *Lingshu*.
- 胕髄病 is understood as an illness reaching the bone marrow, but it is read in various ways.
