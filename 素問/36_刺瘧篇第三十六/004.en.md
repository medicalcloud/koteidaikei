# 素問 刺瘧篇第三十六 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Malaria of the foot greater yin makes one unhappy and given to sighing, without appetite, with much cold and heat and sweating. When the illness comes, there is frequent vomiting; when the vomiting ends, it declines; then take it.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Malaria of the foot greater yin makes one unhappy and given to sighing"] When heart qi flows to the lung there is joy. Now the spleen zang receives illness, and the heart, its mother, rescues it; the qi of fire descends into the spleen and does not rise to the lung. Also, a branch of the greater yin vessel goes again from the stomach up through the diaphragm and pours into the heart. Therefore one is unhappy and given to sighing.

[On "without appetite, with much cold and heat and sweating"] The spleen governs the transformation of grain and nourishes and assists the four sides. Now evil presses on it; the zang all originally receive from it, and earth dominates lodged in the four seasons; so the evil qi contends; therefore one has no appetite, much cold and heat, and sweating.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* says "much cold and little heat".

[On "When the illness comes, there is frequent vomiting; when the vomiting ends, it declines"] The foot greater yin vessel enters the abdomen, belongs to the spleen, connects with the stomach, rises through the diaphragm and runs alongside the throat; therefore when the qi of the illness comes there is vomiting, and when the vomiting ends it declines and recedes.

[On "then take it"] Wait for the illness to decline and then take it. "When it declines, take it" refers to the well and stream points and Gongsun. Gongsun is one body-*cun* behind the base joint of the big toe; it is the network point of the greater yin. One may needle four body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

## Notes

The symptoms and needling of malaria of the foot greater yin.
- The base text writes 己 for the 已 of 嘔已乃衰; the translation takes it as 已.
- "The heart, its mother" in Wang Bing's note refers to the heart (fire) being the mother of the spleen (earth).
