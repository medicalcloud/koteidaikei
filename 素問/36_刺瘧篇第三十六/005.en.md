# 素問 刺瘧篇第三十六 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Malaria of the foot lesser yin makes one vomit severely, with much cold and heat, more heat than cold, and a desire to stay with doors and windows closed. The illness is hard to end.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Malaria of the foot lesser yin ... more heat than cold"] The foot lesser yin vessel passes through the liver and diaphragm, enters the lung and follows the throat; therefore there are severe vomiting and much cold and heat. The kidney is a yin zang, and yin qi produces cold; now the yin qi is insufficient; therefore there is more heat than cold.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* says "severe vomiting, much cold and little heat".

[On "a desire to stay with doors and windows closed. The illness is hard to end"] When the stomach *yangming* vessel is ill, one wishes to stay alone with doors and windows closed. Now the signs of stomach-earth illness appear instead within kidney-water; earth punishes water; therefore the illness is hard to end. Taizhong and Taixi both govern it. Taizhong is in the passage behind the inner ankle; it is the network point of the lesser yin. One may needle two body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Taixi is in the depression at the artery behind the inner ankle above the heel bone; it is the stream point of the lesser yin. One may needle three body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* says "The illness is hard to end; take Taixi." Moreover, for the Taizhong point, the *Jiayi jing* writes "in the thoroughfare behind the heel", the note to the *Ci yaotong pian* writes "at the artery in the passage behind the heel", the note on the water points says "behind the inner ankle", and this note says "in the passage behind the inner ankle". The notes differ; the *Jiayi jing* should be taken as correct.

## Notes

The symptoms of malaria of the foot lesser yin. The text gives no needling; Wang Bing's note supplies Taizhong and Taixi.
- Taizhong (太鍾) in Wang Bing's note is the Dazhong (大鍾) point.
