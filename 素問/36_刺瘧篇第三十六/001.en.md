# 素問 刺瘧篇第三十六 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Malaria of the foot greater yang makes one's lumbus ache and head heavy; the cold rises from the back; first cold then heat, blazing and scorching. When the heat stops and sweat comes out, it is hard to end. Needle the cleft centre and let out blood.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Malaria of the foot greater yang ... the cold rises from the back"] The foot greater yang vessel enters from the vertex to connect with the brain, comes out again and branches down the nape, follows the inside of the shoulder blade, runs alongside the spine and reaches the lumbus; a branch separates left and right from inside the shoulder blade, descends through the paraspinal muscles and passes the hip joint. Therefore it makes the lumbus ache and the head heavy, and the cold rises from the back.
Song editors: The note to the *Sanbu jiuhou lun* writes 貫臋 for 貫胂, as does the note to *Ci yaotong*; the note to the *Jue lun* writes 貫胂, as does the *Jiayi jing*.

[On "first cold then heat, blazing and scorching"] 熇熇 describes intense heat; 暍暍 also means abundant heat. The greater yang is insufficient; therefore first there is cold. When cold reaches its extreme it produces heat; therefore heat follows.

[On "When the heat stops and sweat comes out, it is hard to end"] Arising heat means the qi is depleted; stopping heat means the qi recovers. That sweat comes out when the qi has recovered means the evil qi is abundant and the true does not prevail; therefore it is hard to end.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition, the *Jiayi jing*, the *Taisu* and Chao Yuanfang all read "first cold then heat and thirst; when the thirst stops, sweat comes out", differing from this text.

[On "Needle the cleft centre and let out blood"] The cleft of the greater yang is called Jinmen. Jinmen is below the outer ankle, also called Guanliang, where the *yangwei* branches off and attaches. One may needle three body-*fen*; for moxibustion, three cones. The *Huangdi zhonggao tujing* says, "Weizhong governs it." Thus the ancient method took Weizhong as the cleft centre. Weizhong is at the artery in the transverse crease at the centre of the popliteal fossa, where the foot greater yang vessel enters. One may needle five body-*fen* and retain seven breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.
Song editors: On examination, the *Jiayi jing* writes 膕中 "popliteal centre" for 郄中. Mr Wang annotates both; 膕中 should be taken as correct.

## Notes

This chapter describes the symptoms and needling of malaria of the six channels, the five zang and the stomach; it contains no dialogue. This unit concerns malaria of the foot greater yang.
- 郄中: Wang Bing gives both Jinmen and Weizhong. The Song editors, following the *Jiayi jing*, take it as 膕中 (Weizhong).
