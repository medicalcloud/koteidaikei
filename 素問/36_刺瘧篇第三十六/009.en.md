# 素問 刺瘧篇第三十六 §9 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When in malaria the pulse is full, large and tense, needle the back transport point with a medium needle, and the five flank transport points beside it once each, letting out blood according to whether the patient is fat or thin. When in malaria the pulse is small, replete and tense, apply moxibustion to the lesser yin on the shin and needle the well point of the toe. When in malaria the pulse is full, large and tense, needle the back transport point, using the five flank transport points and the back transport point once each, adjusting [the needling] to reach the blood. When in malaria the pulse is relaxed, large and depleted, one should use drugs, not needles. In treating malaria, only about the time of a meal before the attack can one treat it; after that, the time is missed. In all malaria where the pulse cannot be found, needle between the ten fingers and let out blood; when the blood is gone it will surely end. First look for red spots on the body like adzuki beans and take them all.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When in malaria the pulse is full, large and tense ... according to whether the patient is fat or thin"] For the thin, needle shallowly and let out little blood; for the fat, needle deeply and let out much blood. The back transport point means Dazhu; the five flank transport points means Yixi.

[On "When in malaria the pulse is small, replete and tense ... needle the well point of the toe"] "Apply moxibustion to the lesser yin on the shin" means Fuliu. Fuliu is in the depression two body-*cun* above the inner ankle, the river point of the foot lesser yin. One may needle three body-*fen* and retain three breaths; for moxibustion, five cones. "Needle the well point of the toe" means needling Zhiyin. Zhiyin is on the outer side of the little toe, a chive-leaf's breadth from the corner of the nail, the well point of the foot greater yang. One may needle one body-*fen* and retain five breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "When in malaria the pulse is full, large and tense ... adjusting [the needling] to reach the blood"] It means adjusting to fatness or thinness and the depth of the points, following the method of the *Sanbei* in applying the needle, so that it reaches the blood vessels. The back transport point means Dazhu; the five flank transport points means Yixi governs it.
Song editors: On examination, from "When in malaria the pulse is full, large" in this item to the end of this note, text and note together are fifty-five characters and should be deleted. The classic's text repeats the text just before, and Mr Wang annotated it accordingly without any distinct rationale; this is not as careful as Shi'an [Huangfu Mi], who does not repeat it.

[On "When in malaria the pulse is relaxed, large and depleted ... not needles"] Relaxed means wind strike; large means the qi is replete; depleted means the blood is depleted. With the blood depleted and the qi replete, wind attacks as well; therefore one should treat with drugs to dispel the evil, not drain with needles and let out blood.

[On "In treating malaria ... the time is missed"] Before the time of the attack, the true and the evil reside apart and the waves do not swell; therefore it can be treated. After the time, the true and the evil combine, and attacking it instead harms the true qi; therefore it says "the time is missed".
Song editors: On examination, from the earlier "When in malaria the pulse is full, large" to here was in juan 4 of Quan Yuanqi's edition; Mr Wang moved it here as a continuation.

## Notes

Needling of malaria according to the pulse, and the timing of treatment.
- The Song editors hold that the second "When in malaria the pulse is full, large and tense ... to reach the blood" repeats the previous text and should be deleted.
- 伍 in 傍伍胠兪 means 五 "five" (Daizhige writes 五).
- 三備 in Wang Bing's note matches the name of the Daoist book *Beidi sanbei jing* recorded in the bibliographical section of the *Tongzhi*, and may refer to it, but its content has not been verified.
