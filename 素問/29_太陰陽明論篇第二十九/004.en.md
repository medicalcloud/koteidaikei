# 素問 太陰陽明論篇第二十九 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Yang is the qi of heaven and governs the outside; yin is the qi of earth and governs the inside. Therefore the way of yang is replete and the way of yin depleted. Thus those who incur bandit wind and depleting evil receive it in yang; those whose eating and drinking are unregulated and whose daily life is untimely receive it in yin. When yang receives it, it enters the six fu; when yin receives it, it enters the five zang. When it enters the six fu, the body is hot, one cannot lie down at times, and above there is panting and shouting. When it enters the five zang, there is distension, fullness and blockage, below there is diarrhoea with undigested food, and in time intestinal flux. Thus the throat governs the qi of heaven, the gullet governs the qi of earth. Thus yang receives wind qi and yin receives damp qi. Thus yin qi rises from the feet to the head and descends along the arms to the fingertips; yang qi rises from the hands to the head and descends to the feet. Therefore it is said: yang illness rises to its extreme and then descends; yin illness descends to its extreme and then rises. Thus those harmed by wind receive it first above; those harmed by damp receive it first below."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Yang is the qi of heaven ... governs the inside"] This is what is meant by "yin and yang differ in position".

[On "Therefore the way of yang is replete and the way of yin depleted"] This is what is meant by "depleted and replete in turn".

[On "Thus those who incur bandit wind ... receive it in yin"] This is what is meant by "either from within or from without".

[On "When yang receives it, it enters the six fu ... in time intestinal flux"] This is what is meant by "what they follow differs; therefore the illnesses have different names".

[On "Thus the throat governs ... yin receives damp qi"] Like qi seek each other.

[On "Thus yin qi rises from the feet ... descends to the feet"] This is what is meant by "adverse and compliant in turn". The *Lingshu jing* says, "The three yin of the hand run from the zang to the hand; the three yang of the hand run from the hand to the head; the three yang of the foot run from the head to the foot; the three yin of the foot run from the foot to the abdomen." Their courses differ; hence they are adverse and compliant in turn.

[On "Therefore it is said: yang illness ... rises"] This states only the general pattern; the foot lesser yin vessel, however, runs downward and so differs from the qi of the other yin.

[On "Thus those harmed by wind ... receive it first below"] Yang qi blazes upward, so it receives wind; yin qi moistens downward, so it receives damp. Like qi combine.

## Notes

Explains concretely each phrase of unit 002 (difference of position, depletion and repletion in turn, from within or without, different names, adversity and compliance in turn); Wang Bing's notes show the correspondence.
- 陰気従足上行至頭，而下行循臂至指端 is understood to mean that yin qi rises from the feet to the head and [from there] descends along the arms to the fingertips. This differs from the course of the channels in the *Lingshu jing* passage quoted by Wang Bing (similar wording occurs in the *Nishun feishou* chapter).
- 䐜満 is understood as distension and fullness of the abdomen.
