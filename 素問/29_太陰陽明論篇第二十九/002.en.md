# 素問 太陰陽明論篇第二十九 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "Yin and yang differ in position; they are depleted and replete in turn, adverse and compliant in turn; [illness] comes either from within or from without. What they follow differs; therefore the illnesses have different names."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Yin and yang differ in position ... the illnesses have different names"] The spleen, a zang, is yin; the stomach, a fu, is yang. The yang vessels run downward, the yin vessels upward; the yang vessels [receive illness] from outside, the yin vessels from inside. Hence "what they follow differs, and the illnesses have different names".
Song editors: Yang Shangshan says, "In spring and summer the *yangming* is replete and the greater yin depleted; in autumn and winter the greater yin is replete and the *yangming* depleted — this is being replete and depleted in turn. In spring and summer the greater yin is adverse and the *yangming* compliant; in autumn and winter the *yangming* is adverse and the greater yin compliant — this is being adverse and compliant in turn."

## Notes

The illnesses of the greater yin and *yangming* differ because of differences in the position of yin and yang, depletion and repletion, adversity and compliance, and whether the cause is internal or external. Each is explained in the following units.
