# 素問 通評虚実論篇第二十八 §45 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "If the pulse is replete and large, even a long illness can be treated; if the pulse is suspended, small and hard, a long illness cannot be treated."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "If the pulse is replete and large ... cannot be treated"] In a long illness the blood and qi decline, so the pulse should not be replete and large; therefore it cannot be treated.
Song editors: The classic says "if replete and large, even a long illness can be treated", but the note takes it as untreatable. The *Jiayi jing*, *Taisu* and Quan Yuanqi's edition all say "can be treated". Moreover, Chao Yuanfang says, "If the pulse is rapid and large, one lives; if fine, small and floating, one dies," and also, "If sunken and small, one lives; if replete, firm and large, one dies."

## Notes

The prognosis of wasting-thirst by the pulse. The translation follows the text.
- Wang Bing takes even the replete, large pulse as untreatable; the Song editors point out that this disagrees with the text and other editions.
