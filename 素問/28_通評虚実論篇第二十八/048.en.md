# 素問 通評虚実論篇第二十八 §48 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In treating wasting-thirst, sudden collapse, hemiplegia, wilting and reversal, and fullness of qi with counterflow, in fat noble persons, these are illnesses of rich food. Blockage and closure, with upper and lower not communicating, are illnesses of sudden worry. Sudden reversal with deafness, and one-sided blockage and closure without passage, are due to the inner qi suddenly pressing. [Illness] not from within, but from wind striking from outside, therefore causes emaciation, with [the evil] lingering and lodging. Lameness of the feet is an illness of cold, wind and damp."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In treating wasting-thirst ... an illness of cold, wind and damp"] 消 means inner wasting; 癉 means hidden heat; 厥 means counterflow of qi. 高 means 膏 "fat"; 梁 is the character 粱 "fine grain". 蹠 means the foot. Fat things make a person hot within; sweet things make a person full within. Therefore hot qi presses within and breaks out as wasting-thirst, hemiplegia, and fullness with counterflow of qi. "Counterflow" means departing from the usual signs, unlike a healthy person. Those who are anxious and worried have their qi closed and not moving; therefore there is blockage and obstruction, the qi and vessels are cut off, and upper and lower do not communicate. When the qi is fixed within, the passages of urine and stool are blocked on one side and cannot discharge. Why? Because the qi of the zang and fu does not transform; it is restrained and fixed and does not spread. When external wind strikes a person and lies hidden without leaving, the yang qi receives it within and becomes heat, burning outward so that the flesh wastes and melts; therefore it lingers and presses in the divisions of the flesh, one becomes emaciated, and the skin clings to the sinews and bones. When damp prevails in the feet, the sinews are not free; when cold prevails in the feet, they contract and cramp; when wind, damp and cold prevail, the defensive qi knots and gathers; when the defensive qi knots and gathers, the flesh aches; therefore the feet are lame and cannot walk.

## Notes

The causes of illnesses such as wasting-thirst (rich food, worry, inner qi, external wind, cold-wind-damp). Read as a continuation of Huangdi's speech in the previous unit.
- Daizhige has 甘 before 肥貴人 (readable as "fat and sweet"); Wang Bing's note also explains "fat things" and "sweet things" separately.
- 不従内，外中風之病，故痩留著也 is read in various ways; the translation draws on Wang Bing.
