# 素問 通評虚実論篇第二十八 §19 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "What is called depletion of qi means there is no constancy; depletion of the *chi* means walking is unsteady; depletion of the pulse means it does not resemble yin. In such cases, if slippery one lives; if rough, one dies."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "What is called depletion of qi means there is no constancy; depletion of the *chi* means walking is unsteady"] When the *cun* is depleted, the movement of the pulse has no constancy; when the *chi* is depleted, walking is unsteady and insufficient.
Song editors: Yang Shangshan says, "Depletion of qi means the qi of the *danzhong* is unsettled." Mr Wang's saying that "when the *cun* is depleted, the movement of the pulse has no constancy" is wrong.

[On "depletion of the pulse means it does not resemble yin"] It does not resemble the signs of the greater yin. Why? Because the qi opening is the key meeting place of the vessels, where the hand greater yin pulsates.

## Notes

The findings and prognosis of depletion of qi, of the *chi* and of the pulse. The question was about treatment, but the answer gives findings and prognosis.
- 言無常也: Wang Bing takes it as the pulse moving without constancy; Yang Shangshan as the qi of the *danzhong* being unsettled. Some take it as speech being broken and discontinuous.
