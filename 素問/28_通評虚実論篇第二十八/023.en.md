# 素問 通評虚実論篇第二十八 §23 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "In spring and autumn one lives; in winter and summer one dies. When the pulse is floating and rough, and rough with fever in the body, one dies."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In spring and autumn one lives; in winter and summer one dies"] Broadly speaking, cold hands and feet in summer are not an illness; this is summer carrying out the order of winter: if obtained in summer, one dies in winter. A replete, full pulse and hot head in winter are also not an illness; this is winter carrying out the order of summer: if obtained in winter, one perishes in summer. Speaking of them with winter and summer reversed, none die. Obtaining these in spring or autumn is illness. Therefore life and death are all in the first month of the season.

[On "When the pulse is floating and rough, and rough with fever in the body, one dies"] Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* moves this here as a continuation. Formerly it was below "The Emperor said: form measures, bone measures, vessel measures and sinew measures — how does one know these measures?", where question and answer do not match in meaning. Mr Wang largely knew it was a displacement but did not know that Huangfu Shi'an had already moved it here. Now it is removed from the later item and moved here.

## Notes

The prognosis by season when the pulse is replete, the hands and feet cold and the head hot.
- According to the Song editors, 脈浮而濇，濇而身有熱者死 originally stood after unit 046 and was moved here following the *Jiayi jing*.
- The argument of Wang Bing's note is hard to follow; the translation follows the word order.
