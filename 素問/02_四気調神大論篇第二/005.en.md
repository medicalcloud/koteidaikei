# 素問 四気調神大論篇第二 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The qi of heaven is pure, still, radiant and bright. It stores its virtue unceasingly; therefore it does not descend [decline]. If heaven [itself] were bright, the sun and moon would not be bright, and evil would harm the hollow orifices. The yang qi closes and blocks; the earth qi covers the brightness. If the clouds and mists do not become essence, then above there is a response: the white dew does not descend.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The qi of heaven is pure, still, radiant and bright"] This says that heaven's brightness is inexhaustible because it is pure and still. That human life is extended likewise comes from moving in accordance [with heaven]; therefore it speaks of the qi of heaven to show it to people.

[On "It stores its virtue unceasingly"] Song editors: Another edition writes 上 *shang* for 止 *zhi*.

[On "It stores its virtue unceasingly; therefore it does not descend"] The four seasons form their order and the seven luminaries make their rounds, yet heaven does not show itself in form or speak of it; this is storing virtue. When virtue is hidden, its application is never exhausted; therefore it does not descend. The *Laozi* says: "Superior virtue does not take itself as virtue; therefore it has virtue." Heaven is supremely honoured and high, and yet its virtue is still hidden; how much more should the way of preserving life follow heaven!

[On "If heaven [itself] were bright, the sun and moon would not be bright, and evil would harm the hollow orifices"] The reason heaven stores its virtue is that it wishes to hide its great brightness; for when the great brightness shows, the lesser brightness is extinguished. Therefore the virtue of the great brightness must be stored. If heaven were bright of itself, the brightness of the sun and moon would be hidden. What does this illustrate? That a person's true qi likewise must not be leaked or exposed, but one should be pure and still, follow the Way and preserve the heavenly truth. If one departs from the Way, depleting evil enters the hollow orifices.

[On "The yang qi closes and blocks; the earth qi covers the brightness"] "Yang" refers to the qi of heaven, which is also wind and heat; the earth qi refers to dampness, which is also cloud and mist. When wind and heat harm a person, the nine orifices close and block; when mist and dampness cause illness, they cover and cloud the essence brightness [the eyes]. Taking things by category: in heaven, the sun and moon do not shine; in a person, the two eyes conceal their light. The *Lingshu jing* says: "Heaven has the sun and moon; a person has eyes." The *Yi* says: "Losing the brightness in ease" — is this not losing the way of nourishing what is correct?

[On "If the clouds and mists do not become essence, then above there is a response: the white dew does not descend"] Mist is of the kind of cloud, and dew is of the kind of rain. When yang is exuberant, earth does not respond upward; when yin is depleted, heaven does not descend to intermingle. Therefore the clouds and mists do not transform, the subtle essential qi does not rise to respond to heaven, and there is the fault of the white dew not descending. The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* (chapter 5) says: "The earth qi rises to become clouds; the heavenly qi descends to become rain. Rain comes from the earth qi; clouds come from the heavenly qi." This makes clear that only when the two qi intermingle do rain and dew form. The *Fang shengshuai lun* (chapter 80) says: "When the utmost yin is depleted, the heavenly qi is cut off; when the utmost yang is exuberant, the earth qi is insufficient." This makes clear that if the qi do not summon each other, they cannot intermingle.

## Notes

A passage on the nature of the qi of heaven and earth. Heaven stores its virtue and does not display itself, and so does not decline; if the qi of heaven and earth do not intermingle, clouds and mists do not become essence and white dew does not fall. Wang Bing takes this as an analogy for a person storing the true qi and not letting it leak.
- "It stores its virtue unceasingly": according to the Song editors, another edition writes 上 for 止.
- "If heaven were bright, the sun and moon would not be bright": Wang Bing explains that if heaven displayed its own brightness, that of the sun and moon would be hidden, and takes this as an analogy for not exposing one's true qi.
- There are various readings of this passage; the translation follows Wang Bing.
