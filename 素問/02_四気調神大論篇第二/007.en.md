# 素問 四気調神大論篇第二 §7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

If one goes against the qi of spring, the lesser yang does not arise and the liver qi alters within. If one goes against the qi of summer, the greater yang does not grow and the heart qi becomes hollow within. If one goes against the qi of autumn, the greater yin does not gather and the lung qi becomes scorched and full. If one goes against the qi of winter, the lesser yin does not store and the kidney qi sinks alone.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "If one goes against the qi of spring ... the liver qi alters within"] "Arise" means to stir and come out. If the yang qi does not come out but is pent up within in the liver, the liver qi becomes confused and mixed, alters and is harmed.

[On "If one goes against the qi of summer ... the heart qi becomes hollow within"] "Grow" means to flourish outwardly; "hollow" means empty in the middle. When yang does not flourish outward but presses inward on the heart, the stifling heat consumes it from within; therefore the heart becomes empty.

[On "If one goes against the qi of autumn ... the lung qi becomes scorched and full"] "Gather" means to gather in and contain; *jiao* (焦) refers to the upper burner. The greater yin moves the qi and governs the transformations of the upper burner; therefore the lung qi does not gather and the upper burner becomes full.
Song editors: For 焦満 Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 進満; the *Jiayi jing* and the *Taisu* write 焦満.

[On "If one goes against the qi of winter ... the kidney qi sinks alone"] "Sink" means to sink and lie hidden. The qi of the lesser yin communicates inwardly with the kidney; therefore if the lesser yin does not lie hidden, the kidney qi sinks alone.
Song editors: For 独沈 the *Taisu* writes 沈濁 "sinks and becomes turbid".

## Notes

The changes that occur when one goes against the qi of the four seasons, in the yin and yang of each season and in the corresponding organ (liver, heart, lung, kidney).
- Spring is paired with lesser yang and the liver, summer with greater yang and the heart, autumn with greater yin and the lung, and winter with lesser yin and the kidney.
- Wikisource (the plain text) writes lesser yin for autumn and greater yin for winter (the reverse of the base text and the other sources).
- "Scorched and full": Wang Bing takes 焦 as the upper burner. According to the Song editors, Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 進満.
- "Sinks alone": according to the Song editors, the *Taisu* writes 沈濁.
