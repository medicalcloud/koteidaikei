# 素問 四気調神大論篇第二 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The three months of autumn are called "settling of forms". The qi of heaven becomes brisk and the qi of earth becomes clear. Go to bed early and rise early, getting up with the rooster. Let the will be calm and peaceful so as to soften the punishment of autumn; gather in and contain the spirit qi so as to let the autumn qi be even; do not turn the will outward, and let the lung qi be clear. This is the response to the qi of autumn and the way of nourishing gathering. To go against it harms the lung: in winter there will be *sunxie* (undigested diarrhoea), and little will be left to support storage.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The three months of autumn are called 'settling of forms'"] The myriad things grew in summer and their flowers and fruit are already formed; their shapes become even and settled when autumn arrives.

[On "The qi of heaven becomes brisk and the qi of earth becomes clear"] "Brisk" means the sound of the wind is sharp; "clear" means the colours of things change.

[On "Go to bed early and rise early, getting up with the rooster"] Fearing to be struck by the cold dew, one goes to bed early; wishing to be calm, one rises early.

[On "Let the will be calm and peaceful so as to soften the punishment of autumn"] If the will is agitated, one is not careful in one's movements; if one is not careful in one's movements, one aids the urgency of autumn's punishment and harms life by following its killing. Therefore let the will be calm and peaceful to soften the punishment of autumn.

[On "gather in and contain the spirit qi so as to let the autumn qi be even"] If the spirit is unsettled, desire flares; if desire flares, the harmonious qi is harmed; once the harmonious qi is harmed, the autumn qi is not evenly attuned. Therefore gather in and contain the spirit qi so as to let the autumn qi be even.

[On "do not turn the will outward, and let the lung qi be clear"] This too follows the gathering and containing of the autumn qi.

[On "This is the response to the qi of autumn and the way of nourishing gathering"] In the node of Beginning of Autumn, in the first five days the cool wind arrives; in the next five days white dew descends; in the last five days the cold cicada sings. Next, in the qi of End of Heat, in the first five days the hawk sacrifices birds; in the next five days heaven and earth begin to grow austere; in the last five days the grain ripens. Next, in mid-autumn, in the node of White Dew, in the first five days the blind wind arrives and the wild geese come; in the next five days the swallow returns; in the last five days the flocks of birds store food. Next, in the qi of Autumn Equinox, in the first five days the thunder withholds its voice; in the next five days the hibernating insects seal their doors and *jingtian* blossoms; in the last five days the waters begin to dry up. Next, in late autumn, in the node of Cold Dew, in the first five days the wild geese come as guests; in the next five days the sparrow enters the sea and becomes a clam; in the last five days the chrysanthemum has yellow flowers. Next, in the qi of Frost's Descent, in the first five days the jackal sacrifices beasts; in the next five days grasses and trees turn yellow and fall; in the last five days the hibernating insects all lie down. All these six qi and eighteen periods are the decree by which the autumn qi rightly gathers and contains; therefore those who nourish life must carefully serve the seasons of heaven.
Song editors: The three characters "*jingtian* blossoms" are not in the present "Yueling".

[On "To go against it harms the lung: in winter there will be *sunxie*, and little will be left to support storage"] "Going against" means doing the opposite and carrying out the decree of summer. The lung corresponds to metal and is dominant in autumn; therefore if the decree of summer is carried out, the [lung] qi is harmed. In winter water is dominant and metal is retired, so the illness breaks out in winter. *Sunxie* is the discharge of food that has not been transformed. Going against autumn harms the lung, so there is little qi to serve the decree of winter storage.

## Notes

How to live during the three months of autumn. Since autumn is the season when the forms of things settle and the qi of heaven and earth tightens, one should go to bed early and rise early, keep the will calm, and gather the spirit qi inward. Going against this harms the lung and leads to *sunxie* (diarrhoea with undigested food) in winter.
- "Settling of forms" (*rongping*): Wang Bing explains it as the shapes of things becoming even and settled.
- "Punishment of autumn": the austere, killing qi of autumn. Wang Bing says that an agitated will only aids it.
- In the base text there are blank spaces after 以緩秋刑 and between 収 and 斂 (they do not affect the translation).
- In Wang Bing's note, the base text writes 巳 in 巳成; it is translated as 已 "already".
- Wang Bing writes simply "the qi is harmed" without "lung"; the translation supplies [lung].
