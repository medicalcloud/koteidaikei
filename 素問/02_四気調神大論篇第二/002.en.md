# 素問 四気調神大論篇第二 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The three months of summer are called "luxuriance and flowering". The qi of heaven and earth intermingle, and the myriad things flower and bear fruit. Go to bed late at night and rise early; do not weary of the [long] days; let the will be without anger; let the blossoms come to flower; let the qi be discharged, as if what you love lay outside. This is the response to the qi of summer and the way of nourishing growth. To go against it harms the heart: in autumn there will be *jienüe* (intermittent fever), little will be left to support gathering, and at the winter solstice there will be grave illness.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The three months of summer are called 'luxuriance and flowering'"] Yang, arising in spring, becomes vast and exuberant by summer; things are born and grow. Hence "luxuriance and flowering". *Fan* means flourishing, abundant; *xiu* means flowering, beautiful.

[On "The qi of heaven and earth intermingle, and the myriad things flower and bear fruit"] This refers to the summer solstice. The *Maiyao jingwei lun* (chapter 17) says: "For forty-five days after the summer solstice, the yin qi rises slightly and the yang qi descends slightly." By this, the qi of heaven and earth intermingle. Yang qi bestows and transforms; yin qi binds and completes. Completion and transformation join, so the myriad things flower and bear fruit. The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* (chapter 5) says: "Yang transforms qi; yin completes form."

[On "let the blossoms come to flower; let the qi be discharged, as if what you love lay outside"] If the yang qi is relaxed, things transform; if the will is broadened, the qi is discharged. When things transform, the blossoms come to flower; when the qi is discharged, the skin and interstices open and communicate. The seasonal decree spreads forth; therefore what one loves also follows yang and lies outside.

[On "This is the response to the qi of summer and the way of nourishing growth"] In the node of Beginning of Summer, in the first five days the mole cricket sings; in the next five days the earthworm comes out; in the last five days *chijian* grows [Song editors: the "Yueling" has "the wild cucumber grows"]. Next, in the qi of Lesser Fullness, in the first five days the mallow blossoms [Song editors: the "Yueling" has "the sow thistle flowers"]; in the next five days the fine grasses die; in the last five days the lesser heat arrives. Next, in midsummer, in the node of Grain in Ear, in the first five days the mantis is born; in the next five days the shrike begins to call; in the last five days the "reverse tongue" falls silent. Next, in the qi of Summer Solstice, in the first five days the deer sheds its antlers; in the next five days the cicada begins to sing; in the last five days *banxia* grows and the hibiscus flourishes. Next, in late summer, in the node of Lesser Heat, in the first five days the warm wind arrives; in the next five days the cricket dwells in the walls; in the last five days the young hawk learns [to fly]. Next, in the qi of Greater Heat, in the first five days rotten grass turns into fireflies; in the next five days the soil is moist and the heat is sultry; in the last five days heavy rains fall in season. All these six qi and eighteen periods are the decree by which the summer qi spreads forth luxuriance and flowering; therefore those who nourish life must respectfully follow the seasons of heaven.
Song editors: "The hibiscus flourishes" is not in the present "Yueling".

[On "To go against it harms the heart ... at the winter solstice there will be grave illness"] "Going against" means doing the opposite and carrying out the decree of winter. *Jie* is the intermittent fever of emaciation. The heart corresponds to fire and is dominant in summer; therefore if the decree of winter is carried out, the heart qi is harmed. In autumn metal is dominant and fire is retired, so the illness breaks out in autumn as intermittent fever. Of the qi of the four seasons, autumn gathers and winter stores; going against summer harms the heart, so there is little qi to serve the decree of autumn gathering. In winter water overcomes fire, so there is grave illness at the time of the winter solstice.

## Notes

How to live during the three months of summer. Since summer is the season when the qi of heaven and earth intermingle and all things flower and fruit, one should go to bed late and rise early, not resent the long days, avoid anger, and let the qi flow outward. Going against this harms the heart, bringing intermittent fever in autumn and grave illness at the winter solstice.
- "Luxuriance and flowering" (*fanxiu*): Wang Bing explains it as flourishing and flowering.
- "As if what you love lay outside": Wang Bing takes it as the qi following yang and turning outward.
- *Jienüe*: Wang Bing calls it "the intermittent fever of emaciation". The form 𭼠 in his note is the base text's character, translated as a variant of 瘧.
- In Wang Bing's note, 揚 is written in the base text with a slightly different glyph (an image in the transcription).
- Daizhige (the plain text) writes 為 for 謂 and 勿 for 無 in "without anger".
