# 素問 四気調神大論篇第二 §8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The yin and yang of the four seasons are the root and foundation of the myriad things. Therefore the sages nourish yang in spring and summer and nourish yin in autumn and winter, so as to follow the root; thus, together with the myriad things, they sink and float at the gate of birth and growth. To go against the root is to cut down the foundation and ruin the truth.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The yin and yang of the four seasons are the root and foundation of the myriad things"] The order of the seasons revolves, yin and yang change, and heaven and earth combine their qi to give birth to and rear the myriad things; therefore the roots of all things return here.

[On "Therefore the sages nourish yang in spring and summer and nourish yin in autumn and winter, so as to follow the root"] Yang qi is rooted in yin and yin qi is rooted in yang. Without yin, yang has no means to arise; without yang, yin has no means to transform. If yin is preserved, the yang qi does not go to extremes; if yang is preserved, the yin qi is not exhausted. In spring eat cool things and in summer cold things to nourish yang; in autumn eat warm things and in winter hot things to nourish yin. Whoever would nourish the shoots must strengthen the root; whoever cuts below will wither what is above. Therefore by regulating in this way and following the root, the two qi are constantly preserved — surely because the root is firm. The morning and evening meals of each day of a hundred marks should also be like this.

[On "thus, together with the myriad things, they sink and float at the gate of birth and growth"] That the sages have no strange illnesses and their vital qi is not exhausted is because they follow the root.

[On "To go against the root is to cut down the foundation and ruin the truth"] This is losing the way of the yin and yang of the four seasons.

## Notes

Since the yin and yang of the four seasons are the root of all things, the sages nourish yang in spring and summer and yin in autumn and winter, following that root.
- "Nourish yang in spring and summer, nourish yin in autumn and winter": Wang Bing explains that yang is rooted in yin and yin in yang, and that one nourishes yang by eating cool and cold things in spring and summer, and yin by eating warm and hot things in autumn and winter. Later commentators offer many other interpretations of this phrase.
