# 素問 四気調神大論篇第二 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

If their intermingling and communication is not manifest, the life of the myriad things is therefore not bestowed; when it is not bestowed, many fine trees die. If the noxious qi is not dispersed, wind and rain are without measure, and the white dew does not descend, then [plants] become stifled and withered and do not flourish. Thieving winds come repeatedly and violent rains arise repeatedly; heaven, earth and the four seasons do not sustain one another and lose the Way together; then [life] is cut off and extinguished before it has lasted long. Only the sages follow [the seasons]; therefore their bodies have no strange illnesses, they do not lose the myriad things, and their vital qi is not exhausted.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "If their intermingling and communication is not manifest ... many fine trees die"] The clouds and mists do not transform their subtle essence, and rain and dew do not moisten the plains and marshes; this means the qi of heaven does not descend and the qi of earth does not rise. Once the way of change is deficient, the source of birth and growth is extinguished; thus the myriad things are born with nothing from which to receive their life. Among those that die, the fine trees are the first to respond; hence it says many fine trees die. "Fine" refers to noted fruit trees and rare trees. "Manifest" means displaying their condition. The "Xici" of the *Yi* says: "Heaven and earth intermingle richly, and the myriad things are transformed and refined." If their intermingling is not manifest, there is obstruction. The *Yi* says: "Heaven and earth not intermingling is *Pi* (Obstruction)."

[On "If the noxious qi is not dispersed ... do not flourish"] "Noxious" means harmful qi; "dispersed" means scattered and released; "measure" means degree; *yu* (菀) means accumulated; *gao* (槁) means withered. It says that the harmful qi lies hidden and is not dispersed, wind and rain are without measure, there is much breaking and damage, and withered trees accumulate and do not flourish in spring. It is not only things that meet with this; people who depart from the Way also meet with it. Hence the text below says:

[On "heaven, earth and the four seasons do not sustain one another ... before it has lasted long"] If one does not follow the harmony of the four seasons, repeatedly violates the harm of the eight winds, and loses the Way, then the qi of heavenly truth comes to extinction without reaching a long span. *Yang* (央) means long, distant.

[On "Only the sages follow [the seasons] ... their vital qi is not exhausted"] It is not that the Way is far from people; it is that people's hearts are far from the Way. Only the sage's heart accords with the Way; therefore their life span is without end. "Follow" is like "comply", meaning to comply with the decrees of the four seasons. The decrees of the four seasons must not be gone against; if they are gone against, the five zang are harmed within and other illnesses arise.

## Notes

If the qi of heaven and earth do not intermingle, things do not grow, fine trees die and plants wither. Likewise, people who are not in harmony with heaven, earth and the four seasons and lose the Way die early. Only the sages follow the seasons, and so they have no illness and their vital qi is not exhausted.
- "Before it has lasted long" (*wei yang*): Wang Bing glosses 央 as "long".
- *Yu gao* (菀藁): Wang Bing explains 菀 as accumulated and 槁 as withered (the base text has 藁, the note 槁). Other sources write 槁 or 稾.
- Wikisource (the plain text) writes 苛病 "grave illness" for 奇病 "strange illness"; Daizhige (*Huangdi neijing suwen*) writes 豪雨 for 暴雨.
- The last sentence of Wang Bing's note leads into the next unit (going against the seasons harms the five zang).
