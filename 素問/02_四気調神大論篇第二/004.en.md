# 素問 四気調神大論篇第二 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The three months of winter are called "closing and storing". Water freezes and the earth cracks; do not disturb the yang. Go to bed early and rise late, always waiting for the sunlight. Let the will be as if hidden, as if concealed, as if harbouring a private thought, as if already having gained something. Leave the cold and approach the warmth; do not let [sweat] leak from the skin, so that the qi is not repeatedly snatched away. This is the response to the qi of winter and the way of nourishing storage. To go against it harms the kidney: in spring there will be *weijue* (limpness and reversal), and little will be left to support generation.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The three months of winter are called 'closing and storing'"] Grasses and trees wither, hibernating insects go into the earth, doors are shut and sealed, and the yang qi lies hidden and stored.

[On "Water freezes and the earth cracks; do not disturb the yang"] The yang qi sinks below, water freezes and the earth cracks; therefore one should keep close and secure and not wish for vexation or toil. "Disturb" means vex and toil.

[On "Go to bed early and rise late, always waiting for the sunlight"] This is to avoid the cold.

[On "Let the will be as if hidden ... as if already having gained something"] All of this means not wishing to go out recklessly and be exposed to cold qi. Hence the text below says:

[On "Leave the cold and approach the warmth; do not let [sweat] leak from the skin, so that the qi is not repeatedly snatched away"] "Leave the cold and approach the warmth" means staying in an inner room. The *Lingshu jing* says: "In winter [the qi] is in the bones; the hibernating insects keep close and secure, and the gentleman stays in his rooms." "Do not let leak from the skin" means do not sweat. If one sweats, the yang qi is discharged; if the yang qi is discharged, it is repeatedly pressed and snatched away by the cold qi. *Qi* (亟) means repeatedly.

[On "This is the response to the qi of winter and the way of nourishing storage"] In the node of Beginning of Winter, in the first five days water begins to freeze; in the next five days the earth begins to freeze; in the last five days the pheasant enters the sea and becomes a giant clam. Next, in the qi of Lesser Snow, in the first five days the rainbow hides and is not seen; in the next five days the qi of heaven rises and the qi of earth descends; in the last five days all is closed and sealed and winter is formed. Next, in midwinter, in the node of Greater Snow, in the first five days the ice grows ever thicker and the earth begins to crack, and the *he* bird does not call; in the next five days the tiger begins to mate; in the last five days rue begins to grow and the *li* plant pushes out. Next, in the qi of Winter Solstice, in the first five days the earthworms knot together; in the next five days the elk sheds its antlers; in the last five days the springs stir. Next, in late winter, in the node of Lesser Cold, in the first five days the wild geese turn north; in the next five days the birds of prey become fierce and swift; in the last five days the waters and marshes freeze solid. All these six qi and eighteen periods are the decree by which the winter qi rightly nourishes and stores; therefore those who nourish life must carefully serve the seasons of heaven.

[On "To go against it harms the kidney: in spring there will be *weijue*, and little will be left to support generation"] "Going against" means doing the opposite and carrying out the decree of summer. The kidney corresponds to water and is dominant in winter; therefore if the decree of summer is carried out, the kidney qi is harmed. In spring wood is dominant and water is retired, so the illness breaks out in spring. Going against winter harms the kidney, so there is little qi to serve the decree of spring generation.

## Notes

How to live during the three months of winter. Since winter is the season when the yang qi lies hidden and stored, one should not disturb the yang, go to bed early and rise after sunrise, keep the will hidden within, stay warm and avoid sweating. Going against this harms the kidney and leads to *weijue* in spring.
- "Closing and storing" (*bicang*): Wang Bing explains it as all things being shut and the yang qi lying hidden and stored.
- "So that the qi is not repeatedly snatched away": Wang Bing explains that sweating discharges yang qi, which is then repeatedly snatched away by the cold. He reads 亟 as "repeatedly".
- Wang Bing's list ends with the node of Lesser Cold; the three periods of Greater Cold are missing (as in the base text).
- Wang Bing describes going against winter also as "carrying out the decree of summer" (the same expression as for autumn).
- Daizhige (the plain text) writes 為 for 謂, 勿 for 無 in "do not disturb", and 極 for 亟.
