# 素問 四気調神大論篇第二 §9 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Therefore yin and yang and the four seasons are the end and beginning of the myriad things and the root of death and life. Go against them and calamity arises; follow them and grave illness does not occur. This is called attaining the Way. As for the Way, the sages practise it, while the foolish merely wear it [as an ornament]. Follow yin and yang and you live; go against them and you die. Follow them and there is order; go against them and there is disorder. To turn compliance into opposition is called inner resistance.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "follow them and grave illness does not occur. This is called attaining the Way"] This means attaining the way of nourishing life. 苛 *ke* means grave.

[On "As for the Way, the sages practise it, while the foolish merely wear it"] The sage's heart accords with the Way; therefore they diligently practise it. The nature of the foolish clings to delusion; therefore they merely wear it. The *Laozi* says: "One who follows the Way becomes one with the Way; one who follows virtue becomes one with virtue; one who follows loss becomes one with loss. Becoming one with the Way, the Way also gains them; becoming one with virtue, virtue also gains them; becoming one with loss, loss also gains them." The foolish have not yet become one with the Way and virtue; they may be called those who have lost the Way.

[On "To turn compliance into opposition is called inner resistance"] *Ge* (格) means to resist. It means that the inner nature resists the Way of heaven.

## Notes

To follow yin and yang and the four seasons is to attain the Way; following them one lives, going against them one dies.
- "The foolish merely wear it": Wang Bing explains that the foolish only wear the Way and do not practise it. Some read 佩 as 背 "turn one's back on".
- "Inner resistance" (*neige*): Wang Bing glosses 格 as "resist", meaning that the inner nature resists the Way of heaven.
- Daizhige (the plain text) has 生 before "the root of death and life".
