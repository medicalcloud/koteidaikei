# 素問 四気調神大論篇第二 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The three months of spring are called "issuing forth and spreading out". Heaven and earth both give birth, and the myriad things flourish thereby. Go to bed late at night and rise early; stroll at ease in the courtyard; let down your hair and relax your body, so as to let the will come to life. Give life and do not kill; give and do not take away; reward and do not punish. This is the response to the qi of spring and the way of nourishing generation. To go against it harms the liver: in summer there will be cold disorders, and little will be left to support growth.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The three months of spring are called 'issuing forth and spreading out'"] In spring the yang rises and the qi that lay hidden issues forth and disperses, giving birth to and rearing all things, which spread out their forms. Hence it is called "issuing forth and spreading out". "The three months of spring" is named according to the nodal terms; summer, autumn and winter are likewise.

[On "Heaven and earth both give birth, and the myriad things flourish thereby"] The qi of heaven is warm and the qi of earth issues forth; warmth and issuing combine, so the myriad things grow and flourish.

[On "Go to bed late at night and rise early; stroll at ease in the courtyard"] Warm qi arises and cold qi disperses; therefore go to bed at night, rise early, and stroll at ease in the courtyard.

[On "let down your hair and relax your body, so as to let the will come to life"] This models itself on the image [of heaven]. The qi of spring issues forth at the beginning of all things; therefore let down the hair and relax the body so as to let the will and intention issue forth.

[On "Give life and do not kill; give and do not take away; reward and do not punish"] The qi of spring brings forth life and bestows without seeking a return; therefore those who nourish life must follow the season.

[On "This is the response to the qi of spring and the way of nourishing generation"] This is what is called according with the order of the seasons. In the node of Beginning of Spring, in the first five days the east wind thaws the ice; in the next five days the hibernating insects begin to stir; in the last five days the fish rise up to the ice. Next, in the qi of Rain Water, in the first five days the otter sacrifices fish; in the next five days the wild geese come; in the last five days grasses and trees sprout. Next, in mid-spring, in the node of Waking of Insects, in the first five days the little peach blossoms [Song editors: for "the little peach blossoms" the "Yueling" has "the peach begins to blossom"]; in the next five days the oriole sings; in the last five days the hawk turns into a dove. Next, in the qi of Spring Equinox, in the first five days the swallow arrives; in the next five days the thunder sounds and the peony flourishes; in the last five days lightning begins. Next, in late spring, in the node of Clear and Bright, in the first five days the paulownia begins to blossom; in the next five days the field mouse turns into a quail and the tree peony blossoms; in the last five days the rainbow begins to appear. Next, in the qi of Grain Rain, in the first five days duckweed begins to grow; in the next five days the cooing dove brushes its feathers; in the last five days the hoopoe alights on the mulberry. All these six qi and eighteen periods are the spring yang spreading the decree of generation; therefore those who nourish life must carefully serve the seasons of heaven.
Song editors: "The peony flourishes" and "the tree peony blossoms" are not in the present "Yueling".

[On "To go against it harms the liver: in summer there will be cold disorders, and little will be left to support growth"] "Going against" means doing the opposite and carrying out the decree of autumn. The liver corresponds to wood and is dominant in spring; therefore if the decree of autumn is carried out, the liver qi is harmed. In summer fire is dominant and wood is retired, so the illness arises in summer. Of the qi of the four seasons, spring generates and summer grows; going against spring harms the liver, so there is little qi to serve the decree of summer growth.

## Notes

The opening of the chapter, on how to live during the three months of spring. Since spring is the season when all things are born, one should go to bed late and rise early, let body and mind expand, and act in the direction of giving life, giving and rewarding. Going against this harms the liver, and illness comes in summer.
- "Issuing forth and spreading out" (*fachen*): Wang Bing explains it as all things spreading out their forms.
- "Go to bed late at night" contrasts with autumn's "go to bed early and rise early".
- Wang Bing lists the six nodal terms from Beginning of Spring to Grain Rain and the natural signs of each five-day period (eighteen periods), corresponding to the seventy-two periods of the "Yueling" of the *Liji* and similar texts.
- "Cold disorders in summer": Wang Bing explains that harm to the liver in spring causes illness in summer; he does not specify the disorder.
- Daizhige (the plain text) writes 為 for 謂 and has 実 after 夏為寒変.
