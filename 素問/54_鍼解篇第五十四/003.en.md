# 素問 鍼解篇第五十四 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

"In needling repletion, wait for it to become depleted" means retaining the needle and removing it only when the yin qi arrives abundantly. "In needling depletion, wait for it to become replete" means removing the needle only when the yang qi arrives abundantly and it is hot under the needle. "Once the channel qi has arrived, guard it carefully and do not lose it" means not changing. "Depth lies in the will" means knowing whether the illness is inside or outside. "Near and far are as one" means the signs are equal whether deep or shallow. "As if standing over a deep abyss" means not daring to slacken. "The hand as if grasping a tiger" means wanting it to be firm. "The spirit not occupied with the multitude of things" means calming the will to observe the patient without looking left or right. "In principle not inserting obliquely" means wanting it upright and straight. "Always set right the spirit" means looking into the patient's eyes to control the spirit and let the qi move easily.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "In needling depletion, wait for it to become replete ... removing the needle"] It says the arrival of qi with effect is the essential.

[On "Once the channel qi has arrived ... means not changing"] 変 means alter; 更 means change; both mean changing the method. It says that once the arrival of qi is obtained, one must guard it carefully; changing the method would instead invite harm.

[On "Depth lies in the will ... inside or outside"] 志 is written 意 in one version; will and intention are both what is used in applying the needle.

[On "Near and far are as one ... equal whether deep or shallow"] It says that although the qi is near or far, its measurement always depends on the arrival of qi with effect.

[On "As if standing over a deep abyss means not daring to slacken"] It says that in observing the qi to supplement or drain, one should be as if standing over a deep abyss, not daring to be careless and lose the method of supplementing and draining.

[On "The hand as if grasping a tiger means wanting it to be firm"] 壮 means holding the needle firmly and steadily. The *Zhenjing* says, "The way of holding the needle: firmness is the substance" — this is the meaning.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 宝 for 実.

[On "The spirit not occupied with the multitude of things ... without looking left or right"] When the eyes cease to look about and the mind concentrates on one task, its use will surely hit the mark without confusion or error.
Song editors: On examination, from "In needling repletion, wait for it to become depleted" to here also appears in the *Baoming quanxing lun*, which this again explains; they too illuminate each other.

[On "In principle not inserting obliquely means wanting it upright and straight"] With the fingers upright, needle straight, the needle not leaning left or right.

[On "Always set right the spirit ... let the qi move easily"] Examine the patient's spirit so that it does not scatter; then the qi serves the spirit and inside and outside are easily regulated.

## Notes

Explains the needling phrases of the *Baoming quanxing lun* (chapter 25).
