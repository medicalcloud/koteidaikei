# 素問 鍼解篇第五十四 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "'In needling depletion, fill it' means heat under the needle; when the qi is replete there is heat. 'When full, drain it' means cold under the needle; when the qi is depleted there is cold. 'When there is long-standing accumulation, remove it' means letting out bad blood. 'When evil prevails, deplete it' means withdrawing the needle without pressing. 'Slow then quick makes replete' means withdrawing the needle slowly and pressing quickly. 'Quick then slow makes depleted' means withdrawing the needle quickly and pressing slowly. 'Speaking of repletion and depletion' refers to the amount of cold and warm qi. 'As if absent, as if present' means it is too quick to be known. 'Examining after and before' means knowing the order of the illness. 'Making depletion or repletion' means the practitioner does not lose the method. 'As if gaining, as if losing' means departing from the method. Among the essentials of depletion and repletion, the nine needles are most marvellous because each has its suitable use. 'The time of supplementing and draining' means matching the opening and closing of the qi. 'The nine needles have different names and forms' means each needle fully serves what should be supplemented or drained.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "'When there is long-standing accumulation, remove it' means letting out bad blood"] 菀 means accumulate; 陳 means long-standing; 除 means remove. It says that blood accumulated for a long time in the network vessels is removed by needling.

[On "'When evil prevails, deplete it' means withdrawing the needle without pressing"] Evil is the name for what is not upright; what is not the qi of the channel itself is called evil; it does not mean the prevailing of ghostly poisons or spectral evils. If one withdraws the needle without pressing, the point stays open for a while; therefore the channel is depleted and the evil qi is released.

[On "'Slow then quick makes replete' ... pressing slowly"] "Withdrawing slowly" means withdrawing only after the channel qi has been obtained for a long time. "Pressing quickly" means pressing quickly as soon as the needle leaves the point; then the true qi does not leak and the qi of the channel is whole; hence "slow then quick makes replete". "Withdrawing the needle quickly" means withdrawing it quickly as soon as the needle in the point has reached the channel. "Pressing slowly" means pressing slowly and gently after the needle leaves the point; then the evil qi can leak and the essential qi becomes firm again; hence "quick then slow makes depleted".

[On "'Speaking of repletion and depletion' refers to the amount of cold and warm qi"] Cold and warm means the yin and yang qi of the channels.

[On "'As if absent, as if present' means it is too quick to be known"] It says it is obscure and cannot be known immediately. Because it cannot be known immediately, it is "as if absent"; because the spirit understands clearly, it is "as if present".

[On "'Examining after and before' means knowing the order of the illness"] Knowing the order of the illness, one then supplements or drains.

[On "'Making depletion or repletion' means the practitioner does not lose the method"] The *Zhenjing* says, "Once the channel qi has arrived, guard it carefully and do not lose it" — this is what is meant.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* reads "as if present, as if gone, making depletion or repletion".

[On "'As if gaining, as if losing' means departing from the method"] Supplementing and draining recklessly departs from and disorders the great channels. Wrongly supplementing the replete makes it seem a gain; wrongly draining the depleted makes it seem a loss; hence "as if gaining, as if losing". The *Zhenjing* says, "Do not fill the full; do not deplete the depleted" — this is the warning.
Song editors: On examination, from the beginning of the chapter to here, the classic's text is the same as the *Jiuzhen jie* chapter of the *Taisu*, but the explanations differ; the two classics illuminate each other.

[On "Among the essentials ... each has its suitable use"] For heat in the head and body, the arrowhead needle is suitable; for qi filling the divisions of the flesh, the round needle; for depleted and scant vessel qi, the spoon needle; for draining heat, letting blood and releasing fixed illness, the sharp-edged needle; for breaking abscesses and letting out pus and blood, the 䤵 (sword) needle; for regulating yin and yang and removing sudden impediment, the round-sharp needle; for painful impediment in the channels and network vessels, the filiform needle; for impediment lodged deep in the clefts of the bones and between the joints of the lumbar spine, the long needle; for depleting wind lodged in the clefts of the bones and the skin, the large needle. This is "each has its suitable use".
Song editors: Another edition writes 鈹 for 䤵.

[On "'The time of supplementing and draining' means matching the opening and closing of the qi"] When the qi corresponds to the hour it is called opening; when it has passed or has not yet arrived, closing. As to the hours: when the water falls one mark, the person's qi is in the greater yang; two marks, in the lesser yang; three marks, in the *yangming*; four marks, in the yin division; as the water falls without ceasing, the qi moves without ceasing. Thus what corresponds to the mark is called opening, and what has passed or not yet arrived is called closing. The *Zhenjing* says, "Carefully observe where the qi is and needle it; this is called meeting the time." This is what is meant by the time of supplementing and draining.
Song editors: On examination, from the beginning of the chapter to here the text comes from the *Lingshu jing*, and the *Suwen* explains it; they illuminate each other. The *Jiayi jing* says, "The time of supplementing and draining is done with the needle"; here the four characters 以鍼為之 have dropped out.

[On "'The nine needles have different names and forms' ... supplemented or drained"] "Different forms" means their lengths and points differ. "Fully serves the supplementing and draining" means each is used according to its treatment.
Song editors: The forms of the nine needles are now given in the *Jiayi jing*.

## Notes

Explains one by one the phrases of an ancient needling text (close to the *Jiuzhen shi'er yuan* and *Xiaozhen jie* chapters of the *Lingshu*). Qibo's speech continues to unit 004.
- 除 in Wang Bing's 除出 is presumably an error for 徐 "slowly" from the text; the translation gives 徐.
- Wang Bing's account of the uses of the nine needles is close to descriptions such as the *Jiuzhen lun* of the *Lingshu*.
