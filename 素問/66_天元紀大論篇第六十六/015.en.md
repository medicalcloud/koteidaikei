# 素問 天元紀大論篇第六十六 §15 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Emperor said: "One who speaks well of beginnings must arrive at the end; one who speaks well of the near must know the far. Then the ultimate numbers are exhausted and the way is free of confusion; this is called clarity. I wish you, Master, to work it out and set it in order, giving it structure, concise yet not lacking, lasting without break, easy to use and hard to forget, as its guiding principles. I wish to hear all the essentials of the ultimate numbers."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "One who speaks well of beginnings ... must know the far"] The art of numbers is clear and its application without error; hence in speaking of far and near, beginning and end, there is no mistake.

[On "as its guiding principles ... the essentials of the ultimate numbers"] 簡 means concise and essential; 匱 means lacking; 久 means far-reaching; 要 means the pivot.

## Notes

The Emperor asks for the essentials of the ultimate numbers to be set out in order.
- 故故遠近於言 in Wang Bing's note presumably contains a doubled or erroneous character; the translation follows the inferred sense.
