# 素問 天元紀大論篇第六十六 §16 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Guiyuqu said: "How illuminating the question! How clear the way! It is like the drum answering the drumstick, the echo answering the voice. I have heard this: in *jia* and *ji* years the earth movement governs; in *yi* and *geng* years the metal movement governs; in *bing* and *xin* years the water movement governs; in *ding* and *ren* years the wood movement governs; in *wu* and *gui* years the fire movement governs."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "It is like the drum answering the drumstick, the echo answering the voice"] 桴 is the drumstick; 響 is what answers the voice.

[On "I have heard this: in *jia* and *ji* years ... the fire movement governs"] At the great beginning, when heaven and earth first separated and yin and yang divided their positions, heaven divided the five qi and earth arrayed the five phases. The five phases fixed their positions and spread their government in the four directions; the five qi divided and flowed, scattering to support the ten stems. Then yellow qi lay across *jia* and 巳 [*ji*], white qi across *yi* and *geng*, black qi across *bing* and *xin*, green qi across *ding* and *ren*, and red qi across *wu* and *gui*. So *jia* and 巳 [*ji*] correspond to the earth movement, *yi* and *geng* to metal, *bing* and *xin* to water, *ding* and *ren* to wood, *wu* and *gui* to fire. The sages of 大 [high] antiquity observed the qi and wrote it in the heavenly tablets; the worthy respectfully kept it to record the heavenly origin. The meaning is fully given in the treatises below.
Song editors: On examination, the movements have excess, deficiency and balanced qi. *Jia*, *geng*, *bing*, *ren* and *wu* govern excess; *yi*, *xin*, *ding*, *gui* and 巳 [*ji*] govern deficiency. This is the general rule. The methods of determining balanced qi are not uniform; they are as given in the various chapters.

## Notes

The correspondence of the ten stems and the five movements.
- 甲巳 and 巳 in the main text and notes are read as 己 (*ji*).
