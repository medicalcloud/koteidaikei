# 素問 天元紀大論篇第六十六 §18 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Guiyuqu said: "In *zi* and *wu* years the lesser yin appears above; in *chou* and *wei* years the greater yin appears above; in *yin* and *shen* years the lesser yang appears above; in *mao* and *you* years the *yangming* appears above; in *chen* and *xu* years the greater yang appears above; in *si* and *hai* years the *jueyin* appears above. The lesser yin is the so-called head; the *jueyin* is the so-called end. Above the *jueyin*, wind qi governs; above the lesser yin, heat qi governs; above the greater yin, damp qi governs; above the lesser yang, ministerial fire governs; above the *yangming*, dry qi governs; above the greater yang, cold qi governs. These are the so-called roots; they are called the six origins."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The lesser yin is the so-called head; the *jueyin* is the so-called end"] 標 means the head; 終 means coinciding with the end of the three *jia* or six *jia*.
Song editors: On examination, the years *wu*, *wei*, *yin*, *you*, *xu* and *hai* are proper transformation, the reality of properly controlling the transformation decrees; the years *zi*, *chou*, *shen*, *mao*, *chen* and *si* are opposite transformation, the emptiness of controlling the transformation decrees from the opposite side. This is the general rule.

[On "Above the *jueyin*, wind qi governs ... they are called the six origins"] The three yin and three yang are the branches; cold, summer heat, dryness, damp, wind and fire are the roots; hence "the so-called roots". The true original qi of heaven divides into six transformations to govern the generating and completing function of the origin of earth. Examining their application, the six transformations differ; tracing them to their source, they are just the one qi of the true origin; hence "the six origins".
Song editors: Another edition writes 天元 for 六元.

## Notes

The correspondence of the twelve branches and the three yin and three yang (controllers of heaven), and the branch and root of the six qi.
- 己亥 in the main text is read as 巳亥 (*si* and *hai*), as a pair of branches.
