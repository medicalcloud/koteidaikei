# 素問 天元紀大論篇第六十六 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Huangdi asked: "Heaven has the five phases, which preside over the five positions and thereby produce cold, summer heat, dryness, damp and wind. Humans have the five depots, which transform the five qi and thereby produce joy, anger, thought, worry and fear. The treatise says: 'The five movements succeed one another and each governs; when the days of the year are completed, they circle round and begin again.' This I already know. I wish to hear how they are combined with the indications of the three yin and three yang."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Heaven has the five phases ... joy, anger, thought, worry and fear"] 御 means presiding over; 化 means generating and transforming. The true qi of heaven reaches everywhere; although vessels and images differ, their participation and correspondence are one.
Song editors: The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* says "joy, anger, sorrow, worry and fear". The two treatises differ because thought belongs to the spleen, by which the four [other] depots are all completed, and sorrow overcomes anger; so the two treatises complement each other.

[On "The treatise says ... how they are combined with the indications of the three yin and three yang"] The treatise is the *Liujie zangxiang lun*. The movements are the five movements in which the five phases correspond to heaven, each circling three hundred and sixty-five days as a period; hence "when the days of the year are completed, they circle round and begin again". Since six is combined with five, the numbers do not yet match; hence the question.

## Notes

The opening question of the chapter, on combining the five movements with the three yin and three yang (six qi). This chapter begins the series of treatises on the movements and qi (the so-called seven great treatises); the interlocutor is Guiyuqu.
