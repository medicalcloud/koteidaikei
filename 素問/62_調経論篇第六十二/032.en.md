# 素問 調経論篇第六十二 §32 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "When will is in surplus, the abdomen is distended with diarrhea of undigested food; when insufficient, there is reversal. When blood and qi have not yet merged and the five depots are stable, there is movement in the bone joints."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When will is in surplus ... there is reversal"] This is the depot of the kidneys. The *Zhenjing* says: "The kidneys store essence, and essence houses the will. When kidney qi is depleted there is reversal; when replete, distension." Distension means swelling up; reversal means moving backward and rushing upward. The foot lesser yin vessel runs downward, but when it makes the qi insufficient, [the qi] follows the *chong* vessel backward and rushes upward.

[On "When blood and qi have not yet merged ... movement in the bone joints"] The kidneys correspond to the bones. So when a pathogen presses on the bones, the bone joints 叚-move, or it is as if something in the joints were drumming them.

## Notes

The symptoms of surplus and insufficiency of will (kidneys), and its slight illness.
- 精含志 in the *Zhenjing* quoted by Wang Bing reads 精舍志 in the *Lingshu*, "Benshen" chapter; the translation follows 舍.
- 叚 in Wang Bing's 叚動 is obscure and may be an error; not checked.
