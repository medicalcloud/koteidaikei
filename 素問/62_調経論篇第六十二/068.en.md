# 素問 調経論篇第六十二 §68 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The five depots are naturally paired with the six bowels as exterior and interior, and the channels, network vessels, limbs and joints each produce depletion and repletion. Regulate according to where the illness resides. When the illness is in the vessels, regulate the blood; when in the blood, regulate the network vessels; when in the qi, regulate the defensive; when in the flesh, regulate the divisions of the flesh; when in the sinews, regulate the sinews; when in the bones, regulate the bones. Use the heated needle to needle forcibly below and where it is tight. When the illness is in the bones, use the fire needle and medicinal hot compresses. When the illness is such that one does not know where it hurts, the two *qiao* [vessels] are best. When the body has pain but the nine indicators show no illness, use crosswise needling of the network vessels. When the pain is on the left and the right pulse is ill, use great crosswise needling. One must carefully examine the nine indicators; then the way of the needle is complete."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "each produce depletion and repletion. Regulate according to where the illness resides"] Regulate according to the channel qi, limbs and joints on the left and right.

[On "When the illness is in the vessels, regulate the blood"] The vessels are the residence of blood. When the vessels are replete, the blood is replete; when the vessels are depleted, the blood is depleted. So when the vessels are ill, regulate the blood.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition and the *Jiayi jing* say "when the illness is in the blood, regulate the vessels".

[On "when in the blood, regulate the network vessels"] When the blood is ill, the network vessels change; so regulate at the network vessels.

[On "when in the qi, regulate the defensive"] The defensive governs qi; so when the qi is ill, regulate the defensive.

[On "when in the flesh, regulate the divisions of the flesh"] Take it according to cold and heat.

[On "when in the sinews, regulate the sinews"] Needle and apply hot compresses as suits tightness or slackness.

[On "when in the bones, regulate the bones"] Regulate it by examining severity.

[On "Use the heated needle to needle forcibly below and where it is tight"] This is the method for regulating the sinews. When the sinews are tight, heat the needle and needle forcibly.

[On "When the illness is in the bones, use the fire needle and medicinal hot compresses"] This is the method for regulating the bones. 焠鍼 is the fire needle.

[On "When the illness is such that one does not know where it hurts, the two *qiao* [vessels] are best"] The two *qiao* are the yin and yang *qiao* vessels. The *yinqiao* vessel emerges at Zhaohai; the *yangqiao* vessel emerges at Shenmai. Shenmai is in the depression below the outer ankle, the size of a fingernail. [Song editors: the note to the *Ci yaotong* says "five *fen* below the ankle".] One may needle three body-*fen* and 㽞 [retain] six breaths; for moxibustion, three cones. Zhaohai is below the inner ankle; one may needle four body-*fen* and retain six breaths; for moxibustion, three cones.

[On "When the body has pain but the nine indicators show no illness, use crosswise needling"] 莫病 means no illness. Crosswise needling needles the network vessels: for pain on the left, needle the right; for pain on the right, needle the left.

[On "When the pain is on the left and the right pulse is ill, use great crosswise needling"] Great crosswise needling needles the channels: for pain on the left, needle the right; for pain on the right, needle the left.

## Notes

The chapter ends with regulation by the location of illness, based on the exterior–interior pairing of the five depots and six bowels, and with crosswise and great crosswise needling.
- Wang Bing takes 燔鍼劫刺 as the method for regulating the sinews and 焠鍼薬熨 as that for the bones.
- 殆知閣 (重广补注黄帝内经素问) places a 巻十八 break at the end of this unit, and 殆知閣 (黄帝内经素问) places sound glosses (隧音遂 etc.) and 巻第十八. These are those sources' juan divisions and appended sound glosses.
