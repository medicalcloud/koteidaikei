# 素問 調経論篇第六十二 §38 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "When qi and blood merge, yin and yang tilt toward each other; qi becomes disordered in the defensive, blood counterflows in the channels, blood and qi leave their places, and one is replete and the other depleted. When blood merges in the yin and qi merges in the yang, there is fright and mania. When blood merges in the yang and qi merges in the yin, there is heat in the center. When blood merges above and qi merges below, the heart is vexed and oppressed and one is prone to anger. When blood merges below and qi merges above, there is disorder and forgetfulness."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "qi becomes disordered in the defensive ... the other depleted"] The defensive travels outside the vessels, so qi is disordered in the defensive; blood travels inside the channels, so blood counterflows in the channels. Since blood and qi are not in harmony, one is depleted and the other replete.

[On "When blood merges in the yin and qi merges in the yang, there is fright and mania"] When qi merges in the yang, yang qi is abundant outside; hence fright and mania.

[On "When blood merges in the yang and qi merges in the yin, there is heat in the center"] When qi merges in the yin, yang qi is abundant inside; hence heat in the center. 炅 means heat.

[On "When blood merges below and qi merges above, there is disorder and forgetfulness"] Above means above the diaphragm; below means below the diaphragm.

## Notes

Depletion and repletion arise from the merging of qi and blood, and the illness differs by where they merge.
