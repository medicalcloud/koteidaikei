# 素問 調経論篇第六十二 §26 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "When form is in surplus, the abdomen is distended and *jing* and urine do not pass freely; when insufficient, the four limbs cannot be used. When blood and qi have not yet merged and the five depots are stable, a squirming of the flesh is called slight wind."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When form is in surplus ... the four limbs cannot be used"] This is the depot of the spleen. The *Zhenjing* says: "When spleen qi is depleted, the four limbs cannot be used and the five depots are not at peace; when replete, the abdomen is distended and *jing* and urine do not pass freely." 涇 is stool; 溲 is urine.
Song editors: Yang Shangshan says 涇 should be 経, menstruation.

[On "When blood and qi have not yet merged ... slight wind"] The pathogen presses on the divisions of the flesh, the defensive qi does not pass, and yang qi drums within; hence the flesh squirms.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition and the *Jiayi jing* write 溢 for 蠕, and the *Taisu* 濡.

## Notes

The symptoms of surplus and insufficiency of form (spleen), and its slight illness (slight wind).
- Wang Bing takes 涇 as stool, Yang Shangshan as menstruation. The translation leaves *jing* untranslated.
