# 素問 調経論篇第六十二 §8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "When spirit is in surplus, there is laughter without stopping; when spirit is insufficient, there is sorrow. When blood and qi have not yet merged and the five depots are stable, a pathogen lodges in the form, arising as shivering in the fine hairs, and has not yet entered the channels and network vessels; this is called the slightness of spirit."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When spirit is in surplus, there is laughter without stopping; when spirit is insufficient, there is sorrow"] This is the depot of the heart. The *Zhenjing* says: "The heart stores the vessels, and the vessels house the spirit. When heart qi is depleted there is sorrow; when replete, laughter without stopping." That 悲 is in one version written 憂 is an error.
Song editors: On examination, Wang's note says that writing 憂 for 悲 is an error, but the *Jiayi jing*, the *Taisu* and Quan Yuanqi's annotated edition all write 憂. Huangfu Shi'an says: "When the heart is depleted there is sorrow, and sorrow brings worry; when the heart is replete there is laughter, and laughter brings joy. The heart and the lungs, the spleen and the heart complete each other. So joy issues from the heart and is completed in the lungs; thought issues from the spleen and is completed in the heart. Once their measure is exceeded, both depots are harmed." Yang Shangshan says: "The worry of the spleen lies in the heart and is a transformation. The worry of the lungs lies in the lungs' own emotion. That is, the lungs govern autumn, and worry is proper to them; the heart governs summer, and by transformation produces worry."

[On "When blood and qi have not yet merged ... this is called the slightness of spirit"] 并 means merging and combining. It has not yet combined with the pathogen; hence "not yet merged". 洒淅 is the appearance of cold. It first arises in the fine hairs and is still in the small network vessels. It is a slight illness of the spirit; hence it is called the slightness of spirit.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 悽厥 for 洒淅, and the *Taisu* 洫泝. Yang Shangshan says: "洫 means the pores. Water flowing backward is called 泝. It means that the pathogenic qi enters the interstices like water flowing back into a ditch."

## Notes

The symptoms of surplus and insufficiency of spirit, and its early, slight form (the slightness of spirit).
- 㒵 in Wang Bing's 寒㒵 is a variant form of 貌.
- In Yang Shangshan's words quoted by the Song editors, 脾之憂在心変動也 may be an error for "the worry of the heart", but this has not been checked. The translation stays close to the characters.
