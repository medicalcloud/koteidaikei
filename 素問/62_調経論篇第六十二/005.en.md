# 素問 調経論篇第六十二 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Emperor said: "A person has essence, qi, fluids and humors, four limbs and nine orifices, five depots and sixteen parts, and three hundred and sixty-five joints, and in these the hundred illnesses arise. In the arising of the hundred illnesses there is always depletion and repletion. Now you, Master, say that there are five kinds of surplus and five of insufficiency. From what do they arise?"

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "A person has essence, qi, fluids and humors ... From what do they arise?"] The *Zhenjing* says: "When two spirits press together and unite to form a body, what always arises before the body is called essence. When the upper burner opens and spreads the flavors of the five grains, fumigating the skin, filling the body and moistening the hair like the irrigation of mist and dew, this is called qi. When the interstices open and discharge, and sweat comes out at the 湊理, this is called fluid." Humor is what seeps into the hollows and orifices and stays without moving. The sixteen parts are the hands and feet (two), the nine orifices (nine) and the five depots (five), making sixteen parts. The three hundred and sixty-five joints are not bone joints but the places where spirit qi goes out and comes in. The *Zhenjing* says: "The so-called crossings of the joints, three hundred and sixty-five meetings, are all places where spirit qi goes out, comes in and travels; they are not bone joints." What the body has is much; what is named [here] is little; how then are the numbers of arising illnesses to be discussed?

## Notes

A question on why there are only five kinds each of surplus and insufficiency when the body has so many parts.
- 汗出湊理 in the *Zhenjing* quoted by Wang Bing is said to read 汗出溱溱 in the *Lingshu* *Jueqi* chapter; this has not been checked.
- From 液之滲於空竅 on, the text can be read as Wang Bing's own summary after the quotation ends at 是謂津; the extent of the quotation is unclear.
