# 素問 八正神明論篇第二十六 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The stars are what regulate the courses of the sun and moon. The eight nodal points are for observing the depleting evils of the eight winds that arrive with the seasons. The four seasons are for distinguishing where the qi of spring, autumn, winter and summer lies, so as to regulate according to the season, and for avoiding and not incurring the depleting evils of the eight nodal points. When the body's depletion meets heaven's depletion, the two depletions stir each other, and the qi reaches the bones; when it enters, it harms the five zang. If the practitioner observes and stops it, it cannot harm. Therefore it is said that the prohibitions of heaven must be known."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The stars are what regulate the courses of the sun and moon"] 制 means regulation. By fixing the stars, one can know the regulation of the courses of the sun and moon. Broadly speaking, the twenty-eight mansions around the heavens [each] have thirty-six *fen*, so that a circuit of the heavens by the person's qi amounts to 1,008 *fen*. One circuit of the body, 16 *zhang* 2 *chi*, is matched to the twenty-eight mansions; in accordance with the hundred marks of the water clock, it travels 810 *zhang* in all, dividing day and night. Thus in ten breaths the qi travels six *chi* and the sun two *fen*; in 270 breaths the qi travels 16 *zhang* 2 *chi*, one circuit of the body, the water falls two marks and the sun moves twenty *fen*; in 540 breaths the qi makes a second circuit, the water falls four marks and the sun moves forty *fen*; in 2,700 breaths the qi makes ten circuits, the water falls twenty marks and the sun moves five mansions and twenty *fen*; in 13,500 breaths the qi makes fifty circuits, the water falls a hundred marks and the sun moves through the twenty-eight mansions. In detail, adding one and six-tenths *fen* for every ten circuits exhausts the remainder. Thus the stars regulate the courses of the sun and moon.
Song editors: From "the twenty-eight mansions around the heavens" to "the sun moves through the twenty-eight mansions" is originally text of the *Lingshu*, now found in the *Jiayi jing*.

[On "The eight nodal points are for observing ... arrive with the seasons"] The eight nodal points are the proper qi of the eight nodal days. The eight winds are: the Infant wind of the east, the Great Weak wind of the south, the Hard wind of the west, the Great Hard wind of the north, the Ominous wind of the northeast, the Weak wind of the southeast, the Scheming wind of the southwest, and the Breaking wind of the northwest. "Depleting evil" means what takes advantage of a person's depletion to cause illness. "Arriving with the seasons" means the winds that, as heaven responds to Taiyi's change of residence, come to pay homage to the central palace before and after the eight nodal days.
Song editors: The meaning of Taiyi's change of residence and the winds' homage to the central palace is fully described in the *Tianyuan yuce*.

[On "The four seasons are for distinguishing ... avoiding and not incurring the depleting evils of the eight nodal points"] "Where the qi of the four seasons lies" means that the qi of spring is in the channel vessels, the qi of summer in the minute network vessels, the qi of autumn in the skin, and the qi of winter in the bones and marrow. If one is exposed to depleting evil, it moves and harms the true qi; if one avoids and does not incur it, one does not fall ill. The *Lingshu jing* says, "The sage avoids evil as he would avoid arrows and stones," because it can harm the true qi.

[On "When the body's depletion meets heaven's depletion ... it harms the five zang"] Depletion stirs depletion; the same qi respond to each other.

[On "If the practitioner observes and stops it, it cannot harm"] Observing and knowing, one stops it; therefore it cannot harm. 救 means stop.

[On "Therefore it is said that the prohibitions of heaven must be known"] They are what people must avoid in relation to heaven; hence "prohibitions of heaven". Violating them causes illness; therefore they must be known.

## Notes

The meaning of observing the stars, the eight nodal points and the four seasons, and how illness deepens when the body's depletion coincides with heaven's.
- The names of the eight winds in Wang Bing's note appear in the *Jiugong bafeng* chapter of the *Lingshu*.
- 東圠方 in Wang Bing's note means 東北方 "northeast" (圠 is presumably an error for 北); the translation gives "northeast".
- 八正之虚邪而避之勿犯也 connects somewhat awkwardly with its context; it is read as continuing the sentence on the four seasons.
