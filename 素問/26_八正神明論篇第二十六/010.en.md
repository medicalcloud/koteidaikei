# 素問 八正神明論篇第二十六 §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Draining must use the square. The square means: when the qi is just abundant, when the moon is just full, when the sun is just warm, when the body is just settled, insert the needle as the breath is just being drawn in; then wait again for the in-breath and turn the needle; then wait again for the out-breath and slowly withdraw the needle. Therefore it is said that draining must use the square, and the qi then moves. Supplementing must use the round. The round means moving, and moving means shifting. In needling one must hit the nutritive, and again withdraw the needle on the in-breath. Thus the round and the square are not the needles. Therefore one who nourishes the spirit must know whether the form is fat or thin and the abundance or decline of the nutritive and defensive, blood and qi. Blood and qi are a person's spirit; they must be carefully nourished."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Draining must use the square ... and the qi then moves"] 方 means upright. When the evil qi is drained out, the true qi flows and moves.

[On "Supplementing must use the round. The round means moving, and moving means shifting"] "Moving" means spreading the qi that does not move so that it surely spreads and moves. "Shifting" means shifting the pulse that has not yet recovered so that it recovers its balance.

[On "In needling one must hit the nutritive, and again withdraw the needle on the in-breath"] When the needle enters and reaches the blood, it is called hitting the nutritive.

[On "Thus the round and the square are not the needles"] The square and round spoken of do not mean the shape of the needle; they mean precisely moving and shifting.

[On "Therefore one who nourishes the spirit ... they must be carefully nourished"] When the spirit is calm, life is prolonged; when the spirit departs, the form decays. Therefore it must be carefully nourished.

## Notes

The "square" of draining and the "round" of supplementing are not the shapes of needles but principles of timing and technique.
- Wang Bing takes 方 as "upright". Many read 方 as "just (at the moment)", referring to timing such as when the qi is abundant. In 方者，以気方盛也 and what follows, 方 is translated "just".
- For 而 in 其気而行焉, Wikisource and Daizhige write 乃.
