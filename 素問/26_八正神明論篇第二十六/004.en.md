# 素問 八正神明論篇第二十六 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The method of needling is always to observe the qi of the sun, moon and stars, of the four seasons and the eight nodal points, and to needle only when the qi is settled. Thus when the weather is warm and the sun bright, a person's blood is moist and flowing and the defensive qi floats; therefore the blood is easy to drain and the qi easy to move. When the weather is cold and the sun hidden, the blood congeals and is rough and the defensive qi sinks. When the moon begins to grow, the blood and qi begin to be refined and the defensive qi begins to move. When the moon's disc is full, the blood and qi are replete and the flesh firm. When the moon's disc is empty, the flesh diminishes, the channels and network vessels are depleted, the defensive qi leaves, and the form remains alone. Thus one regulates the blood and qi according to the times of heaven. Hence when the weather is cold do not needle; when the weather is warm do not hesitate; when the moon is growing do not drain; when the moon is full do not supplement; when the moon's disc is empty do not treat. This is called regulating in accord with the time. Following the order of heaven and the times of fullness and emptiness, fix the positions as the light moves, stand upright and wait for it. Therefore draining when the moon is growing is called depleting the zang; supplementing when the moon is full makes the blood and qi surge and overflow, and the network vessels retain blood — this is called double repletion; treating when the moon's disc is empty is called disordering the channels. Yin and yang are confused, the true and the evil are not distinguished, [the evil] sinks and lingers, the outside is depleted and the inside disordered, and excessive evils arise."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The method of needling ... needle only when the qi is settled"] "Observing the sun and moon" means observing the cold and warmth of the sun and the emptiness and fullness of the moon. "The stars" means first knowing the divisions of the twenty-eight lunar mansions and matching them to the marks of the water clock. Broadly speaking, by always placing the sun over the mansions one knows whether a person's qi is in the greater yang or not; when the sun moves one lodge, the person's qi is in the three yang and the yin division. In detail, from Fang to Bi are fourteen mansions, during which the water falls fifty marks, the measure of half a day; from Mao to Xin are also fourteen mansions, during which the water falls fifty marks, the measure that completes the day. Thus from Fang to Bi is yang and from Mao to Xin is yin; yang governs the day, yin the night. For each lodge the sun moves, the water falls three marks and four-sevenths of a mark. The *Lingshu jing* says, "When the water falls one mark, the person's qi is in the greater yang; two marks, in the lesser yang; three marks, in the *yangming*; four marks, in the yin division. As the water falls without stopping, so the qi moves." It also says, "When the sun moves one lodge, the person's qi travels one and eight-tenths circuits of the body; two lodges, three and six-tenths circuits; three lodges, five and four-tenths circuits; four lodges, seven and two-tenths circuits; five lodges, nine circuits." When the sun moves twenty-eight lodges, the person's qi travels fifty and four-tenths circuits of the body. Therefore one must observe the sun, moon and stars. "The qi of the four seasons and the eight nodal points" means the proper qi of the four seasons and the winds of the eight nodal days that come to pay homage to Taiyi. Carefully observe where the qi is and needle accordingly. "Needle only when the qi is settled" means that only when the winds of the eight nodal days are calm and settled can one needle the channels and regulate depletion and repletion. Hence the *Liji* (calendar prohibitions) says, "For five days before and after each of the eight nodal days, do not needle or apply moxibustion; it is inauspicious." This means that the qi is not yet settled, so one should not apply moxibustion or needle.
Song editors: The homage of the winds of the eight nodal days to Taiyi is fully described in the *Tianyuan yuce*.

[On "When the weather is cold and the sun hidden, the blood congeals and is rough and the defensive qi sinks"] 泣 means like snow lying in water.

[On "Hence when the weather is cold do not needle"] Because the blood is congealed and rough and the defensive qi sinks.

[On "when the weather is warm do not hesitate"] Because the blood is moist and flowing and the qi moves easily.

[On "when the moon's disc is empty do not treat. This is called regulating in accord with the time"] It means obtaining the time of heaven.

[On "Following the order of heaven ... stand upright and wait for it"] Observe the movement of the sun to fix where the qi is, stand upright facing south, wait for the qi to arrive and regulate it.

[On "Therefore draining when the moon is growing is called depleting the zang"] The blood and qi are weak.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 減 for 蔵; 蔵 should be 減.

[On "supplementing when the moon is full ... this is called double repletion"] One version has 経 for 絡, which is wrong. The blood and qi are abundant. One version has 流 for 留, which is wrong.

[On "treating when the moon's disc is empty ... excessive evils arise"] The qi loses its order; therefore excessive evils arise.

## Notes

Needling should follow the weather (cold or warm, clear or overcast) and the phases of the moon; acting against them results in depleted zang, double repletion and disordered channels.
- 日 in 故日月生而瀉 is often taken as an error for 曰 (Daizhige writes 曰). In view of the earlier 月生無瀉, the translation takes it as "draining when the moon is growing". The original keeps the base text.
- 蔵虚: the Song editors hold it should be 減虚 following Quan Yuanqi's edition.
- 𭥦 in Wang Bing's note is a variant of 昴; later in the note it is written 昴.
- 𠕋 in the Song editors' note is a variant of 冊 (Kanripo writes 冊).
- The *Lingshu jing* passages quoted by Wang Bing correspond to the *Weiqi xing* chapter of the *Lingshu*.
