# 素問 疏五過論篇第七十七 §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

One once rich who is gravely injured, with severed sinews and cut vessels, recovers enough to move, but the moisture does not stop [being lost]. Hence the injury knots, lingers and presses, returning to yang, and pus accumulates with cold and heat. When a crude practitioner treats this by repeatedly needling yin and yang, the body goes slack, the limbs cramp, and the day of death is fixed. If the doctor cannot understand it, does not ask how it began, and only speaks of the day of death, he too is a crude practitioner. This is the fifth error in treatment.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "with severed sinews and cut vessels, recovers enough to move, but the moisture does not stop [being lost]"] Severed sinews and cut vessels means injury beyond measure. Though the body has recovered enough to move, the fluids are not replenished. Why? Because the essential qi is depleted. 沢 means fluid.

[On "Hence the injury knots, lingers and presses, returning to yang, and pus accumulates with cold and heat"] Yang means the yang vessels and the six bowels; 炅 means heat. It means that when the qi of the sinews and vessels is injured beyond measure, blood and qi knot within and linger; pressing on the yang vessels, they turn to pus; accumulating long in the abdomen, they produce cold and heat outside.

[On "by repeatedly needling yin and yang, the body goes slack, the limbs cramp, and the day of death is fixed"] Not knowing that the cold and heat arise from accumulated pus, thinking it an ordinary heat illness, he applies his methods indiscriminately, repeatedly needling the yin and yang channels; the qi is robbed and the illness worsens. Hence the body is slack and useless and the limbs fail and cramp. Thus one knows the day of death is fixed. Why call it fate rather than the doctor?

[On "and only speaks of the day of death, he too is a crude practitioner. This is the fifth error in treatment"] A crude practitioner is not necessarily a learner who lacks understanding. Even one fully versed in the classical methods of three generations, if he does not have the three constants in diagnosis, is not careful of the five errors in treatment, does not seek the remaining threads, and does not ask about the person himself, is still a crude doctor.

## Notes

The fifth of the five errors: mistaking cold and heat from accumulated pus after injury for ordinary heat, needling, and causing death.
