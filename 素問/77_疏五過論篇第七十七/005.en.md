# 素問 疏五過論篇第七十七 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

One who is good at pulses must know them by comparing categories, the extraordinary and the constant, and unhurried [examination]. To be a practitioner without knowing this way makes one's diagnosis unworthy of esteem; this is the third error in treatment.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "To be a practitioner without knowing this way ... the third error in treatment"] The extraordinary and constant means the signs of qi being strange compared with the constant signs. Unhurried [examination] means distinguishing the depletion and repletion of the visceral qi, whose pulses appear at nearly similar heights. The *Shi congrong lun* says, "When the spleen is depleted and floating it resembles the lungs; when the kidney is small and floating it resembles the spleen; when the liver is tense, sunken and scattered it resembles the kidneys. These are what often confuse practitioners; but by unhurried distinction one grasps them."

## Notes

The third of the five errors: not diagnosing pulses by comparing categories, the extraordinary and constant, and unhurried examination.
