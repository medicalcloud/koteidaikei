# 素問 疏五過論篇第七十七 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Emperor said, "Before diagnosing any illness, you must ask. One who was once noble and later humble, even without being struck by a pathogen, has illness arising from within; this is called loss of nutritive. One who was once rich and later poor has what is called loss of essence: the five qi linger and the illness combines. When a practitioner diagnoses it, [the illness] is not in the viscera and bowels and the body's form is unchanged; diagnosing it he is in doubt and does not know the name of the illness. The body wastes day by day, the qi is depleted without essence; as the illness deepens there is no qi, and one shivers and is startled at times. The illness is deep because outside it consumes the defensive and inside it robs the nutritive. What even a good practitioner misses is the nature of the illness; this too is one error in treatment.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "even without being struck by a pathogen, has illness arising from within; this is called loss of nutritive"] Because the spirit is humbled. When noble one is honored and flourishing; when humble, humbled and disgraced; the mind longs for the past and the will is knotted with worry and fear. Hence even without being struck by a pathogen, illness arises from within, and the blood vessels are depleted; hence "loss of nutritive".

[On "has what is called loss of essence: the five qi linger and the illness combines"] When rich he followed his desires; when poor he was robbed of abundant wealth; within, worry seethes and knots; without, he grieves over lost things. So the mind follows its longings and the spirit dwells on past plans; the paths of the nutritive and defensive are shut and delayed, qi and blood do not move, and they accumulate and combine into illness.

[On "[the illness] is not in the viscera and bowels and the body's form is unchanged; diagnosing it he is in doubt and does not know the name of the illness"] This speaks of the beginning of the illness. Since it is caused by longing, it does not yet reside in the viscera and bowels; since it arises from feelings and thoughts, the body's form is unchanged. The doctor does not fully understand it, so he is in doubt in diagnosis.

[On "The body wastes day by day, the qi is depleted without essence"] This speaks of the next stage. Qi and blood press on each other, form and flesh are consumed, so the body wastes day by day. The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* says, "Qi returns to essence; essence feeds on qi." Now the qi is depleted and does not transform, so the essence has nothing to nourish it.

[On "as the illness deepens there is no qi, and one shivers and is startled at times"] This speaks of the illness being deep. The illness qi is deep, grain qi exhausted, yang qi presses inward, so one dislikes cold and is startled. 洒洒 describes chill.

[On "The illness is deep because outside it consumes the defensive and inside it robs the nutritive"] Blood is seared by worry and qi diminishes with grief; hence outside the defensive is consumed and inside the nutritive robbed. The illness is deep because of this consuming and robbing.
Song editors: The *Taisu* has 病深以甚 for 病深者以其.

[On "What even a good practitioner misses is the nature of the illness; this too is one error in treatment"] 失 means failing to ask how it began.

## Notes

The first of the five errors: not asking about changes in circumstance (rank, wealth) that cause loss of nutritive and loss of essence. The Emperor's speech continues through unit 16.
