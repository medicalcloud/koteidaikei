# 素問 陰陽別論篇第七 §7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the one yang beats, it is called hooked; when the one yin beats, it is called hair-like; when the beating yang prevails and is tense, it is called wiry; when the beating yang arrives and is cut off, it is called stony; when yin and yang pass each other, it is called flowing. When yin contends within and yang disturbs without, the *po*-sweat is not contained, and the four limbs become cold with counterflow; when this arises, it fumigates the lung and makes a person pant and wheeze. What yin generates has harmony as its root; this is called harmony. Thus with hard upon hard, the yang qi is broken and scattered, and the yin qi then wastes away; when it is sodden, hard and soft are not in harmony and the meridian qi is then cut off. Those of the class of dead yin die within three days; those of the class of living yang recover within four days.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the one yang beats ... it is called flowing"] This says how one knows the pulses of yin and yang illness. When the one yang beats, the pulse appears hooked. Why? The one yang is the sanjiao, the fu of the heart vessel; when the one yang beats, the hooked pulse corresponds to it, and the hooked pulse is the heart pulse. This speaks of its correct appearance. The one yin is *Jueyin*, the wood qi of the liver; hair-like is the metal pulse of the lung. When metal comes and beats upon wood, the pulse is hair-like. When metal qi encroaches on wood within and yang still prevails and is tense, the pulse appearing within is wiry. If the yang qi arrives tense, the pulse is called wiry and belongs to the liver. If the yang qi arrives and is as if cut off, the pulse is called stony and belongs to the kidney. When the qi of yin and yang pass each other with neither prevailing, the pulse is like flowing water.

[On "When yin contends within ... makes a person pant and wheeze"] If the beating of metal does not stop, the yang qi greatly prevails; the two qi hold against each other, contending within and disturbing without. Then sweat flows without stopping and the hands and feet instead become cold. In severe cases the yang qi burns within; when the flowing sweat is not contained, the heat attacks the lung. Therefore when it arises it fumigates the lung and makes a person pant and wheeze.

[On "What yin generates has harmony as its root; this is called harmony"] "Yin" means the five spirit storehouses. It says that the five zang can generate and preserve the harmonious qi of the heavenly truth because each follows its own harmonious nature and is calm and still. If one goes against what suits them, they are encroached upon by other qi and a hundred kinds of illness arise from this. How can one not be careful in the way of serving life!

[On "Thus with hard upon hard, the yang qi is broken and scattered, and the yin qi then wastes away"] "Hard" means yang. It says that when the yang qi steams within and makes sweat flow without, burning without stopping, yang prevails and becomes yang again; therefore though abundant it does not last long, and the yang qi scatters of itself. Once yang is broken and ruined, yin cannot survive alone; therefore when the yang qi is broken and scattered, the yin qi also wastes away. This is contending for victory and inviting defeat.

[On "when it is sodden, hard and soft are not in harmony and the meridian qi is then cut off"] When the blood is sodden, yang always prevails. Seeing that a person's blood is sodden, one should carefully harmonize the qi and always keep it flowing. If one cannot think deeply and have few desires, so that the order of the qi goes astray, yang becomes double yang and burns the zang and fu within, and death is only to be awaited; how can life last long?

[On "Those of the class of dead yin die within three days"] Fire encroaches on metal.

[On "those of the class of living yang recover within four days"] Wood encroaches on fire.
Song editors: Another edition reads "live within four days", and Quan Yuanqi's annotated edition reads "recover within four days"; both make sense. Considering the meaning of the text before and after, "die" is wrong.

## Notes

Distinguishing yin and yang by the beating of the pulse (hooked, hair-like, wiry, stony, flowing); an illness in which yin and yang contend so that sweating does not stop and there is wheezing; the loss of both yin and yang when hard is piled upon hard; and the times of death for the classes of dead yin and living yang.
- "What yin generates has harmony as its root": there are various readings; Wang Bing takes it as each of the five zang following its harmonious nature and being calm.
- "Sodden" (淖): Wang Bing takes it as the blood being wet and turbid.
- Wikisource (the plain text) writes 弦 for 絃.
- The base's 四日而死 is emended to 四日而已. The *Taisu* reads 四日而已, and the Song editors also consider the other edition's 四日而生 and Quan Yuanqi's 四日而已 correct and 死 wrong; the Daizhige *Chongguang buzhu* text also has 已 (the *Leijing*, Wikisource and the Daizhige plain text read 死 like the base). Since the class of living yang, contrasted with 死陰之属，不過三日而死, should be the curable one, 已 is adopted.
