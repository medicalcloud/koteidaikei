# 素問 陰陽別論篇第七 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "The four regular [pulses] correspond to the four seasons; the twelve conformities correspond to the twelve months; the twelve months correspond to the twelve vessels. The pulse has yin and yang; one who knows yang knows yin, and one who knows yin knows yang. In all, yang has five; five times five makes twenty-five yang. What is called yin is the true-zang [pulse]; when it appears, there is ruin, and ruin means certain death. What is called yang is the yang of the stomach duct. One who distinguishes the yang knows where the illness is; one who distinguishes the yin knows the time of death or life. The three yang are at the head and the three yin at the hand; this is what is called one. One who distinguishes the yang knows the times the illness must avoid; one who distinguishes the yin knows the time of death or life. Be careful and thoroughly versed in yin and yang, and do not consult the crowd.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The four regular [pulses] correspond to the four seasons ... the twelve months correspond to the twelve vessels"] The spring pulse is wiry, the summer pulse surging, the autumn pulse floating and the winter pulse sunken; these are called the regular pulses of the four seasons. "Conformity" means the qi of heaven moving in conformity with the divisions of the twelve branches; therefore they correspond to the twelve months. The twelve months are the months in which the handle [of the Dipper] points to *yin*, *mao* and *chen* in spring, *si*, *wu* and *wei* in summer, *shen*, *you* and *xu* in autumn, and *hai*, *zi* and *chou* in winter. The twelve vessels are the vessels of the three yin and three yang of the hand and of the foot. Because their numbers of qi correspond, they are matched together.

[On "one who knows yang knows yin, and one who knows yin knows yang"] With deep knowledge one fully recognizes their changes.

[On "In all, yang has five; five times five makes twenty-five yang"] The five yang are the yang qi of the five zang. Each of the five zang, in correspondence with the season, shows one pulse, and within each pulse the yang of all five zang are contained; five multiplied by five makes twenty-five yang.
Song editors: The *Yuji zhenzang lun* (chapter 19) says, "Therefore illness has five changes; five times five makes twenty-five changes"; the meaning is consistent with this.

[On "What is called yin is the true-zang [pulse]; when it appears, there is ruin, and ruin means certain death"] The five zang are yin; hence "what is called yin is the true-zang". "Appearing" means: the liver pulse arrives tense inside and out, like running the hand along a knife blade, sharp and taut, like pressing the string of a zither; the heart pulse arrives hard and beating, like running the hand over Job's-tears seeds, string upon string; the lung pulse arrives large and empty, like feathers touching a person's skin; the kidney pulse arrives beating and then cut off, like flicking a stone with the finger, snapping; the spleen pulse arrives weak, now rapid, now sparse. Those in whom such pulses appear all have ruined zang and departed spirit; therefore they must die.

[On "What is called yang is the yang of the stomach duct"] The yang of the stomach duct means the qi of Renying. One examines whether the movement and stillness, smallness and largeness of its pulse correspond with the pulse at the wrist. The stomach is the sea of water and grain; therefore by observing its qi one knows where the illness is. Renying is on both sides of the Adam's apple, where the pulse beats against the hand. Its beat is always smaller on the left and larger on the right; the smaller left is always used to observe the zang, the larger right to observe the fu. One version has "the yang of the stomach and womb", which is wrong.

[On "One who distinguishes the yang knows where the illness is; one who distinguishes the yin knows the time of death or life"] Yang defends the exterior and makes it firm; where external evil has struck, distinguishing the yang one knows the place of illness. Yin stores the spirit and guards within; if one considers the success or ruin of the true and correct, distinguishing the yin one knows the time of the patient's death or life.
Song editors: The *Yuji zhenzang lun* (chapter 19) says: "One who distinguishes the yang knows where the illness comes from; one who distinguishes the yin knows the time of death or life."

[On "The three yang are at the head and the three yin at the hand; this is what is called one"] "Head" means Renying; "hand" means the qi opening. When the two correspond, going and coming together like a pulled rope, equal in size, the person is called normal; hence "this is what is called one". The qi opening is one *cun* behind the thenar border of the hand; Renying is one and a half *cun* on either side of the Adam's apple. Both can be used to observe the qi of the zang and fu.

[On "One who distinguishes the yang knows the times the illness must avoid; one who distinguishes the yin knows the time of death or life"] Recognizing the qi and fixing the times, one knows what the illness must avoid; examining and clarifying success and ruin, one knows the time of death or life.

[On "Be careful and thoroughly versed in yin and yang, and do not consult the crowd"] If one carefully gauges the qi and seasons, is thoroughly versed in yin and yang and knows the rules of what the illness must avoid, doubts about life and death are settled of themselves, and one acts correctly without confusion. What need is there to consult and deliberate with the crowd?

## Notes

The four regular pulses are the pulses of the four seasons, and the twelve conformities are the twelve channels corresponding to the twelve months. The yin and yang of the pulse are discussed: yin is the true-zang pulse, whose appearance means death; yang is the yang of the stomach duct (the Renying pulse). When the three yang (Renying) and three yin (qi opening) match, the person is normal; one versed in yin and yang can know the place of illness and the time of death or life.
- "The yang of the stomach duct": Wang Bing takes it as the qi of Renying and rejects the variant "yang of the stomach and womb".
- "The three yang are at the head and the three yin at the hand": Wang Bing takes the head as Renying (the carotid pulse) and the hand as the qi opening (the radial pulse).
- Wang Bing's description of the true-zang pulses matches passages in the *Pingren qixiang lun* and *Yuji zhenzang lun*.
