# 素問 陰陽別論篇第七 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

What is called yin and yang: what departs is yin and what arrives is yang; what is still is yin and what moves is yang; what is slow is yin and what is rapid is yang. In all cases of taking the zang pulses of the true pulse: if the liver [pulse] arrives suspended, cut off and tense, death in eighteen days; if the heart arrives suspended and cut off, death in nine days; if the lung arrives suspended and cut off, death in twelve days; if the kidney arrives suspended and cut off, death in seven days; if the spleen arrives suspended and cut off, death in four days.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "what departs is yin ... what is rapid is yang"] This speaks of [yin and yang] within the movement of the pulse.

[On "In all cases of taking the zang pulses of the true pulse ... death in four days"] "The zang pulses of the true pulse" means the true-zang pulses. Eighteen days is the remainder of the completing numbers of metal and wood. Nine days is the remainder of the generating and completing numbers of water and fire. Twelve days is the remainder of the generating and completing numbers of metal and fire. Seven days is the remainder of the generating numbers of water and earth. Four days is the remainder of the generating number of wood. Hence the *Pingren qixiang lun* (chapter 18) says: "If the liver appears on *geng* and *xin* days one dies; the heart, on *ren* and *gui*; the lung, on *bing* and *ding*; the kidney, on *wu* and *ji*; the spleen, on *jia* and *yi*." It is because of this. In all such cases, when the expected time arrives they cannot prevail and die. Why? Because of the qi that controls and injures them, which they cannot overcome.

## Notes

The yin and yang of the pulse are defined (departing and arriving, still and moving, slow and rapid), and the times of death for each of the five zang when its true-zang pulse appears "suspended and cut off" are given.
- Wang Bing explains the numbers of days by combinations of the generating and completing numbers of the five phases (wood 3 and 8, fire 2 and 7, earth 5, metal 4 and 9, water 1 and 6).
- 戊巳 in Wang Bing's note is the base text's form, translated as 戊己.
- Daizhige (the plain text) writes 主 for 至 in 肝至.
