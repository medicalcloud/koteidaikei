# 素問 陰陽別論篇第七 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

It is said: illness of the two yang breaks out in the heart and spleen; there is inability in private matters, and in women the menses stop. When it is transmitted it becomes wind wasting; when it is transmitted and becomes rushing breath, the patient will die and cannot be treated.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "illness of the two yang breaks out in the heart and spleen; there is inability in private matters, and in women the menses stop"] The two yang are the *yangming* vessels of the large intestine and stomach. "Private matters" means affairs that are concealed and intimate. When the intestines and stomach fall ill, the heart and spleen receive it. When the heart receives it, the blood does not flow; when the spleen receives it, flavours are not transformed. Because the blood does not flow, women have no menses; when flavours are not transformed, men have little essence. Therefore they cannot perform concealed and intimate affairs. The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* (chapter 5) says: "Where the essence is insufficient, supplement it with flavours." By this, when flavours are not transformed, the essential qi is scant. The *Qibing lun* (chapter 47) says, "The womb is tied to the kidney," and the *Ping rebing lun* (chapter 33) says: "When the menses do not come, the womb vessel is closed. The womb vessel belongs to the heart and connects into the womb. Now the qi rises and presses on the lung, and the heart qi cannot communicate downward; therefore the menses do not come." This is the meaning. Further, the *Shanggu tianzhen lun* (chapter 1) says: "In a girl, at twice seven the *tiangui* arrives, the *ren* vessel opens, the great *chong* vessel is exuberant, and the menses descend at their times; in a man, at twice eight the *tiangui* arrives and the essential qi overflows and is discharged." By this, in women there are no menses and in men the essence is scant.

[On "When it is transmitted it becomes wind wasting ... cannot be treated"] This speaks of deep and long-standing cases. When the stomach illness is deep and long-standing, it is transmitted into the spleen; therefore wind-heat wastes and pares away. When the large intestine illness is severe, it is transmitted into the lung, with panting and the qi rushing upward. When the intestines, stomach, spleen and lung also reach the heart, and three zang and two fu oppress one another, the patient dies and cannot be treated.

## Notes

When illness of the two yang (*yangming*: stomach and large intestine) reaches the heart and spleen, sexual function fails and in women the menses stop; when it is further transmitted and becomes wind wasting or rushing breath, the patient dies.
- "Inability in private matters": Wang Bing takes it as inability to perform "concealed and intimate affairs", i.e. sexual activity. Later commentators also take it as difficulty in urination and defecation (and in unit 009 of this chapter Wang Bing himself glosses 隠曲 as evacuation).
- "Wind wasting" and "rushing breath": Wang Bing explains them as wasting by wind-heat and panting with the qi rushing upward.
