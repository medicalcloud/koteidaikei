# 素問 陰陽別論篇第七 §8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

What are called living yang and dead yin: the liver going to the heart is called living yang; the heart going to the lung is called dead yin; the lung going to the kidney is called double yin; the kidney going to the spleen is called opened yin, and the patient dies and cannot be treated. When yang is bound, the four limbs swell. When yin is bound, one passes a *sheng* of blood; bound twice, two *sheng*; bound three times, three *sheng*. When the binding of yin and yang is lopsided, with much yin and little yang, it is called stony water, and the lower abdomen swells. When the two yang are bound, it is called wasting; when the three yang are bound, it is called blockage; when the three yin are bound, it is called water; when the one yin and one yang are bound, it is called throat impediment. When the yin beats and is distinct from the yang, it is called having a child. When yin and yang are depleted and there is intestinal flux, one dies. When yang is added to yin, it is called sweating. When yin is depleted and yang beats, it is called flooding.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "the liver going to the heart is called living yang"] The mother comes and draws near to the child; hence "living yang". It is not only that wood generates fire, but also that the yang qi governs life.

[On "the heart going to the lung is called dead yin"] Yin governs punishment and killing. Fire again encroaches on metal, and metal perishes on meeting fire; hence "dead".

[On "the lung going to the kidney is called double yin"] This too is mother and child; since both are yin qi, it is called double yin.

[On "the kidney going to the spleen is called opened yin, and the patient dies and cannot be treated"] When the qi above opens and combines, water can then rise; because earth opens and water rises, it is called opened yin.

[On "When yang is bound, the four limbs swell"] Because the four limbs are the root of all yang.

[On "When yin is bound, one passes a *sheng* of blood"] Because yin governs the blood.

[On "bound twice, two *sheng*; bound three times, three *sheng*"] Being twice abundant is called bound twice; being three times abundant is called bound three times.

[On "When the binding ... the lower abdomen swells"] This is what is called losing the norm.

[On "When the two yang are bound, it is called wasting"] The binding of the two yang means the stomach and large intestine both being bound by heat. When the intestines and stomach store heat, they readily consume water and grain.
Song editors: "The binding of the two yin" is missing here.

[On "when the three yang are bound, it is called blockage"] The binding of the three yang means the small intestine and bladder being bound by heat. When the small intestine binds heat, the blood vessels dry; when the bladder is hot, the fluids dry up; therefore there is blockage and evacuation is difficult.

[On "when the three yin are bound, it is called water"] The binding of the three yin means the vessels of the spleen and lung both being bound by cold. When the spleen and lung are bound by cold, the qi transforms into water.

[On "when the one yin and one yang are bound, it is called throat impediment"] The one yin means the heart-master vessel and the one yang the triple-burner vessel. The triple-burner and heart-master vessels both connect with the throat; when the qi is hot and binds within, there is throat impediment.

[On "When the yin beats and is distinct from the yang, it is called having a child"] "Yin" means the *chi* position; "beats" means striking against the hand. When the *chi* pulse strikes and differs distinctly from the *cunkou*, and the yang qi stands out, it is a sign of pregnancy. Why? Because within the yin there is a separate yang.

[On "When yin and yang are depleted and there is intestinal flux, one dies"] 辟 is yin. The stomach qi does not stay, the intestines open and cannot be restrained, and the yin within receives nothing; this is the true qi exhausted and cut off; therefore one dies.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 澼 for 辟.

[On "When yang is added to yin, it is called sweating"] Yang is below and yin above; when the yang qi rises and beats and yin can hold it firm, it steams and becomes sweat.

[On "When yin is depleted and yang beats, it is called flooding"] When the yin pulse is insufficient and the yang pulse beats abundantly, there is collapse within and the blood flows down.

## Notes

The transmission of illness among the five zang (living yang, dead yin, double yin, opened yin); illnesses from the binding of yin and yang (swollen limbs, passing of blood, stony water, wasting, blockage, water, throat impediment); diagnosis of pregnancy by the pulse; and sweating and flooding (uterine bleeding).
- "Living yang": Wang Bing explains it as transmission from liver (wood) to heart (fire), from mother to child.
- "When the yin beats and is distinct from the yang, it is called having a child": Wang Bing takes a *chi* pulse beating differently from the *cunkou* as a sign of pregnancy.
- The Song editors note that "the binding of the two yin" is missing.
- "Intestinal flux": Quan Yuanqi's edition and Daizhige write 腸澼.
