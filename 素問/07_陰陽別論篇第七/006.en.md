# 素問 陰陽別論篇第七 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

It is said: when the one yang produces illness, there is shortness of qi, frequent coughing and frequent diarrhoea; when it is transmitted it becomes heart pulling; when it is transmitted it becomes blockage. When the two yang and one yin produce illness, it governs fright and alarm, back pain, frequent belching and frequent yawning; this is named wind reversal. When the two yin and one yang produce illness, there is frequent distension, fullness of the heart and frequent [passing of] qi. When the three yang and three yin produce illness, there is hemilateral withering and limp change, and the four limbs cannot be raised.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "when the one yang produces illness, there is shortness of qi, frequent coughing and frequent diarrhoea"] The one yang means the lesser yang vessels of the gallbladder and triple burner. The gallbladder qi encroaches on the stomach, so there is frequent diarrhoea; the triple burner is ill within, so there is shortness of qi; yang rises and fumigates the lung, so there is frequent coughing. Why? Because the heart fire responds within.

[On "when it is transmitted it becomes heart pulling; when it is transmitted it becomes blockage"] The blocked qi encroaches on the heart and the heart becomes hot, so the yang qi pulls within. The triple burner binds within and the centre is hot, so there is blockage and difficulty [in evacuation].

[On "When the two yang and one yin produce illness ... this is named wind reversal"] The one yin means the *jueyin* vessels of the heart-master and liver. The heart-master vessel starts in the chest and emerges to connect with the heart. The classic says, "When the heart is ill, there is pain between the breast, back and shoulder blades," and also, "In the qi it is belching." Therefore there is back pain and frequent belching. When the heart qi is insufficient, the kidney qi encroaches on it. The liver governs fright and alarm; therefore there is fright and frequent yawning. The liver qi is wind, and the kidney qi rises and counterflows; being both wind and reversal, it is named wind reversal.

[On "When the two yin and one yang produce illness, there is frequent distension, fullness of the heart and frequent [passing of] qi"] The two yin means the lesser yin vessels of the heart and kidney. The kidney and gallbladder both counterflow, the triple burner does not circulate, and the qi accumulates above; therefore the heart is full. Depleted below and abundant above, the qi leaks out.

[On "When the three yang and three yin produce illness ... the four limbs cannot be raised"] When the three yin are insufficient, hemilateral withering breaks out; when the three yang are in excess, there is limp change. "Change" means that normal function has changed so that one is limp, weak and without strength.

## Notes

The signs when the one yang (lesser yang), the two yang and one yin, the two yin and one yang, and the three yang and three yin produce illness.
- Wang Bing takes the one yang as lesser yang (gallbladder and triple burner), the one yin as *jueyin* (heart-master and liver), and the two yin as lesser yin (heart and kidney).
- Wang Bing gives no specific note on the "two yang" of "two yang and one yin".
- Daizhige (the plain text) writes 萎易 for 痿易 and 肢 for 支, and has 陰三 between 善気 and 三陽三陰.
