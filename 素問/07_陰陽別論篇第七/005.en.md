# 素問 陰陽別論篇第七 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

It is said: illness of the three yang produces cold and heat; below it becomes abscesses and swellings, and limpness and reversal with aching calves. When it is transmitted it becomes exhaustion of moisture; when it is transmitted it becomes descending hernia.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "illness of the three yang ... limpness and reversal with aching calves"] The three yang are the greater yang vessels of the small intestine and bladder. The small intestine vessel starts at the hand, follows the arm, circles the shoulder blade and rises to the head. The bladder vessel branches off from the head, descends the back, pierces the buttocks, enters the back of the knee and follows the calf. Therefore when the illness is above, it produces cold and heat; when below, it becomes abscesses, swellings and aching calves, and also limpness and reversal. 㾓 means aching pain; 痿 means lack of strength; 厥 means cold feet, that is, counterflow of qi.

[On "When it is transmitted it becomes exhaustion of moisture; when it is transmitted it becomes descending hernia"] When the heat is severe, essence and blood wither and dry up; therefore the moistening qi of the skin is all dispersed and exhausted. Then the yang qi sinks below and the yin vessels contend above; contending above, there is much cold; sinking below, the sinews slacken. Therefore the testicles hang down slack and descending hernia forms within.

## Notes

Illness of the three yang (greater yang: small intestine and bladder) produces cold and heat, abscesses, limpness and reversal, and aching calves; when transmitted it becomes exhaustion of moisture (loss of the skin's moisture) and descending hernia.
- "Exhaustion of moisture" (*suoze*): Wang Bing explains it as the moistening qi of the skin being dispersed and exhausted.
- Wikisource (the plain text) writes 頹 for 頽 (a difference of character form).
