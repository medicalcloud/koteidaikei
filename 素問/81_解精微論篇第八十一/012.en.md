# 素問 解精微論篇第八十一 §12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the will grieves, there is inner searing; with searing it rushes to the yin; rushing to the yin, the will leaves the eyes; when the will leaves, the spirit does not guard the essence; when essence and spirit leave the eyes, snivel and tears come out. Moreover, have you not recited and pondered the words of the classic? 'With reversal the eyes see nothing.' When a person has reversal, yang qi combines above and yin qi combines below. When yang combines above, fire alone shines; when yin combines below, the feet are cold, and when the feet are cold there is distension. One water cannot overcome five fires; hence the sight of the eyes is blinded.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When the will grieves, there is inner searing ... snivel and tears come out"] 惋 means inner searing; 沖 means rising. Spirit and will affect each other, and tears arise from this; so when there is inner searing, yang qi rises into yin. Yin means the brain. Leaving the eyes means yin and yang do not guard the eyes. Since the will leaves the eyes, the spirit too floats and wanders. When the will leaves the eyes, the light does not shine inward; when the spirit loses its guard, the essence is not bright outwardly. Hence "when essence and spirit leave the eyes, snivel and tears come out".

[On "When a person has reversal, yang qi combines above and yin qi combines below"] 并 means each combines in its own position.

[On "One water cannot overcome five fires; hence the sight of the eyes is blinded"] 眥 means sight. The one water is the eyes; the five fires are the reversing yang of the five viscera.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* lacks 盲.

## Notes

The Emperor explains that grief makes the will and spirit leave the eyes, producing snivel and tears, and that reversal blinds the eyes.
