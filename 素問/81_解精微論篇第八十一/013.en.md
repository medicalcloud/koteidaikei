# 素問 解精微論篇第八十一 §13 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Hence [the base text has a blank here] when struck by wind, tears fall without stopping. When wind strikes the eyes, yang qi guards the essence within; this is fire qi burning the eyes; hence on meeting wind tears fall. There is something to compare it with: when fire is fierce and wind arises, rain can fall; this is of that kind."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "When wind strikes the eyes, yang qi guards the essence within; this is fire qi burning the eyes; hence on meeting wind tears fall"] Wind presses, yang hides and does not issue, so it burns within.

[On "There is something to compare it with: when fire is fierce and wind arises, rain can fall; this is of that kind"] Hence when yang combines, fire alone shines, abundant above and not bright below. The eyes are produced by yang and attached to the viscera; so when yin and yang are harmonious they are bright. When yang reverses, light does not rise; when yin reverses, the feet are cold and distended. "One water cannot overcome five fires" means the yang of hands and feet are the five fires and the one yin below is the qi of the liver. Tears falling without stopping when struck by wind means wind striking the eyes: yang qi guards the essence within, so yang qi is abundant and fire qi burns the eyes; wind and heat meet, so tears fall. Hence "when fire is fierce and wind arises, rain can fall" is a comparison of this kind, for wind produces tears through the heat of yang fire.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* lacks 火. The *Taisu* has "heaven's fierce wind can bring rain", without 生.

## Notes

Concluding the chapter with a simile explaining why wind makes the eyes water. The Emperor's speech ends here, and with it the main text of the *Suwen*.
- In the base text there is a one-character blank between 是以 and 衝風.
- The phrase in Wang Bing's note 一水不可勝五火者是手足之陽為五火下一陰者肝之気也 is hard to construe; the translation is tentative (not verified).
