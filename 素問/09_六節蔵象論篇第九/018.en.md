# 素問 六節蔵象論篇第九 §18 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "To seek its arrival, all goes back to the beginning of spring. If it arrives before it should, this is called excess; then it presses on what it cannot overcome and encroaches on what it overcomes. This is termed licentious qi. [Without distinguishing, evil and perversity arise within, and the practitioner cannot restrain them.] If it should arrive but does not, this is called insufficiency; then what it overcomes acts recklessly, what it generates suffers illness, and what it cannot overcome presses on it. This is termed pressing qi. What is called seeking its arrival is the time when the qi arrives. If one carefully observes the time, the qi can be predicted. If the time is missed and the signs are contrary, the five governances are not distinguished, evil and perversity arise within, and the practitioner cannot restrain them."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "To seek its arrival, all goes back to the beginning of spring"] The beginning of spring means the day of the Beginning of Spring. Spring is the chief of the four seasons; therefore observation of the qi always goes back to the days before the Beginning of Spring.

[On "This is termed licentious qi. Without distinguishing ... the practitioner cannot restrain them"] The meaning of these ten characters above does not fit; it is probably a displaced slip of the ancients, and its sense belongs after "five governances" below. I now write them in red.

[On "If it should arrive but does not ... What is called seeking its arrival is the time when the qi arrives"] The arrival of the qi always refers to the fifteen days before the Beginning of Spring, which is the beginning of the periods. "Arriving before it should" means that the qi in charge arrives ahead of time before it should; arriving ahead of time means the qi is in surplus; hence it is called excess. "Should arrive but does not" means that the qi in charge should arrive but does not, arriving late; arriving late means the qi is insufficient; hence it is called insufficiency. As for "with excess it presses on what it cannot overcome and encroaches on what it overcomes" and "with insufficiency what it overcomes acts recklessly, what it generates suffers illness, and what it cannot overcome presses on it": of the qi of the five phases, what I control is what I overcome, what controls me is what I cannot overcome, and what generates me is what generates. Suppose the liver-wood is in surplus; then the lung-metal is insufficient, metal does not restrain wood, and so wood is in excess. When the wood qi is in surplus, it presses back on lung-metal and encroaches on spleen-earth; hence "with excess it presses on what it cannot overcome and encroaches on what it overcomes". In all these cases the qi of the five zang are licentious and combine within to cause illness; hence it is termed licentious qi. Other cases of excess are the same. Again, suppose the liver-wood qi is scant and cannot restrain earth; then the earth qi has nothing to fear and acts recklessly, and wood is overridden by earth; hence "what it overcomes acts recklessly, and what it generates suffers illness". When the liver-wood qi is not balanced, the lung-metal qi presses on it of itself; hence "what it cannot overcome presses on it". The wood qi is not balanced, earth and metal press on it in turn, and pressing together they cause illness; hence it is termed pressing qi. Other cases of insufficiency are all the same.

[On "If one carefully observes the time ... the practitioner cannot restrain them"] "Time" means the time when the qi arrives. Observing the year begins with the day of the Beginning of Spring; observing the qi fixes the time from the four qi; observing the day follows the days of the periods. Hence "if one carefully observes the time, the qi can be predicted". 反 means to turn against. "The five governances" means the five phases governing and ruling the qi of a year. If one does not distinguish the five governances and wrongly invokes the eight evils, one has not yet understood the circulation of the qi of heavenly truth; how could one thoroughly understand the origins of human illness? Hence "the practitioner cannot restrain them".

## Notes

Observing the time of arrival of the qi (with the Beginning of Spring as reference), qi arriving early is excess ("licentious qi") and qi arriving late is insufficiency ("pressing qi"), each causing illness through the controlling relations of the five phases.
- Wang Bing regards the ten characters "Without distinguishing, evil and perversity arise within, and the practitioner cannot restrain them" after "licentious qi" as a displaced passage belonging after "five governances" below, and notes that he wrote them in red. The translation shows them in brackets as they stand.
- Wang Bing illustrates excess and insufficiency concretely with the example of liver-wood.
