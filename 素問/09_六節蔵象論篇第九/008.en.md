# 素問 六節蔵象論篇第九 §8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "Five days are called a period; three periods are called a qi; six qi are called a season; four seasons are called a year; and each follows what governs it. The five circulations succeed one another and all govern [in turn]; at the end of the days of the year, they go round and begin again. The seasons are established and the qi spread out, like a ring without end; the periods follow the same rule. Therefore it is said: one who does not know what is added to the year, the abundance and decline of the qi, and where depletion and repletion arise, cannot be a practitioner."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Five days are called a period ... and each follows what governs it"] The sun moves five degrees of heaven: that is five days. Three periods are exactly fifteen days. Six qi are about ninety days, exactly three months. To put it in larger terms, eighteen periods are six qi, and six qi are called a season. Four seasons are about 360 days; hence "four seasons are called a year". "Each follows what governs it" means that the days of a year each return to and follow one qi of the five phases, which acts as master and flourishes. Hence the text below says:

[On "The five circulations succeed one another ... cannot be a practitioner"] The five circulations are the qi of the five phases that respond to the movement of heaven and govern transformation. 襲 means to succeed, as an heir succeeds. It says that the qi of the five phases succeed one another like father and son, governing the days of one cycle, always in this way without end, going round and beginning again. "Season" means the time that should arrive before the Beginning of Spring; "qi" means the pulse qi that is dominant. When the qi arrives before spring, the pulse qi also arrives; hence "the seasons are established and the qi spread out". "Period" means the period in which the sun moves five degrees. It says that the days of one period are also taken in turn by the five qi in their generating order, and if they err there is illness. The *Yijing bianqi lun* (chapter 13) says, "In high antiquity Jiudaiji was made to regulate complexion and pulse and communicate with the spirit illumination, matching them with metal, wood, water, fire and earth, the four seasons, the eight winds and the six unions, without departing from their norms" — this is what is meant. "Practitioner" means one skilled in cultivation. It says that only one who is clear about this can go freely throughout the realm.
Song editors: Wang Bing takes "the seasons are established and the qi spread out" as the time that should arrive before the Beginning of Spring and the dominant pulse qi; but this means precisely that the year establishes the four seasons and the seasons spread out the six qi, like a ring without end. Hence it also says "the periods follow the same rule".

## Notes

The units of qi are defined (five days a period, three periods a qi, six qi a season, four seasons a year), and the five circulations are said to succeed one another in governing the year. One who does not know what is added to the year, the abundance and decline of the qi and where depletion and repletion arise cannot be a practitioner.
- "The seasons are established and the qi spread out": Wang Bing explains it by the time before the Beginning of Spring and the dominant pulse qi, while the Song editors take it as the year establishing the seasons and the seasons spreading out the six qi.
- "Practitioner" (工): Wang Bing takes it as one skilled in cultivation; it is generally taken as "physician".
