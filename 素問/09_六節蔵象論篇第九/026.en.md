# 素問 六節蔵象論篇第九 §26 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The heart is the root of life and the transformation of the spirit; its splendour is in the face and its fullness in the blood vessels; it is the greater yang within yang and communicates with the qi of summer. The lung is the root of qi and the place of the *po*; its splendour is in the body hair and its fullness in the skin; it is the greater yin within yang and communicates with the qi of autumn. The kidney governs hibernation; it is the root of sealing and storing and the place of the essence; its splendour is in the hair of the head and its fullness in the bones; it is the lesser yin within yin and communicates with the qi of winter. The liver is the root of exhaustion and the dwelling of the *hun*; its splendour is in the nails and its fullness in the sinews, by which it generates blood and qi; its flavour is sour and its colour green-blue; it is the lesser yang within yang and communicates with the qi of spring. The spleen, stomach, large intestine, small intestine, triple burner and bladder are the root of the granary and the dwelling of the nutritive qi; they are called vessels; they can transform the dregs, convert flavours and take in and send out. Their splendour is in the four whites of the lips and their fullness in the flesh; their flavour is sweet and their colour yellow; they are of the class of utmost yin and communicate with the qi of earth. All eleven organs take their decisions from the gallbladder. Therefore, if Renying is once as abundant, the illness is in the lesser yang; twice, in the greater yang; three times, in *yangming*; four times or more, it is yang rejection. If the *cunkou* is once as abundant, the illness is in *jueyin*; twice, in the lesser yin; three times, in the greater yin; four times or more, it is yin closure. If Renying and the *cunkou* are both four times as abundant or more, it is closure and rejection. When the pulse of closure and rejection is overfull, it cannot exhaust the essential qi of heaven and earth, and one dies."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The heart is the root of life ... communicates with the qi of summer"] The heart is the office of the sovereign, from which spirit illumination comes. On the sovereign all things depend for their rise and fall; hence "the heart is the root of life and the transformation of the spirit". The qi of fire blazes upward; therefore its splendour is in the face. The heart nourishes the blood and governs the vessels; therefore its fullness is in the blood vessels. The heart flourishes in summer and its qi accords with the greater yang; because the greater yang dwells in the fire of summer, it is called "the greater yang within yang, communicating with the qi of summer". The *Jingui zhenyan lun* (chapter 4) says: "From dawn to midday is the yang of heaven, the yang within yang."
Song editors: For "transformation of the spirit", Quan Yuanqi's edition and the *Taisu* read "place of the spirit".

[On "The lung is the root of qi ... communicates with the qi of autumn"] The lung stores qi, its spirit is the *po*, and it nourishes the skin and body hair; hence "the lung is the root of qi and the place of the *po*; its splendour is in the body hair and its fullness in the skin". The lung organ is the qi of the greater yin and governs flourishing in autumn. The daytime is where the yang qi moves, not a yin position; because the greater yin dwells in a yang portion, it is called "the greater yin within yang, communicating with the qi of autumn". The *Jingui zhenyan lun* (chapter 4) says: "From midday to dusk is the yang of heaven, the yin within yang."
Song editors: For "greater yin" the *Jiayi jing* and the *Taisu* read "lesser yin", and lesser yin is right. Although the lung is greater yin among the twelve channels, being within the yang portion it should be lesser yin.

[On "The kidney governs hibernation ... communicates with the qi of winter"] The doors of the earth are sealed shut and hibernating insects hide deeply; the kidney also governs water and receives and stores the essence of the five zang and six fu; hence "the kidney governs hibernation, is the root of sealing and storing and the place of the essence". The brain is the sea of marrow and the kidney governs the bones and marrow; the hair of the head is nourished by the brain; therefore its splendour is in the hair and its fullness in the bones. Because abundant yin dwells in the yin portion of winter, it is called "the lesser yin within yin, communicating with the qi of winter". The *Jingui zhenyan lun* (chapter 4) says: "From nightfall to cockcrow is the yin of heaven, the yin within yin."
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition, the *Jiayi jing* and the *Taisu* read "greater yin" for "lesser yin", and greater yin is right. Although the kidney is lesser yin among the twelve channels, being within the yin portion it should be greater yin.

[On "its fullness in the sinews, by which it generates blood and qi; its flavour is sour and its colour green-blue"] Song editors: These six characters [its flavour is sour and its colour green-blue] should be removed. In the *Taisu* the heart has "its flavour is bitter, its colour red", the lung "its flavour is acrid, its colour white", and the kidney "its flavour is salty, its colour black". Here only the liver and spleen carry their flavour and colour. Since the *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* already sets out the colours and flavours in detail, they should not appear here. We do not now add the colours and flavours of the heart, lung and kidney, but simply remove those of the liver and spleen. The forty-one characters of the *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* cited in the note should also be removed.

[On "The liver is the root of exhaustion ... communicates with the qi of spring"] All human movement is the work of the strength of the sinews. The liver governs the sinews and its spirit is the *hun*; hence "the liver is the root of exhaustion and the dwelling of the *hun*". The nails are the surplus of the sinews, and the sinews are nourished by the liver; therefore its splendour is in the nails and its fullness in the sinews. The east is the beginning of generation; therefore it generates blood and qi. The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* says, "The east generates wind; wind generates wood; wood generates sourness," and the liver accords with wood; therefore its flavour is sour. It also says, "The spirit among the zang is the liver; among colours, green-blue"; therefore its colour is green-blue. Because the lesser yang dwells in a yang position and flourishes in spring, it is called "the lesser yang within yang, communicating with the qi of spring". The *Jingui zhenyan lun* (chapter 4) says: "From dawn to midday is the yang of heaven, the yang within yang."
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition, the *Jiayi jing* and the *Taisu* read "the lesser yang within yin", and that is right. Since Mr Wang cites as proof the *Jingui zhenyan lun*'s "from dawn to midday is the yang of heaven, the yang within yang", his intention is to read "the lesser yang within yang". Moreover, above, for the heart as the greater yang within yang, Mr Wang cited the account of dawn to midday as proof; now for the liver he cites it again, instead of citing "from cockcrow to dawn is the yin of heaven, the yang within yin". The error of Wang's note is evident. It is better to follow Quan Yuanqi's edition, the *Jiayi jing* and the *Taisu* and read "the lesser yang within yin".

[On "The spleen, stomach ... convert flavours and take in and send out"] All of them can receive and convey without ceasing; therefore they are the root of the granary and are called vessels. The nutritive qi arises from the middle burner, and the middle burner is the position of the spleen and stomach; hence "the dwelling of the nutritive qi". The rich flavours of water and grain enter the spleen and stomach, and the dregs of the spleen and stomach convert their flavours and leave through the triple burner and bladder; hence "convert flavours and take in and send out".

[On "Their splendour is in the four whites of the lips ... their colour yellow"] Song editors: These six characters [their flavour is sweet and their colour yellow] should be removed, together with the forty characters of the *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* cited in the note; this has been explained in the previous item.

[On "they are of the class of utmost yin and communicate with the qi of earth"] The mouth is the official of the spleen, and the spleen governs the flesh; hence "its splendour is in the four whites of the lips and its fullness in the flesh". The four whites are the white-coloured flesh around the edges of the lips. The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* says, "The centre generates dampness; dampness generates earth; earth generates sweetness," and the spleen accords with earth; therefore its flavour is sweet. It also says, "Among the zang, the spleen; among colours, yellow"; therefore its colour is yellow. The spleen stores the qi of earth, and earth accords with utmost yin; hence "of the class of utmost yin, communicating with the qi of earth". The *Jingui zhenyan lun* says: "The utmost yin within yin is the spleen."

[On "All eleven organs take their decisions from the gallbladder"] From the heart above to the gallbladder below there are eleven. The gallbladder is upright, firm and decisive, without partiality; therefore the eleven organs take their decisions from the gallbladder.

[On "Therefore, if Renying is once as abundant ... it is yang rejection"] This is the method of the yang pulse. The lesser yang is the gallbladder vessel, the greater yang the bladder vessel, *yangming* the stomach vessel. The *Lingshu jing* says: "Once as abundant and agitated, it is in the hand lesser yang; twice, in the hand greater yang; three times, in the hand *yangming*." The hand lesser yang is the triple-burner vessel, the hand greater yang the small-intestine vessel, the hand *yangming* the large-intestine vessel. "Once as abundant" means the Renying pulse is once again as large as the *cunkou*; the other degrees follow the same method. Four times or more is the extreme of yang abundance; therefore there is rejection and food cannot enter. The *Zhengli lun* says: "With rejection there is vomiting and counterflow."

[On "If the *cunkou* is once as abundant ... it is yin closure"] This is the method of the yin pulse. *Jueyin* is the liver vessel, the lesser yin the kidney vessel, the greater yin the spleen vessel. The *Lingshu jing* says: "Once as abundant and agitated, it is in the hand *jueyin*; twice, in the hand lesser yin; three times, in the hand greater yin." The hand *jueyin* is the pericardium vessel, the hand lesser yin the heart vessel, the hand greater yin the lung vessel. The method of abundance is the same as for yang. Four times or more is the extreme of yin abundance; therefore there is closure and urine cannot pass. The *Zhengli lun* says: "With closure one cannot urinate."

[On "If Renying and the *cunkou* are both four times as abundant ... and one dies"] "Both abundant" means both are four times larger than the normal pulse. Things cannot remain abundant for long; at the extreme they decline and are ruined; therefore one cannot exhaust the essential qi of heaven and earth and dies. The *Lingshu jing* says, "When yin and yang are both abundant and cannot nourish each other, it is called closure and rejection; those with closure and rejection die before their time" — this is what is meant.
Song editors: 贏 should be 盈. A pulse four times as abundant or more is not emaciation (羸) but the extreme of abundance. In ancient script 贏 and 盈 are used interchangeably.

## Notes

The doctrine of the images of the zang. For the heart, lung, kidney, liver, and the spleen-stomach group, it gives their role as roots of life, the dwelling of their spirits (spirit, *po*, essence, *hun*, nutritive qi), their outer splendour (face, body hair, hair, nails, lips) and fullness (blood vessels, skin, bones, sinews, flesh), their yin-yang assignments and the seasonal qi they communicate with. The eleven organs take their decisions from the gallbladder. Finally, the channel affected is known from how many times the Renying or *cunkou* pulse is abundant, and four times or more is yang rejection, yin closure, or closure and rejection. The chapter ends here.
- On the yin-yang assignments, the Song editors, citing Quan Yuanqi's edition, the *Jiayi jing* and the *Taisu*, argue that the lung should be "lesser yin within yang", the kidney "greater yin within yin", and the liver "lesser yang within yin". The translation follows the base text.
- The Song editors say that "its flavour is sour, its colour green-blue" and "their flavour is sweet, their colour yellow", found only for the liver and spleen, should be removed.
- "All eleven organs take their decisions from the gallbladder" is a well-known phrase on the role of the gallbladder.
- "The pulse of closure and rejection is overfull": the Song editors take 贏 in the sense of 盈 "full". Wikisource (the plain text) writes 嬴 and Daizhige 羸.
- The passages from the *Zhengli lun* cited by Wang Bing have not been checked in other sources.
