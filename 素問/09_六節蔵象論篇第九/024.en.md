# 素問 六節蔵象論篇第九 §24 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "How thorough your question is! Heaven is so vast it cannot be measured, earth so great it cannot be gauged; since this is a question from a great spirit, let me set out its outline. Plants produce the five colours, and the variations of the five colours cannot all be seen; plants produce the five flavours, and the delights of the five flavours cannot be exhausted. Tastes and desires differ, and each has its affinity. Heaven feeds people with the five qi, and earth feeds people with the five flavours. The five qi enter through the nose and are stored in the heart and lung; above they make the five colours refined and bright and the voice clear. The five flavours enter through the mouth and are stored in the intestines and stomach; the flavours are stored there and nourish the five qi. When the qi are harmonious there is generation; the fluids form together, and the spirit then arises of itself."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "Heaven is so vast it cannot be measured ... let me set out its outline"] It says that heaven and earth are vast and cannot be obtained by measuring, and creation is mysterious and subtle; how could the human mind comprehend it all? "A question from a great spirit" praises the sage [Huangdi]'s depth and clarity. He gives the broad account and speaks of the general principles; hence "let me set out its outline".

[On "plants produce the five flavours, and the delights of the five flavours cannot be exhausted"] It says that the things that are born are numerous and each receives transformation differently; even the eyes seeing and the mouth tasting cannot exhaust them, let alone the human mind encompass them.

[On "Tastes and desires differ, and each has its affinity"] It says that although the many colours and flavours cannot be exhausted in their origins, what people like and desire follows what their own hearts love; hence "tastes and desires differ, and each has its affinity".

[On "Heaven feeds people with the five qi, and earth feeds people with the five flavours"] Heaven feeding people with the five qi means: rank odour gathers in the liver, scorched odour in the heart, fragrant odour in the spleen, fishy odour in the lung and putrid odour in the kidney. Earth feeding people with the five flavours means: sour enters the liver, bitter the heart, sweet the spleen, acrid the lung and salty the kidney. Clear yang transforms into qi and rises to become heaven; turbid yin forms flavour and descends to become earth. Therefore heaven feeds people with qi and earth feeds people with flavour. The *Yinyang yingxiang dalun* (chapter 5) says, "Clear yang becomes heaven, turbid yin becomes earth," and also, "Yang is qi, yin is flavour."

[On "The five qi enter through the nose ... the spirit then arises of itself"] The heart makes the complexion flourish and the lung governs the voice; therefore the qi are stored in the heart and lung and above make the five colours refined, clean and distinct and the voice clearly manifest. Qi is the mother of water; therefore the flavours are stored in the intestines and stomach and nourish the five qi within. When the five qi harmonize and transform, the fluids then arise. The fluids and qi match each other and transform, forming the spirit qi, which can then arise and spread its transformations.

## Notes

The circulation of heaven and earth and the transformations of yin and yang are too vast to exhaust, but in outline: heaven feeds people with the five qi (odours) and earth with the five flavours; the five qi are stored in the heart and lung and the five flavours in the intestines and stomach, and when qi and fluids are in harmony, the spirit arises.
- Wang Bing takes the five qi as the five odours (rank, scorched, fragrant, fishy, putrid), each gathering in one of the five zang.
- Wikisource (the plain text) writes 修 for 脩.
