# 素問 五蔵別論篇第十一 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Huangdi asked: "I have heard that among the practitioners of the arts, some take the brain and marrow as zang, some take the intestines and stomach as zang, and some take them as fu. May I ask: they contradict one another, yet each claims to be right. I do not know the Way of it; I would like to hear the explanation."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On the question] "Practitioners of the arts" means scholars who are enlightened in methods and techniques. Differences in calling things zang or fu occur even within the classic: the *Linglan midian lun* (chapter 8) includes the intestines and stomach in the order of the twelve organs that employ one another; the *Liujie zangxiang lun* (chapter 9) says "the eleven organs take their decisions from the gallbladder"; and the *Wuzang shengcheng* (chapter 10) says "the images of the five zang can be inferred by analogy; the appearances and sounds of the five zang can be recognized by the mind". These contradict one another. Taking the brain and marrow as zang must be in another classic.

## Notes

The opening question of the chapter: practitioners variously call the brain and marrow, or the intestines and stomach, zang or fu, contradicting one another; which is right?
- Wang Bing points out that even within the *Suwen*, the *Linglan midian lun*, *Liujie zangxiang lun* and *Wuzang shengcheng* count the zang and fu differently.
