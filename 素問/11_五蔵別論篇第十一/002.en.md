# 素問 五蔵別論篇第十一 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "The brain, marrow, bones, vessels, gallbladder and womb — these six are generated by the qi of earth. All store in the yin and resemble earth; therefore they store and do not discharge, and are called the extraordinary fu. The stomach, large intestine, small intestine, triple burner and bladder — these five are generated by the qi of heaven; their qi resembles heaven; therefore they discharge and do not store. They receive the turbid qi of the five zang and are called the fu of conveyance and transformation; they cannot retain for long but transport and discharge. The *po* gate also serves the five zang, so that water and grain cannot be stored for long. The so-called five zang store the essential qi and do not discharge; therefore they are full but cannot be replete. The six fu convey and transform things and do not store; therefore they are replete but cannot be full. The reason is that when water and grain enter the mouth, the stomach is replete and the intestines empty; when the food goes down, the intestines are replete and the stomach empty. Hence it is said: 'replete but not full, full but not replete'."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The brain, marrow ... are called the extraordinary fu"] Although the brain, marrow, bones and vessels are called fu, they do not properly form exterior-interior pairs with the spirit storehouses. The gallbladder is joined with the liver but differs from the conveying and discharging of the six fu. The womb takes in and sends out; when it takes in, it receives essential qi, and when it sends out, it transforms and produces a form. Producing a form means that transformation reaches completion and gives birth. Its function of taking in and sending out differs from that of the six fu; hence "they store and do not discharge, and are called the extraordinary fu".

[On "The stomach, large intestine ... they cannot retain for long but transport and discharge"] It says that once water and grain have entered, the dregs are transformed and discharged and cannot remain long within; once transformed they are simply transported and discharged. Because they convey and discharge the transformations, they are called the fu of conveyance and transformation.

[On "The *po* gate also serves the five zang, so that water and grain cannot be stored for long"] This means the anus. It communicates within with the lung, hence "the *po* gate". Receiving what has been transformed, it serves the five zang; thus water and grain cannot be stored long within.

[On "The so-called five zang store the essential qi ... full but cannot be replete"] Essential qi is "full"; water and grain are "replete". They store only essential qi; therefore they are full but cannot be replete.
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition, the *Jiayi jing* and the *Taisu* write "essence and spirit" for "essential qi".

[On "The six fu convey and transform things ... replete but cannot be full"] Because they do not store essential qi but only receive water and grain.

[On "when water and grain enter the mouth, the stomach is replete and the intestines empty"] Because they have not yet gone down.

[On "when the food goes down, the intestines are replete and the stomach empty"] The water and grain have gone down.

## Notes

The brain, marrow, bones, vessels, gallbladder and womb are the "extraordinary fu", generated by the qi of earth, storing and not discharging; the stomach, large and small intestines, triple burner and bladder are the "fu of conveyance and transformation", generated by the qi of heaven, discharging and not storing. The five zang store essential qi and are full but not replete; the six fu convey things and are replete but not full.
- The concept of the "extraordinary fu" is well known from this chapter.
- "*Po* gate": Wang Bing takes it as the anus, so called because it communicates with the lung.
