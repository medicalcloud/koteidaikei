# 素問 五蔵別論篇第十一 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said: "The stomach is the sea of water and grain and the great source of the six fu. The five flavours enter the mouth, are stored in the stomach, and nourish the qi of the five zang; the qi opening is also greater yin. Therefore the qi and flavours of the five zang and six fu all come from the stomach and show their changes at the qi opening. Thus the five qi enter through the nose and are stored in the heart and lung; when the heart and lung are ill, the nose is obstructed. In all treatment of illness one must examine what is below, gauge the pulse, and observe the patient's will and intention and the illness. With those bound by ghosts and spirits one cannot speak of the utmost virtue; with those who dislike needles and stones one cannot speak of the utmost skill; when a sick person does not consent to treatment, the illness certainly will not be cured, and treating it will have no effect."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "The stomach is the sea of water and grain and the great source of the six fu"] A person has four seas, and the sea of water and grain is one of them. Having received water and grain, it nourishes all around; because it is the source of circulation and transformation, it is the great source of the six fu.

[On "The five flavours enter the mouth ... the qi opening is also greater yin"] The qi opening is one body-*cun* behind the thenar eminence of the hand. The pulsation observed at the qi opening is where the vessel qi of the hand greater yin runs; hence "the qi opening is also greater yin".

[On "Therefore the qi and flavours ... show their changes at the qi opening"] The way of the nutritive qi takes the taking in of grain as its substance.
Song editors: This note comes from the *Lingshu*, where 実 "substance" is written 宝 "treasure". When grain enters the stomach, the qi is passed to the lung, and the pure essence follows the lung qi to the qi opening; hence "shows its changes at the qi opening".
Song editors: Quan Yuanqi's edition writes 入 "enter" for 出 "come from".

[On "In all treatment of illness one must examine what is below ... and the illness"] "Below" means whether what is seen beneath the eyes is good or bad. One gauges the fullness and emptiness of the pulse, assesses whether the will and intention are evil or correct and what suits the depth and outcome of the illness, and then treats according to the rules.
Song editors: The *Taisu* reads "one must examine above and below, gauge the pulse signs, and observe the will and intention and the manifestations of the illness".

[On "With those bound by ghosts and spirits one cannot speak of the utmost virtue"] When the will is perverse, one is fond of prayers and invocations; speaking of the utmost virtue, things will certainly go contrary; therefore one cannot speak with them of the utmost virtue.

[On "with those who dislike needles and stones one cannot speak of the utmost skill"] If they dislike needles and stones, skill cannot be applied; therefore one cannot speak with them of the utmost skill.

[On "when a sick person does not consent ... treating it will have no effect"] One whose heart does not allow another to treat him will certainly die; even if one forces treatment, it will not succeed. Hence "treating it will have no effect".

## Notes

The stomach is the sea of water and grain and the source of the six fu; the five flavours nourish the five zang from the stomach, and their qi shows at the qi opening (hand greater yin), which is therefore the master of the five zang. In treatment one must observe the pulse, the will and the illness; those bound by ghosts and spirits, those who dislike needles, and those who refuse treatment cannot be cured. This ends the chapter.
- "The qi opening is also greater yin": the qi opening is the site of the pulse of the hand greater yin lung channel.
- The Song editors' note after Wang Bing's 栄気之道内穀為実 is taken to include a passage from the *Lingshu* (where 実 is 宝) and its explanation; the division follows the base text.
- "With those bound by ghosts and spirits one cannot speak of the utmost virtue" is often cited as separating medicine from shamanic practice.
