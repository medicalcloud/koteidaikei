# 素問 病能論篇第四十六 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo replied: "To diagnose it, observe the stomach pulse; the pulse should be sunken and fine. Sunken and fine means the qi is counterflowing; when it counterflows, Renying is very abundant; when very abundant, there is heat. Renying is the stomach pulse. When it counterflows and is abundant, heat gathers at the mouth of the stomach and does not move; therefore an abscess forms in the stomach duct."

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "To diagnose it, observe the stomach pulse ... the qi is counterflowing"] The stomach is the sea of water and grain, with abundant blood and vigorous qi; now that the pulse is instead sunken and fine, it goes against the normal.
Song editors: The *Jiayi jing* writes 沈濇 "sunken and rough" for 沈細; the *Taisu* writes 沈細.

[On "when it counterflows, Renying is very abundant; when very abundant, there is heat"] Sunken and fine indicates cold; cold qi blocks yang; therefore the Renying pulse is abundant. Renying is the *yangming* pulse; therefore when abundant there is heat. Renying means where the pulse is felt beside the laryngeal prominence.

[On "Renying is the stomach pulse"] The stomach vessel follows the throat and enters the supraclavicular fossa; hence "Renying is the stomach pulse".

[On "When it counterflows and is abundant ... an abscess forms in the stomach duct"] Blood and qi are vigorous and abundant, and heat presses within; the two qi combine into heat; therefore it knots into an abscess.

## Notes

The pulse diagnosis (stomach pulse sunken and fine, Renying abundant) and cause of abscess of the stomach duct.
