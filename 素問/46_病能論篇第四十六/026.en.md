# 素問 病能論篇第四十六 §26 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

What is called *qi* (extraordinary) is that extraordinary illness does not lead to death according to the four seasons; *heng* (ordinary) is that it does lead to death according to the four seasons. What is called *kui* is seeking it by pressing, that is, seeking the pattern of the pulse by pressing; *duo* is finding where the illness is and measuring it by the four seasons.

## Translation of Wang Bing's commentary and the Song editors' notes

[On "What is called *qi* ... it does lead to death according to the four seasons"] Song editors: Yang Shangshan says, "When illness is contracted and transmitted, and one dies on reaching the season that overcomes it, this is *heng*. When joy or anger arises within and the illness is not transmitted in order, this is *qi*."

[On "What is called *kui* ... measuring it by the four seasons"] Whenever "what is called" is said, it explains a meaning not yet clarified. But examining the text before and after, this "what is called" does not connect at all with the meaning of this chapter. These few sentences hardly form a coherent sense; in the end they are a separate explanation of a classic's text. The current editions already lack the two chapters of juan 7; this is presumably displaced text from those missing chapters. The ancient text broke apart and was mistakenly continued here.

## Notes

A fragment explaining the terms *qi*, *heng*, *kui* and *duo*; this ends the chapter.
