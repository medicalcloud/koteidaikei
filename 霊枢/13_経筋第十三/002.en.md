# 霊枢 経筋第十三 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The sinew of the foot lesser yang starts at the fourth toe, rises to knot at the outer ankle, rises along the outer edge of the shin and knots at the outer edge of the knee. Its branch separately starts at the outer auxiliary bone and rises to the thigh; the front part knots above Futu, the back part at the sacrum. Its straight part rises over the soft flank and the free ribs, rises to the front edge of the armpit, attaches to the chest and breast, and knots at the empty basin. A straight part rises to come out at the armpit, passes through the empty basin, comes out in front of the greater yang, follows behind the ear, rises to the corner of the forehead, crosses over the vertex, descends to the chin and rises to knot at the cheekbone. A branch knots at the canthus and forms the outer tie. Its illness: propping and cramping of the fourth toe, cramping pulling to the outside of the knee, inability to bend or stretch the knee, tension of the sinews in the back of the knee pulling the thigh in front and the sacrum behind, rising to pain in the soft flank and free ribs, rising to pull the empty basin, chest, breast and neck, and tension of the tie sinews. [Since they cross] from left to right, the right eye cannot open; [the sinew] rises past the right corner [of the head], runs alongside the *Qiao* meridian, and the left connects with the right; hence injury to the left corner makes the right foot useless. This is called crossing of the tie sinews. The treatment is quick needling with a heated needle, taking the effect as the number and the painful spot as the point. It is called early-spring impediment.

## Notes

The sinew of the foot lesser yang.
- 従左之右，右目不開 follows Yang Shangshan's note in the *Taisu*: the sinews cross left and right at the vertex and descend to the canthi, so injury to the left forehead corner disables the right foot and vice versa, because the tie sinews cross. The right eye cannot open because the sinew crosses from left to right.
