# 霊枢 大惑論第八十 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Yellow Emperor asked Qibo, "I once climbed the Qingling Terrace; halfway up the stairs I looked back, and crawling forward I became dizzy. I privately found this strange and inwardly wondered at it; I closed my eyes and opened them, calmed my mind and settled my qi, but for a long time it did not pass. My head spun and reeled; with my hair disheveled I knelt a long while and looked down, but even after a long time it did not stop. Then suddenly it resolved of itself. What qi made it so?"

## Notes

The beginning of the chapter. It explains the "confusion" of dizziness at heights, and the causes and treatment of forgetfulness, hunger without appetite, sleeplessness, closed eyes, excessive sleep, etc.
- Zhang Jiebin's note in the *Leijing*: a high terrace has cold air, hence "Qingling" (clear and cold); when people climb high and gaze far, seeing extraordinary sights, the spirit and soul are shaken and confusion arises in the heart, so the Emperor uses this to ask its cause.
- For 独博 the *Taisu* reads 独転 (the *Leijing* and the *Lingshu jizhu* agree with the base). It is taken as "spinning".
- For 卒然自上 the *Taisu* and the *Jiayi jing* read 卒然自止 (the *Leijing* and the *Lingshu jizhu* agree with the base). The sense is unclear; it is taken as the confusion suddenly resolving of itself.
