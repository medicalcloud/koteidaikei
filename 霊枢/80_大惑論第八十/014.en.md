# 霊枢 大惑論第八十 §14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "Such people have large intestines and stomachs, damp skin, and flesh divisions that are not supple. With large intestines and stomach the defensive qi lingers long; with damp skin the flesh divisions are not supple and its movement is slow. The defensive qi always moves in the yang by day and in the yin by night; so when the yang qi is exhausted one sleeps, and when the yin qi is exhausted one wakes. So with large intestines and stomach, the defensive qi lingers long in its course; with damp skin and stiff flesh divisions, its movement is slow. Lingering long in the yin, its qi is not clear, so one wants to close one's eyes, hence much sleep. Those with small intestines and stomach, smooth and relaxed skin and supple flesh divisions have the defensive qi lingering long in the yang, so they sleep little."

## Notes

The *Leijing* note: 解 means "supple"; the viscera are inside, yin; the skin and flesh divisions are outside, yang. With large intestines and stomach the yin path is long and winding; with damp, stagnant flesh the yang path is slow, so the defensive qi stays long in the yin and moves little in the yang, the yang qi is not refined, hence much sleep. People today who feel sleepy right after a full meal do so because the fierce qi of water and grain suddenly fills the interior, the defensive qi becomes abundant in the yin, and the refined yang qi cannot prevail.
