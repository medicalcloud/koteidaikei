# 霊枢 本蔵第四十七 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Yellow Emperor asked Qibo, "People's blood, qi, essence and spirit are what sustain life and complete their allotted span. The channels are what move the blood and qi, circulate yin and yang, moisten the sinews and bones and ease the joints. The defensive qi is what warms the partings of the flesh, fills the skin, enriches the interstices and governs opening and closing. Will and intention are what control the essence and spirit, gather the ethereal and corporeal souls, adapt to cold and warmth and harmonize joy and anger. Therefore when the blood is harmonious, the channels flow, circulating over yin and yang, the sinews and bones are strong and the joints supple. When the defensive qi is harmonious, the partings of the flesh are loose and smooth, the skin is even and soft, and the interstices are dense. When will and intention are harmonious, essence and spirit are focused and upright, the ethereal and corporeal souls do not scatter, regret and anger do not arise, and the five viscera do not receive pathogens. When cold and warmth are harmonious, the six bowels transform grain, wind impediment does not arise, the channels flow freely and the limbs and joints are at ease. This is the normal balance of people. The five viscera store essence, spirit, blood, qi and the ethereal and corporeal souls; the six bowels transform water and grain and move the fluids. These are what all people receive from heaven, whether foolish or wise, worthy or unworthy, with no partiality. Yet some alone live out their heavenly span without deviant illness, not declining in a hundred years, and even exposure to wind and rain, sudden cold and great heat cannot harm them; while others never leave the shelter of their rooms, have no fear or anxiety, and still cannot escape illness. Why? I wish to hear the reason."

## Notes

The beginning of the chapter, asking about illnesses according to the size, height, firmness and alignment of the five viscera, how to recognize them from outside, and the correspondences of the six bowels.
- 関 in 司関闔 is read as 開 "open".
