# 霊枢 熱病第二十三 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a heat illness has lasted three days and the qi opening is calm but Renying agitated, take the yang channels with the fifty-nine needlings to drain the heat and bring out the sweat, and fill the yin to supplement what is insufficient. When the body's heat is severe but the yin and yang [pulses] are both calm, do not needle; for those that may be needled, take them at once, and if there is no sweating, drain. Not needling is said of cases with signs of death. When a heat illness has lasted seven or eight days, the pulse opening is agitated and the breathing panting and short, needle at once and sweat will come out by itself; needle shallowly between the thumb [and index finger]. When a heat illness has lasted seven or eight days and the pulse is faint and small, with blood in the urine and a dry mouth, death comes in a day and a half; with an intermittent pulse, death comes in one day. When a heat illness has already produced sweat but the pulse is still agitated, with panting and renewed heat, do not needle the skin; with severe panting there is death. When a heat illness has lasted seven or eight days and the pulse is not agitated, or agitated but not scattered or rapid, sweat will come within the next three days; if no sweat comes in three days, death comes on the fourth. For those who have not yet sweated, do not needle the interstices. When a heat illness begins with pain of the skin, a blocked nose and a puffy face, take it at the skin with the first needle, [using] the fifty-nine. With a rash on the nose, seek the skin in the lungs; if not found, seek it in fire; fire is the heart. When a heat illness begins with a rough, leaning [heavy] body and heat, vexed oppression and dryness of the lips, mouth and throat, take it at the skin with the first needle, [using] the fifty-nine. With distension of the skin, a dry mouth and cold sweat, seek the vessels in the heart; if not found, seek them in water; water is the kidneys. When a heat illness has a dry throat with much drinking, frequent fright, and inability to get up, take it at the skin and flesh with the sixth needle, [using] the fifty-nine. With green canthi, seek the flesh in the spleen; if not found, seek it in wood; wood is the liver. When a heat illness has a green face, brain pain and agitated hands and feet, take it between the sinews with the fourth needle at the four counterflows [limbs]. With lame sinews and soaked eyes, seek the sinews in the liver; if not found, seek them in metal; metal is the lungs. When a heat illness has frequent fright, tugging and slackening and madness, take it at the vessels with the fourth needle, quickly draining what is in surplus. With epilepsy and loss of hair, seek the blood in the heart; if not found, seek it in water; water is the kidneys. When a heat illness has a heavy body, aching bones, deafness and a desire to sleep, take it at the bones with the fourth needle, with the fifty-nine needlings. With bone illness, not eating, grinding teeth and green ears, seek the bones in the kidneys; if not found, seek them in earth; earth is the spleen. When a heat illness does not know where it hurts, with deafness, inability to control [the limbs], a dry mouth, intense yang heat and some cold in the yin, the heat is in the marrow; death cannot be treated. When a heat illness has headache, pain of the vessels around the temples and eyes, and frequent nosebleeds, it is reversal heat illness; take it with the third needle, observing surplus and insufficiency. Cold, heat, hemorrhoids. When a heat illness has a heavy body and heat in the intestines, take it with the fourth needle at its transport points and between the toes below, seeking the qi in the stomach network vessel, and the qi will be obtained. When a heat illness has tense pain flanking the navel and fullness of the chest and flanks, take it at Yongquan and Yinlingquan, taking it with the fourth needle, and needle inside the throat. When in a heat illness sweat is about to come out and the pulse is compliant so that sweating is permissible, take it at Yuji, Taiyuan, Dadu and Taibai; draining removes the heat, supplementing brings out sweat; if the sweating is too great, take the transverse vessel above the inner ankle to stop it. When a heat illness has already produced sweat but the pulse is still agitated and abundant, this is the extreme of the yin pulse; there is death. If, having sweated, the pulse becomes calm, there is life. In a heat illness, when the pulse is still abundant and agitated but no sweat is obtained, this is the extreme of the yang pulse; there is death. If the pulse is abundant and agitated but becomes calm after sweating, there is life. There are nine cases of heat illness that must not be needled: first, no sweating, great redness of the cheekbones and hiccup mean death; second, diarrhea with severe abdominal fullness means death; third, unclear vision with unremitting heat means death; fourth, in the elderly and infants, heat with abdominal fullness means death; fifth, no sweating with vomiting and passing of blood means death; sixth, a rotting root of the tongue with unremitting heat means death; seventh, cough with nosebleed, and no sweating or sweating that does not reach the feet means death; eighth, heat in the marrow means death; ninth, heat with convulsions means death, [convulsions being] the back arched as if broken, tugging and slackening, and clenching and grinding of the teeth. All these nine must not be needled. The so-called fifty-nine needlings are: three each on the outer and inner sides of both hands, twelve needle points in all; one each between the five fingers, eight needle points in all, and the same for the feet; on the head, one *cun* into the hairline and three *fen* to the side, three on each side, six needle points in all; further three *cun* into the hairline, five on each side, ten needle points in all; one each in front of and behind the ears and below the mouth, and one in the middle of the nape, six needle points in all; one on top of the vertex, one at Xinhui, one at the hairline, one at Lianquan, two at Fengchi and two at Tianzhu.

## Notes

Diagnosis and treatment of heat illness, the nine cases that must not be needled, and the points of the fifty-nine needlings. The interlinear note is not translated (original text only).
- The first, third, fourth and sixth needles are identified by the order of the nine needles in chapters 1 and 78.
- The readings and meanings of 苛軫鼻, 目浸, 顳顬目痲脈痛 and 寒熱痔, and the counting of the fifty-nine needlings (the total does not come to fifty-nine), are unverified (recorded in the list of deferred points); translated provisionally.
- The base's 「気于胃胳」 is emended to 「気于胃絡」. Wikisource (*Huangdi neijing*), the *Taisu* and *Jiayi jing* read 胃絡; 絡 is adopted.
