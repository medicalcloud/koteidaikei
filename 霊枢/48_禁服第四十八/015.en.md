# 霊枢 禁服第四十八 §15 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the inch opening is once as large as Renying, the illness is in the foot *jueyin*; once as large and agitated, in the hand heart governor. When the inch opening is twice as large, the illness is in the foot lesser yin; twice as large and agitated, in the hand lesser yin. When the inch opening is three times as large, the illness is in the foot greater yin; three times as large and agitated, in the hand greater yin. Abundance means distension and fullness, cold in the center and undigested food; depletion means heat in the center, gruel-like stools, shortness of qi and discolored urine; tightness means painful impediment; an intermittent pulse means pain that comes and goes. Drain when abundant, supplement when depleted; when tight, first needle and then apply moxibustion; when intermittent, take the blood network vessels and then regulate; when sunken, apply moxibustion only. Sunken means the blood of the vessels is knotted within, with stuck blood inside; the blood is cold, so moxibustion is appropriate. When neither abundant nor depleted, take it at the channel. When the inch opening is four times as large, it is called inner block; inner block is both large and rapid, death without cure. One must carefully examine the cold and warmth of root and branch to verify the illness of the viscera and bowels.

## Notes

- The base's 「寸口大干人迎」 is emended to 「寸口大于人迎」. The Siku edition, the *Taisu*, *Leijing* and *Lingshu jizhu* read 于, and Wikisource (*Huangdi neijing*) and Daizhige 於 (same meaning); 干 is taken as a graphic error for 于.
- The base's 「熱中出縻」 is emended to 「熱中出糜」. Wikisource (*Huangdi neijing*), Daizhige, the *Taisu*, *Leijing* and *Lingshu jizhu* read 糜 (gruel-like stools); 糜 is adopted.
