# 霊枢 憂恚無言第六十九 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoshi answered, "The throat is the path of water and grain. The windpipe is where the qi goes up and down. The epiglottis is the door of the voice. The lips are the leaves of the voice. The tongue is the mechanism of the voice. The uvula is the gate of the voice. The nasopharynx is where the qi is divided and released. The transverse bone is directed by the spirit qi and governs moving the tongue. Thus when a person's nose runs uncontrollably, the nasopharynx does not open and the division of qi is lost. So if the epiglottis is small and thin, the qi is emitted quickly, it opens and closes smoothly, and the qi comes out easily; if it is large and thick, opening and closing are difficult and the qi comes out slowly, hence stammering. When a person suddenly loses the voice, cold qi has lodged in the epiglottis, so that it cannot rise, and having risen cannot descend; its opening and closing fail, hence there is no voice."

## Notes

The transverse bone is the soft bone above the throat; below it connects with the heart and lungs, so it is directed by the spirit qi; above it connects with the root of the tongue, so it governs moving the tongue (Zhang Jiebin's note in the *Leijing*).
- 疾 in 疾薄 means "quick", and 重言 means halting, faltering speech (*Leijing*).
- 不致 follows the *Leijing* note: 不致 means "cannot"; when cold qi lodges in the epiglottis the airway is impaired, it can neither raise [the sound] high nor lower it, and opening and closing are both impeded, so the voice is suddenly lost.
