# 霊枢 寿夭剛柔第六 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoshi answered, "Within yin there is yin, within yang there is yang. If one carefully knows yin and yang, there is a method for needling; if one finds where the illness began, there is a principle for needling. Carefully gauge the beginnings of the illness so that they correspond with the season, matching the five viscera and six bowels within and the sinews, bones and skin without. Thus there is yin and yang within, and also yin and yang without. Within, the five viscera are yin and the six bowels yang; without, the sinews and bones are yin and the skin yang. Hence it is said: illness in the yin of yin, needle the brook and stream points of the yin; illness in the yang of yang, needle the uniting points of the yang; illness in the yin of yang, needle the river points of the yin; illness in the yang of yin, needle the network vessels. Hence it is said: illness in the yang is called wind, illness in the yin is called impediment, and illness of both yin and yang is called wind impediment. An illness with form but no pain is of the yang kind; one without form but with pain is of the yin kind. Without form but with pain, the yang is intact and the yin is harmed; treat the yin at once and do not attack the yang. With form but no pain, the yin is intact and the yang is harmed; treat the yang at once and do not attack the yin. When yin and yang both stir, now with form, now without form, and there is also vexation of the heart, it is called yin overcoming yang; this is called neither exterior nor interior, and the form will not last long."
