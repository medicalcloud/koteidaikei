# 霊枢 叙 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Of old the Yellow Emperor composed the *Neijing* in eighteen fascicles: the *Lingshu* in nine and the *Suwen* in nine make up that number. What the world honors and practises is only the *Suwen*. Yueren [Qin Yueren, Bian Que] obtained one or two parts of it and wrote the *Nanjing*; Huangfu Mi arranged it and made the *Jiayi [jing]*. The doctrines of all the schools began from these. Among them there are gains and losses, and some cannot be taken as rules for later ages. For example, the *Nanyang huoren shu* says, "Cough-counterflow is hiccup." Yet on careful examination the *Lingshu jing* says, "New grain qi enters the stomach and contends with the old cold qi; therefore it is called hiccup." Put the two side by side and the principle can be decided. Again, the sixty-fifth chapter [difficulty] of the *Nanjing* is Yueren's outline of the *Benshu* [chapter] of the *Lingshu*, but some in the world take it to be about the flow and pouring [of qi]. On careful examination the *Lingshu jing* says, "What are called joints are the places where spirit qi wanders, goes out and comes in; they are not skin, flesh, sinew or bone." It also says, "Spirit qi is healthy qi." Where spirit qi wanders, goes out and comes in is the flow and pouring; the well, spring, stream, meridian and uniting points are the root points. Put the two side by side, and one knows that the distance between them is greater than that between heaven and earth. I only regret that the *Lingshu* has long been lost, so that no one in the world can study it thoroughly. Being a physician depends on reading medical books. There are those who read and still cannot be physicians, but there has never been anyone who became a physician without reading. Not reading medical books, and not being of a hereditary medical family, [and still practising,] one kills people more cruelly than with a club or a blade. Therefore the ancients said, "To be a son and not read medical books is the same as being unfilial." I am by nature ordinary and dull, but from childhood to manhood I have immersed my mind in this way and have gained some grasp of its principles. So, without measuring my own ability, I compared the various books and again collated the old *Lingshu* in nine fascicles kept in my family, eighty-one chapters in all, added and revised sound glosses, attached them at the ends of the fascicles, and arranged the whole into twenty-four fascicles, hoping that those who love life may open the book and understand it easily, with no discrepancies at all. Apart from having already submitted a written statement through the office to which I belong, following the instructions of the prefectural government I have, according to the regulations, applied to the Fiscal Commission to appoint officials to examine and settle it, and have prepared the book to send to the Palace Library and the Directorate of Education. Now I, Song, especially seek out eminent physicians and ask them further to examine it in detail, so that errors may be avoided in the future. Then the benefit will be without limit, and the merit will truly have its source. Written by Shi Song of Jinguan [Chengdu] on the full-moon day of midsummer in the *yihai* year of Shaoxing [1155] of the Song.

## Notes

The preface of Shi Song of the Southern Song. He tells how he collated the nine-fascicle *Lingshu* handed down in his family, added sound glosses, and had it published.
- *Nanyang huoren shu*: Zhu Gong's work of the Song (also called *Leizheng huoren shu*). Shi Song rejects its identification of cough-counterflow with hiccup by comparing the *Lingshu*. The quoted 新穀気入于胃，与故寒気相争，故曰噦 summarizes a passage in *Kouwen* (chapter 28) and is not a literal quotation.
- 所言節者，神気之所遊行出入也，非皮肉筋骨也 is from *Jiuzhen shier yuan* (chapter 1); 神気者，正気也 corresponds to 神者，正気也 in *Xiaozhen jie* (chapter 3).
- 由 in 由為不孝也 means 猶 (the Daizhige text reads 猶).
- 巳 in 除巳具状 is read as 已. The judged emendations are applied only to the main text of the chapters, so the preface keeps the base's character.
- 功実有自 is hard to translate; it is taken to mean that the merit will truly have its basis.
- 勒為二十四巻: the end of the table of contents says 元二十四巻今併為十二巻; this edition is in twelve fascicles.
