# 霊枢 通天第七十二 §12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoshi said, "The greater-yin person looks dark and dull, black in color; he keeps his thoughts low; he looks down from a tall, large frame; his knees seem bent, though he is not hunchbacked. This is the greater-yin person. The lesser-yin person looks seemingly pure but furtive [like a mouse or sparrow], with a firm, harmful heart; standing, he is restless and treacherous; walking, he seems to crouch. This is the lesser-yin person. The greater-yang person looks lofty and self-satisfied, bending his body back and bending his knees. This is the greater-yang person. The lesser-yang person likes to tilt his head up when standing and to sway when walking; his arms and elbows always stick out behind his back. This is the lesser-yang person. The person of balanced yin and yang looks composed, amiable, dignified and reverent, cheerful, well-turned and orderly; everyone calls him a gentleman. This is the person of balanced yin and yang."

## Notes

The end of the chapter: the appearance of the five dispositions. The bracketed meanings follow the *Leijing* notes throughout.
- The *Leijing* explains the greater-yang person's 反身折膕 as arching the back and thrusting out the belly so that the knees seem bent—signs of self-importance; and the lesser-yang person's elbows sticking out behind as liking display rather than concealment.
