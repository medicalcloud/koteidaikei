# 霊枢 通天第七十二 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoshi said, "The greater-yin person is greedy and unkind, humble and orderly and seemingly clear, likes taking in and hates giving out; his mind is mild and does not reveal itself; he does not keep up with the times, and acts only after others. This is the greater-yin person. The lesser-yin person is petty-greedy with a harmful heart; seeing others lose, he always seems to feel he has gained; he likes to injure and harm; seeing others prosper, he becomes resentful and angry; his heart is envious and without gratitude. This is the lesser-yin person. The greater-yang person is self-satisfied wherever he is, likes to talk of great affairs, is incapable but speaks empty words, his ambitions spread to the four wildernesses, his conduct disregards right and wrong, he handles affairs in an ordinary way yet insists on his own way, and even when things fail he never regrets. This is the greater-yang person. The lesser-yang person is scrupulous and likes to esteem himself; with a minor office he exalts himself and is content; he likes outside connections but does not attach himself to those close to him. This is the lesser-yang person. The person of balanced yin and yang lives calmly and quietly, is neither fearful nor elated, gently goes along with things, gives without contending, changes with the times, is humble when honored, and converses rather than governs—this is called perfect governance. The ancients skilled in needles and moxa looked at people's five dispositions and treated them accordingly, draining the abundant and supplementing the deficient."

## Notes

The temperaments of the five dispositions. The bracketed meanings follow the *Leijing* notes throughout.
- Greater yin: 下斉 "humble and orderly"; 湛湛 "clear like still water—humble and seemingly clear"; 内 equals 納 "take in"; 心和 the softness of yin; 不発 much yin, much stored; 動而後之 not taking the lead. The *Leijing* calls them people of deep feeling and solemn appearance whose cunning does not show.
- Lesser yin: 見人有亡，常若有得 is rejoicing in others' misfortune; 心疾 is envy.
- Greater yang: 于于 self-satisfied; 志発於四野 recklessly ambitious; 為事如常自用 ordinary in practice yet insisting on his own way.
- Lesser yang: 諟諦 examining again and again; 高自宜 shallow capacity, easily satisfied; 好為外交而不内附 devoted to empty show.
- Balanced: 譚而不治 means governing by non-action, which is the height of governance (*Leijing*).
- From 古之善用鍼艾者 on, the *Leijing* notes, the five treatments are discussed.
