# 霊枢 通天第七十二 §8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoshi said, "The greater-yin person has much yin and no yang; his yin blood is turbid, his wei-defensive qi rough, yin and yang are not in harmony, his sinews slack and his skin thick; unless drained quickly, he cannot be changed. The lesser-yin person has much yin and little yang, a small stomach and large intestines; the six fu organs are not in order, his *Yangming* pulse is small and his greater-yang pulse large; he must be carefully regulated, for his blood is easily lost and his qi easily ruined. The greater-yang person has much yang and little yin; he must be regulated with care, draining his yang without causing loss of his yin; if the yang is lost twice over he easily becomes manic, and if both yin and yang are lost he dies suddenly without recognizing anyone. The lesser-yang person has much yang and little yin; his meridians are small and collaterals large, the blood is within and the qi outside; replete the yin and deplete the yang. Draining only his collaterals makes him strong, but if the qi is lost quickly, the middle qi becomes insufficient and he will not rise from his illness. The person of balanced yin and yang has harmonious yin and yang qi and well-ordered blood vessels. Diagnose his yin and yang carefully, observe the pathogenic and healthy qi, settle his demeanor, determine surplus and insufficiency, drain if abundant, supplement if deficient, and if neither abundant nor deficient, treat by the meridian. This is how yin and yang are regulated and the five dispositions distinguished."

## Notes

The treatment of the five dispositions, translated following the *Leijing* notes.
- Greater yin: having no yang, his qi is scant, so the blood is turbid and the wei-defensive qi stagnant; with little qi the sinews are slack, and the yin body, heavy and turbid, has thick skin; with thick skin and turbid blood, only quick draining can change it.
- Lesser yin: the small stomach makes the foot *Yangming* pulse small; the large [small] intestine makes the hand greater yang pulse large. With a small stomach, little is stored and the qi must be feeble; with a large small intestine, transmission is quick and qi is not stored. Scant qi cannot hold the blood, so the blood is easily lost and the qi easily ruined.
- Greater yang: his yin is already scant; draining it would surely cause loss of yin, so only the yang should be drained. When yin is insufficient the yang has no root; overdraining causes repeated loss of yang and mania; loss of both causes sudden death.
- Lesser yang: the meridians are deep and yin, the collaterals shallow and yang; replete the yin meridians and drain the yang collaterals and the body is strong. But since the lesser-yang person relies on qi, overdraining causes quick loss of qi, the middle qi becomes deficient, and he cannot rise.
- Balanced: 不盛不虚以経取之 means that when there is no clear abundance or deficiency but the pathogenic and upright are out of balance, one finds the meridian concerned and treats the illness there.
