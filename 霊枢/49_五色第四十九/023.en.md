# 霊枢 五色第四十九 §23 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Each has its regions; since there are regions, use yin to harmonize yang and use yang to harmonize yin. When the regions are clearly understood, ten thousand attempts hit ten thousand times. Being able to distinguish left and right is called the great way; men and women differ in position, hence yin and yang. One who carefully examines luster and dullness is called a good practitioner. Deep and turbid means inside; floating and lustrous means outside. Yellow and red mean wind; green and black mean pain; white means cold. Yellow and greasy-moist means pus; intense red means blood; severe pain means cramps; severe cold means numbness of the skin. The five colors each appear in their regions: examine whether they float or sink to know depth; examine luster or dullness to see success or failure; examine whether scattered or concentrated to know distance; look at whether the color is above or below to know where the illness is; concentrate the spirit in the mind to know past and present. Thus if one does not observe the qi minutely, one cannot know right from wrong; keep the attention fixed without wandering, and one will know what is new and what is old. When the color is bright and not coarse [it is mild]; when deep and dull, it is severe. When neither bright nor lustrous, the illness is not severe. When the color is scattered, galloping and not yet gathered, the illness is dispersed with qi pain, and the accumulation has not yet formed. When the kidneys ride over the heart, the heart falls ill first and the kidneys respond; the colors are all like this. In men, color at the king of the face means lower abdominal pain; below it, testicular pain; if round and straight, penile pain; high means the root, low means the tip; these are of the fox *shan* and *tui* genital kind. In women, at the king of the face it means illness of the bladder and uterus; scattered means pain, concentrated means accumulation; square or round, left or right, each follows the form of the color. If it follows down to the lips, it means discharge; if moist like grease, it is from overeating or unclean food. Left means left, right means right. When the color is deviant, gathering and scattering irregularly, [the illness is] where the facial color points. The colors—green, black, red, white and yellow—are all upright and full, and some appear in other places. When the color in another place is red, and the red is as large as an elm seed, at the king of the face it means [death] within days. When the top of the color is pointed, the head is empty and it points upward; when the bottom is pointed, it points downward; on the left and right the rule is the same. The five colors name the viscera: green is the liver, red the heart, white the lungs, yellow the spleen, black the kidneys. The liver unites with the sinews, the heart with the vessels, the lungs with the skin, the spleen with the flesh, and the kidneys with the bones."

## Notes

General principles of color diagnosis. The Yellow Emperor's speech ends here, concluding the chapter.
- 大 in 沈大, 亦 in 亦甚 and 色亦大, and 脾 in 肺合脾 of the base are read as 夭, 赤, 赤 and 皮, as other sources have them (see collation).
- This unit contains many difficult expressions (駒駒然, 其圜直, 高為本，下為首, 至胝為淫, 別郷, 不日, 上鋭首空上向, etc.). They are translated provisionally and are unverified (recorded in the list of deferred points).
