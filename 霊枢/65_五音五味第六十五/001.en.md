# 霊枢 五音五味第六十五 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The right-*zhi* and lesser-*zhi* [persons] are regulated at the upper part of the right hand greater yang. The left-*shang* and left-*zhi*, at the upper left hand *Yangming*. The lesser-*zhi* and great-*gong*, at the upper left hand *Yangming*. The right-*jue* and great-*jue*, at the lower right foot lesser yang. The great-*zhi* and lesser-*zhi*, at the upper left hand greater yang. The common-*yu* and lesser-*yu*, at the lower right foot greater yang. The lesser-*shang* and right-*shang*, at the lower right hand greater yang. The confined-*yu* and common-*yu*, at the lower right foot greater yang. The lesser-*gong* and great-*gong*, at the lower right foot *Yangming*. The half-*jue* and lesser-*jue*, at the lower right foot lesser yang. The *dai*-*shang* and upper-*shang*, at the lower right foot *Yangming*. The *dai*-*shang* and upper-*jue*, at the lower left foot greater yang.

## Notes

The beginning of the chapter. For the twenty-five persons of the five notes in the previous chapter (ch. 64), it lists the parts of the three yang of hand and foot to be regulated (this unit), the correspondences of grains, domestic animals, fruits, etc. (unit 2), pairs belonging to the same part (unit 3), and the full list of names of the five notes (unit 4). It is not in dialogue form.
- 調 is taken as regulating and treating that part, following Zhang Zhicong's note in the *Lingshu jizhu*: continuing the previous chapter, it says that persons of the five notes whose blood and qi are insufficient should be regulated with the five flavors of the five grains and five animals. The *Jizhu* explains that persons assigned to different meridians in the previous chapter are regulated at the same part because blood and qi communicate above and below, left and right.
- Many items in this unit and unit 3 do not agree with the assignments in the previous chapter. Zhang Jiebin's note in the *Leijing* marks several as 義似不合 ("the sense does not seem to fit") and remarks at the end that the ancient text is obscure, readers did not understand it and copyists were careless, so that errors among left and right, upper and lower, great and lesser and the five notes multiplied; since much is hard to understand, he declines to force an interpretation and preserves the text for later scholars to correct. The *Jizhu* likewise says it does not know whether these reflect communication of meridian qi or copying errors, and annotates by conjecture. This translation renders the text literally and does not judge which characters are wrong.
- 少角 is a name not found in the previous chapter. The *Jizhu* says 判 means half, so the half *jue* is the lesser *jue*, and its absence from the previous chapter is probably a copying error. The *Jizhu* also remarks on 右角与大角 that the previous chapter has a left *jue* but no right *jue*, so one of the two characters 左/右 is in error.
