# 霊枢 本輸第二 §11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The sanjiao joins above with hand lesser yang and emerges at Guanchong. Guanchong is at the tip of the finger next to the little finger; it is the well, metal. It flows to Yemen; Yemen is between the little finger and the next; it is the brook. It pours into Zhongzhu; Zhongzhu is in the depression behind the base joint; it is the stream. It passes Yangchi; Yangchi is in the depression above the wrist; it is the source. It moves to Zhigou; Zhigou is in the depression between the two bones three *cun* above the wrist; it is the river. It enters Tianjing; Tianjing is in the depression above the large bone on the outside of the elbow; it is the uniting point, found by bending the elbow. The lower transport point of the sanjiao lies in front of the foot greater yang and behind lesser yang, emerging at the outer edge of the back of the knee, and is called Weiyang; it is the collateral of greater yang. This is the hand lesser yang meridian. The sanjiao is led by foot lesser yang and greater yin [yang] and is a branch of greater yang; five *cun* above the ankle it branches in, threads the calf, emerges at Weiyang, runs alongside the main greater yang vessel, enters to network the bladder, and binds the lower jiao. When replete, there is urinary blockage; when deficient, incontinence. For incontinence supplement; for blockage drain.

## Notes

The five transport points and source point of the sanjiao (hand lesser yang), its lower transport point (Weiyang), and the treatment of its illnesses. The interlinear notes are not translated (original text only).
- The base's 「足大指之前」 is emended to 「足太陽之前」. The Lingshu editions agree with the base (大指), but the *Jiayi jing* (entry on Weiyang) reads "in front of the foot greater yang, behind the lesser yang", and Zhang Jiebin's note in the *Leijing* says 大指 should be 小指. Weiyang lies on the lateral side, so 太陽 is adopted following the *Jiayi jing*.
- For 太陰 in 足少陽太陰之所将, the base's interlinear note says "one edition has 陽", and the *Lingshu jizhu* agrees with the base. The same phrase could not be found in the *Leijing*, *Taisu* or *Jiayi jing*. Which is correct is unknown, so the base is kept and [yang] is noted in the translation.
