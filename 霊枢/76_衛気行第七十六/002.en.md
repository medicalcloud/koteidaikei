# 霊枢 衛気行第七十六 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "The year has twelve months and the day twelve double hours; *zi* and *wu* are the warp, *mao* and *you* the weft. Heaven revolves through the twenty-eight lodges, seven stars to each side, four times seven making twenty-eight; Fang and Mao are the weft, Xu and Zhang the warp. Thus Fang to Bi is yang, Mao to Xin is yin; yang governs the day, yin the night. Hence the wei-defensive qi circulates fifty times through the body in a day and a night: by day twenty-five times in the yang, by night twenty-five times in the yin, circling the five zang organs. Thus at dawn, when yin is exhausted, the yang qi emerges at the eyes; when the eyes open, the qi rises to the head, follows the nape down the foot greater yang, and follows the back down to the tip of the little toe. Its dispersing branch separates at the outer canthus, descends the hand greater yang to the outer side of the little finger. Another dispersing branch separates at the outer canthus, descends the foot lesser yang and pours between the little toe and the next. From there it rises along the side of the hand lesser yang and descends to between the little fingers. A separate branch rises to in front of the ear, joins the jaw vessels, pours into the foot *Yangming*, descends to the instep and enters between the toes. Its dispersing branch descends from below the ear along the hand *Yangming*, enters between the thumb [and the next finger] and into the palm. When it reaches the foot, it enters the sole, emerges below the inner ankle, travels the yin division and rejoins at the eyes. This makes one circuit.

## Notes

Qibo's speech continues through unit 4. Translated following Zhang Jiebin's notes in the *Leijing*.
- 子午為経，卯酉為緯: what is fixed in the heavens is the warp, what moves is the weft; *zi* and *wu* correspond to the north and south poles, which stay in place, hence warp; *mao* and *you*, where the lodges rise in the east and set in the west, hence weft. Fang is in the middle of *mao*, Mao in the middle of *you*, hence weft; Xu is in *zi*, Zhang in *wu*, hence warp (*Leijing*).
- Fang to Bi occupies *mao* through *shen*, yang, governing the day; Mao to Wei occupies *you* through *yin*, yin, governing the night (*Leijing*).
- The daytime course of the wei-defensive qi begins at Jingming of the foot greater yang, passes through the six fu organs and reaches the kidney meridian to make one circuit; at the end it passes from the foot lesser yin through the springing vessel to the inner canthus and rejoins at Jingming (*Leijing*).
- The *Leijing* suggests that 分側 should read 外側; that 次指 is missing after the little finger (Guanchong of the hand lesser yang); that 五指 should be 中指 (Lidui of the foot *Yangming*); and that 次指 is missing after 大指 for the hand *Yangming* (Shangyang). None of these is supported by other sources, so the base is translated.
- The base's 周於五歳 is emended to 周於五蔵. The *Jiayi jing*, the *Lingshu jizhu*, the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource and the Daizhige text read 五蔵, and the *Leijing*, though its text reads 五歳, notes that 歳 should be 蔵 and is an error (the *Jiayi jing* notes "another text has 歳"). Since at night the qi travels the yin division and circles the five zang organs, 蔵 is adopted.
