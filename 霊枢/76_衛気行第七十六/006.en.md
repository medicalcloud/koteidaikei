# 霊枢 衛気行第七十六 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Bogao said, "The divisions [of day and night] vary in length, and days are long or short; spring, autumn, winter and summer each have their own divisions. Even so, always take dawn as the reference and the end of night as the beginning. Thus in a day and a night the water clock drops one hundred marks; twenty-five marks are the measure of half a day. It is always so without end; stop at sunset, and according to the length of the day, take each as the reference for needling. Wait carefully for the time, and the illness can be [cured] on schedule; if one misses the time and goes against the signs, none of the hundred illnesses is cured. Hence it is said: needling excess, needle as it comes; needling deficiency, needle as it goes. This means examining deficiency and excess by the times when the qi is present or absent, and needling accordingly. Thus to needle after carefully waiting for where the qi is—this is called meeting the time. [If the illness] is in the three yang, wait until the qi is in the yang and needle; if the illness is in the three yin, wait until the qi is in the yin division and needle. At one mark of the water clock, the human qi is in the greater yang; at two marks, in the lesser yang; at three, in the *Yangming*; at four, in the yin division. At five, greater yang; six, lesser yang; seven, *Yangming*; eight, yin division. At nine, greater yang; ten, lesser yang; eleven, *Yangming*; twelve, yin division. At thirteen, greater yang; fourteen, lesser yang; fifteen, *Yangming*; sixteen, yin division. At seventeen, greater yang; eighteen, lesser yang; nineteen, *Yangming*; twenty, yin division. At twenty-one, greater yang; twenty-two, lesser yang; twenty-three, *Yangming*; twenty-four, yin division. At twenty-five marks, the human qi is in the greater yang: this is the measure of half a day. From Fang to Bi are fourteen lodges, the water clock drops fifty marks, and the sun travels half a degree. As it turns through one lodge, the water clock drops three marks and four sevenths of a mark. The *Great Essentials* says: whenever the sun is over a lodge, the human qi is in the greater yang. Thus as the sun moves one lodge, the human qi passes through the three yang and the yin division, always so without end, sharing its order with heaven and earth, profuse yet orderly, ending and beginning again; in a day and a night the water clock drops its hundred marks and is done."

## Notes

The end of the chapter, translated following the *Leijing* notes.
- 二十五刻者，半日之度也: a day and night has one hundred marks; twenty-five is a quarter. Dividing the day in two gives day and night; dividing it into four seasons gives morning as spring, noon as summer, sunset as autumn and midnight as winter; hence "half a day" here (*Leijing*).
- 刺実者刺其来，刺虚者刺其去: needling excess means meeting the qi as it arrives and seizing it; needling deficiency means following the qi as it leaves and supplementing (*Leijing*).
- 水下一刻…: counting from dawn; greater yang, lesser yang and *Yangming* each include both hand and foot meridians, while the yin division refers only to the foot lesser yin; every four marks is one circuit of the yang division (*Leijing*). The *Leijing* also remarks that in twenty-five marks the wei-defensive qi makes only a little over six circuits, which does not agree with fifty circuits in a day and night; there may be another explanation, which he leaves for later scholars to correct.
- 迴行一舎，水下三刻与七分刻之四: dividing one hundred marks by twenty-eight gives 3.57… marks per lodge, exactly four sevenths (*Leijing*).
- 紛紛盼盼 means orderly amid profusion (*Leijing*). The final 盼盼 is the headword of a sound gloss; the interlinear note says, "The *Taisu yinyi* gives the reading *pu-ba*."
- The base's 母巳 is emended to 毋已. The *Taisu* and the Daizhige text read 毋已, the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource and the *Lingshu jizhu* 無已, the *Jiayi jing* 無巳 (the *Leijing* 毋巳). The sense is "always so without end", so the graphically similar 母巳 is taken as an error for 毋已.
- The base's 半月之度 is emended to 半日之度. The *Taisu*, the *Leijing*, the *Lingshu jizhu*, the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource and the Daizhige text all read 半日. Twenty-five marks are the measure of half a day, as in 二十五刻者半日之度也 above, so 日 is adopted.
