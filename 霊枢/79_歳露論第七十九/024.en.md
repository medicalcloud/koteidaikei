# 霊枢 歳露論第七十九 §24 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoshi said, "To observe this, always use the day of the winter solstice, when Taiyi stands in the palace of Xiezhe. When it arrives, heaven always responds with wind and rain. Wind and rain coming from the south are deficiency wind, which harms and injures people. If it arrives at midnight, when all the people are lying down, they do not encounter it, so that year the people have little illness. If it arrives in the daytime, when all the people are relaxed, they are all struck by the deficiency wind, so many people fall ill. The deficiency pathogen enters and lodges in the bones without manifesting outside; when the Beginning of Spring comes, the yang qi issues greatly and the interstices open, and if on the day of the Beginning of Spring the wind comes from the west, the people are again all struck by deficiency wind. When these two pathogens strike each other, the meridian qi becomes knotted and displaced. Hence all who meet that wind and encounter that rain are said to meet the year's exposure. If the year is harmonious with few thief winds, the people have little illness and few deaths; if the year has many thief winds and pathogenic qi, and cold and warmth are not harmonious, the people fall ill in great numbers and die."

## Notes

経気結代 follows the *Leijing* note: on the day of the Beginning of Spring the month-pointer is in the east, so wind from the west is also deficiency wind; struck at the winter solstice and again at the Beginning of Spring—these are the two pathogens; the pathogen lingers without leaving, hence 結; it occurs in that season but is not its qi, hence 代.
- 歳露: Yang Shangshan's note in the *Taisu* says there are two kinds of dew: spring dew, which gives birth to things, and autumn dew, which makes them decline; when a year has thief winds and heavy rain that make things decline, it resembles autumn wind and dew, hence "the year's exposure".
- For 民小病 the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource and the Daizhige text read 民少病. 小 and 少 are interchangeable, so the base is translated as "little".
