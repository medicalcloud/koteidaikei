# 霊枢 歳露論第七十九 §26 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoshi answered, "On the first day of the first month, when Taiyi resides in the palace of Tianliu, if that day there is a northwest wind and no rain, many people will die. On the first day of the first month, if there is a north wind at dawn, many people will die in spring. If at dawn a north wind blows on, many people fall ill—three in ten. If there is a north wind at midday, many people will die in summer. If there is a north wind in the evening, many people will die in autumn. If the north wind blows all day, there will be great illness, and six in ten will die. On the first day of the first month, wind from the south is called the drought quarter; wind from the west is called white bones—the country is about to suffer calamity and many people will die. On the first day of the first month, if wind comes from the east, lifting roofs and raising sand and stones, the country will have a great disaster. If wind blows from the southeast, there will be deaths in spring. On the first day of the first month, if the weather is mild and warm without wind, grain is cheap and the people do not fall ill; if it is cold and windy, grain is dear and many people fall ill. This is what is meant by observing the year's winds that harm and injure people. If there is no wind on the *chou* day of the second month, many people have illness of the heart and abdomen; if it is not warm on the *xu* day of the third month, many have cold and heat; if it is not hot on the *si* day of the fourth month, many have heat illness; if it is not cold on the *shen* day of the tenth month, many die suddenly. All the winds referred to are those that lift roofs, break trees, raise sand and stones, make the fine hairs stand up and open the interstices."

## Notes

The end of the chapter. The *Leijing* notes: New Year's Day is the head of early spring and the start of growth; a strong north wind then means yin overcoming yang and much harm; a strong pathogenic wind on New Year's Day is not auspicious, and the disasters differ by direction; the second to fourth months are when yang flourishes, so if there is no wind on *chou*, no warmth on *xu*, no heat on *si*, yin prevails and yang is blocked, and many fall ill; the tenth month is when yin flourishes, so if it is not cold on *shen*, yang prevails and yin is not stored, and many die suddenly.
- For 白骨将国有殃, the *Taisu* doubles 将 (Xiao Yanping notes that the *Lingshu* and the *Jiayi jing* do not). Here 白骨 is taken as the name of the wind and 将 as "is about to".
- The final 理郄 is the headword of a sound gloss, with the interlinear note "reading *qi-ni*" (apparently referring to the character in 腠理㕁 in unit 13).
- The base's 正月朔天利温 is emended to 正月朔日天和温. For 利, the *Taisu*, the *Leijing*, the *Lingshu jizhu*, the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource and the Daizhige text all read 和 (the *Jiayi jing* 時和温); it pairs with 天寒而風 and describes mild weather, so the graphically similar 利 is taken as an error for 和. Also, after 朔 the *Taisu*, the *Jiayi jing*, the *Lingshu jizhu*, the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource and the Daizhige text all have 日, and the preceding items all read 正月朔日, so 日 is supplied.
- 残 is supplied in the blank of the base's 歳之風　傷人. The base has a one-character blank here. The *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource and the Daizhige text read 残 (the *Leijing* and the *Lingshu jizhu* have a character here that cannot be displayed in the digital text). 残 ("harm") fits the context, so it is supplied.
- The base's 四月已 is emended to 四月巳. The *Taisu*, the *Leijing*, the *Jiayi jing* and the *Lingshu jizhu* all read 巳. The text names branch days (二月丑, 三月戌, 十月申), so the graphically similar 已 is taken as an error for 巳.
