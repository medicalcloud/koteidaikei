# 霊枢 九宮八風第七十七 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Thus Taiyi enters and moves to stand in the central palace, receives the homage of the eight winds, and divines good and ill fortune. Wind from the south is called the great weak wind; when it injures people, it lodges inside in the heart and outside in the vessels, and its qi governs heat. Wind from the southwest is called the scheming wind; it lodges inside in the spleen and outside in the muscles, and its qi governs weakness. Wind from the west is called the hard wind; it lodges inside in the lungs and outside in the skin, and its qi governs dryness. Wind from the northwest is called the breaking wind; it lodges inside in the small intestine and outside in the hand greater yang vessel; when the vessel is cut off there is overflow, when it is blocked there is knotting and obstruction, and sudden death is common. Wind from the north is called the great hard wind; it lodges inside in the kidneys and outside in the bones and the paraspinal sinews of the shoulders and back, and its qi governs cold. Wind from the northeast is called the baleful wind; it lodges inside in the large intestine and outside below the bones of both flanks and armpits and in the joints of the limbs. Wind from the east is called the infant wind; it lodges inside in the liver and outside in the sinew knots, and its qi governs bodily dampness. Wind from the southeast is called the weak wind; it lodges inside in the stomach and outside in the flesh, and its qi governs heaviness of the body. These eight winds all come from the direction of depletion, and so can make people ill. When the three depletions combine, there is sudden illness and sudden death. With two repletions and one depletion, the illness is cold and heat from soaking and exposure. Trespassing on rainy, damp ground causes wilting. Therefore the sage avoids wind as one avoids arrows and stones. One who has the three depletions and is struck on one side by pathogenic wind will collapse as if struck and become hemiplegic.

## Notes

The end of the chapter, translated following the *Leijing* notes.
- 太一入徙立於中宮，乃朝八風: this makes clear that Taiyi is the North Pole; if the center is not established, the climates of the directions are not correct, so Taiyi stands in the central palace while the Dipper sets the seasons outside, and only then can the eight winds pay homage and fortune be divined (*Leijing*).
- The names and illnesses of the eight winds, per the *Leijing*: the south is the *li* fire palace, a direction of great heat where wind is faint—the great weak wind; the southwest is the *kun* earth palace, where yin and yang seem to deliberate their comings and goings—the scheming wind; the west is the *dui* metal palace, with hard, strong metal qi—the hard wind; the northwest is the *qian* metal palace, metal governing breaking—the breaking wind; the north is the *kan* water palace, with cold qi and fierce wind—the great hard wind; the northeast is the *gen* earth palace, where yin has not withdrawn and yang warmth is not yet strong—the baleful wind; the east is the *zhen* wood palace, where wind is born—the infant wind; the southeast is the *xun* wood palace, warm with gentle wind—the weak wind.
- The three depletions are the declining year, the empty moon and loss of seasonal harmony (*Leijing*); 両実一虚 means that even one of the three depletions causes illness—cold and heat from soaking in rain or exposure to wind, or wilting from trespassing on damp ground (*Leijing*).
- 撃仆 means being struck by wind and falling down (*Leijing*).
