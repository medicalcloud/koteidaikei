# 霊枢 九宮八風第七十七 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

On the day Taiyi moves, heaven always responds with wind and rain. If wind and rain come on that day, it is auspicious: the year is good, the people at peace and seldom ill. If they come before it, there is much rain; if after, much drought. If there is an anomaly when Taiyi is at the winter solstice, the omen concerns the ruler; at the spring equinox, the minister; in the central palace, the officials; at the autumn equinox, the generals; at the summer solstice, the common people. An anomaly means that on the days when Taiyi resides in the five palaces there are violent winds breaking trees and raising sand and stones. Divine noble and base by what each governs, and divine by observing where the wind comes from. Wind coming from the direction where it resides is excess wind, governing growth and nourishment of all things; wind coming from behind the opposite direction is deficiency wind, which injures people and governs killing and harm. Watch carefully for deficiency winds and avoid them. Thus the sage daily avoids the path of deficiency pathogens as one avoids arrows and stones, and pathogens cannot harm him—this is what it means.

## Notes

Translated following the *Leijing* notes.
- 太一移日 is the day when one solar term gives way to another and Taiyi passes to the next palace (*Leijing*).
- The omens: the winter solstice is north, the image of the ruler; the spring equinox east, of the minister in charge of education; the central palace earth, flourishing at the four corners, of the officials with divided duties; the autumn equinox west, of the generals in charge of warfare; the summer solstice south, of the multitudes (*Leijing*).
- 太一居五宮之日 refers to the four cardinal terms (*zi*, *wu*, *mao*, *you*) and the days corresponding to the central palace, i.e. the earth periods of the four seasons (*Leijing*).
- On excess and deficiency winds: the residing place is Taiyi's direction; when the month-pointer is at *zi*, wind from the north is the correct winter qi. 衝 means the opposite point and 後 that it comes from far away; when Taiyi is at *zi* and wind comes from the south, the qi has lost its correctness and the healthy qi is insufficient, hence deficiency wind (*Leijing*).
- The base's 多汗 is emended to 多旱. The *Taisu* and the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource read 多旱, and the *Leijing*, though its text reads 多汗, notes that 汗 should be 旱: if wind and rain arrive early the qi is in surplus and there is much rain; if late the qi is insufficient and there is much drought. It pairs with 先之則多雨, so 旱 is adopted.
