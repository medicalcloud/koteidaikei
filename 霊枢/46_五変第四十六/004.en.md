# 霊枢 五変第四十六 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shaoyu said, "What a good question! Let me explain by comparing it to a carpenter. A carpenter sharpens his axe and knife and cuts timber. Even the yin and yang [sides] of a tree have hard and brittle parts; the hard parts resist [the blade], the brittle parts have loose bark, and at the knots the axe is notched. Within one tree, hardness and brittleness differ: the hard parts are tough, the brittle parts easily damaged; how much more so when the timbers themselves differ, with thick or thin bark and much or little sap. Trees that flower early and put out leaves first, meeting spring frost and fierce wind, lose their flowers and their leaves wither. Under long exposure to sun and great drought, brittle trees with thin bark have little sap in their branches and their leaves wither. Under long overcast and excessive rain, trees with thin bark and much sap have their bark rot and ooze. When a sudden violent wind arises, hard but brittle trees have their branches broken and trunks damaged. Under autumn frost and swift winds, hard but brittle trees have their roots shaken and leaves fall. Each of these five suffers its own damage; how much more so people?"

## Notes

本 in 材本 of the base is read as 木 "timber", as other sources have it (see collation).
