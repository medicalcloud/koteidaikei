# 霊枢 脹論第三十五 §14 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Liver distension: fullness and pain below the flanks pulling into the lower abdomen. Spleen distension: frequent hiccup, vexed oppression of the four limbs, a body so heavy it cannot bear its clothes, and restless sleep. Kidney distension: abdominal fullness pulling into the back, with distressing pain of the lower back and hips. Distension of the six fu organs: stomach distension: abdominal fullness, pain in the stomach duct, a burnt smell in the nose, interference with eating, and difficult defecation. Large intestine distension: rumbling and gurgling pain in the intestines; if one is again affected by cold on winter days, there is swill diarrhea with undigested food. Small intestine distension: distension of the lower abdomen with pain pulling into the lower back. Bladder distension: fullness of the lower abdomen with qi urinary block. Sanjiao distension: qi fills the skin, light and puffy but not firm. Gallbladder distension: pain and distension below the flanks, a bitter taste in the mouth, and frequent sighing. For all these distensions the way is one: clearly know counterflow and compliance, and do not lose the art of needling. Draining deficiency and supplementing excess make the spirit leave its chamber, bring in the pathogen and lose the proper, so that the true cannot settle; this is the failure of the crude, called shortening life. Supplementing deficiency and draining excess make the spirit return to its chamber and long fill its emptiness; this is called a good practitioner."

## Notes

The forms of distension of the five zang organs and six fu organs and the principle of supplementing and draining. Qibo's speech ends here.
- 央央然 follows Zhang Jiebin's note in the *Leijing*: "a state of distress".
