# 霊枢 癲狂第二十二 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In wind counterflow, the four limbs suddenly swell, the skin shivers with goose flesh, one catches the breath and at times feels cold, is vexed when hungry and changeable when full. Take the hand greater yin and its paired [meridian], and the meridians of the foot lesser yin and *Yangming*; if the flesh is cold, take the brook points; if the bones are cold, take the well and river points. As an illness, reversal is sudden coldness of the feet, a chest as if about to burst, intestines as if being cut with a knife, vexation with inability to eat, and a hesitant pulse whether large or small. If warm, take the foot lesser yin; if cold, take the foot *Yangming*; supplement when cold and drain when warm. For reversal with abdominal distension and fullness, rumbling intestines, and fullness of the chest with inability to breathe, take it below the chest at the two flanks, where coughing makes the hand feel movement, and at the back transport points, where pressing with the hand brings immediate relief. For internal blockage with inability to urinate, needle the foot lesser yin and greater yang and above the coccyx with a long needle. For counterflow of qi, take the greater yin, *Yangming* and *Jueyin*; in severe cases take the pulsing meridians of the lesser yin and *Yangming*. For shortness of qi with the body soaked, broken speech, aching bones, a heavy body, and lassitude with inability to move, supplement the foot lesser yin. For short breath that cannot be sustained and qi exhausted by movement, supplement the foot lesser yin and remove the blood collaterals.

## Notes

Wind counterflow, reversal and other illnesses and their treatment; the end of the chapter.
- 凊 is read as "cold".
- 身漯漯 follows Zhang Jiebin's note in the *Leijing* ("the skin shivers with cold"); 唏然 "the qi chokes, the breath is drawn in and held"; 飽則善変 "[the pathogen counterflows inside and] causes restlessness". Yang Shangshan's note in the *Taisu* takes the later 身漯漯 in this chapter as a state of exhaustion.
- The base's 「取手大陰」 is emended to 「取手太陰」. The *Taisu*, *Lingshu jizhu* and Wikisource (both) read 手太陰, the meridian name; 太 is adopted.
