# 霊枢 本神第八 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The liver stores blood, and the blood houses the ethereal soul; when liver qi is deficient, there is fear; when excess, anger. The spleen stores the ying-nutrient, and the ying-nutrient houses the intention; when spleen qi is deficient, the four limbs are useless and the five zang organs are not at peace; when excess, the abdomen is distended and urination is obstructed. The heart stores the vessels, and the vessels house the spirit; when heart qi is deficient, there is sorrow; when excess, incessant laughter. The lungs store qi, and the qi houses the corporeal soul; when lung qi is deficient, the nose is blocked and shortness of qi; when excess, dyspnea and shouting, fullness of the chest, and breathing with the head thrown back. The kidneys store essence, and the essence houses the will; when kidney qi is deficient, there is reversal; when excess, distension, and the five zang organs are not at peace. One must carefully examine the forms of illness of the five zang organs to know the deficiency and excess of their qi, and regulate them with care."

## Notes

What each of the five zang organs stores and the illnesses of their deficiency and excess. Qibo's speech ends here, concluding the chapter.
- Following Zhang Jiebin's note in the *Leijing* ("経 should be 涇"; the *Jiayi jing* also has 涇溲, as does *Suwen*, *Tiaojing lun*), it is taken as urination. Yang Shangshan's note in the *Taisu* takes it as menstruation and urination and defecation.
