# 霊枢 本神第八 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The liver stores blood, and the blood houses the ethereal soul; when liver qi is depleted, there is fear; when replete, anger. The spleen stores the nutritive, and the nutritive houses the intention; when spleen qi is depleted, the four limbs are useless and the five viscera are not at peace; when replete, the abdomen is distended and urination is obstructed. The heart stores the vessels, and the vessels house the spirit; when heart qi is depleted, there is sorrow; when replete, incessant laughter. The lungs store qi, and the qi houses the corporeal soul; when lung qi is depleted, the nose is blocked and shortness of qi; when replete, panting and shouting, fullness of the chest, and breathing with the head thrown back. The kidneys store essence, and the essence houses the will; when kidney qi is depleted, there is reversal; when replete, distension, and the five viscera are not at peace. One must carefully examine the forms of illness of the five viscera to know the depletion and repletion of their qi, and regulate them with care."

## Notes

What each of the five viscera stores and the illnesses of their depletion and repletion. Qibo's speech ends here, concluding the chapter.
- Following Zhang Jiebin's note in the *Leijing* ("経 should be 涇"; the *Jiayi jing* also has 涇溲, as does *Suwen*, *Tiaojing lun*), it is taken as urination. Yang Shangshan's note in the *Taisu* takes it as menstruation and urination and defecation.
