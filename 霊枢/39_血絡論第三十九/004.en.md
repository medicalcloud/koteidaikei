# 霊枢 血絡論第三十九 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "When the vessel qi is abundant but the blood deficient, needling causes loss of qi, and loss of qi causes fainting. When blood and qi are both abundant and yin qi plentiful, the blood is slippery, and needling makes it spurt. When yang qi has accumulated and stayed long without being drained, the blood is dark and turbid, so it cannot spurt. When one has just drunk and the fluid has seeped into the collaterals but has not yet blended with the blood, the blood comes out with the watery part separated. In those who have not just drunk, there is water in the body, and in time it becomes swelling. When yin qi accumulates in the yang and its qi relies on the collaterals, needling makes the qi move before the blood comes out, hence swelling. When the yin and yang qi have just met but are not yet harmonized, draining them causes both yin and yang to collapse and exterior and interior to separate, so the color drains and the face turns pale. When needling lets out much blood and the complexion does not change but there is vexation and oppression, needling the collateral has depleted the meridian; when the deficient meridian belongs to the yin, the yin collapses, hence vexation and oppression. When yin and yang have met and combined to form impediment, it overflows into the meridians within and pours into the collaterals without; in such cases both yin and yang are in surplus, so even letting much blood cannot deplete them."
