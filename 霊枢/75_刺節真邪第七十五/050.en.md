# 霊枢 刺節真邪第七十五 §50 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a depletion pathogen strikes a person, there are shivers that shake the body, the fine hairs stand up and the interstices open. When it enters deep and strikes the bones, it becomes bone impediment; striking the sinews, sinew cramps; striking within the vessels, blood blockage, and if blocked, an abscess. Striking the flesh and contending with the defensive qi, if yang prevails there is heat, if yin prevails there is cold; with cold the true qi leaves; when it leaves there is depletion, and with depletion the cold strikes between the skin and flesh. When its qi issues outward, the interstices open and the fine hairs sway; when the qi moves to and fro there is itching; when it stays and does not leave there is impediment; when the defensive qi does not move there is numbness. When a depletion pathogen lodges in one half of the body and enters deep, residing in the nutritive and defensive, and these gradually decline, the true qi leaves, the pathogenic qi alone remains, and hemiplegia develops; if the pathogen is shallow, there is one-sided pain in the vessels.

## Notes

The *Leijing* notes: 洒淅 is shivering with cold; when cold prevails, yang is depleted—qi is yang and people depend on qi, so when cold prevails the yang is depleted and yin cold gathers between the skin and flesh. When the pathogen is at the surface its qi issues outward: open interstices mean sweat is not contained, swaying hairs mean the hair deteriorates, qi moving to and fro means itching, a lingering pathogen means painful impediment, and injured, depleted defensive qi that does not move means no sense of pain or itch, called 不仁. When a depletion pathogen strikes half the body, if deep and severe the nutritive and defensive decline, the true qi leaves and hemiplegia results; if shallow, there is pain on one side.
- The base's 徧容 is emended to 偏容. The *Leijing*, the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource and the Daizhige text read 偏容 (the *Taisu* 遍容). The context—lodging in half the body and producing hemiplegia (偏枯)—favors 偏 "one-sided".
