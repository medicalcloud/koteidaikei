# 霊枢 刺節真邪第七十五 §51 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When a deficiency pathogen enters the body deeply, cold and heat contend, linger long and attach inside. If cold prevails over heat, the bones ache and the flesh withers; if heat prevails over cold, the flesh rots and the muscles putrefy into pus, injuring the bones inside, and injury of the bones inside becomes bone erosion. When illness begins in the sinews, the sinews bend and cannot stretch; the pathogenic qi stays between them and does not withdraw, and a sinew tumor develops. When there is a knot to which qi returns and the wei-defensive qi lingers without withdrawing, fluids stay long and combine into an intestinal tumor; long-standing ones take several years to form and feel soft to the hand. When there is already a knot to which qi returns, fluids stay, and the pathogen strikes it, the congealing easily worsens day by day, gathering and settling into an old tumor that feels hard to the hand. When a knot strikes deep into the bone, the qi follows the bone, bone and qi combine and it grows larger daily: this is bone abscess. When a knot strikes the flesh and the ancestral qi returns to it, and the pathogen stays without leaving, with heat it transforms into pus; without heat it becomes a flesh abscess. These several qi arise in no fixed place, but have fixed names."

## Notes

The end of the chapter, translated following the *Leijing* notes.
- 骨蝕 is erosion reaching the bone (*Leijing*).
- 有所疾前筋 means the illness begins in the sinews; a sinew tumor is something that flows and gathers in the sinews, a kind of wen (*Leijing*).
- 腸溜: the wei-defensive qi loses its course, lingers and does not return, accumulates within and flows between the intestines and stomach, knotting into an intestinal tumor (*Leijing*).
- 昔瘤 means one that did not form overnight (*Leijing*).
- 骨疽 is called "bone-attached abscess"; in 肉疽, 宗 means great, referring to the *Yangming* qi; without heat, a pasty substance knots and gathers without dispersing—this is a flesh abscess (*Leijing*).
- For 発於筋溜, the *Taisu*, the *Leijing* and the Daizhige text read 発為筋溜. The meaning is unchanged, so the base is translated.
