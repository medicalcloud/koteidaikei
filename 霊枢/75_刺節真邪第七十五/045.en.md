# 霊枢 刺節真邪第七十五 §45 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Let me explain the discussion of untying. [The human being] corresponds to heaven and earth and accords with the four seasons; the human being forms a triad with heaven and earth, and therefore can be untied. Below there are hidden springs, above grow reeds and rushes; by this one knows the amount of form and qi. Yin and yang are cold and heat. In heat, rain moistens and [the qi] is above; the roots have little sap; the human qi is outside, the skin slack, the interstices open, blood and qi diminished, sweat pours out and the skin is moist. In cold, the ground freezes and water turns to ice; the human qi is inside, the skin dense, the interstices closed, no sweat comes out, blood and qi are stiff, and the flesh firm and rough. At such a time, even one skilled at traveling on water cannot go on ice, one skilled at digging cannot cut frozen ground, and one skilled with the needle cannot treat reversal in the four limbs; blood vessels congealed and knotted, hard and beating without coming and going, cannot be softened at once. So one who travels on water must wait for the weather to warm, the ice to melt and the frozen ground to thaw before water can be traveled and ground dug. Human vessels are just the same. One who treats reversal must first apply hot compresses to harmonize the meridians—the palms and armpits, elbows and feet, neck and spine—to regulate them; once the fire qi gets through, the blood vessels flow. Then look at the illness: where the pulse is moist, needle and level it; where it is hard and tight, break it up and disperse it; stop when the qi descends. This is what is called untying knots. The essence of the needle lies in regulating qi. Qi accumulates in the stomach to supply the ying-nutrient and wei-defense, each going its own way; the ancestral qi lodges in the sea; its descending part pours into Qijie, its ascending part runs to the airways. Thus when reversal is in the feet, the ancestral qi does not descend, and the blood in the vessels congeals and stays; without regulating it by fire, one cannot treat it. One using the needle must first examine the deficiency and excess of the meridians, press and follow them, press and flick them, observe where there is a responding pulsation, and then needle it to bring it down. When the six meridians are in order, it is called no illness; even if ill, it is said to cure itself. When one meridian is in excess above and deficient below and does not communicate, there must be a transverse collaterals swollen and imposed on the great meridian, blocking it; find it and drain it. This is called untying knots. With cold above and heat below, first needle the greater yang at the nape, retain long, and after needling apply hot compresses to the nape and shoulder blades until the heat meets the lower part, then stop. This is called pushing up. With heat above and cold below, look for deficient vessels deep in the meridians and collaterals and needle them; stop when the qi descends. This is called drawing down. With great heat throughout the body, mania, seeing, hearing and saying wild things, look at the foot *Yangming* and its great collaterals and needle them, supplementing the deficient and draining those with blood and excess. With the patient lying on his back, sit at his head, grip and press the neck arteries with four fingers of both hands and hold long, then roll and press, pushing down to the supraclavicular fossa, stop, and repeat as before until the heat is gone. This is called pushing and dispersing."

## Notes

The discussion of untying knots, translated following the *Leijing* notes.
- 解論 is the discussion of untying knots; since humans form a triad with and correspond to heaven and earth, one must know the way before speaking of untying (*Leijing*).
- 漸洳 are hidden springs; outer and inner correspond universally, and the interior and exterior of a person reveal abundance and decline in the same way (*Leijing*).
- 熱則滋雨而在上…: in summer heat the earth's qi steams into rain and the qi is above, so plant qi is in the branches and leaves and the roots have little sap; in people, heat makes the yang float at the surface, so blood and qi diminish and sweat pours out; with heat things move easily, so it is suitable for needling (*Leijing*).
- 治厥者…: if one must needle before the weather is warm, one must use fire to compress and regulate the meridians; the palms, armpits, elbows, feet, neck and spine are meeting places of valleys and great joints; warming them lets the fire qi through and the blood flow. A moist pulse means the wei-defensive qi is floating, so needle and level it; a hard, tight one means the pathogen is in excess, so break and disperse it; once reversal is removed and the ancestral qi descends, stop. Knots are where pathogens gather; needling to remove them is untying (*Leijing*).
- 宗気留於海: the ancestral qi is the great qi lodged in the upper and lower seas of qi; its descending part is stored in the *dantian*, pours into Qijie of the foot *Yangming* and goes down to the feet; its ascending part accumulates in the chest and emerges at the airways as respiration (*Leijing*).
- 上寒下熱…推而上之: yang is deficient above and excess below; needle Dazhu, Tianzhu, etc. of the foot greater yang at the nape, retain and supplement, warm the shoulders and nape, and stop when the qi arrives and the upper heat meets the lower (*Leijing*).
- 上熱下寒…引而下之: yang is in excess above and deficient below; find the deficient, deep meridian below and supplement it to make the yang qi descend (*Leijing*).
- 大熱徧身…推而散之: the *Yangming* meridian has much qi and blood and is the sea of the zang-fu organs, so heat throughout the body is treated at the foot *Yangming*. With the patient lying on his back, the physician sits at his head, grips the neck arteries at Renying, Daying, etc. with four fingers of both hands, presses from above downward and holds long, rolls and presses down to the supraclavicular fossa, stops, and repeats until the heat leaves; since the three yang are in the head, one can push and disperse the heat at Renying alone (*Leijing*).
- The base's 汁大泄 is emended to 汗大泄. The *Leijing*, the *Lingshu jizhu*, the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource and the Daizhige text all read 血気減，汗大泄, and the *Leijing* note explains that heat makes the yang float at the surface so that blood and qi diminish and sweat pours out. It pairs with 汗不出 in cold, so the graphically similar 汁 is taken as an error for 汗.
