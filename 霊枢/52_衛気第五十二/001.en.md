# 霊枢 衛気第五十二 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Yellow Emperor said, "The five zang organs store the essence, spirit, ethereal and corporeal souls; the six fu organs receive water and grain and move the transformed matter. Their qi enters the five zang organs within and connects with the limbs and joints without. The floating qi that does not follow the meridians is the wei-defensive qi; the essential qi that moves in the meridians is the ying-nutrient qi. Yin and yang follow each other, outside and inside run through each other like a ring without end, gathering yet clear and unconfused; who can fathom it? Yet when yin and yang are distinguished, all have root and branch, and places where deficiency and excess part. One who can distinguish the twelve yin and yang meridians knows where illness arises; one who examines where deficiency and excess lie can find whether illness is high or low; one who knows the qi streets of the six fu organs can untie knots and knows where [the vessels] join and continue at the gates; one who knows the firmness and softness of deficiency and excess knows where to supplement and drain; one who knows the root and branch of the six meridians will not be confused by anything under heaven."

## Notes

The beginning of the chapter, describing the root and branch of the twelve meridians and the four qi streets.
- 亭亭淳淳 follows Zhang Jiebin's note in the *Leijing*: 亭 = stop, 淳 = clear; "though much is gathered, it is not confused".
- 契紹于門戸 follows the *Leijing* (契 = join, 紹 = continue; the gates are the key places of exit and entry), and 石 in 虚石 is read as "excess" following its note "石 means 実" (Wikisource *Huangdi neijing* has 実).
