# 霊枢 衛気第五十二 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Yellow Emperor said, "The five viscera store the essence, spirit, ethereal and corporeal souls; the six bowels receive water and grain and move the transformed matter. Their qi enters the five viscera within and connects with the limbs and joints without. The floating qi that does not follow the channels is the defensive qi; the essential qi that moves in the channels is the nutritive qi. Yin and yang follow each other, outside and inside run through each other like a ring without end, gathering yet clear and unconfused; who can fathom it? Yet when yin and yang are distinguished, all have root and branch, and places where depletion and repletion part. One who can distinguish the twelve yin and yang channels knows where illness arises; one who examines where depletion and repletion lie can find whether illness is high or low; one who knows the qi streets of the six bowels can untie knots and knows where [the vessels] join and continue at the gates; one who knows the firmness and softness of depletion and repletion knows where to supplement and drain; one who knows the root and branch of the six channels will not be confused by anything under heaven."

## Notes

The beginning of the chapter, describing the root and branch of the twelve channels and the four qi streets.
- 亭亭淳淳 follows Zhang Jiebin's note in the *Leijing*: 亭 = stop, 淳 = clear; "though much is gathered, it is not confused".
- 契紹于門戸 follows the *Leijing* (契 = join, 紹 = continue; the gates are the key places of exit and entry), and 石 in 虚石 is read as "repletion" following its note "石 means 実" (Wikisource *Huangdi neijing* has 実).
