# 霊枢 官能第七十三 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

If one understands the five transport points, where to use slow and quick needling, and the bending, stretching, exit and entry [of the meridians], all have their order. Yin and yang accord with the five phases, and the five zang organs and six fu organs store them too; the four seasons and eight winds all have yin and yang, each in its position, corresponding to the face, each assigned to a color region. For the five zang organs and six fu organs, examine where it hurts, and by left and right, upper and lower, know their cold and warmth and in which meridian [the illness] lies. Examine the cold, warmth, smoothness and roughness of the skin to know what troubles the patient. The diaphragm has an upper and a lower side; know where the qi is. First find the path, needle sparingly and seldom, slightly deep and retained, so that one can insert slowly. When great heat is above, push it down; when it rises from below, draw it out; look for where the pain began and always treat that first. When great cold is outside, retain the needle and supplement; when it has entered within, drain it from the sea point. What the needle cannot do, moxibustion is suited for. When the upper qi is insufficient, push and raise it; when the lower qi is insufficient, retain [the needle] and follow [the qi]. When yin and yang are both deficient, fire alone is the remedy. With reversal and severe cold, the bony edges deep and the cold extending past the knees, [moxa] Xialing, i.e. Sanli. Where the yin collaterals pass, if [cold] lodges and stays and enters within, push it along. Where a meridian is deep, fire is the remedy. Where collaterals are knotted, hard and tight, fire treats them. When the patient cannot tell where the trouble is, [moxa] below the two springing vessels; [using] yin for men and yang for women is forbidden to the good practitioner. The discussion of needling is complete.

## Notes

Translated following the *Leijing* notes.
- The five transport points are well, spring, stream, river and sea; slow and quick are needling methods; bending, stretching, exit and entry are the comings and goings of the meridians (*Leijing*).
- From 各処色部: when a pathogen is inside, its color shows outside, and examining the regions of the face reveals the illness (*Leijing*).
- 膈有上下: above the diaphragm is the *tanzhong*, the upper sea of qi, where the heart and lungs lie; below it lie the spleen, liver and kidneys, with the *dantian* as the lower sea of qi (*Leijing*).
- 稀而疏之，稍深以留: having first found the path of the meridians, one needles; sparingly, to value precision; slightly deep and retained, to insert slowly (*Leijing*).
- 上気不足，推而揚之；下気不足，積而従之: pushing and raising draws the qi to supplement above; retaining and following keeps the needle to follow the qi and fill below (*Leijing*).
- 下陵三里: Xialing is Sanli of the *Yangming* meridian (*Leijing*).
- 両蹻之下，男陰女陽，良工所禁: when there is no pain or sensation and the patient cannot tell where the trouble is, moxa below the two springing vessels, i.e. Shenmai of the foot greater yang and Zhaohai of the foot lesser yin. Men count yang and women yin, so using yin for men and yang for women is a reversal, forbidden to the good practitioner (*Leijing*). The *Jiayi jing* reads 男陽女陰.
- The base's 言陰与五 is emended to 言陰与陽. The *Taisu*, the *Lingshu jizhu*, the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource and the Daizhige text all read 言陰与陽, and the *Leijing* note also explains that the transformations of yin and yang are the five phases. 陰与五 makes no sense, so 陽 is adopted.
