# 霊枢 官能第七十三 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The practice of needling must have rules. Look up at the light of heaven and attend below to the eight regular [seasonal nodes], so as to avoid abnormal pathogens and watch over the people; examine deficiency and excess and do not offend against the pathogen. Meeting heaven's exposure [unseasonable wind and rain] and encountering the deficiency of the year, one tries to save [the patient] but cannot prevail, and suffers calamity instead. Hence it is said: only one who knows heaven's taboos can speak of the meaning of needling. Model oneself on antiquity, verify in the present, observe the obscure and penetrate the boundless—what the crude do not see and the good practitioner values; none knows its form, which is as feeble as the divine. When a [deficiency] pathogenic qi strikes a person, there are shivers that shake the body; when an upright pathogen strikes, it is subtle, first showing in the complexion while the body feels nothing—as if present, as if absent, as if gone, as if there, with form and without form; none knows its true state. Therefore the superior practitioner takes the qi to save it at the sprouting stage; the inferior practitioner waits until it has formed and thereby ruins the body. Therefore when the practitioner uses the needle, he knows where the qi is and guards its gates, understands regulation of the qi, where to supplement and drain, the meaning of slow and quick, and the places to needle. In draining, one must use the round [flowing] method: press toward [the illness] and turn the needle, and the qi moves; insert quickly and withdraw slowly, and the pathogenic qi comes out; extend and meet it, shake the hole wide, and the qi comes out quickly. In supplementing, one must use the square [upright, calm] method: draw the skin outward to align it with the gate; pull the pivot with the left hand and push the skin with the right; rotate slightly and push slowly; be upright and correct, calm and still, firm of mind and unflagging, intending a slight retention; when the qi descends, withdraw quickly, push the skin and cover the outer gate, and the genuine qi is preserved. The key to using the needle is not to forget the spirit."

## Notes

Translated following the *Leijing* notes.
- 服 means "practice, affair" (the *Leijing* note on the same phrase in the *Suwen*'s *Bazheng shenming lun*). 天光, 八正 and 天忌 are explained in that chapter of the *Suwen*.
- 是得天之露，遇歳之虚: all unseasonable wind and rain are called 露; the *Suilu lun* (ch. 79) says that meeting the wind and encountering the rain is called "meeting the year's exposure". The deficiency of the year—the decline of the year, the emptiness of the moon, and loss of seasonal harmony—are the three depletions (*Leijing*).
- 邪気 here means deficiency pathogens, which strike severely and shake the body; upright pathogens strike subtly, appearing in the complexion without being felt (*Leijing*).
- 因敗其形: not knowing the difficulty, one harms instead (*Leijing*).
- 瀉必用員……遥大其穴 follows the *Leijing*: 員 is flowing and smooth, 切 is pressing straight toward the site of illness, 迎 is "seize", and 遥 equals 揺 "shake". 方 in 補必用方 means upright and calm (*Leijing*). The *Leijing* also remarks that the square and round of supplementing and draining here seem the reverse of the *Bazheng shenming lun* but each has its own meaning.
- The base's 若在若無 is emended to 若有若無. Xiao Yanping's note on the *Taisu* (the *Lingshu* reads 若有若無), the *Leijing*, the *Lingshu jizhu*, the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource and the Daizhige text all read 若有若無. It parallels 若亡若存 and 有形無形, so 有 is adopted.
