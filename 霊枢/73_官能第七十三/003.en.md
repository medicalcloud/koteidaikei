# 霊枢 官能第七十三 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Yellow Emperor said, "The principle of using the needle is to know where the form and qi are—left and right, above and below, yin and yang, exterior and interior, the amounts of blood and qi, the counterflow and compliance of their courses, the meeting places of exit and entry—and to plan an attack on what is at fault. Know how to undo knots; know how to supplement deficiency and drain excess and the upper and lower gates of qi; understand thoroughly the four seas; determine where [the illness] lies; [know that] cold, heat, soaking and exposure are transported to different places; be careful in regulating qi; understand the meridian pathways; know all the meeting points of the branch collaterals on left and right. When cold and heat contend, be able to combine and regulate them; when deficiency and excess are close neighbors, know how to decide and open them; when left and right are not in order, take hold and move them; understand counterflow and compliance, and you will know what can be treated; when yin and yang are not one-sided, you will know when the patient will rise; examine the root and branch, observe cold and heat, find where the pathogen is, and ten thousand needlings will be without danger. Knowing how to employ the nine needles, the way of needling is complete.

## Notes

The teaching on the nine needles recited by the Yellow Emperor. His speech continues through unit 5. Translated following the *Leijing* notes.
- 出入之合: where meridian qi exits from within and enters from without; knowing these one can attack where there is fault (*Leijing*). The interlinear note at the end of the chapter and the *Leijing* say 合 is written 会 in another text.
- 上下気門: the roots and branches of the meridians and the qi streets; alternatively, the hand meridians as upper and the foot meridians as lower, the gates being places through which the qi must pass (*Leijing*).
- 寒熱淋露: soaked by rain and exposed to wind, the pathogens affect different places, so one must determine the meridian (*Leijing*).
- 虚与実隣: 隣 means close; when close they are easily confused, and mistaking one for the other is as opposite as ice and charcoal, so one must know how to decide and open (*Leijing*).
- 左右不調，把而行之: when a pathogen lodges in a great collateral, flowing from left to right and right to left, "take hold and move it" means crosswise needling (*Leijing*). The interlinear note and the *Leijing* say 把 is written 犯 in another text, and the *Lingshu jizhu* reads 犯.
- 陰陽不奇: 奇 means "unpaired, one-sided"; if not one-sided, they are in harmony, so one knows when the patient will rise (*Leijing*). In 知官九鍼, 官 means "employ" (*Leijing*).
