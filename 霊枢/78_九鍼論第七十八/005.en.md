# 霊枢 九鍼論第七十八 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The first is heaven. Heaven is yang. Among the five zang organs, the one corresponding to heaven is the lungs; the lungs are the canopy of the five zang organs and six fu organs; the skin is joined to the lungs and is the yang of the human being. Therefore the needle made for it [the arrowhead needle] must have a large head and a sharp tip, so that it does not go deep and only the yang qi comes out. The second is earth. What in the human being corresponds to earth is the flesh. Therefore the needle made for it [the round needle] must have a tube-like body and a round tip, so as not to injure the divisions of the flesh; if they are injured, the qi is exhausted. The third is humans. What makes human life is the blood vessels. Therefore the needle made for it [the spoon needle] must have a large body and a round tip, so that it can press the vessels without sinking in, bringing the qi and letting only the pathogenic qi out. The fourth is the seasons. The seasons are the four seasons and eight winds that lodge in the meridians and collaterals and cause lingering illness. Therefore the needle made for it [the sharp-edged needle] must have a tube-like body and a sharp tip, so that it can drain heat and let blood, exhausting stubborn illness. The fifth is the notes. The notes are the division of winter and summer, divided at *zi* and *wu*, where yin and yang separate, cold and heat contend, the two qi strike each other and combine into abscesses and pus. Therefore the needle made for it [the sword needle] must have a tip like a sword's point, so as to remove large amounts of pus. The sixth is the pitch pipes. The pitch pipes regulate yin and yang and the four seasons and accord with the twelve meridian vessels; [here] deficiency pathogens lodge in the meridians and collaterals and cause sudden impediment. Therefore the needle made for it [the round-sharp needle] must have a tip like a yak hair, both round and sharp, slightly thicker in the middle of the body, to remove sudden qi. The seventh is the stars. The stars are the seven orifices of the human being; pathogens lodge in the meridians, causing painful impediment, and settle in the collaterals. Therefore the needle made for it [the filiform needle] has a tip like the beak of a mosquito or horsefly; insert it quietly and slowly, retain it gently for a long time, so that the healthy qi follows it, the true [qi] and the pathogen both go, and after withdrawing, [the genuine qi] is nourished. The eighth is the winds. The winds are the eight joints of the limbs. The deficiency winds of the eight directions—the eight winds—injure people and lodge inside in the bone clefts, the joints of the lumbar spine and the interstices, causing deep impediment. Therefore the needle made for it [the long needle] must have a long body and a sharp tip, to remove deep pathogens and remote impediments. The ninth is the fields. The fields are the clefts of the joints and the spaces in the skin. Excess pathogens flow and overflow in the body, like wind edema, flowing but unable to pass the pivots and great joints. Therefore the needle made for it [the large needle] has a tip like a cudgel, with a slightly rounded point, to remove the great qi that cannot pass the joints."

## Notes

The origin and shape of each of the nine needles. The bracketed needle names follow unit 7. Translated following the *Leijing* notes.
- On the second (round needle): the needle is egg-shaped to ease its way between the flesh divisions; it is not sharp, for fear of injuring the flesh and exhausting the spleen qi (*Leijing*).
- 瘤病 in the fourth: 瘤 means "lingering" (*Leijing*).
- 音者冬夏之分，分於子午 in the fifth: the notes accord with the five phases and correspond to the heavenly stems, hence the division of winter and summer, *zi* and *wu* (*Leijing*).
- The filiform needle of the seventh is used finely and slowly to disperse the pathogen gradually and nourish the genuine qi (*Leijing*).
- 尖如挺 in the ninth means thick and large (*Leijing*).
- The base's 故其之治鍼 is emended to 故為之治鍼. The *Leijing*, the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource and the Daizhige text read 故為之治鍼 (the Siku edition on Wikisource 故其為之治鍼). The preceding eight needles all have 故為之治鍼, so 為 is adopted.
