# 霊枢 九鍼論第七十八 §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the body is at ease but the mind suffers, illness arises in the vessels; treat it with moxibustion and needling. When the body labors but the mind is at ease, illness arises in the sinews; treat it with hot compresses and guiding exercises. When both body and mind are at ease, illness arises in the flesh; treat it with needles and stones. When both body and mind suffer, illness arises in the throat; treat it with sweet medicines. When the body is often startled and frightened and the sinews and vessels are blocked, illness arises as numbness; treat it with massage and medicinal wine. These are called the [five] forms [and minds].

## Notes

Illnesses of the five forms and minds. The *Suwen*'s *Xueqi xingzhi* chapter has almost the same text, and the *Leijing* comments: body at ease and mind suffering—no physical labor but much worry; the heart governs the vessels, so the vessels fall ill. Both at ease—idle, full of food and inactive, injuring the spleen, so the flesh falls ill. Body laboring—labor injures the sinews; 熨 is a medicinal compress, 引 guiding exercise. Both suffering—much worry and pondering injure the qi of spleen and lungs, whose vessels rise along the throat, so the illness arises there. Frequent fright scatters qi and blood so that the channels are blocked, causing numbness (stiffness and weakness); 醪薬 is medicinal wine.
- 咽喝: the *Taisu*, the *Jiayi jing* and the *Lingshu jizhu* also read 咽喝. The *Suwen*'s *Xueqi xingzhi* chapter reads 咽嗌, and the *Lingshu jizhu* notes that 喝 should be 嗌. It is translated here as illness of the throat.
- For 是謂形, the *Taisu* reads 是謂五形, and the *Lingshu jizhu* commentary and the *Leijing* (on the *Suwen*) speak of the "five forms and minds". The literal text is incomplete, so the translation supplies the missing words in brackets.
