# 霊枢 九鍼論第七十八 §11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The qi of the five viscera: the heart governs belching, the lungs coughing, the liver speech, the spleen swallowing [acid], the kidneys yawning. The qi of the six bowels: the gallbladder gives anger, the stomach qi counterflow and hiccups, the large and small intestines diarrhea, the bladder when uncontrolled enuresis, the lower burner when overflowing water [edema]. The five flavors: sour enters the liver, acrid the lungs, bitter the heart, sweet the spleen, salty the kidneys, bland the stomach—these are called the five flavors. The five combinations: when essence qi combines in the liver there is worry; in the heart, joy; in the lungs, sorrow; in the kidneys, fear; in the spleen, dread—this is called the essence qi of the five combining in the viscera. The five aversions: the liver dislikes wind, the heart heat, the lungs cold, the kidneys dryness, the spleen damp—these are what the qi of the five viscera dislikes. The five fluids: the heart governs sweat, the liver tears, the lungs nasal mucus, the kidneys spittle, the spleen drool—these are where the five fluids come from. The five strains: prolonged looking injures the blood, prolonged lying the qi, prolonged sitting the flesh, prolonged standing the bones, prolonged walking the sinews—these are the illnesses of the five prolonged strains. The five goings: sour goes to the sinews, acrid to the qi, bitter to the blood, salty to the bones, sweet to the flesh—these are called the five goings. The five restraints: when illness is in the sinews, do not eat sour; in the qi, not acrid; in the bones, not salty; in the blood, not bitter; in the flesh, not sweet. Even if the mouth craves it, do not eat much; one must restrain oneself—this is called the five restraints. The five onsets: yin illness arises in the bones, yang illness in the blood, [illness from] flavors in the qi; yang illness arises in winter, yin illness in summer. The five pathogens: when a pathogen enters the yang there is mania; when it enters the yin, blood impediment; when it enters the yang and turns, epilepsy; when it enters the yin and turns, loss of voice; when yang enters the yin, the illness is quiet; when yin comes out into the yang, the illness is irascible. The five viscera: the heart stores the spirit, the lungs the corporeal soul, the liver the ethereal soul, the spleen intention, the kidneys essence and will. The five governances: the heart governs the vessels, the lungs the skin, the liver the sinews, the spleen the muscles, the kidneys the bones.

## Notes

A table of correspondences nearly identical in content to the *Suwen*'s *Xuanming wuqi* chapter.
- For 以味発於気 among the five onsets, the *Taisu* also reads 以味病発於気, and Yang Shangshan explains that illnesses from the five flavors arise from imbalance of qi. The *Leijing* (on the *Suwen*), the *Lingshu jizhu*, the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource and the Daizhige text read 陰病発於肉. Since the *Taisu* supports the base form, the base is translated.
- For 并肺則悲, the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource reads 憂.
