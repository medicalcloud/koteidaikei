# 霊枢 九鍼論第七十八 §9 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "Let me explain how the body corresponds to the nine fields. The left foot corresponds to the Beginning of Spring; its days are *wuyin* and *jichou*. The left flank corresponds to the Spring Equinox; its day is *yimao*. The left hand corresponds to the Beginning of Summer; its days are *wuchen* and *jisi*. The chest, throat and head correspond to the Summer Solstice; its day is *bingwu*. The right hand corresponds to the Beginning of Autumn; its days are *wushen* and *jiwei*. The right flank corresponds to the Autumn Equinox; its day is *xinyou*. The right foot corresponds to the Beginning of Winter; its days are *wuxu* and *jihai*. The lower back, buttocks and lower orifices correspond to the Winter Solstice; its day is *renzi*. The six fu organs and the three zang organs below the diaphragm correspond to the central region; its great prohibition is on the days when Taiyi is there and on all *wu* and *ji* days. For these nine, carefully observe where the eight regulars are; when there is an abscess or swelling on the left, right, upper or lower part of the body governed by it and one wishes to treat it, do not lance it on the day that corresponds. These are called heaven's taboo days.

## Notes

Qibo's speech continues through unit 12. Translated following the *Leijing* notes.
- The *Leijing* assigns the left foot to the *gen* palace (northeast), the left flank to *zhen* (east), the left hand to *xun* (southeast), the chest, throat and head to *li* (south), the right hand to *kun* (southwest), the right flank to *dui* (west), the right foot to *qian* (northwest), the lower back, buttocks and lower orifices to *kan* (north), and below the diaphragm to the central palace, noting that the four corner palaces (*qian*, *kun*, *gen*, *xun*) all correspond to *wu* and *ji* while the four cardinal palaces each have their own ruling day.
- 三蔵 are the liver, spleen and kidneys; 八正 are where the ruling qi of the eight directions are, i.e. Taiyi (*Leijing*).
- 無以其所直之日潰治之: lancing on that day risks letting the original qi escape and violating heaven's taboo (*Leijing*).
- The base's 左毛 is emended to 左手. The *Leijing*, the *Lingshu jizhu*, the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource and the Daizhige text read 左手 (the *Jiayi jing* 左足). It pairs with 右手応立秋, so the graphically similar 毛 is taken as an error for 手.
- The base's 戊巳 is emended to 戊己. The *Lingshu jizhu* reads 戊己 (the *Leijing* and the *Jiayi jing* read 戊巳, but the *Leijing* note takes it as the stems 戊己, belonging to earth). Since the text names stem days, the graphically similar 巳 is taken as an error for 己.
