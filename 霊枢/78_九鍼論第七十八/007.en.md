# 霊枢 九鍼論第七十八 §7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "The first is the arrowhead needle, modeled on the cloth needle; from one and a half *cun* before the end it tapers sharply; it is one *cun* six *fen* long and governs heat in the head and body. The second is the round needle, modeled on the floss needle, with a tube-like body and an egg-shaped point, one *cun* six *fen* long; it governs treating the qi between the flesh divisions. The third is the spoon needle, modeled on the point of a millet grain, three and a half *cun* long; it governs pressing the vessels to bring the qi and let the pathogen out. The fourth is the sharp-edged needle, modeled on the floss needle, with a tube-like body and a sharp tip, one *cun* six *fen* long; it governs letting blood in hot abscesses. The fifth is the sword needle, modeled on a sword's point, two and a half *fen* wide and four *cun* long; it governs large abscesses with pus where two heats contend. The sixth is the round-sharp needle, modeled on the yak-hair needle, slightly larger at the tip and narrower in the body so it can be inserted deep, one *cun* six *fen* long; it governs removing abscess and impediment. The seventh is the filiform needle, modeled on a fine hair, one *cun* six *fen* long; it governs cold, heat, pain and impediment in the network vessels. The eighth is the long needle, modeled on the *qi* needle, seven *cun* long; it governs removing deep pathogens and remote impediments. The ninth is the large needle, modeled on the sharp-edged needle, with a slightly rounded point, four *cun* long; it governs removing great qi that cannot pass through the joints. This completes the shapes of the needles. These are the rules for the sizes and lengths of the nine needles."

## Notes

Translated following the *Leijing* notes.
- 卒鋭之: 鑱 means sharp and 卒 the tail; the needle has a large body and tapers gradually from about one and a half *cun* before the end (*Leijing*). The *Leijing* says the cloth needle, floss needle and *qi* needle are probably names of ancient needles whose meaning is unknown.
- 黍粟之鋭 means round and slightly pointed (*Leijing*).
- The *Leijing* remarks that the *Jiuzhen shier yuan* chapter (ch. 1) describes the sharp-edged needle as three-edged, so "tube-like body" here may be an error.
- 氂 is a stiff hair (*Leijing*).
- 両熱争者 means cold and heat out of balance, the two qi striking each other (*Leijing*).
- The base's 卯其鋒 is emended to 卵其鋒. The *Taisu*, the *Leijing*, the *Lingshu jizhu*, the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource and the Daizhige text all read 卵, and the *Leijing* note explains it as round and pointed like an egg. Since it describes the shape of the round needle, the graphically similar 卯 is taken as an error for 卵.
