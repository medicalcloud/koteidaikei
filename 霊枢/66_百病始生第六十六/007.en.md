# 霊枢 百病始生第六十六 §7 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "Accumulations formed by adhering to the minute collaterals move back and forth, up and down. These are where the minute collaterals of the arm and hand lie; being superficial and slack, they cannot hold the accumulation and keep it in place, so it moves to and fro between the intestines and stomach; water gathers, seeps and pours, sloshing audibly; with cold there is swelling and fullness with thunderous pulling, hence occasional cutting pain. Those adhering to the *Yangming* meridian lie on either side of the navel, growing larger when one has eaten fully and smaller when hungry. Those adhering to the slack sinews resemble the *Yangming* accumulation; they hurt when one is full and are calm when hungry. Those adhering to the membranes of the intestines and stomach hurt and connect outward to the slack sinews; they are calm when full and hurt when hungry. Those adhering to the hidden thoroughfare vessel throb under the pressing hand; when the hand is lifted, hot qi descends into both thighs, as if hot water were poured on them. Those adhering to the paraspinal sinews behind the intestines appear when one is hungry and disappear when full, and cannot be felt by pressing. Those adhering to the transport vessels cause blockage; the fluids do not descend and the orifices are dry and obstructed. This is the pathogenic qi entering from outside to inside, from above to below."

## Notes

The features of accumulations according to where the pathogen adheres.
- 句 is 拘, "hold" (*Leijing*): those collaterals are superficial and slack, not tense, so they cannot hold and retain the accumulation.
- For 䐜䐜満 the *Leijing* reads 䐜脹満 and the *Jiayi jing* 腹䐜満. The meaning, swelling and fullness, is unchanged, so the base is translated.
- 揣之応手而動: the descending branch of the hidden thoroughfare vessel comes out at Qijie and follows the inner thigh, so pressing the thigh it throbs under the hand, and when the hand is lifted hot qi descends between the thighs—a sign that the pathogen adheres to the hidden thoroughfare (*Leijing*).
- 従外入内，従上下也 sums up the above: pathogenic qi arising in the yang always moves from outside to inside, from above to below (*Leijing*).
