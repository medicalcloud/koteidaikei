# 霊枢 百病始生第六十六 §11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "Reversal qi produces stiffness of the feet; stiffness produces cold shins; when the shins are cold the blood vessels congeal and become rough; when they do, cold qi rises into the intestines and stomach; entering them, it causes distension; with distension, the juices and foam outside the intestines are pressed together and cannot disperse, and day by day an accumulation forms. If one suddenly eats and drinks to excess, the intestines become full; if daily life is irregular and one exerts oneself excessively, the collaterals are injured. When the yang collaterals are injured, blood overflows outward, and with outward overflow there is nosebleed; when the yin collaterals are injured, blood overflows inward, and with inward overflow there is blood in the stool. When the collaterals of the intestines and stomach are injured, blood overflows outside the intestines; if there is cold outside the intestines, the juices and foam and the blood contend, merge, congeal and gather, and cannot disperse, and an accumulation forms. If one is suddenly struck by cold from outside, or injured inwardly by worry or anger, the qi counterflows upward; with upward counterflow the six transport points are blocked, warming qi does not move, the congealed blood is wrapped up and does not disperse, the fluids become rough and seep, adhering and not leaving, and accumulations all form."

## Notes

Three ways in which accumulations form: reversal from cold, injury of the collaterals by diet and exertion, and external cold combined with internal injury.
- 足悗 follows the *Leijing* note: reversal qi is counterflow qi; cold counterflowing below produces 足悗, meaning the joints are painful, stagnant and not supple.
- 六輸不通: Yang Shangshan's note in the *Taisu* says the six transport points of the yang meridians of the six fu organs are all blocked; the *Leijing* note says the transport points of the six meridians are blocked.
- The base's 凝血藴裏 is emended to 凝血藴裹. The *Taisu*, the *Lingshu jizhu* and the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource read 蘊裹, and Yang Shangshan's note in the *Taisu* also reads "wrapped up and not dispersing" (the *Leijing* reads 裏 like the base). This fits the context of congealed blood being wrapped up and not dispersing, so the graphically similar 裏 is taken as an error for 裹.
