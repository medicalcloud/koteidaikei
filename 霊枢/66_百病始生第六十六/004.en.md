# 霊枢 百病始生第六十六 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "Wind, rain, cold and heat, without finding a depletion, cannot by themselves injure a person. Those who suddenly meet a gale or a rainstorm and do not fall ill have no depletion, so the pathogen alone cannot injure them. It must be that the depletion-pathogen wind and [the depletion of] one's body meet—two depletions together—before it lodges in the body. When two repletions meet, people's flesh is firm. Being struck by a depletion pathogen depends on the season of heaven and on one's body; combining depletion and repletion, a great illness forms. The qi has fixed lodgings, named by their places; upper and lower, middle and outer are divided into three groups.

## Notes

Qibo's speech continues through unit 5.
- Zhang Jiebin's note in the *Leijing*: wind coming from the direction opposite [the position of Taiyi] is depletion wind and injures people; wind from the occupied direction is repletion wind and nourishes all things. If a person's qi is not depleted, even a depletion wind cannot injure him; hence the body's depletion and heaven's depletion must meet for it to lodge. When heaven has repletion wind and the person repletion qi, two repletions meet, people's flesh is firm and the pathogen cannot enter. Yang Shangshan's note in the *Taisu* glosses 参 as "combine".
- 三員 follows the *Leijing* note: the illnesses arising from the exterior when depletion pathogens strike, the illnesses arising from inside when accumulations form, and the yin illnesses when emotions injure the viscera—the three parts, inner and outer.
