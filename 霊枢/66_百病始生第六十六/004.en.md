# 霊枢 百病始生第六十六 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "Wind, rain, cold and heat, without finding a deficiency, cannot by themselves injure a person. Those who suddenly meet a gale or a rainstorm and do not fall ill have no deficiency, so the pathogen alone cannot injure them. It must be that the deficiency-pathogen wind and [the deficiency of] one's body meet—two depletions together—before it lodges in the body. When two repletions meet, people's flesh is firm. Being struck by a deficiency pathogen depends on the season of heaven and on one's body; combining deficiency and excess, a great illness forms. The qi has fixed lodgings, named by their places; upper and lower, middle and outer are divided into three groups.

## Notes

Qibo's speech continues through unit 5.
- Zhang Jiebin's note in the *Leijing*: wind coming from the direction opposite [the position of Taiyi] is deficiency wind and injures people; wind from the occupied direction is excess wind and nourishes all things. If a person's qi is not deficient, even a deficiency wind cannot injure him; hence the body's deficiency and heaven's deficiency must meet for it to lodge. When heaven has excess wind and the person excess qi, two repletions meet, people's flesh is firm and the pathogen cannot enter. Yang Shangshan's note in the *Taisu* glosses 参 as "combine".
- 三員 follows the *Leijing* note: the illnesses arising from the exterior when deficiency pathogens strike, the illnesses arising from inside when accumulations form, and the yin illnesses when emotions injure the zang organs—the three parts, inner and outer.
