# 霊枢 病伝第四十二 §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "When the illness first breaks out in the heart, in one day it goes to the lungs, in three days to the liver, in five days to the spleen; if it does not end in three days, there is death, in winter at midnight, in summer at midday. When the illness first breaks out in the lungs, in three days it goes to the liver, in one day to the spleen, in five days to the stomach; if it does not end in ten days, there is death, in winter at sunset, in summer at sunrise. When the illness first breaks out in the liver, in three days it goes to the spleen, in five days to the stomach, in three days to the kidneys; if it does not end in three days, there is death, in winter at sunset, in summer at breakfast time. When the illness first breaks out in the spleen, in one day it goes to the stomach, in two days to the kidneys, in three days to the spine and bladder; if it does not end in ten days, there is death, in winter when people settle down for the night, in summer at the late meal. When the illness first breaks out in the stomach, in five days it goes to the kidneys, in three days to the spine and bladder, in five days up to the heart; if it does not end in two days, there is death, in winter at midnight, in summer in the early afternoon. When the illness first breaks out in the kidneys, in three days it goes to the spine and bladder, in three days up to the heart, in three days to the small intestine; if it does not end in three days, there is death, in winter at daybreak, in summer in the early evening. When the illness first breaks out in the bladder, in five days it goes to the kidneys, in one day to the small intestine, in one day to the heart; if it does not end in two days, there is death, in winter at cockcrow, in summer in the late afternoon. When illnesses are transmitted in this order, they all have a time of death and must not be needled. Those that skip one viscus, or two, three or four viscera, may be needled."

## Notes

The order in which illness passes through the viscera and bowels and the time of death; the end of the chapter. It is nearly identical in content to *Suwen*, *Biaoben bingchuan lun*. The final 昳 is the headword of a sound gloss; the interlinear note (pronunciation) is not translated (original text only).
- The time expressions (蚤食, 人定, 晏食, 日昳, 大晨, 早晡, 下晡, etc.) are provisionally translated in their usual senses.
