# 霊枢 邪客第七十一 §26 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "When the lungs and heart have a pathogen, its qi lingers in both elbows; when the liver has a pathogen, its qi flows into both armpits; when the spleen has a pathogen, its qi lingers in both hips; when the kidneys have a pathogen, its qi lingers in both knee hollows. These eight depletions are all chambers of the pivots and joints, places where the genuine qi passes and the blood collaterals roam. Pathogenic qi and bad blood must certainly not stay there; if they stay, they injure the sinews, collaterals, bones and joints, and the pivots and joints cannot bend and stretch, hence crooked cramping."

## Notes

The end of the chapter. Yang Shangshan's note in the *Taisu*: the eight depletions are the two elbows, two armpits, two hips and two knee hollows, called depletions because they are hollow places. The *Leijing* note: 機 is a pivot, 関 an important junction, and 室 like a chamber; when the healthy qi dwells there they function, when pathogenic qi dwells there it injures the meridians and joints so that bending and stretching are impeded. It gives 痀 the reading of 拘.
- For 痀攣 the *Jiayi jing* reads 拘攣, and the *Lingshu jizhu* and Wikisource 病攣. The *Leijing* agrees with the base, so the base is translated.
- The *Jiayi jing* reads 肺心有邪，其気留於両股；肝有邪，其気留於両肘, with different assignments.
