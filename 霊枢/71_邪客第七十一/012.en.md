# 霊枢 邪客第七十一 §12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "The hand greater yin vessel emerges at the tip of the thumb, bends inward, follows the border of the white flesh to Dayuan behind the base joint, where it lingers and pulsates; bends outward and rises over the base joint; descends and bends inward to meet the various yin collaterals at Yuji, where several vessels pour together and its qi is smooth; travels hidden beneath the prominent bone, bends outward, emerges at the inch opening and goes on; rises to the inner edge of the elbow, enters beneath the large sinew, bends inward, rises along the yin side of the upper arm, enters below the armpit, and bends inward to run to the lungs. These are the windings of the compliant course counted in reverse. The heart governor vessel emerges at the tip of the middle finger, bends inward, rises along the inner edge of the middle finger, lingers in the palm, travels hidden between two bones, bends outward and emerges between two sinews at the border of bone and flesh, where its qi is smooth; it rises two *cun*, bends outward and travels between two sinews, rises to the inner edge of the elbow, enters beneath the small sinew, lingers at the meeting of two bones, rises into the chest and inside networks with the heart vessel."

## Notes

The *Leijing* note: these two sections describe the windings through the five transport points. The tip of the thumb is Shaoshang (well), behind the base joint Taiyuan (stream); 澹 is the swaying of water—the vessel pulsates on reaching Taiyuan, hence 留以澹; Yuji (spring); it travels hidden under the high bone behind the palm and emerges at the inch opening at Jingqu (river); beneath the large sinew at the elbow is Chize (sea). The lung meridian's compliant course runs from the zang organ to the hand, but here it is counted from the hand to the zang organ, hence "the compliant course counted in reverse". Likewise the heart governor vessel: Zhongchong (well), Laogong (spring), Daling (stream), Jianshi (river), Quze (sea).
- 壅骨 is taken as the high bone behind the palm, following the *Leijing*.
