# 霊枢 経脈第十 §23 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The twelve meridians run hidden between the partings of the flesh, deep and invisible. The one that is always visible is where the foot greater yin passes above the inner ankle, because there is nothing to hide it. All vessels that float and are always visible are collaterals. Of the collaterals of the six meridians, the great collaterals of hand *Yangming* and lesser yang start between the five fingers and rise to unite in the elbow. When one drinks wine, the wei-defensive qi first moves in the skin and first fills the collaterals, so the collaterals become abundant first; thus when the wei-defensive qi has leveled off, the ying-nutrient qi becomes full and the meridians become greatly abundant. When a vessel suddenly pulses, it is always because pathogenic qi dwells in it and lodges at the root or branch; if it does not pulse, there is heat; if it is not firm, it is deep and empty; being unlike the others, it tells one which vessel is pulsing."

## Notes

The difference in visibility between meridians and collaterals. The Yellow Emperor's speech ends here.
- 六経絡，手陽明少陽之大絡 follows Zhang Jiebin's note in the *Leijing*: "among the collaterals of the six hand meridians, those of the *Yangming* and lesser yang are the largest".
- The base's 「過于外踝之上」 is emended to 「過于内踝之上」. The Lingshu editions, *Leijing* and *Lingshu jizhu* agree with the base (外踝), but the *Taisu* reads 内踝. The foot greater yin runs up in front of the inner ankle, so 内 is adopted following the *Taisu*.
