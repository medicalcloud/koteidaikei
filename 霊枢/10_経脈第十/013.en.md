# 霊枢 経脈第十 §13 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The heart governor hand *Jueyin* pericardium vessel starts in the chest, comes out to belong to the pericardium, descends through the diaphragm and connects in turn with the sanjiao. Its branch follows the chest out to the flanks, [goes] three *cun* below the armpit, rises to reach the armpit, descends along the inside of the upper arm running between the greater yin and lesser yin, enters the elbow, descends the forearm running between the two tendons, enters the palm, follows the middle finger and comes out at its tip. Its branch separates in the palm, follows the ring finger and comes out at its tip. When it is stirred, the illness is heat in the palms, cramping of the forearm and elbow, swelling of the armpit, and in severe cases propping fullness of the chest and flanks, violent pounding in the heart, a red face, yellow eyes, and incessant laughter. The illnesses produced by the vessels, which it governs, are: vexation of the heart, heart pain and heat in the palms. For these illnesses, drain when abundant, supplement when deficient, be quick when hot, retain when cold, apply moxibustion when deep, and when neither abundant nor deficient, take it at the meridian. When abundant, the inch opening is once as large as Renying; when deficient, the inch opening is instead smaller than Renying.

## Notes

The course and illnesses of the pericardium meridian. 三膲 is the sanjiao.
