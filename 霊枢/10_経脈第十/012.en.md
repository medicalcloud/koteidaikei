# 霊枢 経脈第十 §12 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The kidney foot lesser yin vessel starts below the little toe, runs obliquely to the center of the sole, comes out below Rangu, follows behind the inner ankle, separates to enter the heel, then rises into the calf, comes out at the inner edge of the back of the knee, rises along the back of the inner thigh, passes through the spine, belongs to the kidneys and connects with the bladder. Its straight part rises from the kidneys through the liver and diaphragm, enters the lungs, follows the throat and flanks the root of the tongue. Its branch comes out from the lungs, connects with the heart and pours into the chest. When it is stirred, the illness is hunger without desire to eat, a face like lacquered firewood, blood when coughing and spitting, wheezing and dyspnea, wanting to get up when seated, blurred vision as if seeing nothing, the heart feeling suspended as if hungry; when the qi is insufficient, frequent fear, the heart pounding as if someone were about to seize one; this is called bone reversal. The illnesses produced by the kidneys, which it governs, are: heat in the mouth, dry tongue, swollen throat, rising qi, a dry and painful throat, vexation of the heart, heart pain, jaundice, intestinal *pi*, pain along the spine and the back of the inner thigh, wilting with reversal, somnolence, and heat and pain in the soles. For these illnesses, drain when abundant, supplement when deficient, be quick when hot, retain when cold, apply moxibustion when sunken, and when neither abundant nor deficient, take it at the meridian. After moxibustion, make [the patient] eat raw meat, loosen the belt, let down the hair, and walk with a large staff and heavy shoes. When abundant, the inch opening is twice as large as Renying; when deficient, the inch opening is instead smaller than Renying.

## Notes

The course and illnesses of the kidney meridian, with care after moxibustion added.
