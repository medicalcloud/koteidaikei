# 霊枢 経脈第十 §16 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The liver foot *Jueyin* vessel starts at the border of the tuft of hair on the big toe, rises along the upper edge of the instep, [passes] one *cun* from the inner ankle, crosses eight *cun* above the ankle to come out behind the greater yin, rises to the inner edge of the back of the knee, follows the yin side of the thigh, enters the pubic hair, passes the genitals, reaches the lower abdomen, flanks the stomach, belongs to the liver and connects with the gallbladder, rises through the diaphragm, spreads in the flanks and ribs, follows behind the throat, rises into the nasopharynx, connects with the eye connection, rises to come out at the forehead, and meets the *Du* meridian at the vertex. Its branch descends from the eye connection inside the cheek and encircles the inside of the lips. Its branch again separates from the liver, passes through the diaphragm and rises to pour into the lungs. When it is stirred, the illness is lower back pain with inability to bend forward or back, *tui shan* in men, swelling of the lower abdomen in women, and in severe cases a dry throat and a dusty, colorless face. The illnesses produced by the liver, which it governs, are: fullness of the chest, vomiting and counterflow, swill diarrhea, fox *shan*, enuresis and urinary block. For these illnesses, drain when abundant, supplement when deficient, be quick when hot, retain when cold, apply moxibustion when sunken, and when neither abundant nor deficient, take it at the meridian. When abundant, the inch opening is once as large as Renying; when deficient, the inch opening is instead smaller than Renying.

## Notes

The course and illnesses of the liver meridian.
- The base's 「是肝所生」 is emended to 「是主肝所生」. Wikisource (*Huangdi neijing*) and Daizhige read 是主肝所生病者; the *Leijing* agrees with the base. All the other eleven meridians have the form 是主…所生病者, so 主 is supplied.
