# 霊枢 経脈第十 §10 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The small intestine hand greater yang vessel starts at the tip of the little finger, follows the outer side of the hand up to the wrist, comes out at the [wrist] protuberance, rises straight along the lower edge of the forearm bone, comes out between the two tendons on the inner side of the elbow, rises along the back of the outer upper arm, comes out at the shoulder joint, winds around the shoulder blade, crosses on top of the shoulder, enters the empty basin, connects with the heart, follows the throat, descends through the diaphragm, reaches the stomach and belongs to the small intestine. Its branch goes from the empty basin along the neck up to the cheek, reaches the outer canthus, and turns back to enter the ear. Its branch separates at the cheek, rises below the eye, reaches the nose and the inner canthus, and connects obliquely with the cheekbone. When it is stirred, the illness is a sore throat, swollen chin, inability to turn the head, the shoulder feeling as if pulled out and the upper arm as if broken. The illnesses produced by the fluids, which it governs, are: deafness, yellow eyes, swollen cheeks, and pain along the back of the outer neck, chin, shoulder, upper arm, elbow and forearm. For these illnesses, drain when abundant, supplement when deficient, be quick when hot, retain when cold, apply moxibustion when deep, and when neither abundant nor deficient, take it at the meridian. When abundant, Renying is twice as large as the inch opening; when deficient, Renying is instead smaller than the inch opening.

## Notes

The course and illnesses of the small intestine meridian.
