# 霊枢 経脈第十 §8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The spleen foot greater yin vessel starts at the tip of the big toe, follows the border of the white flesh on the inner side of the toe, passes behind the protruding bone, rises along the front edge of the inner ankle, rises into the calf, follows behind the shinbone, crosses to come out in front of the *jueyin*, rises along the front of the inner knee and thigh, enters the abdomen, belongs to the spleen and connects with the stomach, rises through the diaphragm, flanks the throat, connects with the root of the tongue and disperses below the tongue. Its branch again separates from the stomach, rises through the diaphragm and pours into the heart. When it is stirred, the illness is stiffness of the root of the tongue, vomiting after eating, pain in the stomach duct, abdominal distension, frequent belching; after passing stool or gas one feels relieved as if [the illness] had abated; the whole body is heavy. The illnesses produced by the spleen, which it governs, are: pain at the root of the tongue, inability to move the body, food not going down, vexation of the heart, tense pain below the heart, sloppy, conglomerating and draining diarrhea, water blockage, jaundice, inability to lie down; if one forces oneself to stand, swelling and reversal of the inner thigh and knee; and uselessness of the big toe. For these illnesses, drain when abundant, supplement when depleted, be quick when hot, retain when cold, apply moxibustion when sunken, and when neither abundant nor depleted, take it at the channel. When abundant, the inch opening is three times as large as Renying; when depleted, the inch opening is instead smaller than Renying.
