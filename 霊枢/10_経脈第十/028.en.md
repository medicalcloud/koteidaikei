# 霊枢 経脈第十 §28 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The separate [collateral] of hand greater yin is called Lieque. It starts in the partings above the wrist, runs alongside the greater yin meridian, enters straight into the palm and disperses into the fish border. Its illness: when excess, heat in the [area of the] sharp bone of the hand and the palm; when deficient, yawning and stretching, and frequent and incontinent urination. Take it half a *cun* from the wrist; it separates and runs to the *Yangming*. The separate [collateral] of hand lesser yin is called Tongli. One and a half *cun* from the wrist it separates and runs upward, follows the meridian into the heart, attaches to the root of the tongue and belongs to the eye connection. When excess, propping in the diaphragm; when deficient, inability to speak. Take it one *cun* behind the palm; it separates and runs to the greater yang. The separate [collateral] of the hand heart governor is called Neiguan. Two *cun* from the wrist it comes out between the two tendons, follows the meridian upward and attaches to the pericardium. When the heart connection is in excess, heart pain; when deficient, stiffness of the head. Take it between the two tendons.

## Notes

The first three of the fifteen collaterals (the three yin of the hand). 列欠 is Lieque (列缺).
- Following Zhang Jiebin's note in the *Leijing*: 手鋭 is the high bone behind the palm, and 欠㰦 is "opening the mouth and stretching the waist" (yawning and stretching). The *Leijing* also says 半寸 should be 寸半; the *Taisu* has 一寸半 and the *Jiayi jing* 一寸 (Lieque is generally placed one and a half *cun* from the wrist).
- For 去腕半寸 the Daizhige edition differs by one character (see collation).
