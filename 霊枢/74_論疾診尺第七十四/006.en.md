# 霊枢 論疾診尺第七十四 §6 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

In the changes of the four seasons and the alternate prevailing of cold and heat, double yin must turn to yang and double yang must turn to yin. Thus yin governs cold and yang governs heat; hence when cold is extreme it becomes heat, and when heat is extreme it becomes cold. So it is said: cold produces heat and heat produces cold. These are the transformations of yin and yang. Hence it is said: injured by cold in winter, one develops warm-heat illness in spring; injured by wind in spring, one develops diarrhea and intestinal flux in summer; injured by summer heat, one develops intermittent fever in autumn; injured by damp in autumn, one develops cough in winter. This is called the order of the four seasons.

## Notes

The end of the chapter. The *Leijing* note: when the qi of yin and yang reaches its extreme it must change, so extreme cold produces heat and extreme heat produces cold—the way of waxing, waning and alternation of heaven, earth and the seasons; the sense is nearly the same as in the *Suwen*'s *Yinyang yingxiang dalun*. 癉 is warm-heat illness.
- For 後泄, the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource, the Daizhige text and the *Lingshu jizhu* read 飧泄 (as does the *Suwen*'s *Yinyang yingxiang dalun*: 春傷於風，夏生飧泄). The *Leijing* reads 後泄 like the base, which makes sense as "diarrhea", so the base is translated.
