# 霊枢 論疾診尺第七十四 §2 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "Examine the slackness or tension, smallness or largeness, smoothness or roughness of the forearm and the firmness or brittleness of its flesh, and the form of the illness is determined. If there is slight swelling above the eye sockets, as if one had just risen from sleep, the neck vessels pulsate, there is occasional cough, and pressing on the hands and feet leaves a dent that does not rise, it is wind edema and skin distension. If the skin of the forearm is smooth and moist, it is wind. If the flesh of the forearm is weak, there is lassitude and a wish to lie quietly; if the flesh is wasted, with cold and heat, it cannot be cured. If the skin of the forearm is smooth and glossy as fat, it is wind. If it is rough, it is wind impediment. If it is coarse like the scales of a dried fish, it is overflowing water-fluid retention. If it is very hot and the pulse abundant and agitated, it is a warm illness; if the pulse is abundant and smooth, the illness is about to leave. If it is cold and the pulse small, there is diarrhea and shallow breathing. If it is burning, first hot and later cold, it is cold and heat; if it is first cold and after long [pressure] becomes hot, that too is cold and heat. If only the elbow is hot, it is hot above the waist; if only the hand, below the waist. If only the front of the elbow is hot, the front of the chest is hot; if only the back of the elbow, the shoulders and back. If only the middle of the forearm is hot, the waist and abdomen are hot. If it is hot three or four *cun* below the rough part behind the elbow, there are worms in the intestines. If the palm is hot, the abdomen is hot; if the palm is cold, the abdomen is cold. If there are blue blood vessels in the white flesh above the thenar eminence, there is cold in the stomach. If the forearm is burning hot and Renying large, there will be loss of blood. If the forearm is firm and large and the pulse very small, with shallow breathing and increasing oppression, death is immediate."

## Notes

Diagnosis by the skin of the forearm, translated following Zhang Jiebin's notes in the *Leijing*.
- 独調其尺: the pulse at the inch opening runs from the chi to the cun, so feeling the chi pulse reveals the inside; the condition of the flesh always shows behind the wrist, so examining the flesh of the chi region reveals the outside (*Leijing*).
- 目窠 is the "sleeping silkworm" area below the eye; 癕 is 壅, a newly arisen slight swelling; 窅而不起 means pressing leaves a dent (*Leijing*).
- 解㑊 means bodily weariness; in 水泆飲, 泆 equals 溢 (*Leijing*).
- 病且出也 follows the *Leijing*: if the pulse, though abundant, is also smooth, it is no longer agitated and the healthy qi is about to recover, so it will soon heal; 出 means gradually healing.
- 炬然 describes burning heat; 肘後粗以下三四寸 is the area below Sanli and above Neiguan (*Leijing*).
- 悗有加: yin deficiency is already extreme and oppression is added to it (*Leijing*).
- The base's 人膚滑 is emended to 尺膚滑. The *Leijing*, the *Lingshu jizhu*, the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource and the Daizhige text all read 尺膚滑. This passage is about examining the forearm skin, and 尺膚滑而沢脂者風也 follows, so the graphically similar 人 is taken as an error for 尺.
