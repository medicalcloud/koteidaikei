# 霊枢 官鍼第七 §4 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

There are twelve techniques of needling, corresponding to the twelve meridians. The first is called paired needling: place the hands on the chest and back, right on the painful place, insert one needle in front and one behind, to treat heart impediment; in needling this, insert the needles obliquely. The second is called repeated needling: needling pain with no fixed location that moves up and down; insert straight without withdrawing the needle, press the diseased place with the left hand following it, then withdraw the needle and needle again. The third is called relaxing needling: needle straight beside [the sinew], lift it forward and backward to relax the tension of the sinew, to treat sinew impediment. The fourth is called aligned needling: insert one straight and two beside it, to treat cold qi that is small and deep; it is also called triple needling, which treats impediment qi that is small and deep. The fifth is called scattered needling: insert one in the center and four beside it, keeping them shallow, to treat cold qi that is widespread. The sixth is called straight needling: pull up the skin and then needle, to treat shallow cold qi. The seventh is called transport needling: insert straight and withdraw straight, withdrawing the needle rarely and going deep, to treat abundant qi with heat. The eighth is called short needling: needling bone impediment, shaking slightly while going deeper, bringing the needle to the bone and rubbing the bone up and down. The ninth is called floating needling: insert beside and keep shallow, to treat tense and cold muscles. The tenth is called yin needling: needle both left and right, to treat cold reversal of the limbs; to hit cold reversal of the limbs, [needle] the lesser yin behind the ankle. The eleventh is called side needling: one straight and one beside, to treat lingering impediment that has lodged long. The twelfth is called assisting needling: insert straight and withdraw straight, withdrawing the needle frequently and keeping shallow to let blood; this is called treating abscesses and swellings.

## Notes

Twelve kinds of needling corresponding to the twelve meridians.
