# 霊枢 邪気蔵府病形第四 §36 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

When the kidney pulse is very tense, there is bone epilepsy; when slightly tense, sinking reversal and running piglet, the feet cannot be drawn in, and there is no passage front or back. When very slack, the spine feels broken; when slightly slack, there is *dong* [throughflux]: food is not transformed and comes back out after going down the throat. When very large, there is impotence; when slightly large, stone water, which from below the navel to the lower abdomen hangs heavily and, when it rises to the stomach duct, means death without cure. When very small, there is throughflux diarrhea; when slightly small, wasting thirst. When very slippery, there is urinary block with *tui*; when slightly slippery, bone wilting: when seated one cannot rise, and on rising one cannot see. When very rough, there is a great abscess; when slightly rough, absence of menses and sunken hemorrhoids."

## Notes

Illnesses of the kidney pulse; Qibo's speech ends here.
- 胃腕 is read as 胃脘 (stomach duct), as some sources have it (see collation).
- 腄腄然 is provisionally translated as hanging heavily; unverified (recorded in the list of deferred points).
