# 霊枢 癰疽第八十一 §1 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The Yellow Emperor said, "I have heard that when the intestines and stomach receive grain, the upper jiao sends out qi to warm the flesh divisions, nourish the joints and open the interstices. The middle jiao sends out qi like dew, which rises and pours into the valleys and seeps into the minute vessels; the fluids are harmonized and transformed, turning red to become blood. When the blood is harmonious, the minute vessels fill and overflow first, then pour into the collaterals; when these are all full, they pour into the meridians. Once yin and yang [ying-nutrient and wei-defense] are extended, they move with the breath; their movement has its order, their circuit its principle, in accord with heaven, never ceasing. Feel and regulate them: following deficiency, remove excess—draining makes [it] insufficient; quickness reduces the qi; retention [creates differences of] before and after; following excess, remove deficiency—supplementing makes [it] abundant. When blood and qi are regulated, the form and qi are kept steady. I already know whether blood and qi are balanced or not, but I do not know where abscesses and deep abscesses arise from, the times when they form or break, and how near or far the time of death is. How can these be measured? May I hear?"

## Notes

The beginning of the chapter. It describes how abscesses (*yong*) and deep abscesses (*ju*) arise, the names, symptoms, time of death and treatment of abscesses in each region, and the difference between *yong* and *ju*. This is the last chapter of the *Lingshu*.
- 中焦出気如露 is the ying-nutrient qi; 陰陽已張，因息乃行 means the ying-nutrient qi is transformed from the ancestral qi (Zhang Jiebin's note in the *Leijing*). Yang Shangshan's note in the *Taisu*: yin is the ying-nutrient qi, yang the wei-defensive qi; when the breath moves, the ying-nutrient and wei-defense qi move.
- 疾則気減，留則先後: in draining one should be quick, in supplementing one should retain; supplementing and draining differ in quickness and retention, before and after (*Leijing*).
- 持 in 形気乃持 means "settled" (*Leijing*).
- The base's 後虚去虚 is emended to 従実去虚. The *Taisu*, the *Jiayi jing*, the *Lingshu jizhu* and the Daizhige text read 従実去虚 (the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource 従虚). It forms a pair with 従虚去実，瀉則不足 as 従実去虚，補則有余, so 従実 is adopted. The base's 後 is taken as influenced by the preceding 先後, and 虚 by the following 去虚.
