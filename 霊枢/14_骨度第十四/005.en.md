# 霊枢 骨度第十四 §5 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Where the hair covers, from the skull to the nape is one *chi* two *cun*. From the hairline down to the chin is one *chi* long; in the gentleman it divides into three equal parts. From the laryngeal prominence down to the center of the empty basin is four *cun* long. From the empty basin down to the xiphoid is nine *cun* long; if longer, the lungs are large; if shorter, the lungs are small. From the xiphoid down to Tianshu is eight *cun* long; if longer, the stomach is large; if shorter, the stomach is small. From Tianshu down to the transverse bone is six and a half *cun* long; if longer, the winding intestine is wide and long; if shorter, it is narrow and short. The transverse bone is six and a half *cun* long. From the upper edge of the transverse bone down to the upper edge of the inner auxiliary bone is one *chi* eight *cun*. From the upper edge of the inner auxiliary bone down to its lower edge is three and a half *cun*. From the lower edge of the inner auxiliary bone down to the inner ankle is one *chi* three *cun*. From the inner ankle down to the ground is three *cun*. From the back of the knee down to the instep attachment is one *chi* six *cun*. From the instep attachment down to the ground is three *cun*. Hence when the bones' circumference is large, [these] are exceeded; when small, they are not reached.

## Notes

The bone measurements of the front of the body and the lower limbs.
- The base's 「君子終折」 is emended to 「君子参折」. The Lingshu editions and *Leijing* agree with the base (終), but the *Taisu* and *Jiayi jing* read 参折 (the *Jiayi jing* notes "also written 三 or 終"), and Yang Shangshan's note in the *Taisu* explains that the one *chi* from hairline to chin is divided into three (heaven, earth, man), equal in the gentleman. Zhang Jiebin's gloss (終 = end and beginning, 折 = balance) is hard to follow, so 参 is adopted following the *Taisu* and *Jiayi jing*.
