# 霊枢 陰陽二十五人第六十四 §8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The fire-form person belongs to the upper *zhi* and resembles the Red Emperor. In makeup he is red in color, broad in the flesh along the spine, sharp in the face, small in the head, with fine shoulders, back, thighs and belly, and small hands and feet; he walks steadily on the ground, is quick of mind, sways as he walks, has full flesh on the shoulders and back, has spirit, makes light of wealth, has little trustworthiness, thinks much, sees matters clearly, has a good complexion, is impatient, is not long-lived and dies suddenly. He endures spring and summer but not autumn and winter; affected in autumn and winter, illness arises. [He belongs to] the hand lesser yin, knotted [核核然: fire that cannot disperse]. The *zhi*-*zhi* person belongs to the left hand greater yang, the upper part, shallow-skinned [肌肌然]. The lesser-*zhi* person belongs to the right hand greater yang, the lower part, not turning back, or doubtful [慆慆然]. The right-*zhi* person belongs to the right hand greater yang, the upper part, leaping [鮫鮫然]. The *zhi*-half person belongs to the left hand greater yang, the lower part, branching and self-satisfied [支支頤頤然].

## Notes

The fire-form person and its four types.
- 䏖 is the flesh along the spine (*Leijing*).
- The base's 脱面 is emended to 鋭面. The *Leijing* and the *Lingshu jizhu* read 鋭面, the *Jiayi jing* 兌面 (兌 standing for 鋭). This fits the context that fire is pointed at the top (*Leijing* note 火上尖也), so the graphically similar 脱 is taken as an error for 鋭.
- The interlinear notes say *zhi zhi* is "also called *zhi zhi ren*, also called great *zhi*", that 鮫鮫然 is "also written 熊熊然", and that *zhi* half is "also called *zhi zhi*". The *Leijing* takes *zhi zhi* as the great *zhi*, assigned to the upper left.
