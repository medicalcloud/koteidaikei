# 霊枢 陰陽二十五人第六十四 §11 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The water-form person belongs to the upper *yu* and resembles the Black Emperor. In makeup he is black in color, with an uneven face, large head, angular chin, small shoulders and large belly; he moves his hands and feet, sways his body as he sets off, is long below the buttocks, and his back is long [延延然]. He neither respects nor fears, readily deceives others, and dies by execution. He endures autumn and winter but not spring and summer; affected in spring and summer, illness arises. [He belongs to] the foot lesser yin, moist [汗汗然]. The great-*yu* person belongs to the right foot greater yang, the upper part, self-pleased [頰頰然]. The lesser-*yu* person belongs to the left foot greater yang, the lower part, winding [紆紆然]. The common person belongs to the right foot greater yang, the lower part, clean [潔潔然]. The confined person belongs to the left foot greater yang, the upper part, settled and still [安安然]. Thus the twenty-five variations of the five-form persons are why people get the better of one another."

## Notes

The water-form person and its four types. Qibo's speech ends here.
- The base's 欺給人 is emended to 欺紿人. The *Leijing*, the *Jiayi jing* and the *Huangdi neijing* on Wikisource read 欺紿人 (the Daizhige text 紹). 欺紿 means "deceive" and fits the context, so the graphically similar 給 is taken as an error for 紿.
- On 戮死, the *Leijing* note says water has no constant disposition and so meets many calamities, while the *Lingshu jizhu* says such people often die of exhaustion from overexertion; the interpretations differ. It is translated literally here.
- 衆 means "ordinary" and 桎 equals 窒, "confined, blocked" (*Leijing*). The interlinear note says the common person is "also called *jia zhi ren*".
- 衆之所以相欺者 follows the *Leijing* note: when endowments are one-sided, people cannot avoid getting the better of one another in strength and weakness.
