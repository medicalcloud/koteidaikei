# 霊枢 九鍼十二原第一 §8 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qibo said, "The five zang organs have five transport points each, five times five making twenty-five; the six fu organs have six transport points each, six times six making thirty-six. There are twelve meridians and fifteen collaterals, twenty-seven qi in all, moving up and down. Where it emerges is the well, where it flows is the brook, where it pours is the stream, where it moves is the river, where it enters is the uniting point; where the twenty-seven qi move is all at the five transport points. The meeting places of the joints number three hundred sixty-five; one who knows the essentials ends in a single word; one who does not scatters without end. What are called joints are where the spirit qi wanders, exits and enters; they are not skin, flesh, sinews or bones. Look at the complexion and examine the eyes to know whether [the qi] is scattered or restored; unify one's form, listen to its movement and stillness, to know whether it is pathogenic or proper. The right [hand] governs pushing, the left holds and controls; when the qi arrives, withdraw. Whenever one is about to use needles, one must first examine the pulse and see whether the qi is severe or mild; only then can one treat. When the qi of the five zang organs is already cut off within, and the needler instead fills the outside, this is called double exhaustion; double exhaustion means certain death, and the death is quiet; it is because the treater acts against the qi, taking the armpit and chest. When the qi of the five zang organs is already cut off outside, and the needler instead fills the inside, this is called counterflow reversal; counterflow reversal means certain death, and the death is agitated; it is because the treater instead takes the four extremities. As for the harms of needling: if one hits [the illness] but does not withdraw, the essence leaks; if one does not hit it but withdraws, qi is brought. When essence leaks, the illness grows worse with debility; when qi is brought, abscesses and sores arise.

## Notes

The five transport points (well, brook, stream, river, uniting) of the five zang organs and six fu organs and the twenty-seven qi, examining the pulse before needling, and the harms of double exhaustion and counterflow reversal. Qibo's speech continues through unit 10.
- The base's 「所以為合」 is emended to 「所入為合」. Wikisource (*Huangdi neijing*), Daizhige, the *Taisu*, *Leijing*, *Jiayi jing* and *Lingshu jizhu* all read 所入為合, which parallels 所出為井, 所溜為滎, etc.; 入 is adopted.
- The base's 「害中而去則致気」 is emended to 「不中而去則致気」. The Lingshu editions and *Jiayi jing* agree with the base (害中), but chapter 21 (*Hanre bing*) has the same sentence as 不中而去則致気, and Zhang Jiebin's note in the *Leijing* says: "害中而不去 means withdrawing the needle too late, 不中而去 too early; the *Hanre bing* reading 不中 is correct and 害中 here is an error." It pairs with 中而不去則精泄, so 不 is adopted.
