# 霊枢 九鍼十二原第一 §9 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

The five zang organs have six fu organs; the six fu organs have twelve source points; the twelve source points emerge at the four gates [elbows and knees], and the four gates govern the treatment of the five zang organs. When the five zang organs are ill, one should take the twelve source points. The twelve source points are how the five zang organs receive the qi and flavors of the three hundred sixty-five joints. When the five zang organs are ill, [the response] appears at the twelve source points; each of the twelve source points has its place of emergence; clearly knowing the source points and seeing their responses, one knows the harm to the five zang organs. Lesser yin within yang is the lungs; its source emerges at Dayuan; Dayuan, two. Greater yang within yang is the heart; its source emerges at Daling; Daling, two. Lesser yang within yin is the liver; its source emerges at Taichong; Taichong, two. Extreme yin within yin is the spleen; its source emerges at Taibai; Taibai, two. Greater yin within yin is the kidneys; its source emerges at Taixi; Taixi, two. The source of the fat emerges at Jiuwei; Jiuwei, one. The source of the membranes emerges at Boyang; Boyang, one. These twelve source points govern the treatment of illnesses of the five zang organs and six fu organs. For distension take the three yang; for swill diarrhea take the three yin.

## Notes

The twelve source points (two for each of the five zang organs, plus the sources of the fat and the membranes) and their use in treating illnesses of the zang organs.
- The relation to later meridian theory (for example, the heart's source being Daling, a point of the hand *Jueyin* pericardium meridian) is not discussed.
