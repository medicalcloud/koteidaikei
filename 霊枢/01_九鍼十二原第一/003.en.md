# 霊枢 九鍼十二原第一 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Whenever needles are used, fill what is depleted, drain what is full, remove what is stagnant and old, and deplete what pathogens prevail in. The *Great Essentials* says, 'Slow then quick makes repletion; quick then slow makes depletion. Speaking of repletion and depletion, it is as if present, as if absent; examining after and before, as if surviving, as if perishing; producing depletion and repletion, as if gaining, as if losing.' For the essentials of depletion and repletion the nine needles are most wondrous; the time for supplementing and draining is managed with the needle. Draining: hold it firmly and insert it, release it and let it out, pushing aside the yang to receive the needle, so that the pathogenic qi can drain. If one presses and withdraws the needle, this is called warming within: the blood cannot disperse and the qi cannot get out. Supplementing: follow it. In following it, act as if 妄 [forgetting] it, as if going, as if pressing, like a mosquito or gadfly alighting, as if staying, as if returning; withdraw like a snapped string, letting the left [hand] follow the right; then the qi stops, the outer gate is closed, and the inner qi becomes replete. There must be no lingering blood; if there is, take it quickly and remove it. In the way of holding the needle, firmness is the treasure; with straight fingers, needle straight, not letting the needle go left or right; the spirit is on the finest detail, the attention is on the patient; examine the blood vessels carefully and needle them without danger. At the moment of needling, attention must be on the suspended yang and the two guards; the spirit attends without leaving, knowing whether the illness persists or is gone. Blood vessels lie crosswise at the transport points; looking at them they alone are clear, feeling them they alone are hard.

## Notes

The principles of supplementing and draining, the techniques, and the attitude in holding the needle.
- For 妄 in 若妄之, some other sources have 忘 "forget" (see the collation notes), and it is read in that sense.
- There are various interpretations of 懸陽 and 両衛. The translation follows the common view that the suspended yang is the nose and the two guards are above the eyebrows, but this has not been verified (recorded in the list of deferred points).
