# 霊枢 九鍼十二原第一 §3 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Whenever needles are used, fill what is deficient, drain what is full, remove what is stagnant and old, and deplete what pathogens prevail in. The *Great Essentials* says, 'Slow then quick makes repletion; quick then slow makes deficiency. Speaking of repletion and deficiency, it is as if present, as if absent; examining after and before, as if surviving, as if perishing; producing deficiency and repletion, as if gaining, as if losing.' For the essentials of deficiency and repletion the nine needles are most wondrous; the time for supplementing and draining is managed with the needle. Draining: hold it firmly and insert it, release it and let it out, pushing aside the yang to receive the needle, so that the pathogenic qi can drain. If one presses and withdraws the needle, this is called warming within: the blood cannot disperse and the qi cannot get out. Supplementing: follow it. In following it, act as if forgetting it, as if going, as if pressing, like a mosquito or gadfly alighting, as if staying, as if returning; withdraw like a snapped string, letting the left [hand] follow the right; then the qi stops, the outer gate is closed, and the inner qi becomes replete. There must be no lingering blood; if there is, take it quickly and remove it. In the way of holding the needle, firmness is the treasure; with straight fingers, needle straight, not letting the needle go left or right; the spirit is on the finest detail, the attention is on the patient; examine the blood vessels carefully and needle them without danger. At the moment of needling, attention must be on the suspended yang [raising the spirit qi] and the two guards [the wei-defensive qi of the surface and the guarding spleen qi of the zang organs]; the spirit attends without leaving, knowing whether the illness persists or is gone. Blood vessels lie crosswise at the transport points; looking at them they alone are clear, feeling them they alone are hard.

## Notes

The principles of supplementing and draining, the techniques, and the attitude in holding the needle.
- Following Zhang Jiebin's note in the *Leijing*, 懸陽 is taken as "raising the spirit qi" (懸 = raise, 陽 = spirit qi), and 両衛 as the two guards: the wei-defensive qi of the surface (yang) and the spleen qi guarding the zang organs (yin). The *Taisu* reads 両衡, and Yang Shangshan's note takes 懸陽 as the nose (hanging below the 衡) and 両衡 as above the eyebrows.
- The base's 「若妄之」 is emended to 「若忘之」. The Siku edition, the *Taisu* and the *Jiayi jing* have 忘; the *Leijing* and the *Lingshu jizhu* agree with the base (妄). Reading "follow it as if forgetting it" fits better and the older *Taisu* and *Jiayi jing* support it, so 忘 is adopted.
